Practice run - I am still in training

Insurance CST

A practice walk-through with a friend

A new financial consultant in training, walking you through how I would map out a protection plan when I get licensed.

Open warm. Smile, sit on the same side of the table if possible. State the friend's name. Time check: aim for 20 minutes max for the whole deck.
Read this out loud

I am in training. This is practice.

I cannot sell you anything yet. I am not licensed.

What I am asking for is 20 minutes of your time and your honest feedback.

If something is unclear, boring, or feels pushy - tell me. That is how I get better.

Goal of this session: I deliver, you observe, and at the end you tell me what landed and what did not.

This is the most important slide. If you skip the honest framing, the friend feels sold to and gives you fake-polite feedback. The whole point of this exercise is real feedback - earn it by being upfront.

Before I start - one question

If something happened to you tomorrow - an illness, an accident - how long could life carry on the way it is now before something broke?

Pause. Listen. Most people have not actually thought about it. That is normal - it is exactly why this conversation exists.

  • If they say "a few months" - ask what would break first.
  • If they say "I don't know" - that is the most honest answer. Use it.
Do not jump to product. Sit with the silence. The point of this question is to anchor the rest of the talk in their reality, not in insurance jargon.

Your income can stop in two ways

Temporary

You can recover

Sacked, retrenched, between jobs.

Solution: 3 to 6 months of expenses sitting in the bank.

Permanent

You may not recover

Death. Long-term disability. Critical illness. Serious accident.

Solution: savings will not catch up. This is what insurance is for.

You are probably already handling the temporary side. The question is whether the permanent side is handled.

Keep tone calm and matter of fact. This is not about scaring them. It is about drawing a clear line between "your savings can fix this" and "your savings cannot fix this."

A simple scaling question

If you needed help one day - say a major medical bill - could your family lend you...

  • $1,000? Probably yes.
  • $10,000? Maybe. With effort.
  • $1,000,000? Almost certainly not.

Roughly $25,000 per year of expenses across 40 years of life is $1M of total exposure.

The honest line: if you do not have a financial plan, your family becomes the financial plan.

The math is not the point. The point is to make a million dollars feel concrete and personal, not abstract. Walk them through the numbers slowly. Let it land.

Three ways to handle a risk like this

1. Avoid

Stay away from danger

You cannot avoid getting sick. You cannot avoid getting old. Not realistic.

2. Retain

Pay it yourself

Means having the full lump sum sitting in cash today. Almost no one has this.

3. Transfer

Insurance

Use about 10% of your income to protect the other 90%.

Once 1 and 2 are off the table, 3 is the only honest answer.

Walk them through the elimination logic. Do not skip steps - the close lives in the elimination, not in the product. By the time you reach option 3, the friend is already nodding.

Where insurance fits

Top - Wealth Management

Estate, legacy. Later in life.

Middle - Wealth Accumulation

Savings and investments.

Foundation - Risk Management

Build this first.

Why foundation first

If you invest before you protect, one $200K medical bill can wipe out years of savings.

The clients who reach retirement on time are almost always the ones who covered the foundation first.

The pyramid is the conceptual hook for the whole talk. Do not rush it. If they only remember one image from this session, this is the one.

The four risks that can derail a plan

1

Death

Family loses your future income.

2

Disability

You cannot work, but bills keep coming.

3

Critical Illness

Cancer, stroke, heart. Years of recovery, no income.

4

Hospital / Accident

The bills themselves.

Each one needs its own type of cover. We will walk through them next.

Do not stack scary statistics. Just name the four risks calmly and move on. Each one gets its own slide.
Coverage 1 of 5

Life Insurance

What it does: if you pass on or become totally disabled, a lump sum goes to your family.

Picture this

You earn $5K a month. Your family relies on that. If you are gone tomorrow, the rent, food, parents' allowance - all of that needs to keep happening for years.

A life policy covers that loss of future income with a single payout.

AIA product: AIA Pro Lifetime Protector / AIA Secure Flexi Term, depending on whether you want lifetime cover or pure term.

Plain English. Do not get into IRR, bonus rates, or par fund details - the friend does not care and you cannot legally explain them yet anyway. Stick to "what does it do for you."
Coverage 2 of 5

Critical Illness

What it does: when you are diagnosed with one of 30+ major illnesses, you get a lump sum payout.

Why this is the one most people skip

Cancer recovery in Singapore is on average 3 to 5 years. During that time you are not earning, but expenses are still running.

Hospital bills are covered separately. The CI payout is what replaces your lost income while you recover.

AIA product: AIA Absolute Critical Cover / AIA Power Critical Cover, paired with the life policy as a rider.

Most clients get this confused with hospital. Spend an extra 30 seconds on the distinction: hospital plan pays the hospital. CI plan pays YOU.
Coverage 3 of 5

Hospital Plan

What it does: covers your hospital bills. As-charged, up to high limits.

The simple line

Hospital plan pays the hospital. CI plan pays you.

If you are admitted overnight, this is the policy that handles the bill. Singapore private ward bills routinely hit $50K to $200K. MediShield Life alone does not stretch that far.

AIA product: AIA HealthShield Gold Max - upgrade your MediShield to private, public A, or public B ward level.

Lock this in early. Once a medical condition shows up, you cannot upgrade later. That is the urgency for hospital plans, and it is a real one - not a manufactured one.
Coverage 4 of 5

Personal Accident

What it does: covers the everyday outpatient stuff that hospital plans miss.

The gap-filler

Sprain your ankle. X-ray, MRI, physio. $3K to $4K, no admission. Hospital plan does not pay.

Lose a limb in an accident. Life and CI may not pay. Personal accident does.

AIA product: AIA Solitaire PA Secure / AIA Personal Accident plans.

This is the one most young Singaporeans underrate. Cheap. Covers the awkward middle ground that nothing else covers.
Coverage 5 of 5 - briefly

Saving for retirement

Once protection is sorted, the next layer is what you set aside for the long term.

The "old age" problem

This is the only one of the six big risks where insurance is not the answer. Retirement is funded through long-term savings and investments.

Aim for around 20% of take-home pay going toward this once protection is in place.

AIA product: AIA Pro Achiever 3.0 (ILP) / AIA Smart Wealth Builder (endowment) / AIA Retirement Saver - depending on goal and risk profile.

Briefly mention this so they see the full picture, but do not pivot the deck into wealth accumulation. Today is about protection.

How much cover is "enough"

RiskIndustry guideline
Death10x annual income
Disability~75% of monthly income until retirement
Critical Illness$100K + 3 to 5x annual income
Accident5x annual income
HospitalisationBased on ward preference

These are rough industry guidelines, not rules. The actual numbers depend on your life - whether you have kids, a mortgage, parents to support.

Do not promise specific numbers. State the guideline, anchor it to the friend's situation, and move on. You are not licensed to recommend specific cover amounts yet.

If we were planning for someone like you...

Illustrative only - real numbers come later, with proper fact-find.

Example: $4K a month take-home, mid 20s, no dependents yet

  • Life + CI (term + rider) - around $80 to $130 / month
  • Hospital (private ward upgrade) - around $50 to $90 / month
  • Personal accident - around $15 to $30 / month
  • Total protection budget - around $200 / month (~5% of take-home)

The "1/3 rule" framing - 1/3 short term needs, 1/3 medium term, 1/3 long term (protection + accumulation).

Keep numbers loose and clearly illustrative. The point is to show the order of magnitude, not to quote a real premium. If they push for exact numbers, redirect: "I cannot quote you - that needs a licensed plan and a fact-find."

Three options. Always three. It removes the binary "buy / do not buy" - replaced with "which of three." When this becomes a real meeting, the friend almost always picks the middle one.

Questions you might ask me

"Don't I already have this through my company?"

Company cover ends the day the job ends. By then a diagnosis can make you uninsurable.

"Isn't it cheaper to just save?"

For a $200K bill - yes, if you already have $200K. For most people, the math does not work.

"What if nothing ever happens?"

The hospital and term policies cover for the years you need them. The whole life policy still has cash value at 60+.

"I want to think about it / ask my parents."

Fair. I would rather meet your parents with you - that way they get to ask me directly, not get a secondhand version.

These are the four objections that come up in 80% of real conversations. Practice answering them out loud. The point of the practice run is not perfection - it is hearing yourself say the answers so they sound like you when it counts.

Soft next step

When I am properly licensed - probably in a few weeks - would you be open to going through this again, this time with real numbers and a proper fact-find? No commitment to buy anything.

  • If yes: log it. Follow up the day you are licensed.
  • If no: thank them anyway. The feedback is what you came for.
  • If maybe: ask what would make it a yes - that is the most useful answer of all.
Do not push. The whole point of starting with the honest framing is so the soft close lands without feeling like a bait-and-switch. If they say no, you still got the practice rep and the feedback - that is a win.

Three quick questions. Honest answers only.

  1. Which slide or section landed best?
  2. Which part felt boring or confusing?
  3. If a friend asked you what I do, how would you describe it in one sentence?

Write the answers down or send them to me afterwards. This is the part of the session that actually makes me a better advisor.

Capture the feedback in writing - notes app, voice memo, anything. Within 24 hours, sit with your mentor and walk through the three answers. This is how the practice rep compounds.
