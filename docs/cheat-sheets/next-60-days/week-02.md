# Week 2: Your Voice I - Intent & Positioning - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** The first 60 seconds decide the next 60 minutes. Your intent statement, your positioning, and your profile are the three artefacts that engineer that opening instead of hoping for it.

> **Week 2 KPI:** Intent statement v2 delivered to 3 real people.

## The intent statement - 4 ingredients

```
Hook -> Clear impression -> Pattern interrupt -> Buy-in
```

| Ingredient | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Hook** | Stop the scroll - first sentence pulls them in | *"Before we start, I want to put something on the table upfront..."* |
| **2. Clear impression** | Pick ONE of: objective / sincere / extremely competent | Objective is the safest starting choice |
| **3. Pattern interrupt** | Something they did NOT expect to hear from an advisor | *"Not every one of my company's products will be the best fit for you"* |
| **4. Buy-in** | Get them to agree to the frame | *"Does that sound fair?"* |

**Lady's Skirt principle:** long enough to cover the essentials, short enough to be interesting. ~30-45 seconds spoken. 3-4 sentences max.

**Three pattern-interrupt archetypes - pick one:**
- **Objectivity:** *"If I find something better solved elsewhere, I'll be honest about it."*
- **Transparent-commercial:** *"I only want you to work with me if the value you get is more than the value I'm getting."*
- **Selection:** *"I don't work with everyone. So today is as much me finding out if we're a fit as it is you finding out about me."*

**4 traps to avoid:**

| Trap | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Task-oriented (*"I review portfolios and recommend products"*) | Prospects care about the benefit, not your process |
| Industry jargon (*"holistic wealth accumulation"*) | Alienates - they stop listening |
| Generic (*"I help people with financial planning"*) | Blends with every other FC they've met |
| Too long (3 paragraphs) | Loses punch by sentence two |

**Where it belongs:** top of the frame - right after small talk settles, before you open the CST. NOT on a cold call (no frame yet) and NOT mid-meeting (the frame has formed).

## The "what do you do?" answer - 5 profiles

Formula: **"I help [who] do [what outcome] so [why it matters]."**

| Profile | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Business owners** | *"I help business owners find inefficiencies in their portfolios so more of their resources and time go back into their business."* |
| **Young professionals** | *"I help young professionals set up a structured financial system and automate their investing so they can focus on their careers."* |
| **C-suite / HNW** | *"I help high-net-worth clients optimise inefficiencies and structure their legacy for the next generation."* |
| **Parents** | *"I help parents secure their family's risk protection and grow their wealth 10-15% year-on-year so their kids get a head start."* |
| **Pre-retirees** | *"I help affluent individuals secure their desired retirement lifestyle 5 to 10 years earlier than planned."* |

**The swap test:** read your statement aloud. Could 10 other AIA FCs say the same line and have it work? If yes, it isn't yours - the fix is specificity. Narrower audience, sharper outcome, more concrete interrupt.

## The 7 commandments of positioning - the 3 that move first

Branding = how they think of you BEFORE the meeting (weeks-to-years). Positioning = how they think of you AFTER the meeting (every appointment).

Three commandments do disproportionate work in the first 60 days:

| # | Move | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Always frame** | Narrate the meeting up front - they know the route, gut comes down |
| **6** | **Call the elephant** | Name the uncomfortable topic both of you feel before they do - signals you've handled it 1,000 times |
| **7** | **Detach from outcome** | *"The more you need a prospect, the less likely you are to close them"* - volume is the structural antidote |

**Frame script:** *"Just to give you an idea of what to expect today - first 30 minutes is core principles. We won't look at products today unless you ask. After today, I'll build a recommendation, we'll meet again to walk through it, and final step is implementation when the time's right. Sound okay?"*

**Sub-rule: never say *"I'm not trying to sell you anything."*** The only people who say that line are the ones trying to sell something. Replace with: *"Of course I'd love to work with you if it makes sense. But today is about finding out whether it does."*

**3-move ladder when sales resistance is up:** Frame again -> Call the elephant -> Detach. If all three fail, it's a fit issue, not a positioning issue.

## Profile as compound asset - 6 elements + KLR

Every personal brand needs three signals simultaneously:

```
Known -> Loved -> Respected
```

- **Known** = profile name + category + bio
- **Loved** = stories - PPVV personality (Day 11)
- **Respected** = posts - frameworks, client wins, insights

Missing any one breaks the conversion: *"fun person but would I trust her?"* / *"competent but robotic"* / *"I like her content but what does she do?"*

**The 6 profile elements - score each 1-5, fix the lowest tonight:**

| Element | Do |
|---|---|
| **Profile picture** | Professional faceshot, smile visible |
| **Name** | Real name (add `real` or `official` if taken) |
| **Category** | Financial services / Insurance agent / Public figure |
| **Bio** | Why / What / How structure - 150 chars |
| **Link** | Single CTA - Telegram, lead magnet, booking link |
| **Highlights** | Always-on proof - wins, BTS, FAQs, testimonials |

**Why / What / How bio:**
- **Why** = credibility (results OR niche specificity if new)
- **What** = the promise - *"I help [who] do [outcome] without [common objection]"*
- **How** = single CTA - link, channel, lead magnet

**Authority vs Social:** Authority converts easier but grows slower (lead with profession + niche). Social grows easier but converts slower (lead with interest / identity, profession in category field). Pick based on your stage.

## 5 Levers of Familiarity - profile is 1 of 5

1. **Persistent daily prospecting** (calls, DMs, voice notes)
2. **Referrals** (borrowed trust)
3. **Networking** (events, communities)
4. **Company + brand familiarity** (AIA, team)
5. **Personal branding** (profile, content)

**The compound:** a prospect who has seen your profile (5), heard about you from a friend (2), AND received your DM (1) replies at ~10x the rate of a single-lever touch.

**Where new FCs break:** they obsess over lever 5 alone while ignoring 1, 2, 3. The profile polishes; the pipeline stays empty. Profile is necessary, not sufficient.

## Numbers worth memorising

- **30-45 seconds** spoken length for an intent statement.
- **150 characters** - the bio budget.
- **1:21 ratio** - 1 feed post + 21 stories across the week (3 stories/day).
- **1,000 / 100 / 10** - 30-day targets (followers / conversations / sales). Aspirational - point yourself in this direction.
- **2:1 to 5:1** - healthy follower:following ratio for an advisor profile.
- **10x reply rate** when 3 familiarity levers stack vs 1.

## Common failure modes

- **Wearing all three "impressions" at once.** Pick objective OR sincere OR extremely competent. Trying all three reads as none.
- **Pattern interrupt that 10 other FCs could use.** Run the swap test. Fix with specificity, not new words.
- **Saying *"I'm not trying to sell you anything."*** Active gut-raiser. Replace with the commercial-honesty line.
- **Forcing the intent statement on a cold call.** No frame to set yet. It belongs at the top of a warm meeting, not before one.
- **Building the perfect profile while pipeline is empty.** Profile is 1 of 5 levers. Without daily prospecting + referral work + networking, the profile compounds into nothing.
- **Tasking the *"what do you do?"* answer (*"I review portfolios"*) instead of outcome-framing it (*"I help [who] do [outcome] so [why it matters]"*).
- **Attached positioning - voice tightens, pacing speeds up.** The structural antidote is volume (5-7 appointments/week). The behavioural bridge is lowering voice mid-sentence and meaning *"this is entirely up to you."*
