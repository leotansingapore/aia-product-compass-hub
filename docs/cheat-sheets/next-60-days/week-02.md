# Week 2: Your Voice I - Intent & Positioning - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** the first minute of a meeting shapes how the prospect hears the rest of it. The intent statement lets you plan that minute instead of leaving it to chance.

> **Week 2 KPI:** Intent statement v2 delivered to 3 real people.

## The intent statement: 4 ingredients

```
Hook -> Clear impression -> Pattern interrupt -> Buy-in
```

| Ingredient | Job | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Hook | First sentence introduces you and names no product | *"I want to put something on the table upfront..."* |
| 2. Clear impression | Pick one: objective, sincere, or so competent the messy paperwork gets done | Objective is the usual start: believable and low-pressure |
| 3. Pattern interrupt | A line they don't expect from an advisor | *"Not every one of my company's products will be the best fit for you."* |
| 4. Buy-in | They agree to how the meeting will run | *"Does that sound fair?"* |

Lady's Skirt rule: long enough to cover the essentials, short enough to be interesting. 30-45 seconds spoken, three or four sentences at most.

Three pattern-interrupt archetypes, pick one:
- Objectivity: *"If I find something is better solved elsewhere, I'll be honest about it."*
- Transparent-commercial: *"I only want you to work with me if the value you get is more than the value I'm getting."*
- Selection: *"I don't work with everyone. So today is as much me finding out if we're a fit as it is you finding out about me."*

| Trap | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| Task-oriented (*"I review your portfolio and recommend products"*) | Prospects care what they get out of it |
| Jargon (*"holistic wealth accumulation"*) | They stop listening |
| Generic (*"I help people with their financial planning"*) | Every advisor has said it |
| Too long (three paragraphs) | Lost by the second sentence |

When: as early as you can in the first appointment, once small talk settles and before the CST or fact-find. A shorter version works on an appointment-setting call. Skip it on a cold call nobody has agreed to, and don't start it halfway through a meeting.

## The "what do you do?" answer: 5 profiles

Formula: *"I help [who] do [what outcome] so [why it matters]."*

| Profile | Answer |
|---|---|
| Business owners | *"I help business owners find the inefficiencies in their portfolios, so more of their money and time go back into the business."* |
| Young professionals | *"I help young professionals set up a structured financial system and automate their investing, so they can focus on their careers."* |
| C-suite / HNW | *"I help high-net-worth clients tidy up the inefficiencies in their portfolios and plan their legacy for the next generation."* |
| Parents | *"I help parents get their family's protection sorted and build a plan to grow their savings, so their kids get a head start and they get their weekends back."* |
| Pre-retirees | *"I help people in their 50s work out what retirement lifestyle they want and how early they can realistically get there."* |

**No return figures or retirement dates.** You can't guarantee either, and promised returns are what MoneySense tells consumers to be wary of.

Swap test: could 10 other AIA FCs put their name on your line and have it still work? If yes, get more specific: narrower audience, sharper outcome, more concrete interrupt.

## Positioning: the 3 commandments that move first

Branding is how people think of you before the meeting and moves over weeks to years. Positioning is how they think of you after, and moves every appointment. Of the 7 commandments, three do most of the work in your first 60 days. The other four come in Week 8.

| # | Move | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Always frame | Tell them how the meeting will run, so their guard comes down |
| 6 | Call the elephant | Name the awkward topic first, so you sound like you've handled it many times |
| 7 | Detach from outcome | *"The more you need a prospect, the less likely you are to close them."* Volume is the fix. |

Frame script: *"Just to give you an idea of what to expect today, we'll focus on the core principles for the first 30 minutes. We won't look at specific products unless you have questions. After today I'll build a recommendation and we'll meet again to walk through it. Final step is implementation, when the time's right. Does that sound okay?"*

Never say *"I'm not trying to sell you anything."* Salespeople say it all the time, so it raises the guard. Use: *"Honestly, of course I'd love to work with you if it makes sense. But today is really about finding out whether it does."*

3-move ladder when resistance rises: frame again, call the elephant, detach. If none works, it's a fit problem. End politely and keep the door open.

## Your profile: KLR and the 6 elements

```
Known -> Loved -> Respected
```

- Known: profile name, category, bio
- Loved: stories (PPVV: passion, pain, values, vision)
- Respected: posts (frameworks, client wins, insights)

Miss one and it shows: *"fun, but would I trust her?"*, *"competent but robotic"*, *"I like her content, but what does she do?"*

Score each element 1-5 and fix the lowest tonight:

| Element | Do |
|---|---|
| Profile picture | Headshot or candid shot, smile visible, plain background |
| Name | Real name (add "real" or "official" if taken) |
| Category | Financial service / Insurance agent / Public figure |
| Bio | Why / What / How in 150 characters |
| Link | One call to action: Telegram, YouTube or a free download |
| Highlights | Client wins, behind-the-scenes, FAQs, testimonials |

Bio: Why = credibility (figures only if you can back them up; if new, a specific niche). What = *"I help [who] do [outcome] without [common worry]."* How = one call to action. No return, income or timeline promises: MAS's September 2025 digital advertising guidelines cover social media, and a bio counts.

Authority converts more easily but grows slower (profession and niche in the bio). Social grows faster but converts slower (interest or identity in the bio, profession in the category field). Have a niche? Start with Authority. No niche yet? Go Social.

## 5 levers of familiarity

1. Persistent daily prospecting (calls, DMs, voice notes)
2. Referrals (borrowed trust)
3. Networking (events, communities)
4. Company and brand (AIA, your team)
5. Personal branding (profile, content)

A prospect who has seen your profile, heard about you from a friend and got your DM is much warmer than one who has had only one of those (the mere exposure effect). New FCs polish lever 5 and ignore 1, 2 and 3. The profile looks great and the pipeline stays empty.

## Numbers worth memorising

- 30-45 seconds: intent statement length.
- 150 characters: the bio.
- 1 post a week, 3 stories a day.
- 1,000 / 100 / 10: the source course's first-month stretch target (followers / conversations / sales).
- Story-selling waits until week 4 of posting.

## Common failure modes

- Trying all three impressions at once. Pick one.
- A pattern interrupt 10 other FCs could use. Run the swap test.
- Putting a return figure or a retirement date in your answer or bio.
- Forcing the intent statement onto a cold call.
- Building the perfect profile while the pipeline is empty.
- Describing your process (*"I review portfolios"*) when the prospect wants the outcome.
- Getting attached mid-meeting: voice tightens, pace speeds up. Lower your voice, slow down and mean *"this is entirely up to you."*
