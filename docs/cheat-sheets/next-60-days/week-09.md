# Week 9: The Close - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** You don't close at the end. You close in small commitments throughout, then ask for the decision the prospect has already been moving toward. A good close is almost boring from the outside.

> **Week 9 KPI:** 5 objection drills recorded; first case closed.

---

## Trial closes - the ladder up to the main close

A new FC's default: warm-up -> Fact-Find -> Present -> Close in one moment at minute 65. The prospect hits the full weight of the decision at once.

**Fix:** 3-5 trial closes scattered across the meeting. Each is a tiny commitment - *"if you decided to do this, would [X] work?"* By the time the main close arrives, they've agreed to the underlying shape 5-7 times. The final yes is confirmation, not decision.

| When | Trial close stem |
|---|---|
| **Mid-Fact-Find** | *"If we were to put something in place to address this - would you see it sitting more in the $300, $500, or $800 monthly range?"* |
| **Mid-pitch** | *"Looking at this coverage structure - does the 20-year term feel right, or would you prefer something shorter?"* |
| **Post-recommendation** | *"If this all made sense and we moved on it - would you want the start date end of this month, or start of next?"* |
| **Pre-close** | *"Before we get into final details - is there anything still unclear we should address first?"* |

Read each answer: clean = tracking. Hesitation = hidden concern (Iceberg). Push-back = reinforce the underlying point.

## The 4 main-close structures + DISC fit

| Profile | Trial | Assumptive | Choice | Urgency | Summary / Reassurance |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| **D** | OK | Best | Too many options | Real deadline | Keep short |
| **I** | OK | After warm yes | They hedge | Too pushy | Best (story + social proof) |
| **S** | OK | Pressure - avoid | Best (feels safe) | Withdraws | Assurance-heavy |
| **C** | OK | Pushy - avoid | Loves options | Only if real | With data backing |

**The single most common Week-9 mistake:** defaulting to Assumptive because it feels confident. Half your prospects aren't D.

### Close-language stems by structure

- **Assumptive** - *"Great - I'll proceed with the application. Credit card or bank transfer for the first premium?"*
- **Choice** - *"Plan A at $400/mo with $500K, or Plan B at $550/mo with $750K - which one feels right?"* (Max 3 options. Beyond 3 = paralysis.)
- **Urgency (only when real)** - *"Underwriting rules update in 2 weeks - if we submit before then, you're grandfathered into current terms."*
- **Summary** - *"Based on everything we've talked about - your priorities around [X], the structure that fits your cashflow, the coverage that hits what matters most to you - I believe this fits. Shall we start the application?"*

**Ethical rule on Urgency:** never manufacture. Real urgency types: product changes, prospect-side life events (baby/wedding/job), health windows. If none apply, use Assumptive or Choice.

## Emotional vs Logical close - sequence by who they are

Decisions are emotional, justifications are logical. Best closes fire both. **The prospect's lead through the meeting decides which you lead the close with.**

### Sequence A - Emotion first (I / S)
1. **Emotional close** - callback hot button + paint concrete scenario + bridge to action
2. **Silence**
3. **Logic as justification** - *"and the math supports it too..."*

### Sequence B - Logic first (D / C)
1. **Logical close** - recap key numbers + comparison vs alternatives + direct ask
2. **Silence**
3. **Emotional punctuation** - *"and when we fast-forward to the future we talked about, this is what makes that non-negotiable"*

**Emotional close 3-beat:**
- Callback a hot button - quote them
- Paint the concrete scenario - specific, sensory
- Bridge to action - *"that's what we can do today"*

## The 3 Magic Questions - diagnostic for any objection

Most surface objections aren't what they sound like. 3 questions surface the real concern in ~90 seconds, before you spend 10 minutes on the wrong one.

```
Q1 "Is there anything else?"   ->   Q2 "Suppose that wasn't a concern..."   ->   Q3 "What would need to be true?"
```

### Q1 - *"Is there anything else?"*
*"Got it, fair concern. Before I address it - is there anything else giving you pause, or is that the main thing?"*
**Surfaces:** decoy objections. The first one stated is often easier to say than the real one.

### Q2 - *"Suppose that wasn't a concern..."*
*"Suppose the premium wasn't a concern - if the monthly was exactly where you wanted - would you move forward? Or is there something else underneath?"*
**Surfaces:** whether the stated objection is the real one. Lets them name the real block without losing face.

### Q3 - *"What would need to be true?"*
*"For you to feel confident saying yes - what would need to be true? What would I need to show you, or what would you need to see?"*
**Surfaces:** their actual decision criteria. Concrete deliverable, spouse conversation, or honest signal that it's a Follow-up situation.

**Hold 10+ seconds of silence after each.** The silence is where the real answer lives.

## Top 10 objections + verbatim response patterns

The 10 cover ~90% of Year-1 objections. Memorise the structure, deliver in your voice.

### 1. *"Too expensive / can't afford it"*
**Diagnostic first** - Magic Q2: *"suppose the premium was exactly right, would you move forward?"*
**If real:** *"Fair - the monthly is real. Two options: (a) scale coverage to today's budget, or (b) staggered structure - start lower, step up as income grows. Which feels closer?"*
**If decoy:** re-anchor value: *"The plan isn't $X/month - it's [specific benefit] for $X/month. Let me walk through the comparison..."*

### 2. *"I already have an advisor / coverage"*
**Technique: ART.** *"Completely makes sense - honestly I'd be surprised if you didn't. Most of my clients had an advisor when we first met. I'm not here to replace - most of my conversations are a second set of eyes, not a switch. 30 minutes to look at gaps - even if the answer is 'stay with your guy,' at least you'll know that for certain."*

### 3. *"Let me think about it"*
**Technique: full 3 Magic Questions + Iceberg.** *"Of course - take the time. Before you do - is there anything specific giving you pause, or is it more a general wanting-to-sit-with-it feeling?"*

### 4. *"I need to discuss with my spouse / parents"*
**Technique: ART + offer to join.** *"Absolutely right - a decision this size should involve both. Two options - either I join when you discuss so I can answer questions directly in 15 min, or you discuss first and we catch up Thursday. Which works better?"* If they refuse the join, use Q2 to test for deflection.

### 5. *"I'm not interested"*
**Technique: ART.** *"Totally fair - it'd be weird if you were interested in something you haven't really heard yet. Let me share what it is in 30 seconds, judge for yourself at the end. Worst case, you've heard an idea. Sound fair?"*

### 6. *"Bad time - getting married / new house / new baby"*
**Reframe the timing as the reason to act.** *"Honestly - these are the moments I most want people to be covered. Cashflow is tight, responsibility just grew. That's the exact stage where not having the right structure hits hardest. Middle path - we start with coverage that fits current cashflow, top up when the dust settles."*

### 7. *"I'll do it myself / I invest on my own"*
**Separate categories.** *"Love that - self-directed investing is great when you know what you're doing. Quick clarification - what we're discussing isn't investment, it's protection. Different jobs. Even excellent self-directed investors have protection products because downside scenarios don't care about portfolio performance."*

### 8. *"Insurance is a scam"*
**Technique: ART, heavy on Acknowledge.** *"I hear that - and honestly, there's a reason people say that. What's the specific experience that shaped that view? Was it yours, or someone you know?"* Listen. Then: *"That's a real story. The way I work is explicitly structured to not do that. Can I walk you through how my process handles exactly the thing you just described?"*

### 9. *"I'm young / healthy - I don't need it"*
**Math reframe.** *"You're right the probability is low this year. But the arithmetic: locking in coverage at your age and health profile is the cheapest you'll ever get. Wait 10 years, health changes, the cost jumps 3-5x for the same coverage. The question isn't 'do I need this in 2026?' - it's 'do I want the 30-year version now, or the 20-year version later at triple the price?'"*

### 10. *"Email me the details, I'll look at it"*
**Honest reframe + book follow-up.** *"Happy to send documentation - but plans like these don't land well in email. Without the conversation, the doc reads as generic. What usually works better: I send a 1-pager as the starting point, then we book 20 minutes Thursday to walk through it. Sound right?"*

## The close-the-close move

After you've delivered the main close, if the prospect says *"can I have a moment?"*:

> *"Of course. Take your time."*

Then **physically step back** - look at your iPad, organise paperwork, give them 10-30 seconds of space. The moment is often where the decision happens. Don't fill the silence. Don't re-pitch. Let it land.

If they come back with yes - move to logistics. If they come back with a specific concern - address it via the 10-objection table above. If they come back with another *"let me think"* - that's a Follow-up situation: book the specific date before they leave.

---

## Hard no vs Soft no vs Hidden concern

After running the 3 Magic Questions, you'll land in one of three territories:

| Type | Signs | Move |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard no** | Firm, specific, fit-related (*"I don't want insurance at all"*) | Accept gracefully, leave door open, follow up in 6-12 months |
| **Soft no** | Specific and addressable (price, term, feature, timing) | Address via ART. One or two turns, then re-close. |
| **Hidden concern** | Stated objection doesn't match their energy; Q1/Q2 revealed depth | Address the hidden concern, not the surface phrasing |

Hard no's happen. Trying to overturn them damages the relationship and kills the Year-2+ possibility.

## Common failure modes

- **Only 1 close at minute 60** - prospect hits the decision cold. Need 3-5 trial closes throughout warming them in.
- **Defaulting to Assumptive on every prospect** - half aren't D. S fake-agrees and ghosts; C re-analyses and disqualifies.
- **Re-pitching during the *"let me think"* silence** - pulls the prospect out of decision-mode. Step back physically and let them decide.
- **Manufactured urgency** - C profiles detect it instantly; trust collapses. Only use real deadlines.
- **Skipping the 90-second diagnostic** when an objection lands - 10 minutes addressing the wrong objection while the real block sits untouched.
- **Arguing statistics at *"insurance is a scam"*** - strong opinions form from strong experiences. Validate the story first.
- **Accepting *"of course, take your time!"*** without a specific revisit date - that's how decisions drift into forever.
- **Working a hard no** - damages relationship and kills the 12-month possibility.

---

## Numbers worth memorising

- **3-5 trial closes + 1 main close + 1 reinforcement** = healthy 60-minute pitch shape.
- **3 magic questions** = 90 seconds. Saves 10+ minutes of wrong-objection response.
- **10+ seconds silence** after each magic question. The silence is where the answer lives.
- **3 options max** in Choice Close. More = decision paralysis.
- **6-12 months** = follow-up window for a graceful hard no.
- **10 objections cover ~90%** of Year-1 prospect resistance. Memorise structure, deliver in your voice.
- **3-5x** premium increase 10 years later if the *"I'm young and healthy"* prospect waits.
