# Week 9: The close - cheat sheet

> **The one idea:** You close in small agreements all through the meeting, so the final ask confirms a decision the prospect has already been moving towards. From the outside, a good close looks almost boring.

> **Week 9 KPI:** 5 objection drills recorded. A first closed case is a bonus.

---

## Trial closes: the ladder to the main close

The new-FC default closes once, at minute 65, so the whole decision lands on the prospect at once. Scatter 3 to 5 trial closes through the meeting instead, because fewer leaves them meeting the main close cold and more starts to feel like an interrogation.

| When | Trial close |
|---|---|
| Fact-find | *"If we did put something in place for this, what monthly range feels comfortable: around $300, $500 or $800?"* |
| Presentation | *"Does a 20-year term feel right for your stage, or would you prefer something shorter?"* |
| After the recommendation | *"If this all makes sense, would you want to submit the application this month or next?"* |
| Just before the close | *"Before we get into the final details, is there anything that still feels unclear?"* |

A clean answer means they're following. Hesitation means an unspoken concern (use Iceberg). Push-back means go back over the point underneath.

## The main closes by profile

| Profile | Works well | Use with care |
|---|---|---|
| D | Trial, assumptive (best), urgency with a real deadline | Choice (too many options); keep any summary short |
| I | Trial, summary with the story, assumptive after a warm yes | Choice (they hedge), urgency (feels pushy) |
| S | Trial, choice (best, feels safe), reassurance | Assumptive (pressure), urgency (they withdraw) |
| C | Trial, choice, summary with data | Assumptive (pushy), urgency unless real and documented |

The most common Week 9 mistake is reaching for assumptive because it feels confident, when most prospects aren't D profiles. An S agrees to keep the peace and backs out within 48 hours, and a C goes back into analysis.

- Summary: *"Based on your priority on [X], a premium that fits your budget and cover for what matters most, I think this plan fits. You'll also have 14 days after you receive the policy to review it and cancel for a refund. Shall we start the application?"*
- Assumptive: *"Great, let's do the application now. For the first premium, card or GIRO? It goes straight to AIA."* Premiums go to AIA, never to you, and FCs can't collect cash for AIA.
- Choice: *"A is about $400 a month for $500K, B is about $550 for $750K, and both fit your budget. Which feels right?"* Use the client's real quote figures. It decides *how* after they've agreed to *whether*. Stop at three options, and never add a plan you'd never recommend to make the other look better.

### Urgency: only when it's real

Real urgency comes from three places, and the client can check each one:

- An insurer's announced change with a date on it: *"AIA has announced a premium revision for this plan from [date]. Here's the notice."* Confirm with your manager how it applies, and never estimate the increase.
- The client's own timing: *"You want this in place before the baby arrives. AIA can take up to 30 days to decide once it has everything, and any health change before acceptance has to be declared."* Or company cover that stops when they change jobs.
- Their health, said once and factually, never used as a lever.

If none apply, use assumptive or choice. Never invent a deadline. MoneySense tells consumers they're never obliged to buy on the spot.

## The paper-flip close, kept honest

After the main close, once they're leaning yes, flip through sheets of real cases, each showing only a life stage and the need covered: *"Nurse, early 30s, hospital and critical illness cover before starting a family."* No names, initials, employer or dates. If you have no cases yet, use your team's with your mentor's permission and say they're the team's.

Stop at the life stages. Never add returns, payouts or "almost all of them bought". A return claim is a misrepresentation even said casually, and identifying details break the PDPA.

Cialdini's seven principles sit behind it: social proof (real, anonymised cases), liking and unity (their life stage), commitment and consistency (their fact-find words), authority (never a track record you don't have). Leave scarcity out, because deadlines are too easy to fake.

## Emotional and logical closes

Emotion moves the decision and logic backs it (Damasio's somatic marker hypothesis). Lead with whatever the prospect led with in the meeting.

- Sequence A, emotion first (I, S): callback in their words, a specific scenario, a bridge to today. Silence. Then *"and the numbers support it too."* Describe what the plan pays as the policy says it; never promise nothing will change for the family.
- Sequence B, logic first (D, C): recap the numbers, compare like with like on compareFIRST, ask calmly. Silence. Then one emotional line to settle it. Never make up a percentage showing your plan "beats" anyone.

Still hesitating? *"If there was one thing, just one, giving you pause right now, what would it be?"* Stay silent for at least 10 seconds.

## The 3 magic questions

The first objection is often a decoy. Three questions, about 90 seconds, find the real one.

1. *"Is there anything else giving you pause, or is that the main thing?"* Brings out the objection they haven't said.
2. *"Suppose the premium was exactly where you wanted it. Would you go ahead, or is there something else underneath?"* Tests whether the stated objection is real, without them losing face.
3. *"For you to feel confident saying yes, what would need to be true?"* Gets their decision criteria: a side-by-side, a spouse meeting, or an honest need for a follow-up.

Then sort it:

| Type | Signs | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Hard no | Firm, specific, about fit | Accept it, leave the door open, ask to check in next year. A second no gets respected. |
| Soft no | Price, term, a feature, timing | ART for one or two turns, then close again |
| Hidden concern | Words don't match their manner | Work on the concern, not the surface phrase |

## The top 10 objections

A short list makes up most of what you'll hear in Year 1. Learn the structure, say it in your own voice.

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| *"Too expensive"* | Magic question 2 first. If price is real, size cover to today's budget or start smaller and add later (adding cover may need fresh underwriting). If it's a decoy, rebuild the value. |
| *"I already have an adviser"* | ART: *"Most people I talk to already have someone. I'm not here to replace anyone, a lot of the time I'm a second pair of eyes."* Never run the other adviser down. Replacing a policy happens only inside a full fact-find, with the switching disclosure and a clear comparison of what they'd lose and gain. |
| *"Let me think about it"* | All 3 magic questions, then Iceberg. |
| *"I need to discuss with my spouse"* | Offer to join for 15 minutes, or catch up Thursday. Turning the offer down may be deflection, so test with question 2. |
| *"I'm not interested"* | Offer the 30-second version. A no after that is a hard no. |
| Bad time (wedding, house, baby) | The timing is the reason to act. Start with smaller cover that fits cash flow now, then review and top up once AIA has accepted and things settle. |
| *"I invest on my own"* | Separate the jobs. A portfolio can do very well and still pay nothing if a critical illness stops them working at 35. |
| *"Insurance is a scam"* | Ask for their story and take it seriously, then explain how you work. FIDReC handles disputes between consumers and financial institutions. |
| *"I'm young and healthy"* | Show two real compareFIRST quotes, today's age and 10 years older. A health change could bring a higher premium, an exclusion or a decline. Never guess a multiple. |
| *"Email me the details"* | Offer a one-pager plus a 20-minute call. If they only want the email, send it and book a follow-up about a week out. |

When they ask for a moment after the close: *"Of course, take your time."* Step back for 10 to 30 seconds and don't re-pitch. Another *"let me think"* means book a date before you leave.

---

## Numbers worth memorising

- 3 to 5 trial closes, 1 main close, the paper-flip after it, a follow-up only if needed
- 90 seconds for the 3 magic questions
- 3 options at most in a choice close
- 30 days: how long an insurer can take to decide once it has all the information
- 14 days: the free-look period from receipt of the policy
