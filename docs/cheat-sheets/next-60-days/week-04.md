# Week 4: Prospecting at Volume - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** a full calendar with cases pending comes from prospecting. Year-1 advisors who drop out rarely lack closing skill. Their calendars are empty because they never built the volume habit.

> **Week 4 KPI:** 30 outreaches; 5 appointments booked.

## Market Temperature: the 4 rings

```
Hot -> Warm -> Semi-Warm -> Cold
```

An illustrative 500-contact network (yours will differ, but the shape usually holds):

| Ring | Who | % | Note |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Hot | Parents, siblings, best friends | 4% | Easiest to meet, first to run out |
| Warm | Friends, ex-colleagues, relatives you see often | 20% | Most new FCs spend too long here |
| Semi-Warm | Old school friends, old colleagues, distant relatives, referrals | 66% | The biggest ring, least competition, longest runway |
| Cold | Strangers | 10% | Only works at volume |

New FCs work Hot and Warm in months 1-3, close a handful and run out. Reconnected old contacts can be as useful as current ones (Levin, Walter and Murnighan, 2011), so start reactivating Semi-Warm in Week 4.

Warm and cold need different voices:

| | Warm market | Cold market |
|---|---|---|
| Opener | Something from your shared history | A problem people in their niche commonly have |
| Credibility | The relationship you already have | A clear framework and your credentials |
| Tone | Open, honest | Professional, useful first |
| Ask | Easy out, no pressure | A clear next step with something useful attached |

## The 4 types of prospects: 10/20/60/10

| Tier | % | What to do |
|---|---:|---|
| 1. Definitely will buy (loyal family) | 10% | Keep it warm, they'll ask when ready |
| 2. Most likely to support (friends, relatives) | 20% | Warm outreach that's relevant to their life |
| 3. Can be converted (anyone, with good information) | 60% | Most of your work happens here |
| 4. Die-die will not buy | 10% | Don't spend your time here |

Don't pour 40% of your effort into the bottom 10%. Put this week's outreaches into the middle 60%.

## The 6-step honest warm-market message

```
1. Real connection -> 2. Honest about why -> 3. Say the awkward thing ->
4. Offer help without pressure -> 5. Give an easy out -> 6. Relationship close
```

1. *"Hey! Hope Vietnam's been treating you well, saw the travel posts."*
2. *"I've been meaning to message you about something a bit more personal, financial planning. Honestly, I was a little nervous to bring it up."*
3. *"I didn't want to make things weird, or have it seem like I was only messaging to pitch you something."*
4. *"If you're open to it, I'd be happy to do a quick, no-obligation review of where you're at."*
5. *"And if it's not something you're keen on right now, I completely understand."*
6. *"Would be nice to catch up properly anyway, it's been way too long. Where are you working now?"*

Follow up a silence with something useful, never another *"can we meet?"*

## Market Survey: the warm-market phone script

A 5-minute call, 4 questions, Q4 is the appointment ask. Do at least 30 with warm and semi-warm contacts before any cold work.

| Beat | Script |
|---|---|
| Opening | *"Hi, is this Amir? Amir, it's [your name]. Do you have a moment to talk? How have you been this period?"* |
| Agenda | *"As you may have heard, I recently got licensed as a financial consultant with AIA. As part of building my practice, I'm doing a quick 5-minute survey with people I know. It's just 4 questions, nothing to buy or sign. Could you help me out?"* |
| Q1: coverage | *"Do you have any insurance policies or financial plans at the moment?"* |
| Q2: last review | *"When was the last time you did a review of your policies?"* |
| Q3: service | *"How do you feel about your current advisor's service? Are they responsive, do they explain things clearly, do you get regular reviews?"* |
| Q4: the ask | *"May I get in touch for a 30-minute session to share the kind of work I do? You have my assurance I won't ask you to do anything you don't want to. Is that fair?"* |
| Logistics | *"Office, home or Zoom? Weekdays or weekends? Thursday at 7, or Saturday at 2?"* |

If you ask for their email and date of birth at the close, take only what they agree to give.

**Business calls to friends are not exempt from DNC.** Only personal or domestic messages are excluded, so the Day 22 rules apply to Market Survey calls too. Unsure about a friend's number? Ask compliance before your first block.

Has a plan and is happy to have it reviewed? That's an FHR. Never thought about it and said yes mostly to see you? That's a Warm Up: no iPad, no CST. Presenting at a Warm Up burns the contact.

Objections: acknowledge, reframe, re-ask. Every reply ends with *"would you be free Thursday or Saturday?"*

## Cold prospecting: 5 pillars

```
Niche -> Framework -> Profile -> Sharp DM -> Follow-up
```

| Pillar | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| 1. Niche | F&B owners with two or more outlets instead of "business owners"; parents of primary-school kids in the East instead of "parents" |
| 2. Framework | A named structure (P.I.S.A, F.A.T, A.P.E.X), only if your process already has those phases |
| 3. Profile | The only thing a cold prospect can check. Testimonials only in a compliance-approved form. |
| 4. Sharp DM | A common niche problem, something useful upfront (compliance-approved), written person to person |
| 5. Follow-up | Something new every time, never *"just checking in"* |

Strong cold DM: *"Hi [name], saw you're running a 2-outlet F&B operation. A lot of owners at that stage hit the same cashflow squeeze when they open a third outlet. I put together a 1-page checklist to stress-test it, happy to send it over if useful. No pitch, no call needed."*

## The unconventional cold call

Every line has to be true for you today.

1. Favour: *"Hi Mark, this is [name], a financial consultant with AIA. I actually need to ask you a favour, please don't hang up right away. Honestly, it's been a long day of calls and most people have hung up on me. If at any point you're not interested you can hang up and I'll still be grateful. Is that okay?"*
2. Say what the questions are for: *"My team and I are trying to understand the money issues that matter most to professionals aged 30 to 45. Would you mind answering a few quick questions?"* You've said you're an FC, so don't dress it up as independent research.
3. Three hot-button questions about their finances, goals and retirement.
4. Thank them and offer something useful: *"Can I send you our retirement planning checklist as a thank-you?"*

5-10% of conversations becoming meetings is a healthy cold-call script (team benchmark).

Cold-call KPI ratios (the team's working benchmarks; check your own log after a few weeks):

| Lever | Working baseline |
|---|---|
| Dials per hour | 40-50 (50+ for 3 hours straight) |
| Pick-up rate | ~20% of dials |
| Appointment-set rate | ~5% of pick-ups |
| Show-up rate | ~1 in 3 (cautious estimate) |
| Close rate | ~1 in 3 of those who show |

1 closed case = about 3 held = 9 set = 180 pick-ups = 900 dials. At 50 dials an hour that's about 18 hours, so 3 hours a day for 5 days gets a little under one case a week from cold alone. Two phones: dial on both, and when both answer, call the second person back straight after.

## DNC screening before any cold list

**Screen every list first.** The rules cover calls, SMS and apps like WhatsApp on a Singapore number, and an individual can face a financial penalty of up to $200,000 under the PDPA.

1. Put the numbers in one Google Sheet column and download it as `leads.csv` (no spaces or special characters in the filename).
2. DNC Registry portal: Check Registry, then Bulk Filtering. Upload and click Proceed.
3. Wait 5-10 minutes and download the results.
4. There are three registers (No Voice Call, No Text Message, No Fax). Check the one for how you'll contact each person and remove any number registered for that channel.

Results are valid for 21 days. Re-check anything older before contacting it.

## ABCD Four Promises: the first 30 seconds of every Fact-Find

| Promise | Script |
|---|---|
| A: Assurance | *"Before we start, this meeting isn't to sell you anything. I'm here to understand you and your goals. If we end up with a proposal, it's because I shared something valuable and you felt comfortable moving forward."* |
| B: Best Interest | *"If we do reach the proposal stage, my promise is that what I plan for you is exactly what I'd do for myself in your shoes."* |
| C: Confidentiality | *"Everything you share with me today stays in strictest confidence."* |
| D: Disclosure | *"With that said, I hope you can share openly, because the quality of the recommendation depends on the quality of what I learn."* |

About 40 seconds, before the first Fact-Find question.

## Strategic Target List and inventory

20 names a month minimum. One sheet, five columns: Name, Tag (ABC / 1-2-3), Trigger, Approach (Market Survey / Attraction / 6-step / value drop / CRAB / cold DM), Last contact.

| Type | Analogy | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| Current Inventory | Money in the bank | ABC clients and 1/2/3 prospects on your books today |
| Recurring Inventory | A monthly income | New leads each month from referrals, content, events, collaborations |

Work only Current and the list runs dry, often around months 9-12. Give about 20% of weekly prospecting time to Recurring from the start: content, a referral ask at every Fact-Find, one new relationship-building activity a month.

## Numbers worth memorising

- 30 outreaches, 5 appointments: about 17%. Above 20%, add volume. Below 15%, find where the no's happen: no replies means fix the opener, replies without bookings means fix the ask.
- Team working assumption of 1 booking per 20 warm calls: 100-140 calls a week (20-28 a day) for 5-7 appointments.
- Roughly 100 or more rejections per sale on the lesson's illustrative ratios.
- Cold is 0-5 of the 30 outreaches, and optional. The large majority of first-year business comes from warm and semi-warm.
- Law of Familiarity (the team's rule of thumb): warm inbound 5-12 touches, some familiarity 5-20, cold 20-50.

## Common failure modes

- All 30 outreaches on one channel. Mix Market Surveys, 6-step messages, Attraction, CRAB, value drops and 0-5 cold.
- Counting *"let's meet sometime"* as a booking. A booking has a confirmed time, a confirmed place or format, and both sides acknowledged.
- Padding the count. 24 real outreaches beat 30 padded ones.
- Quitting the call block at dial 3 or 4. The block ends at 4 dials and no bookings when it would have reached 20 and one.
- Skipping DNC screening, or assuming friends' numbers are exempt.
