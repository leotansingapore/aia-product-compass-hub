# Week 6: Solitaire PA - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** Solitaire PA is the only product where a lose-ONE partial-disability event (single finger, single eye, single ear, fracture-with-non-union) gets paid out at meaningful sums - because hospital plans only trigger on inpatient, and life / TPD only trigger on lose-TWO catastrophic events.

---

## What this product is + who it is FOR

A SGD yearly-renewable Personal Accident plan issued by AIA. Covers accidental death, dismemberment, partial disability via Schedule of Indemnity, accident medical reimbursement, TCM/chiropractic, plus extensions for food poisoning, insect/animal bites (dengue, Zika), drowning, terrorism, motorcycling, amateur sports. **No medical underwriting** - guaranteed issue. 24/7 worldwide coverage.

**Ideal client (3 markers):**
- Universal-fit - office workers, manual labour, active lifestyles, parents of young kids, pre-retirees, frequent travellers
- Already has HSGM (this is the outpatient + partial-disability companion plan)
- Issue age 16-70 last birthday; coverage steps down 50% at 75 and terminates at 80 - lock in young

## The pitch angle - opener stems

Open with a discovery question that surfaces the existing-cover gap:

> *"Out of curiosity - if your kid sliced their hand chopping veg and needed stitches at KK Hospital tomorrow, do you know if any of your existing policies actually pay for that?"*

Most prospects pause. Then bridge:

> *"That's the gap Solitaire PA fills - outpatient injury, food poisoning, insect bites, partial-disability events. Your hospital plan only kicks in if you're warded six hours or have surgery. Your life and CI only trigger on the catastrophic stuff. Everything in between sits in this product."*

The partial-disability gap reframe (verbatim from source):

> *"Death coverage - you have to lose two legs, two arms, or two eyes. Accident plan - just one finger."*

> *"Your term plan covers TPD - that's losing two arms, two legs, or two eyes. But what happens if you fracture a bone or lose just one finger? Won't trigger TPD, but it still impacts your life and income. That's where accident coverage becomes essential."*

The price-anchor reframe vs death cover:

> *"You pay $100-200/month for death coverage and most people go their whole lives without it triggering. Solitaire PA is about $20/month and the claim window is the everyday stuff - fall off a bike, burn a hand, food poisoning. Same insurer, very different probability of using it."*

## Objection cluster

| Objection | Cushion -> reframe -> close-to-action |
|---|---|
| "I'm careful - I won't have an accident" | *"Respect that. The thing is, 'accident' by definition means unexpected. Recreational sports, insect bites, food poisoning, kitchen cuts - none of that is reckless. Across my book I process 1-2 accident claims every single week. These are careful people right up until something landed they didn't see coming."* |
| "My employer's group PA already covers me" | *"Three things to check. Caps are typically low - S$50K-S$200K maximum. Tied to your job - resignation or retrenchment ends it overnight. Rarely covers TCM, chiropractic, or the full partial-disability Schedule. Plan 4 carries S$2.25M on dismemberment and stays yours regardless of employer. Layer it under, not replace."* |
| "PA is cheap - not worth a separate appointment" | *"Exactly why I don't run PA standalone. It's part of a basic-medical conversation - HSGM plus Solitaire PA, inpatient plus outpatient. Bundle a young adult on Plan 1 with HSGM B = ~S$45/month total. Single conversation, two products, one close."* |
| "What's the actual claim probability?" | *"Probability is the wrong lens - frequency is the right one. Across my book the accident plan is the policy I claim on the most. Every week I process 1-2 claims - TCM visits, chiropractor, food poisoning, polyclinic stitches. Claim screenshots S$14.50 to S$156. Death and TPD claims are the rare ones. Accident claims are the routine ones."* |
| "I'll think about it" | *"What specifically would you want to consider? The plan tier, the cost, or whether you need it at all? Across Plans 1-4 the premium is S$0.62 to S$2.34/day - less than your coffee. The actual question isn't price - it's whether next time someone falls off a bike or a kid needs stitches, you'd rather not pay out of pocket. Plan 1, 2, 3, or 4?"* |

## Cross-sell / sequencing

| Layer | Where Solitaire PA sits |
|---|---|
| HSGM (Week 5) | **The mandatory pair - always sold together in a basic-medical conversation.** Bundle 1 (young adult, public): HSGM B + Plan 1 = ~S$45/month. Bundle 2 (private): HSGM A + Plan 4 = ~S$174/month. |
| GPP (Week 3) | Death + CI cover. PA fills the partial-disability gap GPP's TPD definition leaves open. |
| UCC (Week 4) | Multi-claim CI. UCC + PA = both pay out independently. |
| AIA Star Protector Plus (kids) | **Plan 2+ unlocks 10% discount on Star Protector Plus for children until 21** - the family-conversion hook. |
| APA / PWV | Wealth layer - sells later once the protection floor (HSGM + PA + GPP/UCC) is in. |

**Total Wealth four-bucket position:** Solitaire PA is **Bucket 4** (accident / outpatient / partial disability). Bucket 1 wealth, Bucket 2 life cover, Bucket 3 hospitalisation, Bucket 4 accident. Never sold as Bucket 1, 2, or 3.

## Common pitch failure modes

- **Sell PA as a standalone meeting.** Burns an appointment slot on a small-ticket product. Always bundle with HSGM as the basic-medical conversation.
- **Argue probability instead of showing frequency.** "What's the probability of an accident?" is the wrong lens. Use your own claim book - 1-2 claims/week, real screenshots. Frequency > probability.
- **Forget the partial-disability gap diagram.** The single conversion lever. TPD pays lose-TWO; Schedule of Indemnity pays lose-ONE. Draw it.
- **Pitch the wrong Class.** Class 3-4 loading is ~75% above Class 1-2 - manual labour and high-risk professions. Get the occupation right at fact-find.
- **Sell Plan 1 to a Plan 2 prospect.** Plan 2+ unlocks the AIA Star Protector Plus 10% family discount. Surface this for any prospect with kids.
- **Conflate guaranteed-issue with pre-existing covered.** Guaranteed-issue means no underwriting at entry. Pre-existing conditions are still excluded from claims. Make the distinction crystal clear.
- **Recite all exclusions back-to-back before signature.** Reframe each as needed when objections surface. Distinguish covered Policy Extensions (food poisoning, dengue, motorcycling, amateur sports) from exclusions (war, intentional, alcohol, professional sport).
