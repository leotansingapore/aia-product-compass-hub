# Week 3: Guaranteed Protect Plus (GPP) - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** GPP is the answer for the prospect who wants serious CI / death cover but refuses to pay for term that "expires worthless" - heavy temporary cover via the booster during the high-responsibility years, a smaller lifetime base after the booster falls off, and cash refunded at maturity if no claim.

---

## What this product is + who it is FOR

A SGD participating whole-life plan issued by AIA. Two structural components: **Accumulator** (lifetime base with cash value to age 100) + **Booster** (temporary high cover, no cash value, drops off at 65 or 75). Limited premium-pay term locks in for the life of the policy.

**Ideal client (3 markers):**
- Age 18-60 with 15+ years of working life ahead and dependants (spouse, child, ageing parents)
- Hates the idea of "paying for cover and getting nothing back" - the cash-refund triage answer is yes
- Cases land most often in the $300K-$500K death-cover band with 3x multiplier as the default

## Key specs / mechanics

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Structure | SGD participating whole-life, cover to age 100 |
| Pay term (LOCKED at issue) | 15 / 20 / 25 years - 25-yr is most popular |
| Multiplier (LOCKED at issue) | 2x / 3x / 5x - **3x is the default** |

| TPD cut-off | Age 70 |
| Free-look | 14 days |

## The pitch angle - opener stems

Open with the cash-value triage question - this is the single most important question in a GPP fact-find:

> *"Mr Prospect, just say you pay this $500 a month for insurance, you pay all the way, and at the end of the day you don't get any money back. How would you feel?"*

Wait. If they say *"quite a waste"*, GPP is the right product. If they say *"I don't care, I just want max cover for the lowest premium"*, pitch term and respect the close.

The buying-vs-renting reframe (handles 80% of pushback):

> *"Term insurance is renting a house - cheaper monthly but nothing at the end. GPP is buying a house - bit more expensive monthly, but you own the cover for life and get the money back at 65."*

The rocket-ship + travel-class reveal:

> *"This booster is like a rocket ship - the side boosters drop off after launch. Three multipliers: 5x is economy class, 3x is business class, 2x is first class. Most of my clients sit at 3x. That OK?"*

The sure-win close (verbatim):

> *"Three outcomes. One - you get cancer or another CI, the plan pays out 100%. Two - you pass away, the plan pays the death benefit. Three - you live long and never claim, the plan refunds all your premiums plus bonuses by 65. No matter which path your life takes, you get something back. It's a sure-win."*

## Objection cluster

| Objection | Cushion -> reframe -> close-to-action |
|---|---|
| "GPP is too expensive vs term" | *"Right that monthly premium is higher. Term gets significantly more expensive on renewal at 65 - over a lifetime, total cost of term is often higher than GPP, and you have nothing at the end. GPP - same premium for 25 years, then nothing more to pay, cover for life, cash back at maturity. Renting vs buying."* |
| "Whole life is overkill - I just want pure protection" | *"The booster IS pure protection - high-cover, temporary, drops at 65 like term would. The difference is GPP layers it on top of a permanent base. Base makes the cash refund possible. Without the base, term expires with nothing back."* |
| "I already have CI cover" | *"What's the sum-assured on your existing CI? At your income the benchmark is 5 years of income for CI. Shortfall is $X. We layer GPP only for the gap, not as a full restart. Sound fair?"* |
| "Why pay for cover I might never use?" | *"Three outcomes - claim CI and 100% pays out, pass away and death benefit pays the family, live long and the premiums plus bonuses come back at 65. Whichever path, you get something. Sure-win."* |
| "I'd rather BTID - Buy Term and Invest the Difference" | *"Mathematically defensible IF you actually invest the difference AND outperform GPP's 2% guaranteed-floor yield AND maintain discipline for 25 years. Even if you do - 100% of your protection budget is tied to market performance. Layer GPP and you get a guaranteed-return floor underneath the investment risk. Diversification, not either/or."* |

## Cross-sell / sequencing

| Layer | Where GPP sits |
|---|---|
| HSGM (Week 5) | The hospital foundation - mandatory before GPP. GPP without HSGM means medical bills eat the cash-refund expectation. |
| Solitaire PA (Week 6) | Outpatient + partial-disability gap. Always pair with HSGM + GPP as the basic protection stack. |
| UCC (Week 4) | The canonical pair - **GPP for death + Major CI + savings core; UCC bolted on for Early CI / recurring claims**. Accelerated CI on GPP erodes death cover; UCC keeps CI independent. |
| PLP (Week 2) | The estate-floor + retirement engine if budget allows. GPP + UCC handles cover; PLP adds the wealth dimension. |
| Mum-to-Baby rider | High-leverage for expectant clients. |

**Default close:** GPP $300K / 3x / 25-yr / drop-off 65 + half-half Early CI rider ($150K Early + $150K Major) at ~$3,995/year. The differential vs Major-only is ~$795/year (~$60/month) - small lever, large incremental cover.

## Numbers worth memorising

1. **$300K cover / 3x / age 25 / 25-yr pay** -> ~$2,600/yr premium, ~$169K cash value at 65, ~$285K at 100, **~2.11% yield**
2. **$200K cover / 3x / age 25** -> ~$1,946/yr (the most common entry case)
3. CI coverage benchmark: **5 years of annual income** (3 years for Early CI)
4. Aggregate CI cap across all AIA CI policies: **$3,000,000**
5. Early CI per-episode cap: **$350,000**
6. Power-Up Dollar (3x default): **+15% first-year boost**
7. TPD definition post-65: **2 of 6 ADLs** (washing, dressing, transferring, mobility, toileting, feeding)
8. Half-half Early CI add-on differential: **~$60/month** above Major-CI-only

## Common pitch failure modes

- **Skip the cash-value triage question.** Without surfacing the "quite a waste" answer, you'll argue against term in Phase 4 instead of qualifying for whole-life in Phase 1.
- **Lead with the rocket-ship diagram on a term-only-mindset prospect.** Diagram makes the plan feel complex; you lose the close. Switch to a side-by-side term-vs-GPP comparison and respect the close if they're truly term-only.
- **Pitch the 5x multiplier on a normal prospect.** 5x looks attractive but the base is weakest, cliff at 65 is biggest. Default is 3x. Deviate with a strong reason only.
- **Hide accelerated CI in the fine print.** A CI claim reduces the death benefit by the claim amount. Disclose proactively during the pitch - burying it costs cases later in audit.
- **Replace existing CI instead of layering for the gap.** Run the 5x-income gap calc, layer for the gap only. Replacement pitching destroys trust at scale.
- **Collapse the four hoops into one close.** Hoop 1 (need CI) -> Hoop 2 (buy now) -> Hoop 3 (3x vs 2x) -> Hoop 4 (with Early CI). Each is a micro-yes. Phase 6 close is then the binary that confirms hoop 4 - "without Early CI or with Early CI?"
