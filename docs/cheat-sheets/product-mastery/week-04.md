# Week 4: Ultimate Critical Cover (UCC) - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** UCC is a pure-CI plan that decouples critical-illness cover from death and savings - claim once, the cover resets to 100% twelve months later, claim again, reset again. No DIY platform or bundled CI rider replicates the unlimited Major-stage staircase.

---

## What this product is + who it is FOR

A SGD non-participating regular-premium critical-illness plan issued by AIA. No cash value, no surrender benefit, no maturity benefit - pure CI. **73 underlying critical illnesses** mapped across **150 condition-stage entries** (42 Early / 35 Intermediate / 73 Major). Unlimited claims for Major-stage CI, with the Ultimate Reset Benefit restoring 100% cover 12 months after the last admitted claim.

**Ideal client (3 markers):**
- Has CI bundled into GPP / PLP / whole-life (which collapses on first claim) - layer UCC on top
- No Early-CI cover or no multi-claim cover today - 42 Early-Stage conditions native
- Coverage shortfall against the 5-year income-replacement benchmark - UCC layers cleanly on existing cover

## The pitch angle - opener stems

Open with discovery, not features:

> *"Quick one - your concern with CI cover, is it more about not having enough on one big claim, or more about your other cover surviving if you have to claim?"*

> *"Most CI plans pay once and stop. UCC is multi-claim - after a Major claim, the full cover resets 12 months later. And it's standalone, so a CI claim doesn't touch your other policies. Mind if I sketch how the staircase works?"*

The decoupling reveal (verbatim from Video 4):

> *"Once you claim once on your existing whole-life, can you claim again? No. If you claim once, can you still claim for death? No. If you claim once, can you claim for savings? No. That's why UCC exists - it decouples CI from death and savings, and it lets you claim again."*

The staircase walkthrough (Robert case study from brochure p.10):

> *"Robert claims Coronary Artery Disease at age 43 - $100K. Twelve months later, he claims Coronary Artery Bypass - another $100K. Twelve months later, Heart Attack - $100K again. Heart Transplant after that - another $100K. Total: $400K+ on a $100K base. That's the multi-claim engine - claim, reset, claim again."*

## Objection cluster

| Objection | Cushion -> reframe -> close-to-action |
|---|---|
| "I already have CI in my GPP / PLP / whole-life" | *"What happens if you claim - do you still get your $20K surrender value? No. Do you still get death cover? No. Bundled CI collapses on first claim. UCC is pure CI - claiming on UCC leaves your death cover and savings completely untouched. Layer it on top, don't replace."* |
| "Multi-claim sounds rare - is it worth the premium?" | *"Most CI claims are heart and cancer, both of which commonly recur. Robert's case in the brochure: $500K paid out across 8 years from a $100K base. Enhancer rider adds $7/month for the relapse coverage that catches exactly the recurrence scenario most likely to happen."* |
| "Early CI seems redundant - I'd rather wait until major" | *"Modern imaging catches conditions at stages that didn't have payouts a decade ago. The years you need cash MOST are the diagnosis-to-recovery years - not the worst-case major stage. UCC covers 42 Early-Stage and 35 Intermediate-Stage conditions with the same $350K per-episode cap."* |
| "Prudential / GE / Singlife multi-pay looks similar" | *"For Prudential PRUActive Protect - 37 conditions vs UCC's 73, and any future heart-related claims become non-claimable once you claim on one heart condition (related-illness block). GE GREAT Critical Cover caps at 3 claims, policy ceases. Singlife has 60 conditions, many showing 'Not applicable' at Early/Intermediate. UCC has no related-illness block, no claim-count cap on Major stage, 12-month reset built in."* |
| "What's the actual probability I'd recur?" | *"Probability is the wrong lens. The mechanic is what matters - the policy works the way it's designed regardless of what probability you assign. Robert's brochure case is the illustration: $500K from $100K base across 8 years. The Enhancer relapse rider plus the Catastrophic CI Benefit cover exactly that arithmetic."* |

## Cross-sell / sequencing

| Layer | Where UCC sits |
|---|---|
| HSGM (Week 5) | The hospital foundation - mandatory. UCC pays a lump sum, HSGM pays the hospital. Both needed. |
| GPP (Week 3) | **The canonical pair - GPP for death + Major CI + savings core, UCC bolted on for Early CI + recurring claims**. Half-half split: $150K Early via UCC + $150K Major via GPP base. |
| PLP (Week 2) | Strip the LCC rider on PLP and replace with standalone UCC - cleaner CI, no death-benefit erosion via accelerated structure. |
| ECPWP (Early Critical Protector Waiver of Premium) | Best-case ~679x ROI, worst-case ~$8,400 over 50 years. Asymmetric one-way bet at ~$15/month. |
| Enhancer rider | 5 named relapse conditions (cancer, heart attack, stroke, transplant, heart-valve surgery) at 50% per relapse + 100% one-time Catastrophic top-up. Default in. |

**Default close sequence:** Confirm term -> Enhancer is in by default -> THEN present ECPWP with the asymmetric ROI framing -> THEN AIA Vitality. Never reverse - mentioning ECPWP before the term is locked fragments the decision.

2. **12 months** - Ultimate Reset Benefit waiting period
3. **S$350,000** - Early + Intermediate combined cap per life per same CI
4. **500%** - combined Early + Intermediate aggregate cap (across all stages)
5. **S$3,000,000** - CI per-life limit across all AIA CI policies
6. **7 days** survival / **90 days** cardiac+cancer waiting / **2 years** Enhancer relapse waiting

8. ECPWP ROI: best-case **~679x**, worst-case **~$8,400** over a 50-year lifetime
9. Enhancer relapse: **50% per claim**, capped at **100% total**; 5 named conditions
10. Catastrophic CI Benefit: **100% one-time top-up** that does NOT reduce base cover

## Common pitch failure modes

- **Pitch UCC as "unlimited" without disclosing the same-illness limit.** Unlimited applies to different Major-stage CIs. Same-illness recurrence (e.g. cancer relapse) needs the Enhancer. Disclose upfront - it's the trust move.
- **Lead with the staircase on a prospect whose primary need is death cover.** Wrong product - pivot to GPP or term. UCC's death benefit is only 5% non-accidental by design.
- **Recommend replacing existing GPP / PLP CI cover with UCC.** UCC layers on top; never replaces. The CI on the bundled plan stays; UCC adds Early-stage + multi-claim + decoupled structure.
- **Sequence riders the wrong way.** Don't present ECPWP before the term is locked. Confirm term first, then Enhancer (default in), then ECPWP, then Vitality.
- **Bury the per-episode caps.** S$350K combined Early+Intermediate per life per CI, 500% combined cap across all stages, S$25K Angioplasty cap. Surface these proactively - prospects will find them in the BI later.
- **Recite the 6 compliance pillars as a defensive block before signature.** Use them as objection reframes when they come up naturally. The 7-day survival / 90-day waiting / pre-existing exclusion are real and need to be in the prospect's awareness - but not as a block right before iPOS.
