# Concept Drawings - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** Pen drawing before any presenter tool. Slides and iPulse are for you, not the client - splash everything at once and they freeze. Three diagrams you must memorise to draw fluently: APA retirement flow, PWV staircase, CPF Life depletion. The numbers do the convincing once the client calculates them on their own phone.

---

## The order on the table - non-negotiable sequence

1. **Verbal frame** - *"The reason we're here today is to plan for your future. The most expensive thing you'll ever buy isn't your house - it's your retirement."*
2. **Concept on paper** - draw the retirement / staircase / CPF Life depletion diagram
3. **Get them to calculate** - hand them the phone, make them punch the math
4. **Presenter tool (iPulse, iPaaS Plus)** - only after the concept has landed
5. **Bonuses / charges reframe** - draw the bonus structure last as the closer

If you open the iPad first, you lose the room.

---

## Drawing 1 - APA Retirement Flow (memorise; draw cold)

**Goal:** anchor the $2.4M number and the $1K/month solution before any product appears.

| Step | What you draw | What you say |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Today vs Retirement timeline | *"What's a good monthly passive income for retirement?"* (most say $3K) |
| 2 | Inflation arrow at 2.5% p.a. | *"Will $3K today still be $3K in 40 years?"* Use TWC Retirement Calculator on the spot |
| 3 | Box: $10K/month at age 65 | Let the number land. Don't rescue the silence. |
| 4 | Hand them the phone | *"$10K x 12 x 20 years - what do you get?"* Answer: **$2.4M** |
| 5 | Divide $2.4M / 40 yrs / 12 mo | *"How much per month if you just save?"* ~$5,000/month |
| 6 | Cross out $5K. Write **$1K** | *"With proper investing, we can reduce this to around $1,000/month."* |
| 7 | Retirement income at 6% on $2.4M | $10K/month dividends. Capital stays intact = inheritance. **Two birds, one stone.** |

**The One-Third Rule** (sub-drawing): on a $5K/month income, 1/3 short-term (food, transport), 1/3 mid-term (rent, kids), 1/3 long-term (investments + insurance). *"Most people over-spend the first third and starve the last."*

## Drawing 2 - PWV Staircase (memorise; draw cold)

**Goal:** show how a $500K portfolio built in $100K tranches produces escalating dividends, then attach the secondary insured + capital guarantee features.

```
Year 1: $100K in -> $500/mo dividends (6% of $100K = $6K/yr)
Year 2: $100K in -> $1,000/mo (2 layers)
Year 3: $100K in -> $1,500/mo (3 layers)
Year 4: $100K in -> $2,000/mo
Year 5: $100K in -> $2,500/mo

Total: $500K portfolio -> ~$30K/yr -> ~$2,500/mo
```

After drawing, attach the three killer features:
- **Capital guarantee on death** - $500K guaranteed regardless of fund value
- **Secondary insured** - child inherits the entire income-generating portfolio, not just cash
- **Two lines of defence** - portfolio passes first, capital guarantee passes second

**Engagement question:** *"If you're 50 now and live until 90 - which is more, the $500K capital or the dividends you'd collect?"* Walk them through: 35 years x $30K = ~$1.05M. Dividends exceed the capital, and capital is preserved on top.

## Drawing 3 - CPF Life Depletion (memorise; draw cold)

**Goal:** for prospects 50+. Use CPF Life as anchor, not enemy. Show RA depletes around age 80-81, then PWV is the supplement.

**The math, drawn live (58yo, RA = $250K):**

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| RA grows at 4% to 65 | $250K x 1.04^7 | ~$328,983 |
| Monthly payout from 65 | (from CPF Life Estimator) | ~$1,772/mo |
| Months capital lasts | $328,983 / $1,772 | ~185 months (~15.4 yrs) |
| Capital depletes at age | 65 + 15 | **~80-81** |

Then write **three problems after 81**: bequest = $0, payouts cease, RA was illiquid the whole time.

**The FRS vs ERS twist:** doubling capital (~$213K -> ~$426K) only lifts payout ~1.9x ($1,694 -> $3,336). Capital inefficiency. Use this to pivot to PWV/dividend plan as the better top-up.

**Two-column close:**

```
CPF Life (anchor)          |  PWV / Dividend Plan
Fixed payout               |  Flexible payout
Illiquid                   |  Liquid after ~8 years
Bequest = $0 at ~81        |  Capital preserved
No growth after 65         |  Continues to grow
```

## The Bonuses vs Charges reframe (draw, then close)

**APA bonus structure** ($12K/yr premium): Y1 10%, Y2 10%, Y3 15% = ~$4.2-6.4K upfront. Then **5% from Y10, 8% from Y20** as loyalty bonuses.

**The killer question:** *"Which bonus do you think is bigger - the $6,360 upfront, or the $600-$960/year forever?"* Walk the math: over 30+ years the loyalty bonuses dwarf the welcome bonus, and they pass to the next generation if the policy is inherited.

**PWV bonus structure:** Welcome 3% / 4% / 5% in Y1-3 (~$12K on $100K/yr). Investment bonus 2.5% in Y8-11 (~$10K). **Loyalty bonus 0.4% of policy value forever from Y8** - on $500K that's $2,000/yr, lifetime, including secondary insured's lifetime.

**Breakeven points to memorise:**
- APA: ~34-36 years (after that AIA is losing money on the plan)
- PWV: ~19 years (after that the policy is effectively free)

## AIA vs DIY differentiators - verbal, not drawn

When the client says *"I already do S&P / crypto / Moomoo"*:

| Factor | DIY / S&P 500 | AIA portfolio |
|---|---|---|
| Currency | USD (FX risk) | SGD-hedged |
| Diversification | Often one country | Global + multi-asset |
| Dividend tax | ~30% on US | None |
| Estate tax | ~30% on US assets | None |
| Inheritance | Cash (spouse may liquidate at a loss) | Policy stays managed by AIA |
| Decade-loss precedent | 2000-2010 S&P returned negative | Global diversified rode through |

The inheritance risk point is the one that lands: *"If something happens to you, does your spouse know how to manage your crypto portfolio?"*

## Numbers worth memorising

- **Inflation assumption:** 2.5% p.a. long-term, 3% in some retirement scripts
- **Retirement target reveal:** $3K today -> ~$10K/month at 65 -> **$2.4M corpus**
- **Solution reveal:** $5K/month saving -> ~$1K/month investing (5x reduction)
- **APA welcome bonuses:** Y1 10%, Y2 10%, Y3 15% on a $12K/yr plan
- **APA loyalty:** 5% from Y10, 8% from Y20
- **PWV welcome:** 3% / 4% / 5% in Y1-3 per $100K tranche
- **PWV loyalty:** 0.4% of policy value from Y8, perpetual
- **CPF RA growth:** 4% until 65, **0% after 65**
- **FRS / ERS payouts (2025):** $213K -> $1,707/mo, $426K -> $3,336/mo (only 1.9x for 2x capital)
- **PWV breakeven:** ~19 years. APA: ~34-36 years.

## Common failure modes

- **Opening the iPad first.** Cognitive overload before context. Always paper, then tool.
- **Drawing all three diagrams in one breath.** Pick the one their stage of life calls for - APA for young, PWV for asset-rich, CPF Life for 50+.
- **Doing the math for them.** Hand over the phone. *"Punch this in."* Numbers they calculate themselves stick.
- **Leading with bonuses.** Bonuses are a closer, not an opener. Use them to crush the *"AIA has high charges"* objection at the end.
- **Quoting fund names before drawing the gap.** Product names mean nothing until the shortfall is on the page.
- **Memorising only the diagrams APA needs.** PWV staircase and CPF Life depletion get used as often once you sit with older clients.
