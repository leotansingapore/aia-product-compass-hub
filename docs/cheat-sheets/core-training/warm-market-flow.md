# Warm Market Flow - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** Your first 300 contacts already trust you - their question is *"is he competent yet?"*, not *"do I like him?"* So the warm flow trades novelty for system. Run the Canned Sales Track (CST) - 25 minutes, three questions, memorised to 95% accuracy - and let the structure do the conviction work. When a cold prospect closes, they become warm. The whole career compounds from here.

---

## Market temperature - who's in front of you

| Circle | Type | Typical count (of 300) | Behaviour | First move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot | Parents, siblings, best friends | ~30 | Buy because of who you are | *"I just got my license - can I practise the full thing on you?"* |
| Warm | Friends, ex-colleagues, relatives | ~60 | Respond to your DMs and IG stories | Casual catch-up -> book education appointment |
| Semi-warm | Acquaintances, haven't spoken in years | ~210 | Most untapped - sheer quantity wins | Re-open with a genuine reason (career update + low-bait offer) |
| Cold | Lead-gen, NSF, ads, road shows | Unlimited | 20 leads -> ~3 meetings -> ~1-1.5 sales | Cold call / DM cadence (see appointment-setting cheat sheet) |

**Critical reframe:** when a cold prospect closes, they move into warm. Then semi-warm -> warm -> A-client (advocate who refers). *"Brew old soup."* The work is nurture, not novelty.

---

## The 6-step warm-market outreach

1. **List building** - dump your phone contacts, IG followers, school groups, ex-colleagues into a sheet. Target: 100-300 names. *Project 100* if you want the round number.
2. **Tag by temperature** - hot / warm / semi-warm. Sort by how recently you spoke.
3. **Open with a reason** - never *"want to meet up?"* with no anchor. Use the career update. *"Hey, just transitioned to financial advisory. Updating people in my network - mind if we catch up over coffee?"*
4. **Pick the approach** - Method 1 (direct fact-find appointment) for warm + ready, Method 2 (warm-up first) for semi-warm or rebuilding.
5. **Run the appointment** - CST + TWC for direct, dinner / coffee for warm-up. From a meeting, book a meeting.
6. **Follow up + nurture** - CRM staged. Annual touch on calendar. Birthday, POP, ORD, BTO milestones tagged. Old soup brews here.

**Conversion expectation:** out of 3 warm-up sessions, 1-1.5 will transition to an education / fact-find appointment. Worth it.

## Verbatim warm-market opener - the 5 principles

1. **Sound casual, not like a financial advisor** - you're a friend, not a salesperson
2. **Be genuine** - acknowledge the career transition naturally; don't hide it
3. **Praise the person** - *"I know you're someone responsible..."*
4. **Low bait, not high pitch** - *"...thought you might want to do a quick review"*
5. **Specific time, not open-ended** - *"...Saturday morning or Wednesday after work?"*

**The opener that works:**

> *"Hey [name], been a while - hope you've been good. Quick update: I just transitioned into financial advisory at AIA. I'm reaching out to people I respect in my network to do a free review of their existing policies + financial planning. No pressure, no obligation - I just need to practise on real cases. Saturday morning or Wednesday 8pm easier for you?"*

**Why this works:** career-transition framing handles the *"why now"*. *"Free review"* is low-bait. *"Practise on real cases"* makes them feel like they're helping you, not the other way round.

## The Canned Sales Track (CST) - 25 minutes, 3 questions

**The CST is the canonical AIA pre-rehearsed presentation.** Memorise to 95% accuracy (natural flow, not robotic). Drill until 25 minutes flat.

### Q1. Why Plan?

**Income breakdown** ($50K example): 10% food, 10% transport, 10% parents, 10% misc, 20% CPF, **40% remaining** (usually spent).

**Two income-loss risks:**
- **Temporary** - retrenchment, job loss. Solution: emergency fund, 3-6 months of expenses (~20% of income).
- **Permanent** - death, disability, CI, accident. *"$50K income x 20 years = $1M lost. You can't borrow that."*

**Three risk management methods:** avoidance (impossible) / retention (dangerous) / **transfer** (insurance). Use 10% of income to protect the other 90%.

### Q2. What to Plan?

**Three tiers:**
1. **Risk management** (10% of income) - life, accident, hospitalisation
2. **Wealth accumulation** (10-20% of income) - savings goals, retirement, kids' education
3. **Wealth preservation** (10-50% of liquid assets) - legacy, multi-gen

### Q3. How Much to Plan?

| Coverage | Rule of thumb | Example ($50K income) |
|---|---|---|
| Death / Disability | 10-20x income | $500K - $1M |
| Critical Illness | 100K + 3-5x income | $250K - $500K |
| Hospitalisation | Per analysis (private vs B2) | - |
| Accident | 5x income | $250K |

**CST presentation flow:** 5-10 min rapport / catch up -> 25 min CST -> commit to second appointment (the debrief).

## TWC - the human-capital framing that opens the CST

When young, **human capital > financial capital**. *"Your ability to work is your greatest asset, not your bank account. You are your own golden goose."* Then: *"Which is more important, health or wealth?"* They always say health. *"So we protect health BEFORE we focus on investments."*

**4 pillars of human capital protection:**
1. Hospitalisation - all medical bills covered
2. Accident - injuries
3. Critical Illness - income replacement during recovery
4. Death / Disability - dependents

Then: *"Which one feels most important to you?"* Follow their priority. Use their answer to anchor budget.

**CI coverage rule of thumb:** **100K base + 3-5 years of income.** The 100K is for cancer drug treatments not covered by hosp (single doses ~$10K). The years of income is for the recovery period when they can't work.

**Hospital plan is not a CI plan:** *"Hospital plan pays the hospital. CI plan pays YOU when you can't work."*

## The 3 product packages (warm-market staple)

| Package | Components | Profile | Returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Investment + Whole Life | APA + GPP + HSG + SPA | Highest cost, highest coverage | Moderate |
| 2. Hybrid | PLP + HSG + SPA | Mid, balanced | Better than 1 |
| 3. Investment + Term | APA + SFT + HSG + SPA | Best returns, less long-term coverage | Highest |

On the same $600/mo budget, Package 3 typically wins on returns - $500 goes to APA, $100 to SFT. Package 1 burns $500 on GPP leaving $100 for APA (barely viable).

---

## Progressive closing - one product at a time

Don't try to close the full package in one meeting. Read the priority, close that, then bridge.

- **If priority = CI:** *"Close CI first. Now your base coverage is well covered."*
- **If priority = Hospitalisation:** Close hosp -> *"Hosp doesn't cover outpatient"* -> add accident -> *"Even with hosp, CI is crucial - hosp pays the hospital, CI pays you during recovery."*
- **If priority = Retirement:** Anchor on APA -> bridge to GPP/SFT for protection layer.

Set the **debrief** one week from the close to walk through the remaining gaps. (See `debrief-flow.md`.)

## Prospecting pipeline - the warm-market version

| Pipeline stage | Action | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Lead** | Name on the list, not contacted yet | One reach-out attempt within 7 days |
| **Contacted** | DM / text sent | Follow up if no reply within 5 days |
| **Appointment set** | Date + time + Zoom confirmed | Send confirmation + reminder 24h before |
| **Met** | First appointment done | Post-meeting text within 2 hours |
| **In debrief** | Second meeting booked | 1 week from first meeting |
| **Closed** | Application submitted | Onboarding within 7 days |
| **Reviewed** | Annual review done | Recurring yearly on calendar |
| **Advocate (A-client)** | Actively refers | Referral ask in every review |

**A/B/C client classification:**
- **A** - advocates, refer actively
- **B** - buy + open to repurchase, don't refer
- **C** - minimal engagement

**Dream scenario:** *"Hey bro, my girlfriend is looking at CI - can you talk to her?"* That's an open goal. Build toward it via consistent nurturing - birthday wishes, life-event radar, milestone touches.

## SG Findex - the digital policy consolidation move

For older or "I've forgotten what I have" prospects. SG Findex pulls policy data from insurers, banks, CPF via Singpass.

**The flow:**
1. Send the step-by-step PDF (in your Drive) before the meeting
2. Client logs into AIA+ or Findex site, links insurers + CPF, consents
3. Shares the consolidated report with you
4. You map coverage against planning norms in the policy summary template

**What you can see:** policy names, premiums, coverage, surrender values, CPF.
**What you can't:** detailed fund allocations within other insurers' ILPs.

**Sensitivity:** banking data (credit cards, loans) is touchy - skip unless they offer. Insurance + CPF is usually fine.

**Real-case anchor:** 45yo client thought he had $30K CI - SG Findex + Prudential portal check revealed two forgotten policies worth ~$190K total. Always verify with individual portals when something feels off.

## Diagnostic rules - is the warm flow working?

- **List building done?** If you have less than 100 names, you don't have a list - you have an excuse.
- **Career update sent within 60 days of licensing?** Otherwise the *"just transitioned"* framing expires.
- **CST drilled to 25 minutes?** Time yourself. Recording the run is non-negotiable.
- **Debrief booked from the first meeting?** From a meeting, book a meeting. Always.
- **CRM staged correctly?** Lead -> Contacted -> Set -> Met -> Debrief -> Closed -> Reviewed -> Advocate. If you can't tell where someone is in 5 seconds, the pipeline is broken.
- **Annual review on calendar?** Recurring yearly. If you skip this, the policy goes cold the moment underwriting completes.

- **Activity points target:** 35-40/mo full-timer, 15/mo undergrad
- **Insurance budget rule:** 10-15% of income
- **Investment budget rule:** 20% of income

## Common failure modes

- **Skipping the career-update opener.** Old contacts need a *reason* you're reaching out now. *"Just transitioned"* is the reason.
- **Going straight to fact-find with a semi-warm contact.** Run the warm-up appointment first - dinner, coffee, no pitch.
- **Memorising CST word-for-word.** 95% accuracy = natural flow with structure intact. Word-perfect reads dead.
- **Closing the full package in one meeting.** Progressive closing: priority first, bridge to next, set the debrief.
- **Treating the warm list as one-and-done.** A *no* today is a *yes* in 2 years if you nurture. Old soup.
- **No annual review on the calendar.** Lose this and the relationship dies the moment underwriting completes.
- **Skipping SG Findex for older prospects.** Without it you're working from incomplete data and recommending into gaps.
- **No referral ask at the close.** The warm market's whole compounding power is in advocates. Always ask.
