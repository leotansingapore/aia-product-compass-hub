# Week 3: Wealth Building Principles - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** A client's wealth is two halves - Financial Capital (the money they already have) and Human Capital (their future ability to earn). Your job is to help them convert one into the other, on loop, while protecting the engine that makes it possible. Most plans fail because the engine is left uninsured.

## Total Wealth = Financial Capital + Human Capital

For a 30-year-old earning $100K with 30 working years left, Human Capital is worth roughly **$3M of future income** - usually larger than every Financial Capital line item combined. Most clients only see the visible half.

> *"You are your own golden goose. Your bank account is small. The reason you can eat, live, and have a good life is your ability to keep producing income. That's your greatest asset - not what's in the bank."*

**The conversion engine - four steps on loop:**
1. **Earn more** - career growth, second income, skill compounding.
2. **Spend less** - the gap between income and lifestyle *is* the conversion rate.
3. **Save** - the converted capital, parked safely.
4. **Invest** - let the capital work for itself.

**The "health or wealth" bridge** (use after drawing the two boxes): *"Health or wealth - which is more important?"* -> *"Health"* -> *"Then we can't just talk about investing. We have to protect the engine that produces the wealth in the first place."* That single line gives you permission to talk about coverage before products.

## The TWFPS - four pillars, with benchmark coverage

Three pillars protect Human Capital. One grows Financial Capital. Together they keep the engine from ever restarting from zero.

| Pillar | Protects against | Benchmark |
|---|---|---|
| **Death / Disability** | Family loses the income engine | 10-20x annual income |
| **Critical Illness** *(weakest link)* | Major event + 12-24 months recovery | $100K (cancer drug treatments) + 3-5x annual income |
| **Hospital & Accident** | Inpatient bills + outpatient injury costs | Hospital + Medishield upgrade + PA plan |
| **Investment & Savings** | Inflation eroding capital | Global balanced strategy |

**The CI line that converts more plans than any product feature:** *"The emotional burden of getting cancer is something nobody can take away from you. But the financial burden - that one we can cover."*

**Budget rule of thumb:** ~20% of income to investments + 10-15% to insurance = roughly 30% to *future-you*. Anchor in percentages, ask for the **ceiling** ("max you could realistically set aside") - clients answer truthfully when asked for max.

**The modular close:** close one pillar per meeting. After CI signs, bridge: *"Now that CI's locked in, your hospital plan won't cover outpatient stuff - want to plug that next?"* Each small yes compounds into the full plan.

## Job A vs Job B - the structural argument

Most people evaluate a job offer using only the first column (annual salary). The other five columns are benefit structures - the difference between a job that funds a good life and one that leaves a family exposed.

**You are selling the conversion of Job A income into a Job B life.** Client agrees to set aside 10-20% of income today; future selves get paid across every scenario Job A ignores. The "cost" isn't a premium - it's the Job A version of them who wants to spend that 10-20% on lifestyle right now.

## Wealth Building - five streams, not one bank account

Real wealth is a portfolio of income sources. If one stream is cut off, lifestyle isn't affected. Single-stream clients collapse when their salary stops.

1. **CPF** - mandatory, compounding, tax-advantaged
2. **Property** - if it genuinely cash-flows (primary home usually doesn't)
3. **Investments** - stocks, bonds, ETFs, unit trusts
4. **Insurance-based accumulation** - endowments, ILPs, retirement plans
5. **Business / self-employment** - ventures, renewals, royalties

**Accumulation vs Preservation:** mindset flips in the 50s. A 50% drop two years before retirement pushes retirement back 5-10 years - which is why preservation exists.

## The early-start math (memorise the 25-year-old example)

**Client:** 25 today, wants $4,000/month (today's dollars) at age 65, for 20 years of retirement.

- Adjust for 2% inflation: $4,000 -> **~$8,830/month at 65**
- Lump-sum needed: $8,830 x 12 x 20 = **~$2.12M**
- At bank rate (~0% real): $4,420/month savings required. Impossible.
- At 6% p.a. compounding: **~$500/month** for 40 years -> ~$1M
- Personal outlay: $240K. Compounding does the other $760K.

**A 25-year-old needs $500/month. A 45-year-old needs $2,500+/month** to hit the same target. Time does the heavy lifting. Use iResource's retirement calculator to show clients the cost of a 3-year delay (typically 20-30% of final retirement capital).

## Recurring vs Non-Recurring Revenue

Every business is one of two types. Investors value recurring revenue **5-10x more** than non-recurring at the same revenue figure, because predictability is worth a premium.

**Your renewal income (simplified):** 30 policies/year, $3,000 average annual premium, 40% Y1 commission, 5% renewal Y2+.

| Year | First-year commission | Renewal income | Total |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | $36,000 | $0 | $36,000 |
| 5 | $36,000 | $18,000 | $54,000 |
| 10 | $36,000 | $40,500 | $76,500 |
| 15 | $36,000 | $60,000 | $96,000 |

Add Career Benefit (~100% boost Y2-6) + APF (~1.5%/month from Y7) + PA renewals, and the AIA passive share climbs: **~50% passive by Year 3, ~59% by Year 5, ~66% by Year 10**. That's the structural reason consistent FCs eventually stop chasing FYC.

**A well-served household over 20 years is worth 20-50x the original FYC** once you count cross-sells (wedding, kids, mortgage, parents), referrals, and renewals. Being scared off by a small first-year case is one of the most expensive habits an FC can develop.

**What kills renewals:** over-selling (lapses), poor fit (surrenders), no service (client moves advisor), advisor disappears. **What keeps them:** honest fit + annual touchpoints + life-stage updates + staying in the industry. **Sell slightly less, service a lot more.**

## The wealth-builder's mindset

**The definite aim** has four properties (a "wish" has none):
1. Specific number ("$240K commission income")
2. Specific deadline ("by 31 Dec next year")
3. Defined exchange (hours, calls, study, weekends)
4. Daily action statement (memorised, repeated)

**Total accountability translation.** External attributions are usually true *and* useless - they point to nothing you can change. Translate every "the market is..." into a sentence starting with "I."

| External (no lever) | Internal (lever) |
|---|---|
| "The market is tight." | "I let my pipeline thin in Week 2 - running 30 dials/day this week." |
| "Prospects aren't responding." | "My opener is stale - rewriting it before tomorrow's batch." |
| "Upline isn't sending leads." | "I haven't asked my last 5 clients for referrals - doing those calls today." |

**Peaks and valleys discipline:** the day after a 5-APA weekend and the day after 3 free-looks have the same operational result if you let them - activity stops. **Don't celebrate wins. Don't dwell on losses. Both are the same kind of distraction.** Take ONE day to feel bad about a free-look; next day, continue.

## The 10X Rule - selectively applied

**The 5 principles (in order):** set massive goals -> take massive action -> never reduce the target (raise action instead) -> expect failures (feedback, not stop signs) -> overcome fear with action (move out, don't think out).

**Works for inputs you control:** activity volume (calls, outreach, content), study volume, preparation intensity, fear thresholds.

**Does NOT work for outputs you don't control:** client buying decisions, skill acquisition speed, relationship depth, compliance.

**The worked example (next 30 days):** typical FC plans 5 meetings, 2 closes. 10X version: 20 meetings target, 8 closes target, 150 outreaches, 10 content pieces, 2 mentor role-plays/week. Realistic landing: ~12 meetings, 4 closes. **2-3x your original conservative goal.** That's the rule's actual payoff - 10X target produces 3x results, not 10x.

**The shadow side:** 10X sprints (60-90 days) inside a sustainable year. FCs who try to run 10X for 12 months straight burn out in Year 2. Your first 60 days is one of those sprints.

## Numbers worth memorising

- **10-20x annual income** for Death/Disability coverage (TWFPS benchmark, slightly above the basic 10x rule)
- **$200-400K or 5x annual income** for major CI (broken down: $100K cancer drugs + 3-5x recovery)
- **20% investments + 10-15% insurance** = ~30% of income to *future-you*
- **6% p.a.** - the default modest return assumption for long-horizon planning
- **$500/month for 40 years at 6% ~ $1M** (25-year-old's freedom number)
- **~50% / 59% / 66%** - passive income share at Year 3 / 5 / 10 of consistent production
- **5-10x revenue multiple** - what investors pay for recurring revenue vs non-recurring
- **20-50x first-year commission** - lifetime value of a well-served household

## Common failure modes

- **Selling Level 3 (ILP) to a client with a clear Level 1 (CI) gap** - one diagnosis surrenders the ILP at a loss. Bottom-up always.
- **Quoting a fixed-dollar number ($2.7M) instead of an income stream ($9K/month)** - lump sums scare clients; income streams feel liveable.
- **Skipping clients with small first-year commissions** - the lazy mindset caps Year-15 income at Year-1 size. Every long-term client compounds renewals + Career Benefit + APF + referrals.
- **Treating the 10X Rule as a year-round state** - sprints inside a sustainable year. The shadow side is burnout in Month 12.
- **Spiralling after a free-look or coasting after a big close** - both stop activity, both have the same effect on next month's pipeline.
