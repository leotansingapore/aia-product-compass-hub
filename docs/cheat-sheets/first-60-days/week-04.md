# Week 4: Productivity Principles - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** A slump in your sales is almost always a slump in your prospecting **from 30-60 days ago**. Prospecting is the leading indicator; commissions are lagging. Obsess over the right number, in the right block on your calendar, with the right structure once you sit down with the prospect.

## Prospecting is the lifeblood - the 30-day rule of pipeline gravity

**The sales cycle:** prospecting touch -> first conversation (1-2 wks) -> fact-find (1-2 wks) -> proposal (1-2 wks) -> close -> underwriting (2-6 wks) -> commission paid. **Total: 30-90 days** from first touch to cash. Today's prospecting is next quarter's income.

**Miss a day -> bites you in 30 days. Miss a week -> next quarter's cheque. Miss a month -> 90-day slump with no idea how you got there.** Plant the seeds anyway on bad days.

**The three prospecting buckets - run in parallel, not sequentially:**

| Bucket | Function | Channels |
|---|---|---|
| **A. Getting leads** | Fill the pipeline | Warm market, referrals, digital inbound, natural market, lead lists |
| **B. Making calls** | Names to conversations | Cold calls, WhatsApp/Telegram DMs, networking, follow-up |
| **C. Meeting people** | Conversations to appointments | First meeting, fact-find, proposal/closing |

**Classic new-FC mistake:** 90% time in bucket C (meetings already booked), pipeline (A) dries up, calendar emergency three weeks later.

## The Law of Large Numbers - and count rejections, not appointments

Year-1 baseline ratios (improve with skill):
- **100 dials -> 10-20 connects -> 1-3 appointments -> <1 close per 100 approaches**
- $50K FYC in Year 1 ~ 3,000+ opening approaches across the year ~ **10/day, 60/week**

**The counterintuitive move:** count rejections. You control the number of no's; you do not control the number of yeses. **Target: 50 no's per week** as a Year-1 baseline. Each one is a rep that sharpens opener, objection-handling, and tone.

**Serve, don't sell.** Every call is a service attempt. Some end in "thanks but no" (fine), some in "actually, can you help my sister?" (referral, even better), some in a 20-year client. Your job is to make the attempt with service intent.

## The four business-generating activities

Top producers consistently name the same four as **the only ones that move the income needle**. Everything else (admin, training, CRM organising) is support work.

| # | Activity | Why it's the engine |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Cold-calling / outreach** | Creates new appointments out of nothing |
| 2 | **Opening (1st appointment)** | Converts appointment into budget commitment |
| 3 | **Closing (2nd appointment)** | Converts pipeline into FYC |
| 4 | **Following up** | Recovers the 70%+ that don't close on first attempt |

**The audit question:** how many hours last week went into these four vs everything else? If "everything else" is winning, your next 30 days of income is already in trouble.

## The Time Management Matrix - move 60% of time to Q2

Stephen Covey's four boxes, applied to a new FC.

| | Urgent | Not Urgent |
|---|---|---|
| **Important** | **Q1 Crisis** (minimise) - case deadlines, lapse recovery, complaints | **Q2 Focus Zone** (60% target) - prospecting, fact-find, role-plays, study, sleep |
| **Not Important** | **Q3 Distraction** (delegate/batch) - paperwork, gift delivery, non-strategic queries | **Q4 Waste** (avoid) - scrolling, gossip, Notion reorganisation #5 |

**Typical new FC current state:** Q1 30% / Q2 15% / Q3 25% / Q4 30%. **Target:** Q1 15% / Q2 60% / Q3 15% / Q4 10%. Q1 shrinks naturally because Q2 prevents crises.

**Big rocks first.** Q2 priorities go on the calendar before anything else. Sand and water (Q3 admin, Q4 downtime) fill the gaps. Reverse it and the rocks never fit.

## The day, batched - protect the peak hour

**Find your 60-90 minutes of peak focus** (usually 8:30-10:30am). That hour is off-limits for anything non-revenue-generating. Phone on DND, no Slack, no email.

| Block | Window | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Revenue Block** | 8:30-10:00 | Prospecting calls, proposal writing, deep work |
| **Meeting Block** | 10:00-12:30 | 2-3 back-to-back meetings |
| **Lunch / Break** | 12:30-13:30 | Real recharge, no scrolling |
| **Admin Block** | 13:30-15:30 | Email, Slack, CRM notes, expense claims |
| **Learning Block** | 15:30-17:00 | Product study, role-play prep |

**Context-switching tax: ~20 minutes per switch.** Four task types spread across a day = ~80 minutes/day lost. Batching same-type tasks recovers 5-8 hours/week.

**The "will this matter in 6 months?" filter:** triage interruptions. If yes, handle now. If no, batch into next Admin Block. Most "urgent" client texts get the same outcome at 3 hours as at 30 seconds.

## The 5-phase sales appointment (60 minutes)

Front-load the sales, back-load the servicing. First sale opens a 20-year relationship; the next 20 years are where real income lives.

| Phase | Time | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 1. **Build Rapport** | 5 min max | Personal observation, ask not tell, rephrase to confirm. 30 min of rapport is procrastination disguised as connection. |
| 2. **Agenda + Sell Yourself** | 1 min | State the three-step agenda + your one-sentence mission statement (*"I help [audience] to [outcome] through [framework]."*) |
| 3. **Find Problem, Induce Pain** | 20 min | Five hot-button questions, then *pause* after each. Silence is when clients add the most valuable detail. |
| 4. **Create Desire, Visualize** | 20 min | Benefits not features. *"At 65, this could pay you $4,500/month for life"* beats *"6.75% non-guaranteed return."* |
| 5. **Present Solution + Close** | 10 min | Recommend crisply with one of the four closes below. |

**Closing techniques:**
- **Summary close** - tie multiple benefits together. *"You want to protect X, build Y/month, cover Z. Here's the plan that does all three at $W/month. Shall we proceed?"*
- **Option close** (use when client is close but hesitating) - *"$300/month or $400/month - which do you prefer?"*
- **Small commitments first** (use when client is 80% there) - *"Shall we get the KYC done today so we're ready to move forward?"*
- **Urgency close** (use sparingly, only when real) - *"Premium goes up with your next birthday."*

**Three moments to shut up:** after asking a discovery question, after presenting the recommendation, after asking for the close. First one to speak loses - count to 10 in your head.

## Done over perfect - and the weekly priority ritual

**Schedule your priorities; don't prioritise your schedule.** Decide the 3 most important outcomes for next week, calendar-block them first, defend those blocks. Everything else fights for remaining time.

**Sunday ritual, 30 minutes:** review last week (activity, pipeline, where time leaked) -> pick 3 specific measurable priorities for next week -> block calendar first -> protect one evening for life (Friday dinner, Saturday activity).

**The 80/20 rule on polish:** 80% of the polish takes 20% of the time; the last 20% of polish consumes 80% of the effort and rarely changes outcomes. Ship the proposal, post the LinkedIn draft, run the cold-call script for 20 calls before rewriting.

**The 3-question perfectionism filter:**
1. Is this improving the client outcome, or soothing my anxiety?
2. Would my mentor say this is ready to ship?
3. What's the cost of delaying by a day? (Usually zero. Sometimes a lost client.)

## Feel-Good Productivity - the 3-3-3 framework

Positive emotion is a *driver* of productivity, not a reward. For a new FC running 50 cold calls a week, this is the difference between Month 7 and Month 4.

| Energisers (generate energy) | Unblockers (when work isn't happening) | Sustainers (prevent 12-month burnout) |
|---|---|---|
| **Play** - gamify reps, run streaks | **Procrastination** - name the emotion, start tiny | **Conserve** - A/B/C every task; A's get peak hours |
| **Power** - learn one small thing daily, teach what you learn | **Paralysis** - write the exact next physical action | **Recharge** - sleep 7+, walks without phone, weekends; scrolling is NOT rest |
| **People** - call blocks with peers, weekly mentor check-in | **Perfectionism** - 80% rule, ship rough | **Align** - weekly check that work serves the Day-5 purpose |

**Calendar-driven follow-up - the discipline that compounds.** Every prospect not closed on first attempt gets a calendar event, not a mental note. Default cadence: warm = Day 3/10/30; lukewarm = Day 7/21/60; cold = Day 90 then annual.

## Numbers worth memorising

- **30-90 days** from first prospecting touch to commission paid
- **60/week, 10/day** - opening approaches required for $50K FYC at Y1 ratios
- **50 no's/week** - the rejection quota for a Year-1 FC
- **60-90 min** - your peak focus hour, calendar-blocked, phone on DND
- **20 min** - the context-switch tax per task-type change
- **5 min max** rapport in a 60-minute appointment
- **Q2 60% target** - from a typical 15% baseline
- **3 priorities, not 10** - the Sunday ritual cap

## Common failure modes

- **Spending the week chasing Q1 crises that were Q2 tasks last month** - lapsed policies, compliance deadlines, last-minute submissions. Living in Q1 is the symptom; Q2 neglect is the cause.
- **30 minutes of rapport instead of 5** - procrastination disguised as connection. The meeting runs out of time, "let's continue next session," client ghosts.
- **Filling silence after asking for the close** - the client's pause is evaluative, not negative. Speaking first weakens your position.
- **Polishing scripts/proposals in private** - the cold-call opener will never feel perfect. Run it for 20 calls, then iterate on real reactions.
- **Mental-note follow-up at scale** - once your pipeline hits 30+ active prospects, memory cannot hold it. Calendar event or it didn't happen.
