# Week 10: Product Mastery & Graduation - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** No single plan covers all five pillars - death, CI, hospital, disability, savings. A mature client portfolio is 4-7 policies across 3-5 categories. Stop trying to make one product do everything.

## AIA product taxonomy - 7 categories, 3 buckets

| Bucket | Category | What it does | Example AIA plan |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wealth** | Investment-Linked (ILP) | Investment growth with optional protection wrapper | Pro Achiever (APA), Pro Lifetime Protector (PLP), Platinum Wealth Venture (PWV), Platinum Retirement Elite (PRE), Platinum Wealth Elite (PWE) |
| **Wealth** | Endowment | Structured savings + small insurance element | Various (coupon-paying, lump-sum maturity, retirement-style, SRS-eligible) |
| **Protection** | Whole Life | Lifelong cover (to age 100) + cash value | Guaranteed Protect Plus (GPP) - cornerstone limited-pay par |
| **Protection** | Term | Pure protection, no cash value, low premium | Secure Flexi Term (SFT) |
| **Protection** | Universal Life | High-leverage USD-denominated life cover | HNW legacy planning (3-10x leverage) |
| **Living Benefits** | Disability Income | Monthly income if unable to work | Premier Disability Cover (PDC) |
| **Living Benefits** | Accident & Health | Hospital, accident, outpatient | HealthShield Gold Max + Vital Health rider, Solitaire PA, Ultimate Critical Cover (UCC) |

**Rule:** category-first, product-second. Start with a specific plan name and the client becomes a feature-comparer who shops competitors.

## Coverage benchmarks (memorise the multiples)

For a $102K/year ($8,500/mth) earner:

| Coverage | Multiple | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Premature death / TPD** | 10x gross income + major liabilities | $1.4M ($1M + $400K mortgage) |
| **Critical Illness** | 3-5x annual income + **$100K buffer** | $300-500K |
| **Accident** | 5x annual income | $500K |
| **Hospitalisation** | Based on ward preference | Private (Max A) vs subsidised |
| **Disability Income** | 60-70% of monthly income | ~$5K/mth replacement |

The $100K CI buffer specifically funds cancer drug treatments (one vial ~$10K) not covered by hospital plan.

## ILP mechanics - the conviction stack vs DIY S&P 500

When prospect asks "why not DIY into S&P / Endowus / robo?" - eight specific answers, not four:

| # | Risk in DIY | What AIA structure does | Hook |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dividend withholding tax (30% US-domiciled / 15% Irish-domiciled) | 0% at client level | 30% -> 0% |
| 2 | USD/SGD FX risk - DBS forecasts SGD parity by 2040 | SGD-denominated, hedged | ~35% headwind |
| 3 | US estate tax 40% above $60K exemption (non-US person) | Nominated beneficiaries, non-US-situs | $1M gone on $2.8M |
| 4 | Concentration - 100% one market | Global; S&P 500 = ~18% of Elite Adventurous | 18% not 100% |
| 5 | Lost-decade risk (S&P 2000-2010: -9%; Int'l Small Cap Value: +191%) | Multi-region, multi-asset exposure | -9% vs +191% |
| 6 | Execution on death - family inherits an app they never logged into | Advisor of record + coordinated estate | One phone call |
| 7 | Inheritance mismanagement - cash lump sum gets spent | **Secondary insured** - policy continues on next life | 40yr plan -> 100yr plan |
| 8 | Macro USD pressure (debt, BRICS, crypto de-dollarisation) | SGD hedging insulates portfolio | - |

**Don't spray all eight.** Pick the one that matches the prospect's actual concern.

## Policy Summary - the 5 things to check on every existing policy

1. **Sum Assured** - still appropriate? Rule of thumb: life cover = 10x current income.
2. **Riders** - CI (Major + Early), Waiver of Premium, Multiplier, Multi-pay CI present?
3. **Exclusions** - pre-existing conditions, activities, waiting periods?
4. **Beneficiary nomination** - married/divorced/kids born since policy issue?
5. **Payout structure** - accumulation vs coupon-paying - fits current life stage?

**Common gaps in 90% of existing portfolios:**
- No or insufficient CI (most have life from a parent's time, few have CI, fewer have Early CI)
- Hospital is company plan only - ends with employment
- Zero disability income coverage
- Life policies sized for "then" not "now" ($50-200K from 10+ years ago)
- No structured accumulation vehicle ("bank + CPF" default)
- Outdated beneficiary nomination

**Pre-licence highest-leverage rep:** offer 3-5 friends a free policy summary using the canonical Google Slides template. Build muscle memory for reading docs across insurers. Re-engage post-licence with: *"I made you that summary in March. I'm licensed now - want to look at the gaps together?"*

## Proposal - the 5-part template

| Page | Content |
|---|---|
| **1. Your goals** | Restate in their language, with their numbers |
| **2. Current situation** | Coverage snapshot + gaps named clearly |
| **3. The plan** | Category first, then specific product, with one-sentence "why" per recommendation |
| **4. Illustrations** | Guaranteed + non-guaranteed (4% and 8% where applicable). State assumptions. |
| **5. Next steps** | Full plan OR phased option. Two options reduces the "no". |

**Length:** 4-6 pages max. Length over 10 = client skims, forgets, objects. Tone: their language, not jargon. Personalisation: name used 3-5x, reference specifics they said.

## The close as safe-cracking - 5 numbers in the combination

| # | What it is | Built by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Product** - this plan fits their goals/numbers/life | Proposal + illustrations |
| 2 | **You** - client trusts you personally | Fact-find + concept selling |
| 3 | **Firm** - client trusts insurer/firm behind plan | Claims stats + firm story |
| 4 | **Action threshold** - downside of acting is low | Free-look, reversibility, small start |
| 5 | **Pain threshold** - pain of NOT acting is vivid | Re-introducing the pain from fact-find |

When the handle doesn't turn - diagnose which number is below 9. Most common misses for new FCs: #4 (lower with free-look + small-start) and #5 (re-ask "what happens in 5 years if nothing changes?").

## 5 closing techniques - which technique for which moment

| Technique | When | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Columbo Close** | End of meeting / CFR | "One more thing - if I come back with a plan that addresses [X/Y/Z] within your budget, any reason we wouldn't move forward?" |
| **Option Close** | Client 80% there, hesitating | "Would you prefer the $300/mth or $400/mth option?" - both options are a yes |
| **Assumption Close** | Body language clearly positive | "I'll need your NRIC - can I take a photo now?" Use carefully or it feels manipulative |
| **Silent Close** | After asking the close - DO NOT TALK | Count to 30 internally. He who breaks silence first is most eager to deal. |
| **Summary Close** | Multi-benefit plan, decision fatigue | Restate the 3 benefits + total premium as one "yes" |

**"Let me think about it" reframe:** *"Of course. Premium is locked at today's age (every year you wait it goes up). And there's a 14-day free-look for a full refund. Would it make sense to lock today's rate and use those 14 days to decide?"*

## Late-stage objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Let me think about it" | "Of course. What specifically would you like to think about? Happy to clarify now." |
| "Need to talk to my spouse" | "Absolutely. Want a 1-page summary you can discuss together? Happy to schedule a 3-way call." |
| "Too expensive" | "Understood. Let's work backwards from a budget you're comfortable with - what monthly amount feels sustainable?" |
| "I'll wait till next year" | "What would be different in a year? If nothing, we might as well start while you're thinking about it." |
| "Just send the documents" | "Of course. But let me ask - what would specifically make you want to proceed after reviewing?" |

By the time you're at close, **resistance is largely emotional, not logical.** Don't respond with more logic. Reassure. Slow down. Give them an out ("you don't have to decide today - but let's get the KYC started so we're ready if you say yes next week").

## Claims handling - the part that matters most

**Minor claims (5-step process):**
1. Client documents (photos, receipts, reports)
2. Upload to AIA Claims portal (advisor report + attachments)
3. Keep personal record (claim number, submission date)
4. Follow up within 24 hours on any AIA queries
5. Confirm payment receipt with client

**Major claims (CI / TPD / Death) - different job entirely:**
- **Empathy first.** "I'm so sorry. How are you holding up? How can I help beyond the claim?"
- **Process quickly.** Client needs cash for treatment decisions.
- **Attend the funeral / wake on death claims.** Not optional. Presence says more than anything.
- **Initiate the death claim without being asked.** Handle all paperwork. Only ask them to sign what's required.
- **Be there at Week 3, Week 10, Month 6.** Grief doesn't end when the payout clears.

**A well-handled minor claim is worth 10 marketing touchpoints.** A well-handled major claim is when you genuinely commit to the career.

## Numbers worth memorising

- **10x / 5x / 5x** - life / CI / accident multiples of annual income
- **$100K buffer** - CI buffer for cancer drug treatment
- **60-70%** of income - disability income replacement target
- **4-6 pages** - proposal length ceiling
- **2 options** in proposal close - reduces the "no"
- **30 seconds** silent count after asking the close
- **14 days** free-look period - the risk-reversal anchor
- **4-7 policies** across 3-5 categories = mature client portfolio
- **~$1,000 FYC** Year 1 / **~$25,000 CLV** over 30 years per client

## Operating cycle from Day 61

Reconnect -> Introduce -> Review -> Needs Analysis -> Solution -> Service -> Referral -> back to Reconnect.

**Weekly cadence for first 180 days:**
- 5 reconnecting meetings
- 5 CSTs / fact-finds
- 3 proposals / policy summaries
- 20 referrals asked for

## Common failure modes

- **Trying to find "the one plan that solves everything."** Doesn't exist. Mature portfolios are 4-7 policies.
- **Memorising plan names first.** Categories don't change; plan names do. Learn category -> use-case mapping first.
- **Pitching ILPs to short-horizon clients.** ILPs need 15+ years. Short horizon = endowment / bonds.
- **Recommending replacement of an old whole-life policy lightly.** Loses cash value, lapses benefits, may restart claim waiting periods. Only replace on clear-cut mis-match.
- **Skipping the Solitaire PA / hospital plan in the package.** A&H is the "fries and Coke" side order. ~$50/mth, never propose without it - the missing category becomes the comparison shop.
- **Sending a 12-page proposal.** Clients skim, forget, object. 4-6 pages max.
- **Breaking the silence after asking the close.** Volunteering more options rewards hesitation and confuses the decision. Count to 30. He who speaks first is most eager.
- **Treating "no" as a real no.** 3 out of 10 "no" clients today become "yes" in 6-18 months. Graceful exit + quarterly nurture preserves the future yes.
- **Skipping the funeral on a death claim.** Your presence is the single most important service moment of the entire relationship.

## Graduation - the 3-year target

Today is Day 60. Day 1,095 is three years out. What does Year-3 you look like?

- 150-300 active clients
- Annual review rhythm running for 1-2 years
- First major claims processed
- Renewal income arriving regardless of new sales

The first 60 days were learning. The next 60 are **repeating.** You've been taught the full arc. Run it dozens of times, refining each iteration.
