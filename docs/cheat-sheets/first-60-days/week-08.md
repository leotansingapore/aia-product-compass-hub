# Week 8: Fact Finding & Reading People - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** Objections are mostly reflexes, not refusals. Cushion first, pick the right framework, ask one right question - and 70% of "not interested" reopens to a meeting.

## Cushion-first - 5 stock phrases that go under every framework

Before any "however / but / actually" - one acknowledgement sentence. Cushion is the difference between a script that lands and one that grates.

| Cushion phrase | Use when |
|---|---|
| *"That makes complete sense"* | Default for any objection |
| *"That's totally fair"* | When prospect feels defensive |
| *"Honestly, fair - and..."* | Faster register, casual |
| *"You're not the first person to feel that way"* | Empathy + peer-group lead-in |
| *"I'd probably feel the same way in your shoes"* | High-emotion objections |

If the next word out of your mouth is a reframe word, the cushion comes first. Skip it and even a perfect script feels argued *with*, not *to*.

## Objection diagnostic - Type 1 vs Type 2 vs Uncertainty

| Type | What they're rejecting | Right move |
|---|---|---|
| **Type 1 - concept dismissal** ("don't believe in insurance", "not in the market", "scam") | The whole idea | **Curious-question first** - *"Quick one before I respond - what shaped that view for you?"* Listen. Reframe to what they raised. Counter-arguing deepens dismissal. |
| **Type 2 - specific concern** ("too busy", "no money", "post it out", "what's the idea?") | This call, right now | Cushion + framework + ART back to the appointment ask |
| **Uncertainty (post-pitch only)** | Trust in product, you, or firm - they can't say it out loud | **Looping** (Next 60 Days Day 40). Stop rebutting, rebuild certainty on all three, re-ask. |

Diagnostic in one move: *"Are they rejecting the whole industry or just my specific ask?"*

## Which framework for which objection

| Framework | Use for | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **A - Acknowledge / Right-Question / Reframe** (workhorse) | Default. Most reflex objections. | Acknowledge -> ask which version of the objection they mean -> reframe -> close on two specific times |
| **B - Feel / Felt / Found** (empathy) | Loyalty, family, prior bad experience | "I understand how you feel" -> "many of my clients felt the same" -> "what they found was..." |
| **C - Boomerang** ("that's exactly why") | Prospect names a state they think disqualifies them | Flip the state into the qualifier - "that's exactly why busy people without a plan compound their problems" |

**Default to A.** Use B only on genuinely emotional objections (overuse tips manipulative). Use C only when the objection literally describes why they think they're not a fit - acknowledge step must land first.

## 10 stock reflex objections - skip-Q responses

When the objection is unmistakably a 1-second reflex, skip the right-question diagnostic. Each ends on a binary time close.

| Objection | Quick reframe |
|---|---|
| "Not interested" | "Fair - wouldn't expect you to be interested in something you haven't seen. Tuesday 7pm or Saturday 10am?" |
| "Not in the market" | "Honestly I'd have been surprised if you said you were. I have ideas that'll be useful when you're ready. Tuesday 7pm or Saturday 10am?" |
| "No money" | "Totally fair - that's a good reason to talk, not skip it. More about what you already pay for than adding expenses. Tuesday or Saturday?" |
| "No need" | "Fair, and you'd be the only one to call that. Since it's 20 minutes - Tuesday 7pm or Saturday 10am?" |
| "Too busy" | "Guessed you would be - that's why I called rather than dropped by. Tuesday or Saturday?" |
| "What's the idea?" | "Worst thing I could do is summarise it badly over the phone. 20 minutes with the actual numbers - Tuesday or Saturday?" |
| "Post it out / send info" | "Anything I send blind is too generic or too long. Quicker if I see your situation first. Tuesday or Saturday?" |
| "Already have policies" | (Boomerang) "Most people with cover don't know day-to-day what it does. 30 minutes turns a policy you own into a plan you understand." |
| "Already have an advisor" | (Feel-Felt-Found) "Totally respect - especially if it's family. My clients kept theirs and added me as a same-life-stage second pair of eyes." |
| "Obstinate" (door closing) | (Soft exit) "If I'm in your neighbourhood I'd love to drop in - if you have a few moments at that time, I'll be glad to share what I have in mind." |

## SPIN intro - the grammar of right-questions

| Phase | Job | Question stem |
|---|---|---|
| **S - Situation** | Uncover facts | *"What does your typical month look like financially?"* |
| **P - Problem** | Surface gaps | *"How confident are you that your current saving rate gets you there?"* |
| **I - Implication** | Make them feel the weight (Day 49) | *"What would happen if this gap stayed open another 5 years?"* |
| **N - Need-Payoff** | Client articulates the benefit (Day 49) | *"What would it mean to know - today - your retirement is locked in?"* |

**The right-question 3-point checklist** - apply to every question:

1. Does it lead to my objective?
2. Is it specific (forces a specific answer)?
3. Is it logical and indisputable (can the prospect disagree without sounding unreasonable)?

If any fails, rewrite before asking. Fail rate without this gate: ~30%.

## DISC - read in 60 seconds, adapt for the meeting

| Profile | Motivation | Signals | Sell with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D - Dominance** | Results, control | Firm handshake, "what's the bottom line?" | 2-3 options, fast, no rapport-padding |
| **i - Influence** | Recognition, fun | Talkative, asks about you, animated | Stories, social proof, similar-client narratives |
| **S - Steadiness** | Relationships, stability | Soft-spoken, listens, conflict-averse | Slow, warm, space to consult family, peace-of-mind framing |
| **C - Conscientiousness** | Accuracy, data | Reserved, takes notes, asks for details | Illustrations, fee structure, limitations stated upfront |

**Mismatch costs:** high-D selling to high-S = "aggressive." High-i selling to high-C = "no data, just stories." Adapt to the client, not the other way round.

## 6P Storytelling

| Step | What goes in |
|---|---|
| **1. Prospect** | "Marcus, 34, married, two kids, tech..." |
| **2. Problem** | "Always said his company plan covered him..." |
| **3. Pain** | Emotional consequence - savings drained, plan broken |
| **4. Promise** | "A 2-hour review fixed it..." |
| **5. Proof** | Client's own words (anonymised testimonial) |
| **6. Purpose** | The universal lesson |

**60-90 seconds total.** Close with a right-question that maps the story onto them: *"If a $30K cap was the difference for Marcus's family, what would the cap on your current cover be?"*

**Three story arcs to keep in library:** crisis (Marcus - CI gap caught up), positive (Priya - started at 27, $94K by 38), expensive-lesson (Ben - $44K out-of-pocket on basic hospital plan).

**Never fabricate.** Use anonymised real cases. Clients sense fiction; trust dies.

## Fact-find chaining - the Discovery Funnel

A single Problem question rarely surfaces the hot button. Chain:

1. **Generic opener** - "What does retirement look like for you?"
2. **Specific follow-up** - "When you picture travel - what specifically?"
3. **Emotional deepener** - "What's kept that from happening?"
4. **Actionable close** - "If the math worked - how soon would you actually go?"

The 4th answer is the hot button. The 1st answer would produce a generic pitch.

**Listening discipline:** 80% listen, 20% talk during Situation + Problem phases. Pause 5+ seconds after each Problem question. The sentence after a pause is the most valuable.

## ABCD Four Promises - open every Financial Health Review with these

- **A - Assurance** - "Not here to sell. Here to find out about you and your goals."
- **B - Best Interest** - "What I plan is what I'd do for myself in your shoes."
- **C - Confidentiality** - "Strictest confidence on everything we share."
- **D - Full Disclosure** - "Share freely so I can make the best recommendation."

Memorise. Internalise. Naturalise. Flips the meeting frame from "being sold to" to "someone is here to help me."

## Numbers worth memorising

- **70%** of "not interested" is timing or trust, not a real no
- **80/20** listen-to-talk ratio in Situation + Problem phases
- **30+** market surveys before RNF clears - the floor
- **45-60 min** for a proper SPIN arc. Below 15 min = "Situation + pitch", not SPIN.
- **6-8x** memorability of stories vs facts
- **5 sec pause** after every Implication question - don't rescue, let it land

## Common failure modes

- **Treating every objection as real.** "Not interested" inside 4 seconds is almost always reflex. Cushion + reframe + re-ask.
- **Skipping the cushion.** Going straight to "however" makes prospects defend their position harder. Cushion line must precede every reframe word.
- **Using Feel-Felt-Found on a small reflex objection.** Reads as manipulation. Reserve for genuinely emotional (loyalty, family, prior hurt).
- **Three Implications in a row.** Crosses into emotional manipulation. Pivot to Need-Payoff to shift tone toward solution.
- **Telling stories to a C-profile.** They want data. Storytelling feels manipulative to them. Match tool to audience.
- **Fabricating "client" stories.** Clients sense fiction. Career model collapses. Use real anonymised cases only.
- **Asking publicly available info ("where do you work?")** when LinkedIn says it. Trust leak before the meeting starts. Pre-meeting research = 60-70% of basic facts already known.
- **Filling the silence after an Implication question.** The pause is doing your work. Count to 10 internally. The client's sentence after the pause is the most valuable thing in the meeting.
