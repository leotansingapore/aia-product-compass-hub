# Week 2: Industry Context & The Freedom Business - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** This is not an insurance career; it is a freedom-business that happens to use insurance as one of its instruments. Every client's wealth path runs through five segments, four levels, and four income quadrants. If you cannot place a client on all three maps in real time, you are still pitching products.

## The five segments of personal insurance

Every client conversation maps to one of these. Most clients walk in with one or two at best (employer hospital plan + some savings).

| Segment | Problem it solves | Typical coverage |
|---|---|---|
| **Death / TPD** | Dependents lose your income | 10x annual income |
| **Critical Illness** | 3-5 year recovery, no income, home modifications | 5x (Major CI) + 2x (Early CI) |
| **Hospitalisation** | Inpatient bills - surgery, ward, in-hospital treatment | Maximum private/restructured tier limit |
| **Personal Accident** | Outpatient accident bills - clinic, physio, dental | Reimbursement up to policy limit |
| **Savings / Investments** | Retirement - no income, still alive | Target retirement income x longevity |

**The pattern:** first four are protection (bad things happening); fifth is accumulation (good things taking time). Hospital plan pays the hospital; PA plan reimburses outpatient costs - do not conflate them.

**AIA in one minute:** Singapore since 1931. Mission: *healthier, longer, better lives.* iSmart (phone, campaign sharing) + iPos (iPad, point-of-sale submission) are the two tools you live in.

## The Financial Freedom Pyramid - bottom-up, non-negotiable

You cannot permanently build a level above without the level below. Most stuck clients try to live at Level 3-4 lifestyle on a Level 1 foundation.

| Level | Layer | Components |
|---:|---|---|
| 4 | **Wealth Legacy** | Will, CPF nominations, trusts, estate-equalisation life cover |
| 3 | **Wealth Accumulation** | CPF, endowments, ILPs, direct investments, property (if it cash-flows) |
| 2 | **Short-term Savings** | 3-6 months liquid emergency fund |
| 1 | **Risk Management (foundation)** | Death/TPD, CI, hospital + rider, PA, disability income |

**Diagnostic move in every meeting:** start at the lowest missing level. **Most clients absorb only 1-2 decisions per meeting** - lay out one or two gaps, fix them, return for the next. Selling Level 3 before Level 1 means one CI diagnosis surrenders the ILP at a loss, clawback hits, client tells 10 friends.

## The Cashflow Quadrant - where this career actually lives

Robert Kiyosaki's four boxes, stripped down: most paycheques come from the left column, most wealth is built on the right.

| Active income (trade time) | Passive income (system works) |
|---|---|
| **E - Employee** - works for someone else, income = hours x rate, taxed hardest | **B - Business owner** - owns a system that runs without them |
| **S - Self-employed** - works for themselves, income = hours x rate (higher), tax high | **I - Investor** - capital generates capital (dividends, rent, gains) |

**Your career trajectory:**
- Year 1-2: 100% S (you are the productive unit)
- Year 3+: renewal income starts arriving regardless of effort - partial B
- Year 5+: hired associates + content engine + referral network = real B
- Year 7+: capital from higher income produces I income

**Two passive levers that compound together:**
1. **Renewal commissions** - your past work paying you forward.
2. **Team overrides** - other people's current work paying you (~30% override on each associate's FYC).

**Recruitment starts Day 1, not Year 3.** Your upline runs the recruiting conversation; your job is the introduction. The unguarded warm-market window only opens once.

## The poor, middle, rich - by behaviour, not income

The three classes are defined by what happens *between* pay cheques, not by salary.

| Class | Pay cheque behaviour | Primary need |
|---|---|---|
| **Poor** | Income barely covers expenses; gap closed by borrowing | Build knowledge, skills, experience to raise income |
| **Middle class** | Income covers comfortably, but lifestyle inflates to match. Acquires liabilities they call "assets." | Learn asset vs liability distinction; redirect lifestyle growth into asset accumulation |
| **Wealthy** | Passive income covers expenses regardless of working | Preserve, transfer, sometimes re-invest |

**The 60-second asset test:** does it put money INTO your pocket -> asset. Does it take money OUT -> liability. A primary home with mortgage, a car, a luxury watch, a country-club membership - all liabilities by this test. Rental property with positive cashflow, dividend-paying stocks, CPF, **FC renewal commissions** - all real assets.

**Same income, 4x net worth example.** Person A: $1.1M condo with $900K mortgage + Tesla on loan + watches + ILP = ~$300-350K real net worth. Person B: $800K paid-off HDB + used car + CPF $200K + investments $250K + retirement plan $80K = ~$1.3M. Same $180K/year income. Person A *looks* richer. Person B *is* roughly 4x wealthier.

## Job A vs Job B - the diagnostic for every fact-find

Two job offers, same industry, same seniority - but only Job B pays in every scenario that matters.

| | Well | Sick / injured | Dead | Hospital | Kids' college | Retirement |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|---:|
| **Job A** | $100K | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 | $0 |
| **Job B** | $85K | $66K | $500K | Full cover | $50K | $50K/yr |

**How to use it:** draw the table on paper in 30 seconds, fill in the client's actual numbers from fact-finding, **leave blank columns blank**, pause, ask *"which column worries you most?"* Then listen. That answer is your needs analysis in 90 seconds.

**The standard rebuttal to "my company already gives me insurance":** *"That's great - your company gives you job benefits. They're tied to you keeping the job. If you leave, get laid off, or retire, the coverage disappears. I'd like to help you build benefits tied to you, not your employer."*

## The freedom formula - what you are really selling

> **Passive income > Monthly expenses = freedom**

Not net worth. Not retirement. **Cash flow.** A $200K/year doctor on 70-hour weeks with no savings is less free than a $70K teacher with a paid-off house and 15 years of savings.

**Don't sell products. Sell an income stream.** "$4,500/month for life starting at 65" beats "$2.7M lump-sum target" every time. Lump-sum numbers scare clients; income-stream numbers feel liveable.

**The retirement gap math:**
- Future monthly expenses at retirement = today's x inflation (2% p.a.)
- Gap = future monthly expenses - CPF LIFE payout - other passive income
- That gap is what your accumulation plan funds

## Numbers worth memorising

- **5 segments** of personal insurance
- **4 levels** of the Financial Freedom Pyramid (Risk Mgmt -> Savings -> Accumulation -> Legacy)
- **4 quadrants** of cashflow (E, S, B, I)
- **2% p.a.** - default inflation assumption for Singapore retirement planning
- **4% safe withdrawal rate** - converts a retirement lump sum into an annual income figure
- **~50% passive income by Year 3** of consistent production; **~66% by Year 10**
- **Year 2-3 crossover** - when an FC's income typically passes a corporate peer's

## Common failure modes

- **Confusing hospital plan with CI plan** - hospital plan pays the hospital; CI plan pays *you* when you can't work for years. They solve different problems.
- **Skipping the pyramid sequence** - clients chase Level 3 returns before fixing Level 1 protection. One uninsured CI event forces liquidation of Level 3 at the worst moment.
- **Living in S forever** - new FCs see themselves as freelancers, never build the team layer that takes them to B. Override income caps if you wait until Year 3 to start recruiting.
- **Filling in blanks on the Job A/B table for the client** - the silence is what makes them confront their own gaps. Resist the urge to fill it.
- **Trying to close all four TWFPS pillars in one meeting** - information density crashes the conversation. Modular close, one pillar per meeting, with bridges: *"now that X is locked in, here's what X doesn't cover."*
