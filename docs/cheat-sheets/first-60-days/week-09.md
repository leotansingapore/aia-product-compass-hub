# Week 9: Uncovering Needs & Sales Concepts - Cheat Sheet

> **The one idea:** Situation + Problem questions uncover the gap. Implication + Need-Payoff make the client feel it then want to close it. By the time the proposal lands, the meeting closes itself.

## SPIN - the 4 phases, with question stems

| Phase | Time in 60-min fact-find | Job | Question stems |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **S - Situation** | 5-10 min | Uncover facts | *"What does your typical month look like financially?"* / *"What insurance plans do you have personally + through employer?"* / *"When was your last review?"* |
| **P - Problem** | 10-15 min | Surface gaps (implied needs) | *"How confident are you that your current saving rate gets you there?"* / *"What concerns you most about your financial future?"* / *"What part of your plan keeps you up at night?"* |
| **I - Implication** | 10-15 min | Make them feel the weight | *"If you were unable to work for 3 years, what would happen to your family's finances?"* / *"What will it cost if the education fund comes up 50% short?"* / *"How will you feel at 55 realising it's not enough?"* |
| **N - Need-Payoff** | 5 min | Client articulates the benefit | *"What would it mean to know today your retirement is locked in?"* / *"How would your spouse feel if you could show her the numbers and say 'we're set'?"* / *"Are these benefits important enough to want a plan?"* |

**Emotional arc:** Neutral -> Slight unease -> Real concern -> Felt need -> Hopeful -> Decisive. The client does the work. You ask.

## The buy / don't-buy scale

> **BUY** when: seriousness of problem > cost of solution.
> **DON'T BUY** when: cost > seriousness.

A "too expensive" objection usually means **the problem hasn't been made serious enough**, not that the price is wrong. Don't drop the premium. Go back and deepen Implication.

## The 10-second silence sequence (post-Implication)

| Seconds | What's happening in their brain |
|---:|---|
| 0-2 | Register the question |
| 2-5 | Search for a specific answer |
| 5-8 | Concrete image forms - spouse, kids, scenario |
| 8-10 | Emotional weight lands |

**Interrupt before second 8 and you cut the emotion.** New FCs typically bail at second 2-3. Train the 10-second hold deliberately.

**Productive vs dead silence:**
- Productive (hold) - eyes moving, slight facial movement, breathing slows, body leans in
- Dead (gentle rescue) - eyes glazed, looking at phone, body closed, expression flat

## Right-question construction toolkit (6 Golden Rules)

| # | Rule | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Keyword echo** - repeat their loaded word in your next question | Objection push-back, soften strong positions |
| 2 | **Menu of possibilities** - give 2-3 options when they hedge | Vague "let me think about it" |
| 3 | **Contrast + impactful words** | Implication questions where weight needs to land |
| 4 | **Binary with obviously better option** | Need-Payoff, trial closes |
| 5 | **Non-committal language** (may / might / possibility / could) | Trial closes, rapport-fragile moments |
| 6 | **Quote respected third parties** | Hardened objections, deep concept dismissals |

## CFR (Client Financial Review) - the 3-section structure

| Section | Time | Job |
|---|---:|---|
| **1. Situation + Priorities** | 15-20 min | Open chat about family, goals, life stage. End by ranking goals #1/#2/#3 in client's own voice. |
| **2. Problem Development** | 20-30 min | 3-5 Problem questions per top priority. Probe until a real gap is acknowledged. |
| **3. Financial Numbers** | 15-20 min | Income, expenses, assets, liabilities, existing cover, budget capacity. Last because it's most sensitive. |

**Total: 60-90 min.** Don't rush. Section 3 lands easily only because Sections 1-2 earned the trust.

## Section 3 - the 6 categories of financial data

1. **Income** - gross monthly, bonus, spouse, stability
2. **Expenses** - essential + lifestyle + annual + parents' allowance
3. **Assets** - cash, investments, CPF balances, property net equity, policy cash values
4. **Liabilities** - mortgage, car loan, credit cards, student loans
5. **Existing cover** - life, CI, hospital, disability, PA, employer benefits
6. **Budget capacity** - offer ranges ($200/$500/$1,000), never open question

**The smooth transition:** *"In order to plan for the future, we need to know where you are today. Simple terms - I need to ask one or two basic questions."*

## Colombo Close - end every CFR with it

> *"Before I go - one final question. If I come back with a plan that addresses [priority 1], [priority 2], [priority 3], within the $[budget] you mentioned, can you see any reason why we wouldn't move forward?"*

Three answers:
- **"No, no reason"** -> 90% pre-closed before next meeting
- **"Need to check with spouse"** -> real info to handle
- **"Actually, concerned about X"** -> gold - 5 days to address in proposal

## Retirement Step-by-Step CST - the 5 inputs + 2 diagrams

**Capture first:**

1. Current age
2. Retirement age (default 65)
3. Life expectancy (default 85)
4. Inflation (default 2.5%)
5. Target monthly lifestyle in today's $

**Diagram 1 (red) - The Problem (5 steps):**
1. Mark today, draw inflation slope to age 65
2. Ask them to guess the future cost - then reveal (~$11,800/mth on a $5,000 today)
3. Multiply: future mth x 12 x 20 retirement years = **~$2.85M lump sum**
4. Dashed depletion line to $0 at 85
5. No-investing reality: $2.85M / 35yrs / 12mths = **~$6,800/mth save** to end at zero

**Diagram 2 (green) - The Solution (4 steps):**
1. Invest, not save - same target at $1,700/mth (80%+ reduction)
2. Hit $2.85M at 65 - reinvest for dividends
3. Two paths: dashed grey to $0 (old) vs flat green line preserving capital while paying ~$14,900/mth dividends at 6.3% yield
4. Side-by-side card - $6,800 save vs $1,700 invest. **Same goal. Choice = invest.**

**Door-opener at the end:** "Mind if I run through a proper fact-find, then come back with an actual plan that gets you to $2.85M at $1,700/mth instead of $6,800?"

**Concept-draw FIRST. Slides come SECOND.** Open the laptop and you lose the room.

## CST Risks Angle - the income-loss spine

| Loss type | Examples | Honest solution |
|---|---|---|
| **Temporary** | Sacked, resigned, retrenched | 3-6 months emergency cash |
| **Permanent** | Death, disability, CI, accident, old age | **Insurance transfer** - savings won't catch up |

**The $1 million question (verbatim):** *"If you're 25 and need $25K/year for 40 years, that's $1M. Can your family lend you $1,000? Probably. $10,000? Maybe. $1,000,000? No. We don't want clients to beg, borrow, or steal."*

**3 risk-management methods - elimination logic:**
1. **Avoid** - unrealistic. Can't avoid CI or old age.
2. **Retain (self-insure)** - needs $1M in cash already. Almost no one has it.
3. **Transfer** - 10% of income to protect the other 90%. The only viable answer.

## When to lead with Risks vs Wealth CST

| Client situation | Lead with |
|---|---|
| Young single, no dependents | Wealth |
| New parents, pre-retirees, recent health scare | **Risks** |
| Newly married no kids | Risks (moderate) -> Wealth |
| Sudden income jump (promotion, windfall) | Wealth, then Risks |
| Client who "has everything sorted" | Risks (surface gaps) |

Lead with the angle that matches the client's **present life tension.**

## Concept Selling - why it has to exist

Financial products live in the right column. Sell them like the right column.

| Property | Financial products |
|---|---|
| Can see / touch / feel | **No** |
| Can understand | **Yes** - through concepts |

Three reference concepts:

| Concept | Use when |
|---|---|
| **Non-Property Alternative** | Client says "I want passive income from property" - participating plan delivers similar cash flow without complexity |
| **Bank Deposit Alternative** | Idle cash earning 0.5-1% - systematic savings plan projects 3-4% + estate transfer |
| **Life Insurance as Family Asset** | Breadwinner - $500/mth premium for $500K cover = ~40x multiplier in Year 2 |

**Every concept ends with a recommendation path.** Concepts without product tie-ins are wasted time.

**9 planning solutions** to match client life moment: Basic Financial Planning | Wedding | Home/BTO | Baby Planning | CPF Wealth | Tax Savings | Investments | One Retirement | GoalsMapper.

## Numbers worth memorising

- **45-60 min** for a full SPIN arc. Below 15 = "Situation + pitch", not SPIN.
- **15-25%** product-selling close rate vs **35-50%** concept-selling close rate
- **20%** of take-home at 4-6% return = the magic number for comfortable retirement
- **1/3 rule** - short-term / medium-term / long-term (insurance + investments)
- **~$11,800/mth** at 65 for a $5,000/mth lifestyle today (35 years at 2.5% inflation)
- **~$1,730/mth** invested at 6.6% to hit $2.85M at 65 (vs ~$6,800/mth saved with no growth)
- **CPF LIFE FRS** pays ~$1,730/mth at 65 - the baseline. Gap above this = your product's job.

## Common failure modes

- **Rushing SPIN.** A 15-minute "SPIN" is Situation-only + a pitch. Real arc needs 45-60.
- **Need-Payoff before client feels the problem.** Sounds like empty upsell language. Establish pain first.
- **Three Implications in a row.** Crosses into emotional manipulation. Mix in Need-Payoff to maintain balance.
- **Filling the post-Implication silence.** Cuts the emotion. Hold to 10 seconds.
- **Opening the fact-find with "what 2 problems do you want to fix?"** Tanks the meeting on every profile, especially C. Use non-committal phrasing - *"could there be a possibility one or two areas feel a bit less settled?"*
- **Leading questions** ("wouldn't it be awful if your kids couldn't go to uni?"). Client senses manipulation. Rewrite neutral: *"What would it mean if the education funds weren't available?"*
- **Opening the laptop and projecting AIA slides before drawing the concept on paper.** The brain switches off. Draw first, slides second.
- **Quoting "we'll get 6.6% returns"** as a guarantee. Compliance fail. *"Illustrative historical average, not a guarantee."*
- **Telling clients their existing policy is bad.** Position as "you own a policy - 30 minutes turns it into a plan you understand." Restructure only when math is clearly transparent.
