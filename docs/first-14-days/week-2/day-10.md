Yes, selling to your friends can give you a quick start. Your friends are more likely to trust you because they already know your character and intentions. Many advisors find early success with their warm market, because those relationships let them refine their pitch, build confidence and get their first few clients.

That said, relying only on your warm market isn't sustainable in the long run. Eventually you'll run out of friends and family to approach. That's why we put so much weight on spreading your lead sources and prospecting methods. Cold prospecting takes more hustle and grit, but it opens up an unlimited pool of potential clients. It means more rejections, but with the right frameworks and mentorship it becomes manageable, and even rewarding.

As someone I mentor, you'll have extra support to get started. I run a digital marketing agency, so it's easy for me to give you some initial leads to work with, and to guide you in generating leads from other sources. That gives you a spread of sources to start from:

```layers

Warm market | leads to build trust and confidence || Cold prospecting | methods for growth that scales || Leads and systems I provide | a boost while you build your pipeline
```

The goal is a steady, balanced flow of opportunities from several channels. I'll teach you proven methods for cold prospecting, and help you build a personal brand that attracts prospects to you. With the right branding, marketing and tools, you'll build a pipeline that grows without depending on your warm market.

Personally, 99% of my business comes from cold market prospecting. It's where the most growth happens, and it's what keeps a business going long term. But I also know how much starting strong matters, which is why the people I mentor get several lead sources and clear strategies from day one.

## Where the cold leads come from

These are the cold-market channels we run for you and teach you:

- Subsidised ads on Facebook, Instagram and Google
- In-house appointment setters who book qualified meetings into your calendar
- Marketing funnels that bring in inbound leads
- Cold-calling scripts and frameworks tested on hundreds of real calls
- LinkedIn outreach
- Lead magnets such as policy reviews, BTO calculators and tax relief guides

When I started, my own warm network was small. I was on GEP at NUS with no real social circle, so most of my 1,000+ clients came through cold prospecting. If your network is small too, you'll lean more on cold, maybe 70 to 80% of your pipeline. If your network is big, that's an advantage you bring with you.

## Where will my first clients come from?

Finding clients is one of three jobs, and each one feeds the next. Clients you look after send you their friends, and one day some of those people join your team. It all starts with you.

```layers

Finding clients | a full calendar without leaning on your friends || Keeping clients | clients who stay with you and send their friends || Building a team | a team of your own when you're ready to lead
```

To find clients, you get:

- Funnels and free guides that bring people to you
- Half the cost of approved ad campaigns covered
- Ads and branding done by our digital marketing agency
- Pitch decks and scripts that are ready to use

To keep them:

- Gifts and touchpoints so they remember you
- Webinars and courses they can learn from
- A CRM that tells you who to follow up

When you're ready to lead:

- Bootcamps that train your new joiners for you
- Recorded lessons and an exam chatbot they can use any time
- Onboarding handled from start to finish

## What happened with my own family and friends

People often ask me two things about this.

### Did you get any resistance from family and friends when you joined?

Not really. My parents didn't object since I wasn't giving up anything significant, like my degree, to join this career. Most family members and friends were either neutral or supportive, especially when they saw it as a side hustle. Resistance might come up only if you're dropping out of school or quitting a secure job to go all-in without a clear plan.

### How successful were you in closing cases with your own personal circle?

Initially? Not at all. My friends were mostly students who didn't have the money to commit. It wasn't until I gained traction that some of them started coming to me for advice.

Instead of relying on personal contacts, I built skills, created resources, and focused on cold marketing and lead generation.

## So, do you need to approach your friends?

If you believe your services can truly help them, then yes, it's a great way to start. But you won't be limited to your warm market. Together we'll build a system that lets you do well across many lead channels, so you start with confidence and a strong foundation.
