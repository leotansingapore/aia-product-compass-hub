# Day 10 — Warm + Cold Market: Your Full Pipeline

> **The one idea for today:** Other agencies make you dependent on your warm market. We settle the cold market for you, but warm is still your contribution, and it's a good one. Warm is easier than cold and genuinely serves the people closest to you. Cold is the engine, warm is the heart, and together you've got a full pipeline. Neither side is a trap.

## What you'll walk away with

By the end of today you should be able to:

1. See how warm and cold market fit together in a well-built advisory practice.
2. Reframe warm-market work from "selling to friends" into "being the trusted advisor your friends deserve."
3. Understand why doing only cold, or only warm, leaves 50% of your pipeline and 50% of your impact on the table.

---

## 1. Where other agencies fail, and why we're different

Most agencies hand you a blank CRM and a script that says "make a list of 100 people you know, start with the first 20."

That's their plan. Full stop. No cold-market system, no lead infrastructure, no training for turning strangers into clients. The moment you finish your warm list (usually 3 to 6 months in), the pipeline is empty. That's when most advisors quit.

Our approach is different. We've spent years building a cold-market engine that generates qualified leads systematically:

- Subsidised digital ads across Facebook, Instagram, Google
- In-house appointment setters booking qualified meetings to your calendar
- Centralised marketing funnels generating warm inbound leads
- Cold-market scripts and frameworks tested across hundreds of real calls
- LinkedIn cold-outbound systems, value-first lead magnets

That's roughly 50% of your pipeline handled. We've already settled it.

The other 50% is warm market, and that's your contribution.

---

## 2. Why warm market is actually a good thing

Let me be direct. There's a reason the industry trains warm-market work first, and it isn't because it's a crutch.

### Warm is genuinely easier than cold

When Roy, your secondary school friend, gets your message, he already knows you. He knows your character. He isn't wondering whether you're a scammer. That trust is pre-built. You skip months of rapport-building that a cold-market prospect requires. Your conversion from outreach to conversation to case is much higher.

For a new advisor still learning the craft, warm-market reps are the fastest way to build confidence and close your first cases. That's exactly what they should be doing.

### Warm-market service matters

Here's the frame shift I want you to sit with.

> **If you genuinely believe you can help your friends and family, not reaching out is not a neutral act. It's a disservice.**

Your friends are going to work with someone for their insurance and retirement planning. If it's not you, someone they trust and who actually knows their family situation, it'll be another advisor. Often someone less trained. Often someone less ethical. Often someone they don't know and will never see again after the sale.

You being their advisor is a gift to them when you do it right. Not a burden, not an imposition.

### Warm done well strengthens relationships

A good doctor doesn't avoid giving health advice to family. They're just mindful about it. Same here.

The professional version looks something like this. You send a friend a short, low-pressure message telling them you've moved into financial planning. Something along the lines of:

> "Hey, just to let you know I've moved into financial planning. If you ever want a second opinion on your policies, or want help sorting out CPF or retirement stuff, happy to sit down with you. No pressure either way."

That's it. No follow-up pitch if they don't bite. If they do want help, you help them properly — a real review, honest analysis, and you only recommend what actually fits. You're fine saying "you don't need this yet" when that's true, and you're fine if they decide to stay with their existing advisor.

Friends who've been through this with me have come back years later to thank me for it, because they ended up with honest advice, the right cover, and someone they could actually call when life got complicated.

## 3. Warm + cold = 100% pipeline

Here's the math.

**Warm-only** (what other agencies push):
- Works for 3 to 6 months
- Dries up as the list exhausts
- Pipeline drops to zero
- Advisor quits

**Cold-only** (some modern agencies overcorrect here):
- Takes longer to ramp because strangers need trust-building
- Easier to scale once systems work
- Misses the 50% you already have in your warm network
- Feels impersonal if you never serve the people you love

**Warm + cold together** (what we build):
- Early cases come from warm, giving you confidence and cash flow
- Cold engine runs in parallel, scaling your pipeline independent of your social circle
- Year 1: warm gets you started, cold keeps you fed
- Year 2 onwards: warm referrals expand, cold is your growth engine
- Year 5 onwards: half your book is renewals and referrals from everyone you've served

A new advisor with 30 warm contacts and our cold-market engine running has double the pipeline of an advisor at a warm-only agency, and triple the pipeline of someone trying to do cold-only from scratch.

## 4. What your warm-market work actually looks like

Concretely, over your first 6 to 12 months:

- 30 to 50 warm contacts in your network worth talking to (realistic financial situation, open to planning, you'd be happy to serve)
- You reach out low-pressure, letting them know you're in financial planning now and offering a free review if they want one
- Around 30 to 50% say yes, because they trust you
- Of those, around 50% become clients over 6 to 12 months as their situations evolve
- Each one potentially refers 1 to 2 more people who trust them

That's 30+ real relationships worth of early client base, built from people you already know, serving them well and strengthening the relationship.

Running in parallel, our cold-market engine is feeding you 5 to 15 qualified appointments a week from strangers we've warmed up via ads and funnels.

By month 6 you have a mature pipeline. By month 12 you have a real book. By year 2 most of your new business is coming through referrals from the people you've served well. Warm and cold origins stop mattering by then. They're all just clients.

## 5. The professional version — your pride as an advisor

There's a version of warm-market work that damages relationships, and a version that strengthens them. The difference is in how you carry yourself.

**The bad version** (pushy advisor):

- Cornering a friend at a wedding with a pitch
- Using emotional leverage ("if you trusted me, you'd buy this")
- Recommending products that don't fit
- Not taking no for an answer

**The professional version** (real advisor):

- Telling friends you're available, no pressure
- When asked, helping thoroughly with real analysis
- Recommending only what genuinely fits
- Saying "you don't need this yet" when that's true
- Being completely fine with no
- Treating a friend the way a good doctor would treat family: available, honest, patient

Here's the part most candidates miss. Doing the professional version well is something to be proud of.

You're bringing financial literacy into your circle. You're catching gaps that would've cost them hundreds of thousands over a lifetime: uninsured risks, bad retirement planning, wrong products sold by someone else. You're the reason their family has proper cover when something goes wrong.

That's meaningful work with people you love, not an awkward sales activity to avoid. Show up for it.

---

## 6. Who this matters for most

If you're a candidate worrying about "selling to my friends," ask yourself honestly:

1. **Do you actually believe this career helps people?** If yes, then not offering it to people close to you leaves them to get worse advice from strangers.
2. **Can you do the professional version?** Low pressure, optional, service-oriented. Most people can, with practice.
3. **Are you willing to accept that warm is 50% of the picture?** Not the whole strategy, just your half of the engine.

If all three are yes, warm-market work isn't something to dread. It's something to approach with care, respect, and pride.

---

## 7. If warm is genuinely thin for you, we can still build your book

Some candidates come in with thin warm networks. Fresh overseas grads, career changers into a new social circle, people who moved cities recently. That's fine.

My own example. When I started, my warm network was small. GEP at NUS, no real social circle, new to Singapore's adult professional world. I built my book mostly through our cold-market systems, and it still worked because the engine carried me.

Of my 1,000+ clients over the career, only a minority came from my original warm circle. That wasn't because warm was wrong. It was because I didn't have much to start with. If I'd had a richer network, I'd have served them first.

If your situation is similar (thin warm network), don't worry. The cold-market engine we've built is strong enough to carry you. You'll still build a real book, you'll just do 70 to 80% cold instead of 50/50.

If you have a rich warm network, great. Use it well. That's an advantage you bring to the table, not a liability.

---

## 8. What to ask any agency about their warm/cold split

When evaluating any agency, ours included:

1. **"What percentage of a typical advisor's pipeline at year 2 comes from warm vs cold?"** Healthy is a 40/60 split or better. Red flag is 90%+ warm, which means the agency has no cold system.
2. **"Do you have a documented cold-market training curriculum? Can you show me the outline?"** Good answer is yes, in writing. Bad answer is vague.
3. **"What happens when a new advisor exhausts their warm market? What's the system?"** Good answer is a specific system name. Bad answer is "you keep working your network."

If an agency's answer is warm-only, walk. You'll be exhausted in 6 months. If the answer is cold-only, ask why they're abandoning the natural advantage of warm relationships. The right answer is both, with cold handled systemically and warm done with pride.

## Worksheet — your warm-market reality check

Think honestly:

1. **Who in your warm market would you genuinely be proud to help?** List 5 people. Not "who can I sell to." Ask "who would I feel good being the trusted advisor for?"
2. **What does the professional version of reaching out look like for each?** A text? A coffee? A message in the family group?
3. **Are you comfortable with the professional version?** Low pressure, helping thoroughly, fine with no. If not, what's the specific block? That's what to work on.
4. **What size is your realistic warm network?** Under 20 means you'll lean more on cold. Over 50 means you have a strong warm base. Both are workable.
