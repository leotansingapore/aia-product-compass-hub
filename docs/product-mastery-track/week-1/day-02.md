# Day 2 — The Appointment Flow (Phase 0 → Phase 5B)

A complete APA appointment runs in seven phases — Phase 0 (pre-appointment) through Phase 5B (value-prop and final close). Each phase has a hard time budget and a target micro-commitment. The job is to engineer the close across the phases, not improvise it.

The two ways FCs lose APA cases: skipping the problem build-up (leaping to "$200/month buys you this plan" before the prospect feels the gap), and running over time (a 60-minute first appointment is a lost appointment — energy and attention die at minute 35).

---

## Phase 0 — Pre-Appointment Setup

The work that happens *before* the prospect joins the call. Most FCs underweight this and pay for it with no-shows.

### Reminder cadence

Remind at least 2-3 times. People always prioritise other things over you — you're last priority.

- **3 days before** — first reminder
- **1 day before** — second reminder
- **Day of appointment** — third reminder, optionally 5 minutes before start time

### Double / triple booking

Book 3-4 prospects in the same time block, one hour apart (e.g. 10am, 11am, 12pm). **Expect roughly 1 in 3 to actually show up.**

Better to have too many than an empty calendar.

### Zoom link in every reminder

Always include the Zoom link in the reminder messages. A free Zoom account is sufficient for these calls.

---

## Phase 1 — Rapport + Setup (max 5 min)

Open with **purposeful rapport** — not aimless chat. Rapport with purpose, max 5 minutes. The purpose of Phase 1 is to capture critical dates, build authority, and earn permission for the deeper conversation that follows.

### Capture key dates and milestones (write into CRM in real time)

Questions to surface life-stage data:

- What are you doing now? (job / study)
- When do you start work?
- When is your next exam?
- When is graduation?
- When is your wedding date?
- When is the baby due?
- Any upcoming holidays or trips?

**Critical discipline:** write everything into CRM the moment they say it. If you finish Phase 1 without capturing 3+ dates for your calendar, you didn't run Phase 1 — you interrogated.

### The soft birthday capture

Don't ask for "birthday" directly. Use the age-ladder during retirement context:

> *"How old are you this year?"* → *"I'm 21"*
>
> *"When did you turn 21?"* → *"In July"*
>
> *"Oh, my friend's birthday is also in July! Which day?"*

That sequence captures the date naturally, no "birthday" word required. Alternative softener: *"My close friend's birthday is also in July."*

### Calendar setup — every date becomes a recurring touchpoint

Set every captured date as a **recurring yearly event in Google Calendar**. Examples:

- Graduation — July 2027
- Wedding Anniversary — Dec 15
- Birthday — July 23

This creates years of touchpoints for follow-up — every wish lands on a real date in their life. The discipline turns Phase 1 into a 12-month referral pipeline.

> *"If you wish them and they're not a client yet, it's going to be a lot easier to close next time."*

### Share your "why" (authority + relatability)

Make it relatable, not corporate. The frame:

> *"I went through the same education system as you. Schools teach math, science, physics — but never financial planning. I was frustrated by this gap, so I studied extensively, and now I help young adults avoid the same struggle."*

The underlying message: schools don't teach wealth-building. The system shapes employees, not entrepreneurs. That's why self-learning is critical — and why this conversation matters.

### Introduce Moneybies + free resources (send via WhatsApp NOW)

> *"That's why we created Moneybies — an educational platform for young adults."*

Three free resources to offer:

1. **Budgeting Guidebook** (PDF)
2. **Investment Guidebook** (PDF)
3. **Telegram channel** (ongoing tips)

Match the resource to the prospect's life stage:

| Profile | Resources to send |
|---|---|
| Young Adults | Budgeting + Investment guides |
| Expecting Parents | "What to Expect" guide |
| Working Parents | Family financial planning |

**Send the resources via WhatsApp right now**, not at the end of the call. That sequence sets up the next step.

### Get them to save your contact (the WhatsApp lock-in)

After you've sent the resources:

> *"Can you check your WhatsApp — did you receive my message? Can you scroll to the top of our WhatsApp conversation? Do you see the 'Save Contact' button? Can you click that and save my number?"*

This isn't a vanity step. Three real reasons:

- **WhatsApp restricts numbers that text unsaved contacts** — get banned and your funnel dies
- **Only saved contacts receive broadcast messages** — without this you can't run your nurturing system on them
- **Future follow-ups won't look like spam** — they see your name, not a random number

### Set up the nurturing system

Position yourself for the long game across multiple channels:

- **Telegram channel** — educational content
- **WhatsApp broadcast lists** — personal updates
- **Email newsletters** — formal updates
- **Online community access**

The long-term framing: every lead gets follow-up for *years* until they eventually buy something. Most cases close on the second, third, or tenth touch — not the first.

**Telegram strategy:** create your own personal channel, copy content from the main Moneybies channel, buy 100 initial followers so it looks professional, get friends to join first. Use it as a non-aggressive way to stay top-of-mind with the warm market.

**WhatsApp broadcast strategy:** create a broadcast list, add all saved contacts, send weekly or bi-weekly updates with tips / market notes / events. More effective than Telegram in practice — feels personal because it arrives on their phone as a regular WhatsApp.

### Ask for their "why" — the hot button (LAST, not first)

Only after rapport is built, contact is saved, and nurturing is live:

> *"So what brings you here today? What made you interested in learning more?"*

The reason to ask last, not first: by now they trust you, they're in your system, they're more open to share deeply. If you asked this at minute 1, they'd give a surface answer.

Probe deeper:

- *"Is this something you've been thinking about for a while?"*
- *"What happened recently that made this urgent?"*
- *"This is very common — I totally get it."*

Record every pain point into CRM. You'll use these in Phase 3 to make the problem hit harder.

### Transition into Phase 2

> *"I've helped many people with exactly the same concern. In the next 25-30 minutes, let me show you how we can address this..."*

By the end of Phase 1 you should have: 3+ critical dates captured, contact saved, prospect in your nurturing system, authority established, pain points logged.

## Phase 2 — Financial Health Check (5-7 min)

Frame yourself as a **financial doctor**, not a salesperson. Same as a medical checkup — diagnosis first, prescription after:

> *"Before recommending solutions, we need to understand your current finances."*

Run four ratios in order. The point isn't to "audit" them — it's to surface the gap they'll feel emotionally in Phase 3.

### Ratio 1 — Liquidity Ratio

**Formula:** Bank savings ÷ Monthly expenses · **Target:** 3-6 months

Questions:

- *"How much are you currently spending per month?"*
- *"How much have you saved in the bank so far?"*

Frame the problem from their answer:

- **HIGH liquidity** → *"Most of it is sitting in a bank account at low interest. We should look at investing some portion."*
- **LOW liquidity** → *"Need to build a disciplined savings habit before we can invest much. We can start small."*

### Ratio 2 — Savings Ratio

**Formula:** (Income − Expenses) ÷ Income · **Target:** 10-20% for long-term savings

Ask: *"How much do you earn per month?"* (then compute the savings rate from income minus expenses).

Frame the problem:

- **HIGH savings rate** → *"Great that you save, but all of it is in your bank account. We need to put 10-20% into long-term investments."*
- **LOW savings rate** → *"You're not saving systematically. A regular savings plan is the move — automate it before you can spend it."*

### Ratio 3 — Invested Assets to Net Worth Ratio

**Formula:** Investments ÷ Net worth · **Target:** at least 50%

Questions:

- *"Currently, do you have any investments?"*
- *"Where is your money sitting?"*

**Most common answer for under-30s: 0% invested.** Frame the problem:

> *"As we get older, net worth increases. At least 50% should be invested — otherwise your assets quietly erode to inflation every year."*

### Ratio 4 — Debt to Asset Ratio

**Target:** below 50%

Usually skip this — most prospects don't have meaningful debt at the appointment stage.

### Inflation impact education

Numbers to drop here:

- Bank interest: 0.05% p.a.
- Singapore inflation: roughly 2.8% p.a.
- **Net real loss: ~2.75% per year**

> *"That means after 10 years, you've lost 27.5% of your wealth just by keeping it in the bank."*

### Risk profile (quick read)

> *"Are you more of a low-risk, medium-risk, or high-risk investor?"*

The default guidance:

- **Young people** → higher risk OK (smaller portfolio, longer horizon to ride out volatility)
- **Older people** → medium to low risk (larger portfolio at stake, shorter horizon)

### Retirement Lifestyle Calculator — the shock

> *"What's the biggest purchase in your life? It's actually retirement."*

Run the math in front of them:

1. **Ask** desired monthly retirement income in today's dollars (typical answer: $4,000/mo)
2. **Inflate** at 2.5% — at age 65, that becomes roughly **$10,740/mo**
3. **Total over 20 retirement years** — roughly **$3.29 million**
4. **Monthly savings needed in the bank** to get there — roughly **$6,858/month**

### Reality check + humour

> *"Is saving $6,858 a month possible for you?"*

Then defuse with humour (humour raises agreeability):

> *"Maybe you need multiple jobs… or find a sugar daddy or sugar mommy to cover expenses!"*

### The conclusion that bridges to Phase 3

> *"Obviously that's not realistic. Which is why investing isn't just nice-to-have — it's a MUST-HAVE."*

That's the bridge.

## Phase 3 — Problems of Investing → AIA Solution (5 min)

Now that investing is established as a must-have, surface the problems most people run into when they try to invest themselves.

### The common investing problems

The patterns most people fall into:

- Pick individual stocks
- No patience
- Try to time the market
- Use leverage
- Follow hot tips
- Most of them lose money

> *"Roughly 90% of people lose money in the stock market."*

### The 9-hour course (positions you as authority, then surfaces the gap)

> *"We have a 9-hour investment course — a 7-step process, we can give you access. But… you still have to execute yourself. Some people don't have time, others find it too technical."*

This sets up the next move.

### The single-stock question

The most common client request:

> Client: *"Why don't you just tell me ONE stock I can put my money into?"*
>
> FC: *"What do you think is the problem if you put everything into one stock?"*

Wait for the answer. Let them feel it. They'll usually arrive at: *"Too risky — if it crashes, I lose everything."*

### The relationship analogy

> *"How would you feel if you put all your effort, time, and money into one girl or one boy — and then they break up with you every day?"*

Same emotional impact as concentration risk. The pain lands without an Excel sheet.

### Lead to diversification (and crack a joke)

> *"So what do you think is the solution?"*

Wait for them to say "diversification".

> *"Oh — diversification! Maybe you mean having multiple girlfriends or boyfriends?"*

Humour raises agreeability — it's deliberately structured into the flow.

> *"Obviously not. I mean investing in multiple stocks."*

### The new problem: multiple stocks

> *"What's the problem if you try to invest in multiple stocks yourself?"*

Surface the layered cost:

- Have to run the 7-step process 20-30 times
- No time to do it
- Need to monitor constantly
- Markets open 10-11pm SGT — stay up all night tracking
- Emotionally draining
- Need genuine interest + technical skill

### The outsourcing solution

> *"That's why people outsource. They get a company or an investment expert to manage it for them — and AIA has been doing this for the past few years..."*

### AIA's proven track record (cite verifiable sources)

Bring up the Investing Success & Track Record deck — slide 4 shows 10-year fund performance:

- AIA Global Technology Fund: **447%**
- AIA India Equity Fund: 223%
- AIA International Health Care Fund: 202%
- SPDR S&P 500: 191% (benchmark)
- AIA Regional Equity Fund: 65%

Position it honestly: AIA's funds, on average across the lineup, have a verified long-term track record that competes with or beats the standard ETF benchmarks. Don't claim "#1 on every metric" — point at the deck.

### Transition into Phase 4

> *"That's how people outsource their investments to us. The next part — let me show you exactly what the numbers will look like if you outsource to us."*

## Phase 4 — Investment Illustrations + Option Closing (5-7 min)

Pull the Phase 2 retirement calculation back up — the prospect just calculated they need $6,854/mo in a bank account to retire at 65 with $3M. Now contrast that with the investment route.

### The big reveal

| Route | Monthly savings needed |
|---|---|
| Bank account (0.05% interest) | **$6,854/month** |
| Investment account (with APA-style returns) | **$1,246/month** |

> *"Between $6,854 and $1,246 per month, which one do you think is easier?"*

The cash-flow benefit: **you free up $5,608/month** for everything else in life.

### Cost-of-delay shock

> *"The reason the math works at $1,246 is because you're starting NOW. What do you think happens if you wait 5 years?"*

Let them guess (most say 5-10% drop). Then reveal:

| When you start | Same $3M target → savings/mo needed |
|---|---|
| Now (age 25) | works at $1,246/mo |
| Wait 5 years (age 30) | target slips — ending value $2M instead of $3M |
| Wait 10 years (age 35) | $1.5M |
| Wait 20 years (age 45) | $600K |

> *"A 5-year delay costs you $1 million in projected ending value. That's $200,000 per year of wealth — or $16,667/month — that you forfeit by waiting."*

Real-client anchor:

> *"I have clients who started at age 45 — guess how much they have to save? $6,000/month. You only need $1,246 now."*

### Budget adjustment ladder

If $1,246/mo is too high right now:

> *"What if we start with a more comfortable budget? Even $500/month grows to about $1,100,000 at retirement — still meaningful."*

Then "every dollar counts":

| Add per month | Adds at retirement |
|---|---|
| +$50/mo (MRT-fare worth) | ~$120,000 |
| +$100/mo | ~$250,000 |
| +$200/mo | ~$491,000 |

> *"Looking at your expenses, is there anything you can reduce to put more towards this?"*

### Plan flexibility — handle the lock-in worry

Address the four reassurances before the prospect asks:

- **Premium holidays** — *"Pay for 10 years only ($60K in), let it compound inside the plan, value at 65 is around $654,000. Even if life happens and you stop paying after 10 years, you still get a 10x return."*
- **Regular withdrawals at retirement** — *"You don't have to take it as a lump sum. You can draw $7,500/month till you're 80+, or $10,000/month till 75. So $500/mo of savings now turns into $10K/mo of retirement income later."*
- **Ad-hoc withdrawals** — *"Life happens. Age 35 — $50K for house downpayment. Age 45 — $50K for car. Age 55 — $100K for kids' education. Returns are slightly lower but still substantial."*
- **Payment pauses** — *"If you have a hard year, pause 1-2 years, resume when ready. The plan adapts."*

### The one-third rule

Anchor on a clean budgeting frame:

> *"I suggest investing at least 20-25% of your income every month."*

| 1/3 | 1/3 | 1/3 |
|---|---|---|
| **Short-term** — food, transport, daily needs | **Medium-term** — house, car, wedding, holidays | **Long-term** — 10% insurance + 20% investments |

For someone earning $3,000/month, that puts $600/mo in the long-term investing bucket.

### The option close — three tiers

Always Two Never Three becomes the three-options ladder for APA:

| Option | Premium | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Option 1 | **$500/mo** | "Still effective — but you'll likely need to top up later" |
| Option 2 | **$600/mo** | "Sweet spot. Balanced. Can always increase as income grows." |
| Option 3 | **$700/mo** | "Start here, never have to increase again. Set-and-forget retirement planning." |

Below $500 → *"Probably not effective for your retirement goal — let's at least start at $500."*

### The decision moment

> *"Mr Prospect, which option would you prefer?"*

Goal of Phase 4: get the option close, get buy-in on a monthly amount, confirm they're open to commit at that level.

### Two paths after the option close

- **Conservative:** *"Let me prepare a detailed quotation for our next meeting where we'll go through Phase 5 — the actual application."*
- **Aggressive (case-by-case):** *"Great choice. Let me pull up the application form and get you started right now."*

The case for aggressive: fresh grad picking $1,000/mo with low cognitive friction — close on the spot, lose them to the weekend otherwise.

## Phase 5 — Quotation + Application (8-10 min)

Open iPOS+, configure the policy on screen, and walk them through the quotation.

### Policy configuration (Pro Achiever 3.0)

- **Fund selection:** 100% Elite Adventurous Index Fund — lowest management fee, best long-term track record, index-based diversification
- **Investment Period:** 10 years (most common choice in practice)
- **Payment frequency:** monthly or annually — prospect's preference
- **Optional riders:** add if needed
- Click Next → Add to Quotation

> *"I'm putting 100% into the Elite Adventurous Index Fund because it has the lowest fees of all the available funds."*

### Walk the quotation

Worked example for a 25-yo at $600/mo:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Monthly investment | $600/mo |
| Investment period | 40 years (to age 65) |
| Total amount invested | $600 × 12 × 40 = **$288,000** |
| Projected value at age 65 | **$1,416,700** |

> *"This $1.4 million will supplement your retirement savings."*

### The Interactive Calculation Technique — make the CLIENT calculate

This is the most important Phase 5 move. Don't compute the return multiple for them — hand them the calculator and walk them through.

The impact is roughly **10x stronger** when they do the math themselves.

> *"Can you take out your calculator? Let me show you something interesting."*

Three steps:

**Step 1 — calculate profit**
*"Take $1,416,700 minus $288,000. What do you get?"* → **$1,128,700 profit**

**Step 2 — annualise**
*"Now divide that by 40 years. What's that number?"* → **$28,218 per year**

**Step 3 — monthly**
*"Divide that by 12 months. How much is that per month?"* → **$2,351 per month**

### The reveal

> *"So every month you invest $600 — you're actually earning $2,351."*

The multiple: $2,351 ÷ $600 = **~3.9x monthly return** at the projected IIRR.

Caveat to state honestly: *"This assumes you hold to age 65 and invest long-term."*

### Short-term vs long-term

| Hold horizon | Total paid | Approx. value | Multiple |
|---|---|---|---|
| Stop at year 10 | $72,000 | ~$300,000 | ~4x |
| Hold to 65 | $288,000 | $1,416,700 | ~5x |

> *"Mr Client — would you prefer $300K or $1.4 million? Cancel early and you don't get as much. Hold long-term and you've supplemented your retirement."*

### Fee transparency

> *"The $1,416,700 number is AFTER all fees and charges. No hidden fees. No hidden charges. What you see is what you get."*

Walk through the actual distribution cost:

- **Total distribution cost over 40 years:** $18,583
- **Monthly equivalent:** $18,583 ÷ 40 ÷ 12 = **$38.75/month**

Comparison anchor:

| Service | Monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Private investment advisor | $200-500/month |
| Fund manager fee | 2-3% annually |
| **APA distribution cost (all-in)** | **$38.75/month** |

### Advisor compensation — be upfront

> *"I do earn a fraction of that $38.75 on a monthly basis. That covers my ongoing service and support to you throughout the policy term."*

Transparency builds the trust that handles the close. Show value vs alternatives (investment advisors charging hundreds), emphasise the ongoing service, don't dodge the question if they ask "how do you get paid?".

### Closing the quotation walkthrough

> *"So based on these numbers — you invest $600/mo, you get $1.4M at retirement, all fees included at only $38.75/mo, long-term growth structurally engineered. Are you ready to proceed with the application?"*

## Phase 5B — Value Proposition + Final Close (5-8 min)

Phase 5B is the value-stack — the part that converts a hesitant prospect into a same-day signature. Run it when the prospect is at "interested but not committed".

### The Three Cost Circles (quantify what they're getting)

#### Circle 1 — Cost of professional management

> *"If you were to hire your own investment consultant, how much do you think they earn per month?"*

| Route | Monthly cost |
|---|---|
| Private investment consultant | $3,000-4,000/month for ONE person |
| **AIA via APA** | $38/month total — full professional team |

Why AIA can do it so cheaply: **scale**. 1M+ policyholders, hundreds of consultants, hundreds of millions to billions under management. The cost spreads across the volume.

> *"Do you think you can afford to hire that team yourself? But because AIA has so many clients, the per-person cost is only a fraction."*

#### Circle 2 — DIY trading fees

> *"If you trade yourself, how much do you think each trade costs?"*

DIY platform cost-stack:

- Platform fees: $10-30 per trade
- Currency conversion: 0.5-2%
- Minimum fees per transaction
- Custody fees

> *"Trade frequently? Easily $200-300/month."*

vs APA: **unlimited free fund switching.** Switch US → Healthcare → China → India → high-risk → low-risk → all FREE. 100% fund switches anytime, no charge.

> *"The reason it's free? AIA's running so much volume, the trading costs are absorbed."*

#### Circle 3 — Time, effort, emotions (qualitative)

The first two circles are quantitative. This one is qualitative — and usually the most decisive.

> *"I have friends who, once the market opens at 10pm, become very blackface afterwards if they lost money."*

The DIY emotional cycle:

- Market drops → angry, sad, frustrated
- Market rises → temporarily happy
- Market rallies → *"Oh shit, I should have bought earlier"*

**No matter what, you're always unhappy.**

Plus the time cost:

| Activity | Hours/day |
|---|---|
| Research stocks | 1-2 hours |
| Monitor markets | 2-3 hours |
| Execute trades | 30-60 min |
| Background stress + worry | All day |

That's 4-6 hours/day lost.

Personal-life callback (use their actual hobby from Phase 1):

> *"What do you do in your free time? Oh — you play soccer? If you're trading nightly, you won't have time to play soccer with your friends anymore, right?"*

> *"You're outsourcing to us so you don't have to spend that time, effort, and emotion yourself."*

### "And you get me — for free"

> *"If you hired your own investment consultant, you'd pay a lot. Over here, it's FREE."*

What the FC actually delivers, included:

- Retirement planning
- Mortgage planning
- Insurance review
- Investment strategy
- Regular reviews across life stages

> *"My philosophy is — I treat my clients first, and we do regular reviews throughout your life stages."*

### The Option A vs Option B close

Frame the decision as a binary, not a yes/no on a product:

> *"Mr Client — would you prefer Option A or Option B?"*

| Option A | Option B |
|---|---|
| Learn everything yourself | Hire expert to manage it |
| Waste money + time learning | Negligible fees ($38/mo all-in) |
| Emotional stress | Proven AIA track record |
| High DIY fees | Free advisor support (me) |
| Trial-and-error | Peace of mind |

> *"Most people choose B. There's nothing wrong with A — it's just that B is a good starting point. If you become very confident at investing later, you can always do A on the side. But for now, B is the cleanest way to get started."*

### The Zero-Fees structure (anchor the affordability)

APA has none of the friction items most ILPs charge:

| Charge | APA |
|---|---|
| Allocation charges | ❌ None |
| Bid / offer spread | ❌ None |
| Policy fees | ❌ None |
| Underwriting fees | ❌ None |

The context that makes this land:

- **Other ILPs:** typically only 60-80% of your premium is invested in the early years (rest goes to commission)
- **APA:** **100% invested from day 1**

No bid-offer spread frame: *"If you change SGD to MYR, then back to SGD — can you get the exact same amount? Probably not, because there are FX fees. With APA, you can switch from US → China → Japan → India → high-risk → low-risk → ZERO fees."*

### The Three Wealth Principles (Growing Your Wealth Machine)

Anchor the close on principles, not features.

**1 — Start early**

> *"I have clients who started at 45, putting in $1,000/month — seems high, right? But they're only getting back $500K. You're starting at 25, putting in just $600/month, and getting back $1.4 million."*

**2 — Save first, spend later**

> *"What happens if you spend first and save what's left? You probably won't save much. This plan forces you to save first — auto-debit before you even see the money."*

**3 — Automate everything**

> *"What's the opposite of automation? Manual. If you do it manually and forget, you'll be impulsive. Market high? You'll buy. Market low? You'll panic-sell. Automation removes the emotion."*

### Realistic expectations (preempts the "too good to be true" objection)

> *"This is NOT a magic pill. Even with this plan, you still need to earn more, spend less, and have your insurance in place. And watch the spending trap — when you earn more, the temptation is to spend more. Try to spend less even though you earn more."*

### One-stop financial solution framing

## Drill

1. **Recite the 7 phases in order** — Phase 0 → 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 → 5B. Name each phase, its time budget, and its target micro-commitment. Do it 3 times without looking until it falls out of your mouth.
2. **Practise the Phase 1 birthday capture** — the age-laddered script ("How old are you this year?" → "When did you turn 21?" → "Oh my friend's birthday is also in July, which day?"). Run it twice out loud.
3. **Memorise the Phase 2 retirement-shock math** — $4K today → $10,740/mo at 65 with 2.5% inflation → $3.29M over 20 years → $6,858/mo bank savings needed.
4. **Memorise the Phase 4 contrast** — $6,854/mo bank vs $1,246/mo APA route. Cost-of-5-year-delay: $1M loss = $16,667/mo of future wealth forfeited.
5. **Practise the Phase 5 Interactive Calculation Technique** — hand them the calculator, walk through three steps ($1,416,700 − $288,000 → ÷ 40 → ÷ 12 = $2,351/mo). Don't compute it for them — the impact is 10x weaker if you do.
6. **Practise the Phase 5B Three Cost Circles** — hiring ($3-4K vs $38), DIY trading ($200-300 vs $0), time/emotions (4-6 hrs/day vs automated). Use the prospect's actual hobby for Circle 3.
7. **Run the Option A vs Option B close** in three tonalities — neutral, slightly playful, slightly serious. Pick the one that sounds most like you.
8. **Pick the toughest objection you've heard recently on APA** — "I'll just DIY", "what if it doesn't hit 8%", "what if I need liquidity". Write your reframe for that specific prospect. Run it out loud once.

## Quiz

1. **What is Phase 0 of the APA appointment flow?**
- A) The first 5 minutes of the call, building rapport
- B) Pre-appointment setup — reminder cadence (3 days / 1 day / day-of), double/triple booking 3-4 prospects per slot, Zoom link in every reminder ✓
- C) The closing phase
- D) Phase 0 doesn't exist

**Why:** Phase 0 happens before the call starts. Roughly 1 in 3 prospects show up — double/triple booking is structural, not optional.

2. **Why does Phase 1 ask for the prospect's "why" (hot button) at the END, not the start?**
- A) Compliance reasons
- B) Because by the end of Phase 1 you've built rapport, saved your contact, and put them in your nurturing system — they're more open to share deeply than at minute 1 ✓
- C) Because you don't actually use the answer
- D) Because Phase 2 doesn't need it

**Why:** Asking at minute 1 gets a surface answer. Asking at the end of Phase 1 gets the actual driver — which feeds Phase 3 problem-build.

3. **The Phase 2 Retirement Lifestyle Calculator shocks the prospect with what number?**
- A) The cost of inflation only
- B) Roughly $6,858/month of bank savings needed to retire at 65 with $3.29M (working from a $4K-today retirement income inflated at 2.5% over 20 years) ✓
- C) The supplementary charge of 3.9%
- D) The cost of a private investment consultant

**Why:** The shock that bridges into Phase 3 is the impossibility of saving $6,858/mo in a bank. *"Obviously not realistic — which is why investing is a MUST-HAVE."*

4. **The Phase 3 relationship analogy compares concentration risk to:**
- A) A bad business deal
- B) Putting all your effort, time, and money into one girl/boy who breaks up with you every day ✓
- C) A house fire
- D) A car accident

**Why:** The analogy lands emotionally — same gut feeling as single-stock concentration risk. Then crack the joke ("multiple girlfriends? — no, multiple stocks") to keep agreeability high.

5. **In Phase 4, the cost-of-delay math says a 5-year delay costs the prospect roughly how much?**
- A) $200K total
- B) $1M of projected ending value = $200K/year = $16,667/month of future wealth forfeited ✓
- C) $50K
- D) Nothing — they can always catch up

**Why:** Start at 25 → $3M. Wait 5 yrs → only $2M. The $1M gap = $200K/yr = $16,667/mo of forfeited future wealth.

6. **What is the Phase 4 option-close structure?**
- A) Take it or leave it
- B) Three tiers — $500 ("you'll need to top up later") · $600 ("sweet spot") · $700 ("set-and-forget"). Anchor below $500 as "probably not effective." ✓
- C) One option only
- D) Five options

**Why:** The three-options ladder lets the prospect self-select. Most pick the middle tier. Anchoring below $500 as "not effective" prevents downward drift.

7. **What is the Phase 5 Interactive Calculation Technique?**
- A) Show them the spreadsheet you've prepared
- B) Hand the prospect the calculator and walk them through 3 steps yourself: $1,416,700 − $288,000 → ÷ 40 yrs → ÷ 12 mo = $2,351/mo. **The impact is 10x stronger when they calculate it themselves** ✓
- C) Skip the math entirely
- D) Quote the projected value only

**Why:** When the FC computes it, the prospect listens. When the prospect computes it, they own it. The 10x impact difference is the difference between "interesting" and "I want this".

8. **In Phase 5, the distribution cost of APA works out to roughly how much per month?**
- A) $200/month
- B) $38.75/month all-in ($18,583 over 40 years ÷ 40 ÷ 12) — vs $200-500/month for a private investment advisor ✓
- C) $500/month
- D) $1,000/month

**Why:** $18,583 ÷ 40 yrs ÷ 12 mo = $38.75/mo. The transparency-and-comparison move is what unlocks the value-prop in Phase 5B.

9. **The Phase 5B Three Cost Circles framework compares APA against what three real-world alternatives?**
- A) Term life, whole life, ILP
- B) Cost of professional management ($3-4K/mo vs $38), DIY trading fees ($200-300/mo vs $0), and time + emotions (4-6 hrs/day vs automated) ✓
- C) AIA, Prudential, Great Eastern
- D) Cash, stocks, bonds

**Why:** Two quantitative circles + one qualitative circle (time and emotional cost). Use the prospect's actual hobby from Phase 1 to make Circle 3 hit personally.

10. **The Phase 5B final close is structured as Option A vs Option B. What's Option B?**
- A) "Buy the cheapest plan"
- B) Hire expert to manage it — negligible fees ($38/mo), proven track record, free advisor support, peace of mind ✓
- C) "Wait and think about it"
- D) "Buy multiple plans"

**Why:** Option A (DIY) costs more money, more time, more stress. Option B (APA) is the cleaner starting point. *"Most people choose B. Nothing wrong with A — you can always do A later when you're more confident."*
