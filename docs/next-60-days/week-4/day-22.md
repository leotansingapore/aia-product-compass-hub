# Day 22: Cold prospecting and the ABCD promises

> **Today in one line:** Warm and cold markets need different voices. Warm runs on honesty between people who know each other, cold runs on showing you know your stuff, and mixing them up makes both channels fail.

**What to remember**

1. Use the warm voice with people who know you, and the cold voice (a niche problem, a framework, something useful) with strangers.
2. Build cold outreach on 5 pillars: niche, framework, profile, sharp DM and follow-up.
3. Open every Fact-Find with the ABCD Four Promises.
4. Keep cold to 0 to 5 of this week's 30 outreaches.

## The biggest cold-market mistake

New FCs go into the cold market using warm-market language:

> "Hey! Hope you've been well, I've been meaning to message you about something personal..."

To a friend those words are honest and disarming. Send them to a stranger and they get ignored, or reported. A stranger has no shared history with you, so admitting nerves reads as weakness. They're checking one thing: "does this person know what they're doing?"

So warm runs on vulnerability, and cold runs on authority:

| | Warm market | Cold market |
|---|---|---|
| Opener | Something from your shared history | A problem people in their niche commonly have |
| Credibility | The relationship you already have | A clear framework and your credentials |
| Tone | Open, honest | Professional, useful first |
| Ask | Easy out, no pressure | A clear next step with something useful attached |
| Follow-up | A value drop | A series of useful things in different formats |

Many new FCs struggle in both channels because they blend the two.

## The 5 pillars of cold prospecting

![The 5 pillars of cold prospecting: 1 Niche; 2 Framework; 3 Profile; 4 Sharp DM; 5 Follow up](/next-60-days/images/n60-day-22-m0.webp)

### Pillar 1: pick a tight niche

Market to everyone and nobody feels you're talking to them. Cold outreach converts when it's specific.

Instead of "business owners", try tech founders raising a Series A, or F&B owners with two or more outlets. Instead of "parents", try parents of primary-school kids in the East. A cold prospect thinks "this person is for me" when your content is clearly for people like them.

### Pillar 2: a named framework

A generic line like "I help people grow their wealth" is forgotten the moment they scroll past. A named framework gives them something to remember you by:

- P.I.S.A: Protect, Income, Scale, Assets
- F.A.T: Foundation, Accelerate, Transfer
- A.P.E.X: Audit, Protect, Expand, eXit

Don't invent one for the sake of it. If your planning process already has three or four clear phases, name them, so people recognise you after a few dozen touches.

### Pillar 3: your profile is the shop window

The profile work from [Day 10](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/10) matters far more in the cold market, because your profile is the only thing a cold prospect can check before replying. It needs:

- your framework in the bio, describing what you do in the niche's language
- your credentials: licences held, qualifications, the areas you're certified in
- testimonials or client stories only in a form your compliance team has approved
- recent content, at least 2 posts a week
- nothing unprofessional left on the page

MAS's digital advertising guidelines cover financial institutions and their representatives on social media, so treat your profile as advertising and check anything you're unsure of with compliance. A profile that doesn't hold up kills the cold DM, however good the message was.

### Pillar 4: short, useful DMs

Keep cold DMs short and relevant, with no long intro and no hard sell. Name a problem people in the niche commonly have, so they can tell you understand their world. Offer something useful upfront, such as a compliance-approved checklist or short guide. Write like a person writing to one person.

Weak cold DM:

> "Hi, I saw your profile and I think I could help with your financial planning. Would you like to book a call?"

Strong cold DM:

> "Hi [name], saw you're running a 2-outlet F&B operation. A lot of owners at that stage hit the same cashflow squeeze when they open a third outlet. I put together a 1-page checklist to stress-test it, happy to send it over if useful. No pitch, no call needed."

They're about the same length. The strong one works because it names a real problem, offers something specific and asks for almost nothing.

### Pillar 5: follow up with something new each time

Most cold prospects don't reply to the first message: they're busy, they missed it, or they saved it and forgot. The follow-ups do more of the work. Never send "just checking in", because it tells them nothing. Each follow-up carries something new (an insight, a relevant article, a useful resource), and mixing formats like text, voice note and short video makes you stand out in their inbox.

## The unconventional cold call

The standard opener ("Hi, I'm a financial advisor calling to see if you'd be interested...") often gets hung up on before you finish the sentence. The unconventional opener breaks the pattern by being honest.

Every line of it has to be true. If a line isn't true for you today, change the words until it is.

### Stage 1: the favour opener

> "Hi Mark, this is [name], a financial consultant with AIA. I actually need to ask you a favour, please don't hang up right away. Honestly, it's been a long day of calls and most people have hung up on me. I'm hoping you'll give me one minute. I promise I'm not here to sell you anything you don't need, and if at any point you're not interested you can hang up and I'll still be grateful. Is that okay?"

### Stage 2: say what the questions are for

> "My team and I are trying to understand the money issues that matter most to professionals aged 30 to 45, so we can be more useful to people like you. Would you mind answering a few quick questions?"

Keep this honest. You've already said you're a financial consultant, so don't dress the call up as independent research. The questions are real, and if there's a fit you'll ask to meet.

### Stage 3: three hot-button questions

1. "What do you find most challenging about managing your finances right now?"
2. "When you think about your future, what financial goals or concerns come to mind first?"
3. "What would make you feel more confident about your retirement plans?"

### Stage 4: thank them and offer something useful

> "Really appreciate you taking the time. Since you've been so helpful, can I send you our retirement planning checklist as a thank-you? Would that be useful?"

The favour opener gets past the "he's selling me something" reflex long enough to be heard, and being upfront about the questions gives them a reason to stay on. The questions tell you what they care about. The thank-you gift gives you a natural reason to follow up and suggest a Fact-Find later.

Most calls won't turn into meetings, because cold only works at volume. The team treats 5-10% of conversations turning into meetings as a healthy cold-call script.

## Cold-call structure: an opener that survives the first 30 seconds

There's a second way to open a cold call. It has three beats inside 45 seconds:

1. Opening (10 to 15 seconds). Say your name, your company, and why you're calling this person in particular. Sound confident rather than apologetic.

> "Hi [name], this is Leo from AIA. I work with young professionals on financial planning. Quick check, do you have 30 seconds?"

2. Relevance (15 to 30 seconds). Name a problem that belongs to them.

> "Most people I speak with at your stage tell me they know they should be planning but don't know where to start."

3. The ask (30 to 45 seconds). Offer two specific times instead of asking whether they're interested.

> "Would Saturday 10am or Sunday 2pm work better for a 30-minute Zoom, no obligation?"

What kills cold calls: speaking too softly, sounding scripted, and asking "would you be interested?", which hands them an easy no. How you sound matters at least as much as what you say. Practise the opener out loud, and record it, before your first batch.

For full objection handling on cold calls, use the [Cold Calling: Working Adults (All Angles)](/scripts/b782b7bf-48ec-49d8-9652-87e7b3c9476b) script.

## The ABCD four promises

Whether the appointment came from warm or cold, the first 30 seconds of the Fact-Find set the tone. The [ABCD Four Promises](/scripts/f7947680-5850-4d82-8b17-494e4dfc8346) deal with the prospect's scepticism before you ask your first real question. Deliver all four, in order, roughly like this:

| Promise | Script |
|---|---|
| A: Assurance | "Before we start, this meeting isn't to sell you anything. I'm here to understand you and your goals. If we end up with a proposal, it's because I shared something valuable and you felt comfortable moving forward." |
| B: Best Interest | "If we do reach the proposal stage, my promise is that what I plan for you is exactly what I'd do for myself in your shoes." |
| C: Confidentiality | "Everything you share with me today stays in strictest confidence." |
| D: Disclosure | "With that said, I hope you can share openly, because the quality of the recommendation depends on the quality of what I learn." |

Each one answers a worry. Assurance answers "is he going to push me?" Best Interest answers "why should I trust him?" Confidentiality answers "is it safe to share?" Disclosure hands the job of being open to them.

Four sentences, about 40 seconds, done before the first Fact-Find question. Prospects open up faster afterwards, because you've already answered worries they hadn't said out loud.

## Where cold sits in the week

This week's 30 outreaches don't have to include any cold. For most new FCs in the first 60 days, cold is an optional 0 to 5 of the 30: a chance to test one cold DM with your framework, or one unconventional cold call.

On this team, the large majority of first-year business comes from warm and semi-warm contacts, and cold adds volume on top. So spend this week drilling the Market Survey on your Semi-Warm list. Cold is on the curriculum now for one reason: learning its voice stops you slipping it into your warm-market scripts.

## Team operations: cold-call setup

Before you start any cold volume (cold leads, SMA leads, Facebook lead-gen), get this set up. It's the gate for everything after your RNF (Representative Notification Framework) registration:

- Buy a new number just for business, and set up WhatsApp Business on the same phone. A second phone lets you keep dialling on one line while the other rings out.
- Get access to [the prospecting doc](https://nsgukkz32942.sg.larksuite.com/wiki/OTW3wipXSi0y8nkU5l3llk3Vgrb) and pin it to your Lark sidebar.
- Block 2 to 3 hours a day in your calendar for prospecting.
- Ask Leo for cold leads. New FCs get unlimited cold leads for their first 3 months.
- Complete [the SMA checklist](https://nsgukkz32942.sg.larksuite.com/wiki/WZiRwAtHgipbzfklv0ClplaMgzb) before you run any ads. Facebook lead-gen opens up after RNF.
- Watch [the prospecting daily Loom](https://www.loom.com/share/744a0b57822a464dbf6f98301ef1b6fb) and read the [appointment-setting module on Skool](https://www.skool.com/finternship/classroom/411f0212) (1 to 2 hours).
- After your first 50 dials, book a 1-1 with Leo to go through what worked in your scripts and what didn't.

### DNC screening before any cold list goes live

Screen every cold list against Singapore's Do Not Call (DNC) Registry before you call or message anyone on it. The rules cover voice calls, SMS and messaging apps like WhatsApp that use a Singapore number. Breaking them can mean a financial penalty of up to $200,000 for an individual under the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), so never skip it.

1. Paste the full lead list into a Google Sheet, one column of numbers.
2. Download it as a CSV. The filename can't have spaces or special characters: `leads.csv` works, `Leo's leads.csv` doesn't.
3. Log into the DNC Registry portal and go to Check Registry, then Bulk Filtering.
4. Upload the CSV, click Proceed, and wait 5 to 10 minutes for processing.
5. Download the results. There are three separate registers (No Voice Call, No Text Message, No Fax), so check the right one for how you'll contact each person. Remove any number that's registered for your channel.

Run the check the day you receive a new list. Results are only valid for 21 days, so re-check anything older than that before you contact it.

## Quiz

**Q1. Warm and cold markets need different voices. Which pairing is correct?**
- A) Warm = authority / cold = vulnerability
- B) Warm = vulnerability / cold = authority and a framework (correct)
- C) Both use the same tone
- D) Warm = pitch / cold = storytelling

**Why:** In the warm market you already share context, so being open and honest works. In the cold market there's no context, so the prospect looks for signs of competence: a clear framework, a clear niche, credentials. Blend the two and neither audience gets what it needs.

**Q2. The 5 pillars of cold prospecting are:**
- A) Niche, framework, profile, sharp DM, follow-up (correct)
- B) Reach, engage, convert, retain, refer
- C) Awareness, interest, desire, action, loyalty
- D) Market, message, media, measure, modify

**Why:** The five pillars cover the whole cold funnel. The niche is who you're talking to, and the framework is why they remember you. The profile decides whether they trust you after clicking through, the DM decides whether they reply, and the follow-up decides whether they convert. Miss one pillar and the funnel breaks at that stage.

**Q3. The ABCD Four Promises at the start of a Fact-Find stand for:**
- A) Ask, Build, Close, Deliver
- B) Assurance, Best Interest, Confidentiality, Disclosure (correct)
- C) Acknowledge, Background, Confidence, Deal
- D) Always, Be, Closing, Deals

**Why:** Each promise answers a different worry the prospect walks in with. Assurance answers *"are you going to push me?"*, Best Interest answers *"is the advice really for me?"* and Confidentiality answers *"is it safe to share?"*. Disclosure hands the job of being open to them. Delivered in 40 seconds at the start, the ABCD turns the rest of the Fact-Find from guarded to cooperative.

**Q4. Day 22 compares a weak cold DM (*"Hi, I saw your profile and I think I could help with your financial planning. Would you like to book a call?"*) with a strong one. The key difference is:**
- A) The strong one is longer
- B) The strong one names a real problem, offers something specific upfront, and asks for very little (correct)
- C) The strong one uses hashtags
- D) The strong one includes emojis

**Why:** Length isn't it, since the two are about the same size. The strong version names a problem specific to their niche (the cashflow squeeze when a 2-outlet F&B owner opens a third), offers something concrete (a 1-page checklist) and asks for almost nothing (*"no pitch, no call needed"*).

**Q5. The "unconventional cold call" opener is built around:**
- A) A direct pitch
- B) A "favour, please don't hang up" frame where you say honestly who you are and what's happening, ask for one minute, and tell them they can hang up any time (correct)
- C) A warm-market style vulnerability opener
- D) A price drop

**Why:** Standard openers get hung up on because the prospect recognises the pattern straight away. The favour opener interrupts that pattern. The honesty is unusual enough that people stay on for the few seconds you need to explain the questions. Telling them they can hang up whenever they like removes the urge to hang up first. It only works if every line is true.

**Q6. What does the D in the ABCD Four Promises (Assurance, Best Interest, Confidentiality, Disclosure) change?**
- A) The pitch
- B) Who is responsible for openness: it makes being open their part of the deal, because the advice can only be as good as what they share (correct)
- C) The price
- D) The product

**Why:** After Assurance (no pressure), Best Interest (the advice is for them) and Confidentiality (it's safe to share), the fourth promise changes the prospect's role. They stop feeling interrogated and start seeing honest answers as their part of making this work.

**Q7. For a typical new FC in Week 4, how many of the 30 outreaches should be cold?**
- A) 30, cold is the fastest path
- B) 0-5, optional and experimental, because warm and Semi-Warm contacts bring in most first-year business (correct)
- C) 15, half and half
- D) 0, cold is never useful

**Why:** For a new FC, cold converts far less often than warm, and warm contacts also lead to referrals. Making cold most of this week's volume swaps a high-converting channel for a low-converting one. Doing 0-5 cold outreaches lets you practise switching voices, which is the point of Day 22, without crowding out the warm-market work that pays.
