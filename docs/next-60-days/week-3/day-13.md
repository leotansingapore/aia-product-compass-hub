# Day 13: The 5 silent questions

> **Today in one line:** Before anyone DMs you, they run 5 silent checks on your profile. If your content doesn't answer them, you lose the lead before you knew it existed.

**What to remember**

1. A prospect DMs once your feed answers all 5 silent questions, so give each question its own post.
2. Prove others value your work with a before, during and after testimonial, or an honestly framed live, mentor or training case.
3. Your origin story shows why you do this work, and posts about your life show you'd get along.
4. A one-word DM keyword at the end of a post writes the prospect's first message for them.

## The gap most new FCs miss

You post something useful, the views go up, and nobody DMs. The content usually wasn't wrong. It answered one silent question, "does this person know their stuff?", and left the other four blank.

People message someone they've decided is safe to approach. The 5 silent questions are how they decide, mostly without noticing. Trust forms once all five have an answer on your feed.

## The content pillar map

![The 5 silent questions: Q1 Can I trust others value your work? Q2 Do you actually know your stuff? Q3 Why are you doing this? Q4 Will we get along? Q5 How do I approach you without feeling awkward?](/next-60-days/images/n60-day-13-m0.webp)

Each question has its own content pillar. Post at least one per pillar each month and rotate, so all five show up on your feed or stories.

| Question | Content pillar | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: Others value? | Testimonial | Pre-during-post case study |
| Q2: Know stuff? | Educational | Breakdown, carousel, short reel |
| Q3: Why? | Origin story | 60-sec reel, long-caption post |
| Q4: Get along? | Lifestyle / PPVV | Personal, stories-heavy |
| Q5: Easy to approach? | Offer post | Soft call to action (CTA) with DM keyword |

## Q1: Can I trust that others value your work?

This is **social proof**: testimonials and real client stories. Robert Cialdini lists it as one of his six principles of persuasion, because people look at what others like them have done before deciding. Without it you're a stranger making claims.

Answer it with a **pre-during-post** testimonial: the client's situation before, the work you did together, and where they are after.

> "Before working with me, Ryan felt lost about his finances. During our consultation, we built a clear strategy tailored to his goals. After implementing the plan, he now has a structured roadmap and knows exactly what every dollar is doing."

A real client screenshot, a short video or a specific change in their situation makes it stronger. Get the client's written consent, leave returns, payouts and past performance out of it, and run it through your firm's approval process before it goes live, because under MAS's digital advertising guidelines your firm answers for any testimonial you share.

No closed cases yet? There are three honest options, and [Day 14](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/14) goes deeper:

- A live case-in-progress, anonymised and framed as "here's what we worked through this week".
- A win from your mentor's book, with your mentor's and their client's permission.
- A training case framed as "here's how this kind of situation usually gets solved", never claimed as your own.

A made-up or borrowed testimonial costs you your credibility the moment anyone notices.

## Q2: Do you actually know your stuff?

This is expertise, and it's probably what you post already: carousels, product explainers, tax tips. Prospects assume a licensed advisor knows the basics, so on its own it won't get you DMs.

Answer it with a real client problem, broken down in plain language. Skip jargon ("holistic wealth accumulation strategies"), vague authority claims ("trust me, I know what I'm doing") and textbook summaries, which people scroll past.

If you're new, don't bluff. Frame it as "my take" or "what I've been learning". A post called "3 things I got wrong about CareShield Life before I became an FC" will do better than one that pretends you know everything.

## Q3: Why are you doing this?

Prospects assume every advisor is in it for the commission. "I help people" won't change their mind, but the specific reason you care will.

Answer it with your origin story. Skip the CV and tell them about the moment. Ask yourself:

- What happened that made this a career you chose, and more than a job?
- What personal experience shaped how you think about money?
- Whose financial situation made you angry, confused or scared for them?

You wrote this story in [Day 4](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/4). Cut the villain and guide parts to 60 seconds for a reel, or about 150 words for a caption.

## Q4: Will we get along?

People judge each other on two things, warmth and competence. Susan Fiske and her colleagues found this across decades of social psychology research. Expertise without warmth reads as cold, and people say "I know he's good, but I'd rather work with someone warmer."

Answer it with PPVV content from [Day 11](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/11): passion, pain, values and vision. Post about your hobbies, your family, your Sunday, or your opinion on something that isn't finance.

Posts with nothing to do with money often build more trust than money posts, because they show the whole person behind the job title. You'll put off a few people who were never going to click with you, and that's fine.

## Q5: How do I approach you without feeling awkward?

Many prospects don't know how to start. "Hi, I want to know about insurance" feels strange to type, so they never send it.

Answer it with an **offer post**: a testimonial or insight with a soft call to action at the end.

> "After implementing the plan, Sarah now feels financially secure and confident about her 40s. If you want to see how this approach could work for you, DM me 'plan' and I'll share the same free cashflow audit we used."

The keyword gives the prospect one word to send, so they skip the awkward "hey, can I ask you something".

Two more versions:

- "Comment 'reset' and I'll send you the checklist."
- "Reply with your age and I'll tell you which of the 5 planning gaps you're most likely to have."

## Do this today

1. Open your IG profile and label each of your last 10 posts with the question it answers.
2. Count them. Q2 usually turns up eight times and the other four about once between them.
3. Plan a 5-post rotation, one post per question. Put the questions your audit found missing first.
4. Draft the first post this week. It counts toward the Week 3 KPI of 3 posts, one of which must be a Q5 offer post.

## Quiz

**Q1. The 5 silent questions a prospect runs through before DMing are:**
- A) Price, Products, Process, Proof, Policy
- B) Others value? / Know stuff? / Why doing this? / Get along? / Easy to approach? (correct)
- C) Who, What, Where, When, Why
- D) Need, Budget, Authority, Timeline, Fit

**Why:** The 5 silent questions cover social proof, expertise, purpose, connection and approachability. If your content skips one of them, the prospect doesn't get to trust, and the DM doesn't come. Most new FCs only answer Q2 (expertise) and then wonder why nobody messages.

**Q2. The *pre-during-post* format is used for which content pillar?**
- A) Offer posts
- B) Origin story posts
- C) Testimonial posts (correct)
- D) Lifestyle / PPVV posts

**Why:** Pre-during-post lays out a testimonial in three beats: the client's situation before, the work you did during, and where they are after. It's the simplest template for Q1 (social proof), and it's concrete enough that prospects believe it. A vague "helped a client recently" gets discounted.

**Q3. An "offer post" answers which silent question?**
- A) Q1: do others value your work?
- B) Q2: do you know your stuff?
- C) Q3: why are you doing this?
- D) Q5: how do I approach you without feeling awkward? (correct)

**Why:** An offer post ends a testimonial or insight with a soft call to action ("DM me [keyword]"). The keyword gives the prospect something specific to send, so they don't have to write an opening line. Q5 is the question most advisors forget to plan for.

**Q4. A new FC audits their last 10 posts and finds 8 of them answer Q2 (expertise). What's the most likely diagnosis for "no one DMs me"?**
- A) The posts aren't useful enough
- B) Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q5 are almost entirely unanswered, and trust doesn't form without the other four (correct)
- C) They need to post more often
- D) The algorithm hates their account

**Why:** Posting mostly expertise is the most common new-FC content mistake. Prospects already assume a licensed advisor knows the basics, so expertise alone won't decide it. The decision to DM also needs social proof, connection and approachability, and without those, 8 Q2 posts miss what the reader is actually checking.

**Q5. Q3 ("why are you doing this?") is best answered by:**
- A) A list of credentials and certifications
- B) Your origin story, the villain-to-guide arc from Week 1, Day 4 (correct)
- C) A post about the company you work for
- D) A calendar booking link

**Why:** Prospects start out assuming every advisor is in it for the commission. What changes that is the specific reason you care. Your Week 1, Day 4 story, especially the villain and guide parts, is the raw material. Cut to about 150 words, it tells people the human reason you're in this career.

**Q6. You are a Week 3 FC with zero closed cases. Which of these is still a legitimate way to produce Q1 (social proof) content?**
- A) Making up a testimonial
- B) A live case-in-progress, anonymised and honestly framed as "here's what we worked through this week" (correct)
- C) Borrowing a competitor's testimonial
- D) Waiting until month 6 before posting Q1 at all

**Why:** A live case-in-progress keeps the shape of social proof (a real situation, a real action, an outcome still in progress) without inventing results. A and C destroy your credibility the moment anyone notices. D leaves you with no social proof in the months you need it most.

**Q7. The keyword in an offer post ("DM me 'audit'") removes which specific friction?**
- A) The friction of clicking a link
- B) The friction of writing the first message: the prospect doesn't have to come up with an opening line, they just send the keyword (correct)
- C) The friction of booking a call
- D) The friction of reading the post

**Why:** Plenty of prospects want the resource but don't know how to start the message. "Hi, I'd like to know about insurance" feels awkward. A keyword gives them something short and impersonal to send. They type one word, you reply, and the conversation has started. That's the reason an offer post pulls more DMs than a post with no call to action.
