# Day 13: The 5 silent questions

> **The one idea for today:** Before anyone DMs you, they run 5 silent checks on your profile. If your content doesn't answer them, you lose the lead before you knew it existed.

By the end of today you'll know the 5 questions a cold prospect asks while scrolling your profile. You'll audit your last 10 posts against them and plan a 5-post rotation, one post per question.

---

## The gap most new FCs miss

You post something useful and the views go up, but nobody DMs. So you decide the content was wrong.

Usually it wasn't. The post probably answered one silent question, *"does this person know their stuff?"*, and left the other four blank.

People don't message a stranger because of one useful post. They message someone they've decided is safe to approach, and the 5 silent questions are how they decide, mostly without noticing. Each one needs an answer somewhere on your feed.

---

## The 5 silent questions

![The 5 silent questions: Q1 Can I trust others value your work? Q2 Do you actually know your stuff? Q3 Why are you doing this? Q4 Will we get along? Q5 How do I approach you without feeling awkward?](/next-60-days/images/n60-day-13-m0.webp)

Each question has a type of content that answers it. One post per question is enough, as long as all five show up somewhere on your feed or stories.

---

## Q1: Can I trust that others value your work?

This is social proof: testimonials and real client stories. Robert Cialdini lists it as one of his six principles of persuasion, because people look at what others like them have done before deciding. Without it you're a stranger making claims about yourself.

What answers it is a testimonial post in the **pre-during-post** format.

> *"Before working with me, Ryan felt lost about his finances. During our consultation, we built a clear strategy tailored to his goals. After implementing the plan, he now has a structured roadmap and knows exactly what every dollar is doing."*

It has three beats:

- Before: what the client's situation felt like
- During: the work you did together
- After: where they are now

A screenshot of a real client message, a short video testimonial or a specific outcome makes it stronger. Posts like this count as advertising, so run testimonials through your firm's approval process before they go live.

No closed cases yet? Use a win from your mentor's book, with your mentor's and their client's permission, or a training case framed as *"here's how this kind of situation usually gets solved"* and never claimed as your own. Day 14 goes deeper.

---

## Q2: Do you actually know your stuff?

This is expertise. It's the one question most new FCs already answer, with educational carousels, product explainers and tax tips. You need it, but on its own it won't get you DMs.

What answers it is a real client problem, broken down in plain language.

Avoid:

- Jargon (*"holistic wealth accumulation strategies"*)
- Vague authority claims (*"trust me, I know what I'm doing"*)
- Textbook summaries, which people scroll past

If you're new, don't bluff. Frame what you share as *"my take"* or *"what I've been learning"*, which keeps you credible without overreaching. A post called *"3 things I got wrong about CareShield Life before I became an FC"* will do better than one that pretends you already know everything.

---

## Q3: Why are you doing this?

Prospects start out skeptical and assume every advisor is in it for the commission. *"I help people"* won't change their mind. What changes it is the specific reason you care.

What answers it is your origin story. Skip the CV and tell them about the moment. Ask yourself:

- What happened that made this feel like a career you chose, and more than a job?
- What personal experience shaped how you think about money?
- Whose financial situation made you angry, confused or scared for them?

You wrote this story in Week 1, Day 4. Cut the villain and guide sections down to 60 seconds and you have an origin post.

---

## Q4: Will we get along?

People judge each other on two things, warmth and competence. Susan Fiske and her colleagues found this across decades of social psychology research. Expertise without warmth reads as cold, and you end up as the advisor people describe as *"I know he's good, but I'd rather work with someone warmer."*

What answers it is PPVV content from Day 11: passion, pain, values and vision. Post about your hobbies, your family, your Sunday, or your opinion on something that has nothing to do with finance.

It feels backwards, but posts with nothing to do with money often build more trust than posts about money. They show the prospect a whole person, where a finance post only shows a job title.

You'll put off a few people who were never going to click with you anyway, and that's fine.

---

## Q5: How do I approach you without feeling awkward?

A lot of prospects hesitate because they don't know how to start. *"Hi, I want to know about insurance"* feels strange to type, so they never send it.

What answers it is an **offer post**, which is a testimonial or insight with a soft call to action at the end.

> *"After implementing the plan, Sarah now feels financially secure and confident about her 40s. If you want to see how this approach could work for you, DM me 'plan' and I'll share the same free cashflow audit we used."*

The keyword does the work. With *"DM me plan"* the prospect doesn't have to write an opening line. They have one word to send, so they skip the awkward *"hey, can I ask you something"*.

Variations:

- *"Comment 'reset' and I'll send you the checklist."*
- *"Reply with your age and I'll tell you which of the 5 planning gaps you're most likely to have."*

## The content pillar map

Each question gets its own content pillar. Post at least one per pillar per month, and rotate through them.

| Question | Content pillar | Format |
|---|---|---|
| Q1: Others value? | Testimonial | Pre-during-post case study |
| Q2: Know stuff? | Educational | Breakdown, carousel, short reel |
| Q3: Why? | Origin story | 60-sec reel, long-caption post |
| Q4: Get along? | Lifestyle / PPVV | Personal, stories-heavy |
| Q5: Easy to approach? | Offer post | Soft CTA with DM keyword |

Now open your IG profile and go through your last 10 posts. Write down which of the 5 questions each one answers. Most new FCs find they've answered Q2 eight times and Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q5 about once between them, which is why the DMs aren't coming.

## Quiz

**Q1. The 5 silent questions a prospect runs through before DMing are:**
- A) Price, Products, Process, Proof, Policy
- B) Others value? / Know stuff? / Why doing this? / Get along? / Easy to approach? ✓
- C) Who, What, Where, When, Why
- D) Need, Budget, Authority, Timeline, Fit

**Why:** The 5 silent questions cover social proof, expertise, purpose, connection and approachability. If your content skips one of them, the prospect doesn't get to trust, and the DM doesn't come. Most new FCs only answer Q2 (expertise) and then wonder why nobody messages.

**Q2. The *pre-during-post* format is used for which content pillar?**
- A) Offer posts
- B) Origin story posts
- C) Testimonial posts ✓
- D) Lifestyle / PPVV posts

**Why:** Pre-during-post lays out a testimonial in three beats: the client's situation before, the work you did during, and where they are after. It's the simplest template for Q1 (social proof), and it's concrete enough that prospects believe it. A vague *"helped a client recently"* gets discounted.

**Q3. An "offer post" answers which silent question?**
- A) Q1: do others value your work?
- B) Q2: do you know your stuff?
- C) Q3: why are you doing this?
- D) Q5: how do I approach you without feeling awkward? ✓

**Why:** An offer post ends a testimonial or insight with a soft call to action (*"DM me [keyword]"*). The keyword gives the prospect something specific to send, so they don't have to write an opening line. Q5 is the question most advisors forget to plan for.

**Q4. A new FC audits their last 10 posts and finds 8 of them answer Q2 (expertise). What's the most likely diagnosis for "no one DMs me"?**
- A) The posts aren't useful enough
- B) Q1, Q3, Q4 and Q5 are almost entirely unanswered, and trust doesn't form without the other four ✓
- C) They need to post more often
- D) The algorithm hates their account

**Why:** Posting mostly expertise is the most common new-FC content mistake. Prospects already assume a licensed advisor knows the basics, so expertise alone won't decide it. The decision to DM also needs social proof, connection and approachability, and without those, 8 Q2 posts miss what the reader is actually checking.

**Q5. Q3 ("why are you doing this?") is best answered by:**
- A) A list of credentials and certifications
- B) Your origin story, the villain-to-guide arc from Week 1, Day 4 ✓
- C) A post about the company you work for
- D) A calendar booking link

**Why:** Prospects start out assuming every advisor is in it for the commission. What changes that is the specific reason you care. Your Week 1, Day 4 story, especially the villain and guide parts, is the raw material. Cut to about 150 words, it tells people the human reason you're in this career.

**Q6. You are a Week 3 FC with zero closed cases. Which of these is still a legitimate way to produce Q1 (social proof) content?**
- A) Making up a testimonial
- B) A live case-in-progress, anonymised and honestly framed as "here's what we worked through this week" ✓
- C) Borrowing a competitor's testimonial
- D) Waiting until month 6 before posting Q1 at all

**Why:** A live case-in-progress keeps the shape of social proof (a real situation, a real action, an outcome still in progress) without inventing results. A and C destroy your credibility the moment anyone notices. D leaves you with no social proof in the months you need it most.

**Q7. The keyword in an offer post ("DM me 'audit'") removes which specific friction?**
- A) The friction of clicking a link
- B) The friction of writing the first message: the prospect doesn't have to come up with an opening line, they just send the keyword ✓
- C) The friction of booking a call
- D) The friction of reading the post

**Why:** Plenty of prospects want the resource but don't know how to start the message. *"Hi, I'd like to know about insurance"* feels awkward. A keyword gives them something short and impersonal to send. They type one word, you reply, and the conversation has started. That's the reason an offer post pulls more DMs than a post with no call to action.
