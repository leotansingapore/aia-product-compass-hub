# Day 16: The DM funnel, reply scripts

> **The one idea for today:** Every text has three jobs: link back, give a reason to meet and end with an easy question. When one is missing, the chat usually dies there.

By the end of today you'll be using the 11 texting rules, especially the three non-negotiables (link back, reason to meet, easy question at the end). You'll handle the 6 most common DM objections with a 6-step reply, and you'll swap *"can we meet?"* follow-ups for value-first messages, so blue ticks stop being what you usually get.

---

## Why scripts fail in warm market

New FCs tend to memorise a reply script. It rarely works, because scripts sound scripted, and more so to someone who already knows you.

What works better is a flow with a few rules. You get to sound like yourself, which is the reason a warm contact replies to you in the first place, and the rules keep each text doing its three jobs:

- Link back to the last thing you talked about
- Give them a reason to meet, by offering something and demanding nothing
- End with a question that's easy to answer, so replying costs them little

Tone, emojis and *hahaha*s are up to you. Those three rules don't change.

---

## The 11 texting rules

| # | Rule | One-line example |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Link back to the previous conversation, never a random opener | *"Hey, you were saying you work in Tanjong Pagar right? Which side?"* |
| 2 | Every text adds a reason to meet | *"Been meaning to show you what I've been working on, think you'd find it interesting."* |
| 3 | End every text with an easy-to-answer question | *"Still WFH these days or mostly office?"* |
| 4 | Make them feel safe about the chat | *"Sometimes I advise people not to buy anything. You decide after we meet."* |
| 5 | Find common ground that keeps the chat going | *"A few of my clients are in the same industry, and they usually ask me about X."* |
| 6 | Follow up actively, because most people won't reply to the first text | Don't wait. Bump with a value drop. |
| 7 | Reply within 3 to 4 hours of their reply, which shows you're reliable | (no script needed) |
| 8 | Keep it casual: no jargon, no *"optimisation"*, no *"portfolio reviews"* | Use emojis or *hahaha* if that's how you actually text |
| 9 | Warm up with 2 or 3 touchpoints first (story replies, post comments, a congrats) | They're more likely to reply to someone familiar |
| 10 | Don't ask if they're "free", because nobody feels free. Ask for a specific 30 minutes | *"Grab a 30-min call, Thu or Fri better?"* |
| 11 | Make the CTA easy to answer, with 2 options and never an open question | *"10 Jan 6pm, or 23 Jan 8pm, which works?"* |

Rules 1, 2 and 3 are the non-negotiables. Every text you send in the pipeline follows all three, and the other eight help.

## The 6 DM objections you'll see most

After the first text, new FCs hear the same six objections over and over:

1. *"Thanks bro, not interested."*
2. *"Cool, if I need it I'll let you know!"*
3. *"Thanks, I have an advisor already."*
4. *"All good, thanks."*
5. *"Not looking to invest right now."*
6. *"Getting married / buying house / just had a kid, bad time."*

Expect an objection. It's the normal reply, and a yes is a bonus. When you lose a prospect after an objection, it's usually because the reply was handled badly or you gave up, and seldom because they objected.

Most new FCs answer with *"no worries, thanks anyway!"* and disappear. That's the mistake, because the objection is usually where the real conversation starts.

## The 6-step objection response

![The 6-step objection response: 1 Acknowledge casually, 2 Common ground, 3 Different perspective, 4 Make them feel safe, 5 Easy question, 6 Personal warmth](/next-60-days/images/n60-day-16-m0.webp)

Here's one objection taken through all 6 steps.

**Prospect:** *"Thanks bro, I already have an advisor."*

Step 1: acknowledge casually.
> *"Hahaha yeah, honestly if you didn't, I'd be surprised."*

Step 2: common ground.
> *"Most of the people I end up meeting already have an advisor too. They usually meet me for a second pair of eyes, not a replacement."*

Step 3: different perspective.
> *"What I do is a bit different. I lay everything out side by side, what you're covered for and what you're paying, so you can see how it fits together. Sometimes people spot a gap or an overlap they didn't know about."*

Step 4: make them feel safe.
> *"Sometimes the conclusion is just 'keep everything as it is', which is totally fine. You decide what's useful after you see it."*

Step 5: easy question.
> *"Are you usually around CBD on weekdays, or mostly WFH?"*

Step 6: personal warmth.
> *"Haven't caught up in ages anyway. Would be nice to see you whatever happens with this!"*

Put together naturally, that's 4 or 5 short bubbles. The objection has turned into a conversation, and nobody is being closed.

## Three moves that make a yes more likely

Still getting a soft response after the 6-step reply? Change the ask itself.

### Move 1: push the date further out

New FCs try to book the meeting this week or next, which feels like pressure. Suggest 1 to 2 months ahead:

> *"No rush at all. What if I check in with you mid-March? You'll be past the wedding prep by then and we can look at everything with a clearer head."*

A date far away is easy to agree to. A short-notice ask feels like pressure.

### Move 2: postpone the scheduling, not the meeting

If they can't commit to a date, don't ask for one. Ask if you can text them later to pick one:

> *"All good. I'll text you in February to pick a day that works?"*

That's easy to say yes to, and you've set your own follow-up, so the next move is yours.

### Move 3: show, don't tell

If *"can we meet?"* keeps getting pushback, stop asking and send something useful:

> *"Here's a 2-min video I made on the three things I look for in a Fact-Find. Thought it might be useful whether or not we meet."*

Your next text is a value drop in place of another ask. It could be a video, a PDF, a carousel or a case study. The format matters less than giving something without asking for anything back.

This works because of reciprocity, which Robert Cialdini describes as people's urge to return a favour. Ask after ask makes you look pushy. A run of useful messages makes you look generous, and people reply to generous.

## Hook openers by life stage

When you're warming up a specific person and want a concrete opener, these three work well:

| Life stage | Hook opener | What it opens |
|---|---|---|
| Working young | *"By the way, have you started investing?"* | *"Want me to show you a quick crash course?"* |
| Engaged couple | *"You two BTO-ing already?"* | BTO planning and household budgeting |
| Fresh grad | *"Graduated already right?"* | *"Have you sorted out your insurance and investing yet?"*, then *"Why don't I walk you through what I've been doing for my other clients?"* |

An opener tied to their life stage does better than *"hey, how's life?"*. It tells them you know where they are in life and have something relevant to say.

---

## Splitting and pacing

Two small rules make a big difference:

- Split long messages into chunks. Three short bubbles read like a conversation, while one wall of text reads like a pitch, even with the same content.
- Never open cold with *"How are you?"*, which sounds deliberate and makes people suspicious. Open with something specific to them: *"Hey, still at Singtel?"* or *"Just saw your story about Japan, is that Kyoto?"*

## Quiz

**Q1. The three non-negotiable rules every DM must follow are:**
- A) Emojis, hahas, short sentences
- B) Link back, create a reason to meet, end with an easy question ✓
- C) Be funny, be short, be honest
- D) Open with a compliment, build rapport, ask for the meeting

**Why:** Every text in the warm-market pipeline does all three. Linking back gives context, so the opener isn't random. The reason to meet keeps the conversation heading somewhere. The easy question makes replying cheap, so more people reply. The other eight rules help.

**Q2. The 6-step objection response, in order, is:**
- A) Close, handle, rebut, close again, ask, push
- B) Acknowledge, common ground, different perspective, make them feel safe, easy question, personal warmth ✓
- C) Apologise, retreat, apologise again, close, apologise, close
- D) Deflect, reframe, pitch, close, pitch, close

**Why:** The 6 steps keep the conversation open after an objection. Acknowledging shows you heard them. Common ground and a different perspective reframe it without an argument. Safety takes the pressure off, the easy question gets them talking again, and personal warmth reminds them you're a friend first. Skip a step and the reply feels off.

**Q3. A prospect says *"not now, I just bought a house."* Which response gives you the best chance of keeping the conversation alive?**
- A) *"No worries, let me know if you change your mind!"*
- B) *"Understood, how about we meet next week anyway?"*
- C) *"Totally fair. I'll text you in May to see if it's a better time by then?"* ✓
- D) *"But this is actually the perfect time to plan around that."*

**Why:** A gives up on the lead. B pushes against a real reason, which makes them resist. D is the pushy reframe that loses warm contacts. C is Move 2: you put off picking a date but keep the meeting on. It's easy to agree to, it sets your next follow-up, and it respects what they said while keeping the conversation going.

**Q4. Day 16 says an objection is the normal reply and a yes is a bonus. What does that change about how new FCs handle *"not interested"*?**
- A) They should assume the prospect is lying
- B) They stay in the conversation with the 6-step reply, where they'd otherwise retreat with *"no worries, thanks anyway"* ✓
- C) They should push harder until the prospect says yes
- D) They should stop prospecting that person for 6 months

**Why:** Most new FCs answer any objection with a polite retreat, and the lead dies at the first bit of resistance. If you expect *"not interested"*, you stay in the conversation, and the 6-step reply turns the objection into a dialogue. Pushing harder (C) makes them resist more. Disappearing (A, D) loses the lead.

**Q5. The word "free" is banned from warm-market DMs because:**
- A) It sounds too generous
- B) Nobody feels genuinely "free", and the word makes the ask vague where it should be a specific time ✓
- C) It makes you sound cheap
- D) It's against compliance rules

**Why:** *"Are you free this week?"* is too open. The prospect has to come up with a slot, which takes effort, or say no. A specific 30-minute ask with 2 time options gives them something they can answer in one word. Drop "free" and offer "30 minutes" with 2 options.

**Q6. Repeated follow-ups asking *"can we meet?"* trigger which filter in the prospect's mind?**
- A) The generous filter
- B) The pushy filter: same pipeline, far fewer replies ✓
- C) The professional filter
- D) The curious filter

**Why:** Ask after ask tells the prospect you need this more than they do, so they back away. Useful messages tell them you'll send good things even if they never reply, and that makes them want to engage. With the same pipeline, what you send next decides which way it goes.

**Q7. Move 3 (show, don't tell) replaces the next text's *ask* with a *value drop*. Format examples include:**
- A) Business cards, PDFs, emails
- B) A 2-min video you made on 3 things to check in a Fact-Find, a short carousel, an anonymised case study ✓
- C) Long voice messages explaining your process
- D) A calendar booking link

**Why:** A value drop has to be quick to take in, something the prospect can look at in 2 minutes without committing to anything. A video, carousel or case study fits. A calendar link is another ask dressed up as value, and a long voice message is a monologue. Ask yourself whether they'd get something from it even if they never meet you. If yes, it's a real value drop.

## 2-liner hook (warm prospecting flow)

A low-pressure opener by text that uses a useful resource to lead to an appointment. In two lines you say what you've been helping friends or clients with and offer them a guide on it. If they say yes, send the resource, wait 1 to 2 weeks, then follow up to ask whether they managed to sort out the problem. That follow-up gets you the appointment without a pitch.

Use this when you've already applied the texting rules and the contact would respond better to a resource than to a meeting ask.
