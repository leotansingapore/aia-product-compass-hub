## Slide 3 — KNOW YOUR CLIENT-DISC

- D Profile
- Be CONFIDENT, super EFFICIENT and EFFECTIVE (Be fast in finding answers, give results and be resourceful)
- Make RECOMMENDATIONS (Don’t tell them what they already know)

- Show them OVERVIEW (best and worst scenario)
- I Profile
- They will like you if you are VERY PRESENTABLE
- They will like you if you are INTERESTING (tell stories and have sense of humor)
- They will like you if THEY FEEL YOU LIKE THEM (give them “wow” compliments)
- They will like you if you MAKE THEM HAPPY AND FEEL SPECIAL

## Slide 5 — 2.KNOW THE ART-MANAGING CLEAR OBJECTIONS

- Example:Alex is looking for a good education plan for his new-born baby. He is quite an amateur and checked with many different insurance providers to compare. Eventually, he feels that NTUC is a superior choice because it is government-related and offers lower premium. How would you respond to this objection?
- 1. Acknowledge the Concern (empathy):
- “Alex, I can understand why you do such comparisons…. Who doesn’t want to get the best plan?”
- 2. Relate (cite similar experience):
- “.... This was what most of my clients did too. They compared among many companies, made comparisons and thorough research on their respective products”
- 3. Turn Around (seek his agreement through logical reasoning):
- “However, wouldn’t you wonder, after thorough comparison, most of them eventually chose AIA products? There must be a good reason why they make that decision, right> What do you think could be one of the reasons?

## Slide 6 — 3.KNOW YOUR ICEBERG-AMBIGUOUS OBJECTION

- 3 Steps of Iceberg Theory:
- 1. Identify the concern by asking the right questions (to clarify if it is a genuine concern or just an excuse)
- 2. Acknowledge and help him resolve the concerns
- 3. Redirect the concern towards what matters most to him (bring him back to his hot buttons)

## Slide 7 — 3.KNOW YOUR ICEBERG-AMBIGUOUS OBJECTION

- Example:
- You are advising a prospect to take up a retirement plan. He seemed keen but …..
- Objection: I need to think about it
- Step 1:
- Examples of asking the right clarifying questions:
- Version 1 (use ART thereafter)
- You certainly do agree that you need to have more retirement funds and since you also like this plan, is there something holding you back?
- May I know what is it?
- Is it the monthly premium?

## Slide 8 — 3.KNOW YOUR ICEBERG-AMBIGUOUS OBJECTION

- Elaboration: Version 2
- Step 1: (Clarify concern)
- I could see that you are pretty satisfied with this plan. Hence, am I right to say it is just a habit that you need to consider for a day or two before you conclude your decision? (yes)
- Step 2 (Acknowledge and help resolve concern)
- I can understand that this could be a big financial decision, which is why you need a bit more time to think through.
- May I ask what’s the probability you will go ahead with this plan after thinking for 2 days [Answer: “Likely, just need some time to sit on it]
- If you don’t mind me asking, what’s the chance you really would take time to think through given that your schedule is so packed? [Answer: “Ya, likely not…”]
- If that’s the case, wouldn’t you agree it makes more sense for you to conclude it now since you are most likely to go ahead anyway? Then you can have a peace of mind and focus on the other important things you need to handle?
- Step 3 (Redirect concern to hot buttons)
- Surely there must be instances in the past which you have sat on a decision for too long and eventually missed the opportunity or got too busy and forgot about it?
- Unlike other purchases, surely you agree with me Retirement planning is not something you can afford to forget and sit on for too long, yes?
- The earlier you start, the earlier you can retire with more options. Can you imagine a situation should your retirement funds be insufficient, who do you think would have to be responsible to your livelihood and survival?…. (direct to hot buttons)

## Slide 9 — 3.KNOW YOUR ICEBERG-AMBIGUOUS OBJECTION

- Elaboration: Version 3
- Step 1: (Clarify concern)
- If I may ask, has your hesitation got to do with your parents? (yes)
- Step 2 (Acknowledge and help resolve concern)
- I can understand why this is a joint decision and you do need their agreement.
- Do you foresee any objection from them? (yes)
- What do you think could be their objection? (over-commitment)
- Are you confident that you can convince them? (maybe)
- How do you then intend to convince them?
- Step 3 (Redirect concern to hot buttons)
- I can understand why your parents may have such a concern. They are probably more concerned about the short term “discomfort” due to paying higher premiums for this investment plan and feel the stretch. Some of my clients’ parents also focus more on “now” and often overlook the severe consequences in the future should you not learn to start investing early.
- To help you ease your concern, if you recall, through our calculations earlier, we both agreed this amount to set aside is well within your means, correct?
- And you are making this decision mainly also to ensure that you never have to rely on your parents in the future.
- Since we have already established what you have now is certainly not sufficient and your fear is likely to happen, wouldn’t you agree that chances are, your parents will also be able to see your point of view and support that you are making the right and objective decision for your future?

## Slide 11 — ART VS ICEBERG

- When to use ART?
- When the objection is very COMMONLY HEARD, OBVIOUS and with a SPECIFIC reasoning
- When to use Iceberg?
- When the objection is AMBIGUOUS and subjected to MORE THAN ONE possible reasonings

## Slide 16 — FOLLOW-UP CLOSE

- Following up is the last resort in your sales activities!
- If you give the prospect an opportunity to walk away and “think it over”, chances are the prospect will gradually forget why he/she was ever attracted to your product or service in the first place!!!!!
- Steps to Follow Up:
- Agree to a date for next appointment for follow up before your prospect take his leave
- A Die-Die Must do: Ask the Right Questions! (before he leaves) • “If there is one thing that might be holding you back, what would that be?”
- Do not take more than 3 days to follow up
- Send a message to remind him why he was attracted to the idea
- When you make a follow up call, DO NOT ask: “Do you have any questions for me? Instead:
- “Hi Jasmine, I believe over the past 3 days, you have evaluated my recommendation and probably understand the long-term benefits why I suggested it. Are we good to proceed as per my suggestion?
