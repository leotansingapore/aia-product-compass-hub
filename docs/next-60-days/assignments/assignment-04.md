# Assignment 4: DISC roleplay tape, 4 profiles

> **What you're producing:** 4 deliveries of the same scenario (a fact-find opening with a new prospect), one per DISC profile, 90 seconds each, on camera. The recordings show whether your delivery really changes, and for most new FCs it barely does the first time.

## Why this matters

Knowing the 4 profiles in your head is easy. Changing your delivery live, within 3 minutes, is the hard part. Week 6 is about the gap between knowing how a D differs from an S and actually delivering differently to each. You can only close that gap once you see it, and a recording is the only way to see your own delivery.

Many new FCs are surprised by how little their tone changes across profiles in their first recording. Seeing that is where the improvement starts.

DISC is a conversation aid with little scientific validation behind it, so treat it as a way to practise adjusting your delivery. Nobody is diagnosing you or your prospects.

## The scenario (the same for all 4)

> You're at the start of a first fact-find. The small talk is done. You're about to give your intent statement and your 90-second pitch, then move to the first fact-find question.

Record 4 versions, 90 seconds each. The content stays roughly the same and the delivery changes completely.

### D roleplay (90 sec)
- Pace: fast (15 to 20% faster than your default)
- Opener: straight to the point, no warm-up
- Pitch: outcome first. *"I help families like yours make sure the income keeps coming if something happens. That's what we'll look at today."*
- First question: bottom line. *"What's the one thing you'd want this meeting to achieve?"*

### I roleplay (90 sec)
- Pace: match their high energy
- Opener: warmer and more animated, with a compliment or something you noticed about them
- Pitch: a true story. *"Let me tell you about a friend of mine who was at the same stage as you..."* Use something that really happened, and say whose story it is.
- First question: about feelings. *"What's on your mind right now when you think about money?"*

### S roleplay (90 sec)
- Pace: slow (15 to 20% slower than your default), with longer pauses
- Opener: real interest in them. *"How are the kids? How's the new role going?"*
- Pitch: reassurance first. *"There's no pressure to decide anything today. This meeting is so you can see what you already have."*
- First question: ask permission. *"Would it be okay if I asked about your family first?"*

### C roleplay (90 sec)
- Pace: measured and deliberate
- Opener: a clear agenda. *"Here's the plan for today: 40 minutes on what you have now, 15 on any gaps, 15 on options."*
- Pitch: data first. *"I've prepared a one-page overview. There are two assumptions I want to flag. Shall I go through those first?"*
- First question: precise. *"Can you walk me through the exact policies you have, and when you took each one?"*

## Recording setup

- Camera at eye level on a tripod, or a laptop propped on books. Don't hold it.
- Window light or a lamp on your face.
- A quiet room.
- 4 separate takes, 90 seconds each, each file labelled.
- Start each take by saying *"Roleplay for [profile]"* on camera so what you intended is on record.
- Roleplay with a colleague, mentor or friend who has agreed to be recorded. Never record a real prospect without their consent.

## Self-review: did you actually change?

Watch all 4 the same day and rate each from 1 to 5:

| Dimension | D | I | S | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pace match (did I speed up or slow down?) | | | | |
| Energy match (did my energy or calm fit?) | | | | |
| Content match (did I lead with the right thing?) | | | | |
| Transition (me adapting, or 4 different people?) | | | | |

The roleplay for your own main profile will usually score highest, because it's how you naturally talk. The one for your opposite profile will usually score lowest, because adjusting that far is the hardest skill. That lowest profile is your focus in Week 7.

## Submission

Paste the 4 Loom links and your self-assessment into the form below. In your 15-minute mentor review, your mentor watches all 4 videos with you and gives live feedback on the one that missed the mark most.

## The point

Nobody is grading these videos. They're for you to watch. Once you've seen yourself fail to slow down for an S when you meant to, you'll notice it in real meetings from then on. That awareness is the skill you're building.
