# Day 59: Reviewing your 60-day numbers

> **The one idea for today:** Sixty days in, the numbers tell the truth. Read them honestly, because what you measure now shapes what you build next.

By the end of today you'll have pulled your real 60-day numbers for CAR, FYC, pipeline and referrals, and compared them with the projections you made in Week 1. You'll have worked out honestly where the gap is, picked the one metric that most needs to move in Month 3, and committed to a specific way to move it.

---

## The 60-day reckoning

You started this module 60 days ago with $0 FYC and a plan. Today you count what actually happened. The question is what the numbers say, not how the two months felt.

Without an honest read, you plan Month 3 on a story instead of data. New FCs who tell themselves *"Month 2 was rough but Month 3 will be better"*, without working out what specifically has to change, usually get the same Month 3.

Today's diagnosis is what tomorrow's 12-month plan is built on.

## Compare with your Week-1 projections

Open your Day 2 and Day 3 work. What did you project 60 days ago?

- Projected weekly appointments: ___, actual: ___
- Projected close rate: __%, actual: __%
- Projected case size: $___, actual: $___
- Projected 60-day FYC: $___, actual: $___

The gap between the projection and the actual is your data, and it usually falls into one of three patterns.

### Pattern 1: every projection was optimistic
This is the most common one for new FCs. You projected 7 appointments a week and got 3, a 40% close rate and got 20%, a $750 case size and got $500.

It's normal, because first-year projections tend to run high. The lesson is to reset your expectations to what the market actually gave you, not what you hoped for at the start.

### Pattern 2: one metric is much weaker than the rest
Appointments are on target and Fact-Finds are fine, but few of them close, so close rate is the problem. Or appointments are low but you convert them well, which is a volume problem, not a skill problem.

This is the best result you can get from the diagnosis, because one broken link is much easier to fix than weakness everywhere.

### Pattern 3: your projections were too cautious
It's less common, but it happens. You projected low because you didn't know what was possible yet, and your actuals beat the plan.

Project higher for Month 3. Don't anchor to a cautious baseline that understates where you're heading.

## The CAR diagnostic over 60 days

Apply the CAR framework (Days 3, 23 and 29) to the whole 60-day window. The thresholds below are the team's rules of thumb.

### Calls
- Weekly calls at or above your target on average? Calls are healthy.
- Below target? Calls are the bottleneck, whatever happens further down.

### Appointments
- 25% or more of calls becoming appointments? Appointment setting is working.
- Under 20%? There's a script or targeting problem: warm conversations aren't turning into bookings.

### Referrals
- At least one warm referral per closed case? The referral engine is starting to turn.
- No referrals at all? Either you're not asking, or the ask is falling flat (Day 27).

Pick the weakest link. That's your Month 3 focus. The whole point of the CAR diagnostic is to make you choose one instead of spreading your effort across all three.

---

## The close-rate diagnostic

If closing is the weak spot, find the step where it leaks.

### Step 1: are Fact-Finds leading to qualified pitches?
If most Fact-Finds never get to a pitch, you're not finding what the prospect cares about. Go back to Day 38 (Hot buttons II) and Day 44 (silence).

### Step 2: are pitches leading to commitment?
If prospects say yes in the meeting and then don't follow through, the close may not suit their profile. Go back to Day 41, where the four closes are matched to DISC profiles.

### Step 3: are objections ending the conversation?
If prospects object and the meeting stalls there, objection handling is the gap. Go back to Days 52 and 53.

### Step 4: do deferred decisions ever come back?
If *"let me think about it"* turns into never hearing from them again, you're missing the follow-up close. Go back to Day 41 (the follow-up close) and Day 17 (CRAB).

Find the step that leaks most, and put Month 3's practice there.

## The honest questions

Beyond the numbers, ask yourself four questions.

### 1. Did I actually do the practice submissions each week?
Look at the done-or-missed list above, where each miss is a skill you didn't practise, and reread the material from your weakest week.

### 2. Where is my time actually going?
Look at Week 10 so far and split it into new business, relationships and systems. If systems takes more than 20%, Day 58's ceiling, you're probably hiding from the harder work (Day 58, failure 3).

### 3. Which prospects or clients do I dread?
Usually it's someone whose DISC profile you're pitching against, or whose real concern you haven't found. Don't drop them yet: first work out exactly what about them makes you uncomfortable, because that's what you need to practise.

### 4. If I'm honest, am I avoiding something?
Most new-FC plateaus have a specific avoidance at the centre, like the prospect you never call, the referral ask you skip or the objection you dread. Name it and the next step usually gets obvious.

## Month 3: pick one thing

From all this diagnosis, pick one thing to change in Month 3. Just one.

Good examples:
- *"I'll make 40 calls a week, every week, in a protected Monday morning block."*
- *"I'll ask for a referral after every Fact-Find, using the FACT Method word for word until it's automatic."*
- *"I'll re-record my drills for *too expensive* and *let me think* until my handling is clean."*
- *"I'll hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, and track it daily."*

Each one is specific and measurable, and you'll be able to see within four weeks whether you did it.

Narrowing to one is what makes change happen. *"I'll improve prospecting and closing and referrals and content"* changes nothing. Edwin Locke and Gary Latham's review of 35 years of goal-setting research found that people given specific, hard goals do better than people told to "do your best".

## Quiz

**Q1. The core reason to do the honest 60-day numerical review is:**
- A) To celebrate or mourn the numbers
- B) Month 3 planning needs to run on *data*, not *narrative*, because without honest diagnosis, the same patterns repeat ✓
- C) To submit to your mentor
- D) It's required by compliance

**Why:** Without a numbers review, the Month 3 plan becomes *"it'll be better because I'll try harder,"* which gets the same result. The numbers show which link is broken (calls, appointments, closes or referrals), which step inside it leaks, and what to change, so the plan becomes a targeted fix instead of hopeful optimism.

**Q2. The CAR diagnostic's core discipline is:**
- A) Track all three every week
- B) Pick *one* weakest link and target the Month-3 intervention there, rather than spreading effort across all three ✓
- C) Focus on Calls always
- D) Focus on Referrals always

**Why:** Working on every lever at once moves each a little and none of them much. The diagnostic makes you narrow down to the weakest link and fix that, and once it's healthy another link becomes the weakest, so you move on to it. One at a time gets you further, faster.

**Q3. A useful *one thing* commitment for Month 3 is:**
- A) *"I'll improve overall performance"*
- B) *"I'll work harder"*
- C) *"I'll hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, tracked daily"* ✓
- D) *"I'll think about prospecting more"*

**Why:** Specific, measurable, visible within four weeks and tied to a known gap: that's what changes behaviour. A, B and D feel like commitments, but they're too vague to act on or measure. Pick one narrow behaviour you can test and practise it hard for a month.

**Q4. When pulling your 60-day numbers, the rule is:**
- A) Round up anything close to the target
- B) Literally fill them in, even if small, because small honest numbers beat large inflated ones because the diagnosis depends on them ✓
- C) Only count successes
- D) Only count failures

**Why:** Inflated numbers lead to the wrong diagnosis, the wrong Month 3 plan and the wrong Month 6 results. Honest numbers, even ugly ones, point to the right fix. A 60-day FYC of $3K from few appointments calls for a different fix than $3K from lots of appointments, and rounding up hides the difference.

**Q5. Pattern 2 (one metric is much weaker than the others) is the "best-case" diagnosis because:**
- A) It means you're already succeeding
- B) A single broken link is much easier to fix than system-wide weakness: you target one intervention, not rebuild from scratch ✓
- C) It always resolves itself
- D) It means the other links are compensating

**Why:** One weak link needs one fix, and weakness everywhere needs a rebuild. A result like *"appointments healthy, close rate low"* is something you can act on: Month 3 goes into closing work, meaning objection handling from Days 52 and 53, silence from Day 44, and the closes from Days 41 and 49 to 51. Pattern 1, where every metric fell short, needs a broader reset.

**Q6. The "one thing" commitment rule for Month 3 exists because:**
- A) More than one is illegal
- B) Narrow and deep beats broad and shallow, since trying to improve 5 things simultaneously produces small movement everywhere and meaningful movement nowhere ✓
- C) It's easier to track
- D) Mentors require it

**Why:** Goal-setting research (Locke and Latham) finds that specific goals beat vague ones, and a single clear target is easier to plan around and check. *"Improve overall performance"* doesn't point to any behaviour. *"Hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, tracked daily"* is one thing you can observe, and it feeds straight into your close rate. Narrowing down is what makes Month 3 different from Months 1 and 2.

**Q7. Which is a sign you're avoiding the real work?**
- A) Systems work (admin, CE, plans) is >20% of the week
- B) You keep moving meetings with specific uncomfortable prospects
- C) You're on your fifth rewrite of the perfect Fact-Find form
- D) All of the above ✓

**Why:** Each is a version of the same thing: doing comfortable work like planning, rescheduling and polishing documents so you don't have to call hesitant prospects, face objections or ask for the close. When the avoided task keeps getting pushed to *"next week,"* the avoidance is running your week. Name it honestly, then put the uncomfortable work in a protected block before the comfortable work can spread.
