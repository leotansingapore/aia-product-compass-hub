# Day 59: Reviewing your 60-day numbers

> **Today in one line:** Sixty days in, read your real numbers honestly, because what you measure now shapes what you build next.

**What to remember**

1. Write in your real 60-day numbers, small ones included. Small honest numbers are more useful than big inflated ones.
2. Compare them with your Week-1 projections and name which of the 3 gap patterns you're in.
3. Use the CAR and close-rate checks to find the single weakest link.
4. Pick one specific, measurable thing to change in Month 3.

## The 60-day reckoning

You started this module 60 days ago with $0 FYC and a plan. Today you count what actually happened, and go by what the numbers say, whatever the two months felt like.

Without an honest read, you plan Month 3 on a story instead of data. New FCs who tell themselves "Month 2 was rough but Month 3 will be better", without working out what specifically has to change, usually get the same Month 3. Today's diagnosis is what tomorrow's 12-month plan is built on.

## Pull the raw numbers

- Total referrals received (warm, endorsed): ___

### Pipeline

- Active prospects, tier 1 (hot): ___
- Active prospects, tier 2 (warm): ___
- Active prospects, tier 3 (cold): ___
- Blue-ticked or stalled: ___

### Conversions

- Closed cases (signed): ___

- Close rate (closes divided by Fact-Finds): __%

### Content and digital

- Posts published: ___
- DM conversations that went somewhere: ___
- Average story viewers: ___
- Followers gained: ___

### Practice submissions (mark each done or missed)

- Week 1: 90-second intro recording
- Week 2: 3 intent-statement deliveries
- Week 3: 3 posts and 5 DM conversations
- Week 4: 30 outreaches and 5 appointments
- Week 5: 10 referral asks and 3 referrals
- Week 6: roleplays of all 4 DISC profiles
- Week 7: 3 pitches
- Week 8: 1 live pitch on camera
- Week 9: 5 objection drills

Write the real numbers in, especially if they're small. Inflated numbers lead to the wrong diagnosis and the wrong Month 3 plan. A 60-day FYC of $3K from few appointments needs a different fix from $3K earned off lots of appointments, and rounding up hides the difference.

## Compare with your Week-1 projections

Open your [Day 2](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/2) and Day 3 work. What did you project 60 days ago?

- Projected weekly appointments: ___, actual: ___
- Projected close rate: __%, actual: __%
- Projected case size: $___, actual: $___
- Projected 60-day FYC: $___, actual: $___

The gap between projection and actual is your data, and it usually falls into one of three patterns.

### Pattern 1: every projection was optimistic

New FCs end up here most often. You projected 7 appointments a week and got 3, a 40% close rate and got 20%, a $750 case size and got $500.

It's normal, because first-year projections tend to run high. Reset your expectations to what the market actually gave you, not what you hoped for at the start. With every metric short, you need a broader reset.

### Pattern 2: one metric is much weaker than the rest

Appointments are on target and Fact-Finds are fine, but few of them close, so close rate is the problem. Or appointments are low but you convert them well, which is a volume problem rather than a skill problem.

This is the best result the diagnosis can give you, because one broken link needs one fix, while weakness everywhere needs a rebuild.

### Pattern 3: your projections were too cautious

It's less common, but it happens. You projected low because you didn't know what was possible yet, and your actuals beat the plan. Project higher for Month 3, so a cautious baseline doesn't understate where you're heading.

## The CAR diagnostic over 60 days

Apply the CAR framework of calls, appointments and referrals (Days 3, [23](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/23) and [29](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/29)) to the whole 60-day window. The thresholds below are the team's rules of thumb.

| Link | Healthy | Bottleneck |
|---|---|---|
| Calls | Weekly calls at or above your target on average | Below target. Calls are then the bottleneck, whatever happens further down. |
| Appointments | 25% or more of calls become appointments | Under 20%: a script or targeting problem, where warm conversations aren't turning into bookings |
| Referrals | At least one warm referral per closed case | None at all: either you're not asking, or the ask is falling flat ([Day 27](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/27)) |

Pick the weakest link, and make it your Month 3 focus. Working on every lever at once moves each a little and none of them much. Once that link is healthy, another one becomes the weakest, and you move on to it.

## The close-rate diagnostic

If closing is the weak spot, find the step where it leaks.

1. Are Fact-Finds leading to qualified pitches? If most Fact-Finds never get to a pitch, you're not finding what the prospect cares about. Go back to [Day 38](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/38) (hot buttons II) and [Day 44](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/44) (silence).
2. Are pitches leading to commitment? If prospects say yes in the meeting and then don't follow through, the close may not suit their profile. Go back to [Day 41](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/41), where the four closes are matched to DISC profiles.
3. Are objections ending the conversation? If prospects object and the meeting stalls there, objection handling is the gap. Go back to [Days 52](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/52) and [53](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/53).
4. Do deferred decisions ever come back? If "let me think about it" turns into never hearing from them again, you're missing the follow-up close. Go back to Day 41 (the follow-up close) and [Day 17](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/17) (CRAB: Care, Reason, Alleviate, Book).

Find the step that leaks most, and put Month 3's practice there.

## The honest questions

Beyond the numbers, ask yourself four questions.

1. Did I actually do the practice submissions each week? Each miss on the list above is a skill you didn't practise, so reread the material from your weakest week.
2. Where is my time actually going? Split Week 10 so far into new business, relationships and systems. If systems takes more than 20%, the ceiling from [Day 58](/learning-track/next-60-days/day/58), you're probably hiding from the harder work.
3. Which prospects or clients do I dread? Usually it's someone whose DISC profile you're pitching against, or whose real concern you haven't found. Before you drop them, work out exactly what about them makes you uncomfortable, because that's what you need to practise.
4. If I'm honest, am I avoiding something? Most new-FC plateaus have a specific avoidance at the centre, like the prospect you never call, the referral ask you skip or the objection you dread. Moving meetings with uncomfortable prospects and rewriting the perfect Fact-Find form are both signs. Name it, and the next step usually gets obvious.

## Month 3: pick one thing

From all this diagnosis, pick one thing to change in Month 3. Good examples:

- "I'll make 40 calls a week, every week, in a protected Monday morning block."
- "I'll ask for a referral after every Fact-Find, using the FACT Method word for word until it's automatic."
- "I'll re-record my drills for 'too expensive' and 'let me think' until my handling is clean."
- "I'll hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, and track it daily."

Each one is specific and measurable, and within four weeks you'll be able to see whether you did it.

"I'll improve prospecting and closing and referrals and content" changes nothing. Edwin Locke and Gary Latham's review of 35 years of goal-setting research found that people given specific, hard goals do better than people told to "do your best".

## Do this today

1. Fill in every number above and mark each practice submission done or missed.
2. Write which gap pattern you're in and which link or step is weakest.
3. Write your one Month 3 commitment in a single sentence, with the day and time you'll do it. Tomorrow's 12-month plan starts from it.

- [Building a practically useful theory of goal setting and task motivation - Locke and Latham, American Psychologist (2002), via ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ654871) - specific, difficult goals outperform vague ones across 35 years of studies
- [Implementation intentions: strong effects of simple plans - Peter Gollwitzer, American Psychologist (1999)](https://www.socmot.uni-konstanz.de/publications/implementation-intentions-strong-effects-simple-plans) - deciding when and where you'll act makes follow-through much more likely

## Quiz

**Q1. The core reason to do the honest 60-day numerical review is:**
- A) To celebrate or mourn the numbers
- B) Month 3 planning needs to run on *data*, not *narrative*, because without honest diagnosis, the same patterns repeat (correct)
- C) To submit to your mentor
- D) It's required by compliance

**Why:** Without a numbers review, the Month 3 plan becomes *"it'll be better because I'll try harder,"* which gets the same result. The numbers show which link is broken (calls, appointments, closes or referrals), which step inside it leaks, and what to change, so the plan becomes a targeted fix instead of hopeful optimism.

**Q2. The CAR diagnostic's core discipline is:**
- A) Track all three every week
- B) Pick *one* weakest link and target the Month-3 intervention there, rather than spreading effort across all three (correct)
- C) Focus on Calls always
- D) Focus on Referrals always

**Why:** Working on every lever at once moves each a little and none of them much. The diagnostic makes you narrow down to the weakest link and fix that, and once it's healthy another link becomes the weakest, so you move on to it. One at a time gets you further, faster.

**Q3. A useful *one thing* commitment for Month 3 is:**
- A) *"I'll improve overall performance"*
- B) *"I'll work harder"*
- C) *"I'll hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, tracked daily"* (correct)
- D) *"I'll think about prospecting more"*

**Why:** Specific, measurable, visible within four weeks and tied to a known gap: that's what changes behaviour. A, B and D feel like commitments, but they're too vague to act on or measure. Pick one narrow behaviour you can test and practise it hard for a month.

**Q4. When pulling your 60-day numbers, the rule is:**
- A) Round up anything close to the target
- B) Literally fill them in, even if small, because small honest numbers beat large inflated ones because the diagnosis depends on them (correct)
- C) Only count successes
- D) Only count failures

**Why:** Inflated numbers lead to the wrong diagnosis, the wrong Month 3 plan and the wrong Month 6 results. Honest numbers, even ugly ones, point to the right fix. A 60-day FYC of $3K from few appointments calls for a different fix than $3K from lots of appointments, and rounding up hides the difference.

**Q5. Pattern 2 (one metric is much weaker than the others) is the "best-case" diagnosis because:**
- A) It means you're already succeeding
- B) A single broken link is much easier to fix than system-wide weakness: you target one intervention, not rebuild from scratch (correct)
- C) It always resolves itself
- D) It means the other links are compensating

**Why:** One weak link needs one fix, and weakness everywhere needs a rebuild. A result like *"appointments healthy, close rate low"* is something you can act on: Month 3 goes into closing work, meaning objection handling from Days 52 and 53, silence from Day 44, and the closes from Days 41 and 49 to 51. Pattern 1, where every metric fell short, needs a broader reset.

**Q6. The "one thing" commitment rule for Month 3 exists because:**
- A) More than one is illegal
- B) Narrow and deep beats broad and shallow, since trying to improve 5 things simultaneously produces small movement everywhere and meaningful movement nowhere (correct)
- C) It's easier to track
- D) Mentors require it

**Why:** Goal-setting research (Locke and Latham) finds that specific goals beat vague ones, and a single clear target is easier to plan around and check. *"Improve overall performance"* doesn't point to any behaviour. *"Hold 8 seconds of silence after every loaded Fact-Find question, tracked daily"* is one thing you can observe, and it feeds straight into your close rate. Narrowing down is what makes Month 3 different from Months 1 and 2.

**Q7. Which is a sign you're avoiding the real work?**
- A) Systems work (admin, CE, plans) is >20% of the week
- B) You keep moving meetings with specific uncomfortable prospects
- C) You're on your fifth rewrite of the perfect Fact-Find form
- D) All of the above (correct)

**Why:** Each is a version of the same thing: doing comfortable work like planning, rescheduling and polishing documents so you don't have to call hesitant prospects, face objections or ask for the close. When the avoided task keeps getting pushed to *"next week,"* the avoidance is running your week. Name it honestly, then put the uncomfortable work in a protected block before the comfortable work can spread.
