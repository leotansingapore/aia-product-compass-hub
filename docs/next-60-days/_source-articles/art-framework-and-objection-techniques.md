# ART Framework + Anti-Objection Techniques

> **Goal:** eliminate up to **90% of common objections** before the close, and convert the remaining 10% into conversations — not deadlocks.

This file is the **meta-layer** on top of [[sales-playbooks-objections]] (34-objection × 43-response library). The playbook gives you the *words*; this file gives you the *pattern*.

---

## 1. The ART Framework

> When an objection shows up, don't debate. **A → R → T.**

| Step | Move | Intent |
|---|---|---|
| **A** — Acknowledge | "I hear that." | Show you actually heard the concern. No defensiveness. |
| **R** — Relate | "I understand — you're not alone in thinking that." | Meet them as a human, not a salesperson. Lower their guard. |
| **T** — Throw it back | "…and that's *exactly why* we should meet." | Use their concern as the *reason* the meeting is valuable. |

### Worked example — "I already have an advisor"

> **A — Acknowledge:** *"That's great — you're clearly financially responsible and have someone looking after your interests. Many of the people I meet don't even have that."*
>
> **R — Relate:** *"Honestly, most of my best clients already had an advisor when we first met. What they discovered was the value of a **second perspective** — like getting a medical opinion on something important. Having one doctor is great. Two makes sure nothing gets missed."*
>
> **T — Throw it back:** *"And that's exactly why I'd love a short, no-obligation chat — just to check for blind spots. If there are none, you walk away with peace of mind. If there are, you're glad you made the call. Either way, you win."*

**Pattern signature:** validate → normalize → reframe-as-reason.

## 2. Pre-Framing — Eliminate Objections Before They Arise

> **The goal: pre-frame the components of the sales process so up to 90% of common objections never get raised.**

A pre-frame is a sentence you say *early* that defuses an objection *later*. Before the prospect can say "I need to think about it," you've already said, "…and at the end of this, if you need to think about it, take a week — I'd rather you decide right than decide fast."

### Common pre-frames

| Objection you're pre-empting | Pre-frame line (early in the meeting) |
|---|---|
| *"I need to think about it"* | *"Whatever we discuss today, there's no pressure to decide. My job is to give you clarity. You decide what to do with it."* |
| *"Let me check with my spouse"* | *"If you have a partner involved in financial decisions, I'd actually want them in our next meeting — let's plan that into the flow."* |
| *"It's too expensive"* | *"Everything I propose will fit inside a budget you control. We'll agree on a number before we look at any products."* |
| *"I already have an advisor"* | *"My goal today isn't to replace anyone — it's to offer a second perspective. What you do with it is up to you."* |
| *"I don't trust insurance companies"* | *"The industry has a reputation. Let me show you how I work, and you judge for yourself."* |

**Rule of thumb:** write the 5 most frequent objections *you* hear → pre-frame each one → script it into the first 10 minutes.

## 3. Asking for a "No"

> The classic flawed close: *"Are you open to meeting to learn more?"* → invites *"Let me think about it."*
>
> The Chris Voss flip: *"Would you be **against** me sharing this with you?"* → invites *"No, I'm not against it."*

### Why it works

People are **cognitively wired to avoid commitment-to-yes**. But they're comfortable saying *no* to a small, low-stakes question. By framing the ask as something to say *no* to, you route them through the *yes* as a side effect.

### Other "ask for a no" openers

- *"Would it be crazy to explore this briefly?"* → they'll say *no, not crazy*.
- *"Is it a terrible idea to schedule 20 minutes next week?"* → *not terrible*.
- *"Would you be opposed to looking at a quick outline?"* → *not opposed*.

**Contrast:** don't use "Would you be open to…" — that's asking for a yes.

## 4. Ghosting / MIA Recovery

> When a prospect has gone silent — **don't chase**. Create a safe space for conversation.

### The 4-step ghost-recovery SOP

1. **Acknowledge the delay without guilt-tripping.**
   > *"Hey [Name], I noticed we haven't connected in a while — hope everything's OK on your end."*

2. **Respect their reasons.** Even if you don't know them.
   > *"Totally understand if priorities have shifted or life got busy."*

3. **Offer a low-pressure path forward.** Give them a way back in without losing face.
   > *"Happy to pick up where we left off whenever you're ready, or if the timing's genuinely not right, I'd rather we close the conversation cleanly than leave it hanging."*

4. **Make the "worst case" a closure.** This lowers their defense — they'd rather re-engage than actively close.
   > *"Worst case, we both know where we stand. Best case, we find the 20 minutes to finish what we started."*

## 5. Positive Vocabulary

> **Negative framing plants negative emotions.** Your words cue the prospect's feelings.

| Use this | Not this |
|---|---|
| *"Do you have any **questions**?"* | *"Do you have any **concerns** or **worries**?"* |
| *"Let's look at what **matters most** to you."* | *"Let's talk about what you're **afraid** of losing."* |
| *"This plan **protects** your family."* | *"This plan is for if you **die** or get sick."* |
| *"Where would you like to **start**?"* | *"What are you **stuck** on?"* |

Before every client meeting, scan your deck/script for **concern, worry, afraid, stuck, problem, mistake, wrong**. Replace with **question, matter, protect, start, opportunity, approach, what**.

## How the 5 Techniques Fit Together

```
Pre-framing            → 90% of objections never arise
ART Framework          → the 10% that do arise → become conversations
Asking for a "No"      → re-routes indecisive prospects to forward motion
Ghosting Recovery      → re-opens the 5–20% who go silent post-meeting
Positive Vocabulary    → running default throughout
```
