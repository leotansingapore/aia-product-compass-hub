# Prospecting Training Workbook

> **Thesis:** Sales is not transaction — it is *"helping someone act upon a decision that is important and good for him/her."* Everything that follows (prospecting, rapport, teasers, referrals, closing) is engineered to protect that principle while actually producing revenue.

---

## Part 1 — The 8 Rules + Wisdom

1. **Ask more right questions, make less statements.**
2. Be a good person first, then focus on what you do. *(Focus on the person first, not your process.)**
3. **5% of breakthrough comes from attending class. 95% comes from implementing.**
4. Apply what you learn **immediately**.
5. Practice × 3. New skills need repetition. Be patient, have faith.
6. Don't memorize scripts — **internalize**, then use your own words. (Preserve the *steps*; express with your own voice.)
7. Upgraded skills need upgraded **time management**.
8. Follow what you learn to the "T." **Partial usage = partial breakthrough.**

**Related principles scattered through the workbook:**
- *"How much you change equals how much you gain. Life is very, very fair."*
- **Six months of structured growth beats eleven years of no growth.**
- *"Age is just a figure. It means nothing."*

## Part 2 — Redefining Sales

Lusi's definition: *"Sales is helping someone act upon a decision that is important and good for him/her."*

**Two parts to ethical selling:**
1. **There must be a decision / commitment** — without this, the advisor is just educating.
2. **Your ethics and professionalism** — only maximised when you believe the decision is genuinely good for the person.

**Overcoming the Pushy Myth:** *"A lot of times, pushy is approach, right? It's never about closing on the spot. Closing on the spot 没有错. In fact, if you do it well, you can do it tastefully, elegantly."*

**Client Experience beats script:** the retail-assistant example — prospects buy things they didn't plan to buy when the experience is pleasant and engaging.

## Part 3 — Mindset

### Abundance mindset
*"Leads really, really are abundant. A lot of times, it's due to our limited mindset."*

### Reasons Sales People Fail
1. No strong purpose
2. No discipline
3. No skills / proficiency
4. Resist change, comfort zone
5. Too much distraction

### BRILLIANT in Sales (12-item reflection checklist)
People Skills · Presence · Questions · Storytelling · Hot Buttons · Closing (狠心做好事) · Adaptability · Logic · Conviction · Special Forte · Emotional Management · Time Management

### S Profile Strengths & Weaknesses
S profiles read emotions well *but* overthink — assuming budget or risk-tolerance limits the client hasn't actually expressed.

**Visualization technique for S profiles:** *"Visualize the worst-case scenario if this planning is not implemented for this particular person."* Strengthens resolve to close without crossing into pushy.

### Long-term vs short-term focus
*"Please don't focus on the short-term stress and pain. Focus on a long-term good for the client."* Storytelling is the bridge that makes the long-term benefit tangible enough to override short-term pain.

## Part 4 — Lead Generation vs Prospecting

| | Lead Generation | Prospecting |
|---|---|---|
| Definition | Identifying and attracting potential customers | Qualifying leads; assessing who can convert |
| Metaphor | **Farming** — nurture the base | **Hunting** — actively engage |
| Nature | Strategy | Skill |
| Activities | Campaigns, content, events | Conversations, rapport, assessment |

### The 4 Types of Prospects

| Tier | % | Description | Strategy |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Definitely will buy** | 10% | Loyal / family — trust fully | Relationship maintenance, personalized |
| **Most likely will support** | 20% | Friends, relatives — inclined | Leverage personal connection, WOM |
| **Can be converted** | 60% | Anyone — with info + persuasion | Persuasive marketing, address objections, value-deliver |
| **Die-die will not buy** | 10% | No intent regardless | Avoid wasting resources |

### Sales Cycle Stages (7)
1. Prospecting · 2. Fact Finding to Qualify · 3. Appointment Making · 4. Presentation · 5. Overcoming Objections · 6. Closing · 7. Referrals

*"The more you do in the beginning, the less objections you will face at the end."* — Lusi

### Common excuses to quit
- *"Run out of leads"* → new sources
- *"Not comfortable with warm market"* → role-play, scripts
- *"Not good with strangers"* → networking groups
- *"Can't handle HNW"* → educate yourself on their needs

## Part 5 — 5 Steps to Build Rapport

1. **Eye contact & smile** — non-verbal warmth
2. **Pay compliments, not flattery** — observation-based. Also: receive compliments with a simple *"thank you"* — never contradict them
3. **Ask questions about them** — open-ended, hot-button-seeking, with the "Why" follow-up
4. **Listen attentively** — active listening, no interrupt
5. **Acknowledge** — paraphrase, clarify, sincere personalised compliment, words of empathy

### The Art of Acknowledging
- **Paraphrase** to confirm understanding (*"So it sounds like you're feeling a bit cautious…"*)
- **Ask clarifying questions** (*"Is the upcoming probation end affecting this decision?"*)
- **ICBT compliments** (Intent / Believability / Congruence / Timing)
- **Empathy words** (*"I completely understand how that feels…"*)

### Spotting hot buttons
- Do they **elaborate** on a topic? → hot button
- Do their **emotions change** (faster speech, enthusiasm, bright eyes)? → passion

## Part 6 — The Art of Teaser

**Definition:** intriguing information or questions that pique curiosity without revealing everything.

### Teaser Tip 1 — Lady's Skirt Theory
*"Long enough to cover the essentials; short enough to be interesting."*
- *"Many people overlook one critical aspect of retirement planning that could cost them thousands. Would you like to know what it is?"*
- *"There's a strategy some of my clients use to double their savings in 5 years — without risky investments. Care to find out?"*

### Teaser Tip 2 — Play "Hard to Get"
- Don't reveal intention too soon
- Know when to hold your horses — let *them* ask

### Teaser Tip 3 — Must invite curiosity or clarification
Hit their hot buttons so they feel the need to ask the follow-up. *"You mentioned you're struggling with cash flow during low-sales periods — I have a few insights that might help, but I need to understand your situation better first."*

### Traps to avoid
- **Task-oriented "what you do"** — prospects don't care about process, they care about benefit
- **Industry jargon / acronyms** — alienates
- **Generic / vague** — blends in with every other advisor
- **Too long** — loses punch

## Part 7 — Crafting "What Do You Do?" (5 profiles)

Tailored answers that hit the prospect's hot button:

| Profile | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Business owners** | *"I optimize inefficiencies in business owners' portfolios, helping them redirect more resources and time into their businesses and investments."* |
| **Young professionals** | *"I guide young professionals in setting up a structured financial system and automating their investments, so they can concentrate on investing in their skills and career."* |
| **C-suite / HNW** | *"I help optimize inefficiencies in high-net-worth clients' portfolios, ensuring they secure and grow their legacy for future generations."* |
| **Parents** | *"I work with parents to secure their family's risk protection and grow their wealth 15–20% year on year. Their children get a head start, and parents have more time for what matters most."* |
| **Pre-retirees** | *"I work with affluent individuals to help them secure their desired retirement lifestyle 5 to 10 years earlier than planned."* |

### Principles
- Be upfront but creative — not clever or cheesy
- Connect with their hot button
- Leverage track record (or passion, if new)
- Speak sincerely — never sound memorised

## Part 8 — What If They Keep Talking About Themselves?

**Strategy:** find common ground → interject smoothly → induce curiosity.

### Worked examples
**Travel →** *"I should probably extend my trip after my next company incentive trip to Switzerland."* → *"Oh, which company are you with?"*

**Business →** *"Many of my business owner clients face similar challenges. That's why they got me to customize a talent attraction and retention strategy…"* → *"Oh, what kind of strategy?"*

**Teaching →** *"A few of my clients were in the Ministry of Education before pursuing other paths after their bond."* → *"Oh, what kind of business do you run?"*

**Rules:** be relevant · build on what they say · hint, don't overtly change topic · empathy is key.

## Part 9 — Tailored Strategies Based on DISC

| Profile | Approach | Example opener |
|---|---|---|
| **I — High-energy / extroverted** | Genuine compliments, recognise strengths | *"I can tell you have a natural energy that brings people together…"* |
| **C — Analytical / detail-oriented** | Structured questions, acknowledge their methodology | *"I noticed you've done a thorough job streamlining your processes. Could you share how you approached it?"* |
| **S — Empathetic / supportive** | Questions about their role in supporting others | *"I can see you genuinely care about your team. How do you ensure everyone feels heard?"* |
| **D — Driven / decisive** | Concise, outcome-focused, confident | *"You're someone who values results. Let's discuss how to streamline your strategy to maximise returns in the shortest timeframe."* |

## Part 10 — Unconventional Cold Calling Script

**The "favour" opener** (disarming):
> *"Hi Mark, I really need to ask you a favour — please don't hang up right away. I've called the last 50 people, and every single one of them has hung up on me. I hope you'll be the kind soul to give me just one minute of your time. I promise I'm not here to sell you something you don't need, and if at any point you're not interested, you can hang up, and I'll still be grateful. Is that okay?"*

**Then transition to research-not-sales framing:**
> *"My team and I are currently trying to understand some of the critical areas in financial planning that professionals aged 30 to 45 are looking out for. Could you help me by answering a few quick questions?"*

**3 hot-button questions:**
1. *"What do you find most challenging about managing your finances?"*
2. *"When you think about your future, what financial goals or concerns come to mind first?"*
3. *"What would make you feel more confident about your retirement plans?"*

**Thank + non-intrusive follow-up offer:**
> *"Since you've been so helpful, I'd like to offer you our Redundancy Analysis / Retirement Confidence Index as a thank-you."*

## Part 11 — The FACT Method for Referrals (primary day-28 script)

**F**avour → **A**ngle → **C**onnect → **T**imeline

**Critical rule:** **always set a firm timeline.** Without one, referrals go cold.

### Angle 1 — Specific Needs (Retirement)
> *"Samantha, before you head out, I'd like to ask a quick favour. Do you happen to know someone who is getting closer to retirement, late 40s or 50s, and might be worried about sustaining their lifestyle post-retirement?… I specialise in helping people like them plan for retirement… Would it be okay if you could check in with them in the next 2 days? I'll follow up with you on Saturday."*

### Angle 2 — Demographics (Young Professionals)
> *"Tom… do you happen to know anyone in their 30s or 40s, a working professional who's just started a family and is thinking about setting up financial security?"*

### Angle 3 — Compliment-based (Personal Connection)
> *"Rachel… do you know anyone around your age, late 20s to early 30s, who has a really strong sense of responsibility and ambition?"*

### Angle 4 — Demographics (New Parents)
> *"Linda… do you happen to know any new parents in your circle, maybe people with young kids or expecting their first child?"*

**Pattern across all 4:** favour framing → specific angle that targets a defined segment → "connect us" ask → 2-day-follow-up + Saturday check-in timeline.

### Prepping clients with a sample referral text (page 7–8)

Coach prep script clients can send on your behalf — frames it as *adding a fresh perspective*, not replacing an existing advisor:

> *"Hey, I hope you're doing well! I wanted to share something that's been really beneficial for me recently. You know how we all have our financial advisors, right? Well, I used to have a couple of them too, but I met this amazing advisor, [Your Name], who really opened my eyes to some gaps in my coverage that my previous advisors hadn't addressed.*
>
> *I was sceptical at first, thinking I was already covered. But [Your Name] took the time to really understand my financial situation and my goals. They pointed out a few key areas I hadn't considered before.*
>
> *Even if you feel satisfied with your current advisor, it might be worth having a conversation with [Your Name]. They offer a fresh perspective and could help ensure you're on the right track… If you're interested, I'd be happy to connect you two."*

**Why it works:** acknowledges existing advisor, leads with gap-finding not replacement, frames meeting as *adding* a perspective.

## Part 12 — Client Experience Drives Referrals

**Winning referral formula = strong experience, not a clever script.**

### How to make clients your ambassadors
1. Don't be just competent — be **extraordinarily competent**
2. Make them **like you and trust you very much**
3. Be **generous** in all aspects — give and care
4. **Brilliant after-sales service**

### Extraordinary Competence — 3 versions on a hard case

When a client's insurance case is difficult, the advisor has three possible postures. Only one makes ambassadors:

| Version | Response | Client impact |
|---|---|---|
| **V1 — Easy out** | *"Won't go through, don't proceed."* | Client feels abandoned |
| **V2 — Optimistic shrug** | *"No harm applying, but don't expect much."* | Client feels uninspired |
| **V3 — The fighter** | *"I'll use all my experience and relationships to push this through. No promises, but I'll do my best."* | Client feels seen and defended |

> **Clients want advisors who will fight for them like family.** V3 is how ambassadors are made.

**Going the extra mile** (Melissa real-estate example): sambal chili for the family, toy for the daughter — gestures beyond the transaction deepen trust.

**Resourcefulness** (medical-specialist example): your network of competent professionals reflects on you. Refer only top-tier.

### Coaching clients for referrals
- Help them know **what to say** about you
- Articulate your strengths for them
- Repeated sales = trust; referrals = trust *very much*

### Mental blocks (Jonathan's story)
17-year advisor, avoided asking to preserve image, never made MDRT — until he reframed asking as *responsibility* to help more people. Hit MDRT multiple times after.

### Cultural reality (Singapore)
Conservative cultures don't refer proactively even when satisfied. Take control — don't rely on passive recommendation.

### Quality vs cold (Advisor B's failed system)
Collecting 20 names/client with no endorsement = 20 cold leads, not 20 referrals. True referrals carry personal endorsement; cold asks for anonymity.

### When direct asking doesn't work — events
Events lower the bar for passive clients. Inviting someone to an event is easier than formally recommending a service. Use this for shy ambassadors.

## Part 13 — PA Communication Script (Week-10 scaling)

**Introducing a PA without diluting trust:**
> *"Dear [Client], I want to introduce my Personal Assistant, [PA's Name], who will be helping manage day-to-day scheduling and communications. [PA] has been with me for [time] and is highly skilled and dedicated. They'll handle administrative tasks so I can focus even more on your personal strategies and financial goals. I'll continue to handle all critical and personal matters directly."*
