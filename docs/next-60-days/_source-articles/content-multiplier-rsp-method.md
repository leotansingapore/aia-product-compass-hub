# The Content Multiplier — 1 Framework, Multiple Pieces of Content

Source: David / KING, "Content Multiplier Method" handout, Day 1.

> **Know your audience → know their struggles → challenge their thinking through content → consistently.**

---

## Content Sources — Where to Pull From

| Source | Description |
|---|---|
| **SHARE** | From others' experience (articles, posts) |
| **REPORT** | From your own learning (book or training program) |
| **INSIGHT** | Created from your experimented knowledge |

## The R.S.P. Method

**R**elatable · **S**imple · **P**rovoking

### R — RELATABLE
Common understanding of what matters to your audience **right now**.

| Audience | What's important NOW |
|---|---|
| Students / new grads | Uncertain job market — can I find work? |
| Working singles | Long HDB wait when they get married |
| Married | Hyperinflation raising cost of living |
| Married with children | Covid variants and impact on kids |
| Retirees | Rising healthcare cost, virus protection |

### S — SIMPLE
Simple words. **Focus on one point** so a wider audience can grasp it.

| Current belief | Thought-provoking counter |
|---|---|
| "Crypto is a good investment, some made it big." | 85% of retail investors don't make money. |
| "Dental checkups aren't important." | Prolonged poor oral hygiene contributes to multiple medical conditions in the aged. |
| "I don't need a financial consultant to buy the right plans." | 60%+ of self-help Singaporeans have insufficient insurance coverage. |

### P — PROVOKING
Get them to relook their beliefs. Triggered when you:

1. **Call out something they don't think about but should.**
   *"85% of retail investors lose money in the stock market."*
2. **Challenge their current belief about how success is achieved.**
   *"Is buying crypto really the fastest way to attain financial success?"*
3. **Highlight the consequence of ignoring it.**
   *"More than 50% of Singaporeans lose over half their life savings in crypto."*

## Content Structures — 3 Formats from One RSP

### Format 1 — ONE-LINER
**Structure:** share the one-liner → what it means to you in 2 sentences.

**To craft a powerful one-liner, know:**
| What's important to audience NOW? | What are they doing on their own? | One-liner to challenge their thinking |
|---|---|---|
| Married with kids: rising cost of living | Reduce spending, save more in bank | **Quote:** "The high cost of living is minor compared to the cost of high living." — Mardy Grothe<br>**Question:** "Did you know you could spend on what you want and still have enough for your family?"<br>**Statement:** "The biscuit-tin syndrome — why the sense of security can be your worst friend." |

**Example:**
> *"Did you know you could spend on what you want and still have enough for your retirement?*
>
> *I grew up with the mentality that I never had enough. It's demoralizing — but thankfully there's a way out."*

### Format 2 — TIPS
**Structure:** one-liner → brief statement of what it means → 3 tips to overcome.

**Example:**
> **One-liner:** *"Did you know you could spend on what you want and still have enough for retirement?"*
>
> **Brief:** Many families I've helped think they must cut back on lifestyle to save more. Logical, but compromising on life's pleasures is demoralizing. Here are 3 tips:
>
> **Tip 1:** Coins seem small, but accumulated they add up. Set them aside daily; use once a quarter.
> **Tip 2:** Set a realistic monthly budget to pamper the family on a nice meal.
> **Tip 3:** Make savings work harder through investment vehicles.

### Format 3 — STORY
**Structure:** one-liner → share a negative experience (you or someone else) → consequence → statement of possibility → 3 tips → closing advice.

**Example:**
> **One-liner:** *"Did you know you could spend on what you want and still have enough for retirement?"*
>
> **SHARE:** *"We were a family of 6; my dad was sole breadwinner. We spent less to accommodate everyone. Stomachs full, but we felt demoralized — we couldn't do what other families did like nicer meals or the arcade."*
>
> **CONSEQUENCE:** *"Frustrated, we fought between ourselves, and saw our parents fight too."*
>
> **POSSIBILITY:** *"As a consultant for 5 years, I've helped many families shift how they manage finances so they can still enjoy life together. Here's how:"*
>
> **Tip 1 / 2 / 3** (same as Tips format)
>
> **CLOSING:** *"Time with family matters. With proper financial management, your experience doesn't have to be like mine."*

## The Multiplier Logic

One RSP → three pieces of content:
- **One-Liner** post (fast, high-frequency)
- **Tips** post (tactical value)
- **Story** post (emotional depth, testimonial-adjacent)

Same idea, three formats, three audience segments reached.

## Reflection prompt

Pick one audience from the RSP table. Write one Simple + Provoking one-liner. Multiply it into a Tips post and a Story post.

**Teaching angle:** Advisors don't suffer from lack of ideas; they suffer from lack of reuse. RSP forces the discipline; Multiplier forces the output. RSP = feed-post *production method*; this is distinct from Eric Feng's 4 content types (Useful / Funny / Relatable / Inspiring), which is a *category taxonomy* — see [[instagram-selling-secrets-eric-feng]].
