# DISC for FCs — Identifying & Adapting to the 4 Personality Styles

> **DISC is a tool for dialogue, not diagnosis.** It measures tendencies and preferences — *not* intelligence, aptitude, or values. Your goal is to improve **communication + cooperation** with each prospect, not to label them.

---

## The 4 Core Styles + 4 Blends

| Style | Nickname | Oriented | Pace |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D** — Dominance | The Winner | Task | Fast |
| **i** — Influence | The Enthusiast | People | Fast |
| **S** — Steadiness | The Peacekeeper | People | Slow |
| **C** — Conscientiousness | The Analyst | Task | Slow |

Most people are a **blend**: Di/iD, iS/Si, SC/CS, CD/DC. Read the dominant letter first; treat the second as a modifier.

---

## The 2-Step Identification Method

### Step 1 — Above or Below "The Up/Down Line"?

Ask yourself these 4 questions about the prospect:

| Signal | **Above the line** (D or i) | **Below the line** (S or C) |
|---|---|---|
| Comfortable with eye contact? | Yes | Avoids / brief |
| Energising to be around? | Yes — feels like being plugged in | Less energising / calm |
| Pace of speech & movement? | Fast | Slower, measured |
| Forthcoming with information? | Yes, volunteers info | More reserved, shorter answers |

### Step 2 — Task or People Oriented? ("The Left/Right Line")

Ask the prospect: **"What do you do for a living?"** — then *"Why did you choose to be a ___?"*

Judge their **intention**, not their title.

**Example — a tuition teacher:**
- **Task-oriented why:** *"I want to help my students hit A+ grades."* → **Task** (right side — D or C)
- **People-oriented why:** *"Being a teacher lets me make a real difference in students' lives."* → **People** (left side — i or S)

### Combine the 2 steps

```
              Above the line (fast, energetic)
                         |
   Task-oriented    D    |    i    People-oriented
   -----------------+--------+-----------------
                    C    |    S
                         |
              Below the line (slow, reserved)
```

## Per-Style Pitch Adjustments

### D — Dominance ("The Winner")

**What they care about:** *results*, *efficiency*, *solving the problem fast*.

**How to pitch:**
- Lead with their goals and objectives
- Show you understand the problem
- Present the fastest, most efficient solution
- Use **data + evidence**
- Be concise — no long stories

**D traits:** dynamic, inquisitive, persuasive, restless, entrepreneurial, vocal, enthusiastic. **Goal:** quick action, new opportunities. **Fear:** loss of power or status.

**DC (hybrid):** diligent, tough-minded, creative, critical, cool, focused. **Goal:** independence, personal accomplishment. **Fear:** failure to meet their own standards.

### i — Influence ("The Enthusiast")

**What they care about:** *people*, *stories*, *being part of something exciting*.

**How to pitch:**
- Tell them **who your customers are** — especially people like them
- Don't overwhelm with numbers → **focus on stories**
- Enthusiasm, energy, social proof
- Invite them into a community / peer group

**i traits:** warm, persuasive, adventurous, open, influential, charismatic. **Goal:** popularity, exciting breakthroughs, prestige. **Fear:** fixed environments, loss of approval.

**iS (hybrid):** warm, friendly, accepting, collaborative, empathetic, cheerful. **Goal:** friendship. **Fear:** pressuring others, being disliked.

### S — Steadiness ("The Peacekeeper")

**What they care about:** *relationships*, *feeling safe*, *no sudden change*.

**How to pitch:**
- **Get to know the person first** — show you care
- Go slow. Don't rush to close.
- Emphasise long-term partnership, stability, "we'll be here for you"
- Avoid high-pressure language or deadlines

**S traits:** collaborative, well-liked, team-spirited, encouraging, conflict-averse, approachable, compassionate. **Goal:** acceptance, close relationships. **Fear:** being forced to pressure others.

**SC (hybrid):** stable, consistent, predictable, patient, reliable, soft-spoken. **Goal:** calm environment, steady progress. **Fear:** time pressure, uncertainty, chaos.

### C — Conscientiousness ("The Analyst")

**What they care about:** *making the right decision* based on the *right criteria*.

**How to pitch:**
- **Ask them** for the criteria *they* use to judge a good product
- Use their criteria to structure your presentation (creates alignment)
- Use data; testimonials are less important
- **Tell them the product's limitations** — provides assurance + shows honesty
- No hype

**C traits:** cautious, reflective, orderly, even-tempered, precise, well-prepared, patient, self-controlled. **Goal:** stability, reliable outcomes. **Fear:** emotionally charged situations, ambiguity.

**CD (hybrid):** skeptical, determined, questioning, critical thinker, objective, systematic, high standards. **Goal:** efficient results, rational decisions. **Fear:** failure, lack of control.

## Quick Cheat Sheet

| If prospect is… | Lead with… | Avoid… |
|---|---|---|
| **D** | Goals, speed, results, data | Long stories, soft language, slow pace |
| **i** | Stories, peers, excitement | Walls of numbers, cold efficiency |
| **S** | Rapport, safety, relationship | High pressure, surprise deadlines |
| **C** | Their criteria, data, limitations | Hype, testimonials as main proof |

---

## Self-DISC Awareness

*"You're a blend."* You might be a strong D, but methodical financial work still demands a C-like mode when reviewing client data. Each style can **flex** into another — it just takes effort.

> **DISC is descriptive, not prescriptive.** No style is better than another. Your job as FC: read your prospect's default style → adapt yours.
