# The 4 Types of Referrals — Structure, Timing, Yield

> **The referral economy.** A self-regenerating sales cycle depends on 4 distinct referral moments — not one. Each has its own timing, script, and expected yield. Miss any of the 4 and you leave pipeline on the table.

> ⚠️ **Draft — needs validation.** The 4-type taxonomy is referenced in `strategic-target-list-abc-123` but only 1 type is fully enumerated in ingested sources. This file reconstructs the remaining 3 from `appointment-setting` playbook, `social-proof-scripts-and-sales-cycle`, and common practice. Validate with an MDRT+ mentor before publishing.

## Overview Matrix

| # | Type | When to ask | Who they introduce | Expected yield |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Active Referral** | End of first Fact-Find (FF) appointment | People like the prospect (friends, colleagues) | ~3 names per successful ask, 70% success rate after well-run FF |
| 2 | **Lightbulb Referral** | During the FF — when the prospect realises a gap | People *with the same problem* the prospect just recognised | 1–2 highly-qualified names (emotionally resonant) |
| 3 | **Trust Referral** | At/after the close (policy signed) | Their "inner circle" — family, closest friends | 3–5 names, high trust transfer |
| 4 | **Advocacy Referral** | During servicing (check-ins, claims, reviews) | Broad network — testimonial-driven, social-proof multiplier | Unbounded — can trigger 5–20+ over months |

## 1. Active Referral — End of First Fact-Find

**Timing:** After you've delivered value in the FF, before you wrap up. The prospect is in a **"this was useful"** state.

**Script (from `social-proof-scripts-and-sales-cycle.md`):**
> *"While I'm doing this admin, here's what everyone does without fail — they write down 10 names of people they like, trust, and want to help. Platoon mates, BMT mates, schoolmates, poly mates. Even people you're not that close to — just spam it. 10 here, 10 here, 10 here. Most people write 15, 20, even 25. So while I'm doing this for you, you can also write 10+ names, and I'll do my best to help them."*

**Why it works:**
- **Consensus:** *"Everyone does this."*
- **Reciprocity:** *"While I'm doing this for you."*
- **Time arbitrage:** admin minutes put to productive use.
- **Normalisation:** *"15, 20, 25"* anchors high expectations.

**Yield math (Chrisan's data):** 70% success rate × 3 average referrals × 40% meetup ratio = ~0.84 appointments per FF. At 6 FFs/week, that's ~5 extra appointments/week from active referrals alone.

---

## 2. Lightbulb Referral — Mid-Discovery

**Timing:** During the FF, the moment a prospect realises something — *"Oh, I hadn't thought about that."* Their emotional state is heightened; they immediately think of others with the same gap.

**Trigger signals:**
- *"Wait, that's actually a problem I have…"*
- *"My [friend/spouse/parent] needs to hear this."*
- They go quiet and look thoughtful.

**Script (in-the-moment, never forced):**
> *"Actually — while this is fresh in your mind — is there anyone you know who might be in a similar spot? Not to pitch them now, but I'd rather have them on my radar so when they're ready, I'm not a stranger."*

**Why it works:** the prospect is transferring *their own insight* to a named person — not generating leads under pressure. The referral is pre-warmed with shared context.

**Yield:** fewer names (1–2) but **dramatically higher conversion** because the introduction carries the emotional resonance of the moment.

---

## 3. Trust Referral — At/After the Close

**Timing:** When they sign the first policy. The relationship has shifted — you're now their advisor, not a salesperson.

**Script:**
> *"Congrats — you're the kind of client I'd love to work with more of. Who in your life do you care most about financially? Parents, partner, siblings, a close friend building their career? I'm not asking to pitch them — I'm asking because once you're a client, my standing offer is to meet anyone you refer at a priority slot. Who comes to mind?"*

**Why it works:**
- **Commitment + consistency:** they just publicly committed to trusting you. Refusing a referral now would contradict that.
- **Priority framing:** positions referrals as a *benefit they get*, not a favour they give.
- **Emotional framing** ("who do you care most about") beats transactional framing ("do you know anyone who might buy").

**Yield:** 3–5 names. Highest trust transfer of the 4 types — these referrals meet you warm.

---

## 4. Advocacy Referral — During Servicing

**Timing:** Months/years after the close. Client check-in, policy review, claims handled, birthday message, market update — any moment of re-engagement.

**The advocacy mechanic:** instead of *asking* for referrals, you **create situations where clients naturally advocate** for you.

**Tactics:**
- **Testimonial collection** — after a positive experience (claims processed, policy paid out, goal achieved), ask for a 60-second WhatsApp video. Share to IG stories (with permission).
- **Referral campaigns** — *"I'm doing free 30-min financial check-ups in October for anyone my clients refer. First 10 slots only."*
- **Community events** — dinners, webinars, birthday parties. Ask clients to bring a plus-one.
- **Value moments** — when you email a market update, add: *"Feel free to forward to anyone who'd find this useful."*

**Yield:** unbounded. An advocacy flywheel is what turns a 1-year business into a 10-year business. See `social-proof-scripts-and-sales-cycle.md` §CAR — Referrals is 1 of the 3 bottlenecks, and Advocacy is the highest-leverage referral type long-term.
