## 1\. CORE PRESENTATION PRINCIPLE

**Concept drawing FIRST, presenter tool SECOND.**

-   Never open slides/iPulse immediately — it overwhelms the client
    
-   Draw the concept on paper/whiteboard first to build understanding
    
-   Presenter tools (iPulse, iPoS) are **tools for you**, not for the client to read
    
-   Splashing everything at once = cognitive overload = lost prospect

## 2\. THE RETIREMENT OPENING (Concept Drawing #1)

**Purpose:** Create urgency and anchor the retirement number early

### Step-by-step flow:

**Step 1 — Set the scene**

-   "The reason we're here today is to plan for your future."
    
-   Acknowledge near-term goals: marriage, kids, renovation, house
    
-   Then pivot: *"But the most expensive thing you'll ever buy isn't your house — it's your retirement."*
    

**Step 2 — Anchor their desired passive income**

-   Ask: *"What do you think is a good monthly passive income for retirement?"*
    
-   Common answer: ~$3,000/month
    

**Step 3 — Introduce inflation**

-   "Do you think $3K today will still be $3K in 40 years?"
    
-   Long-term inflation rate: **2.5% per annum**
    
-   Use the **Retirement Calculator** inside Total Wealth Concept (TWC) to show the inflated figure
    
-   Result: **$3K today → ~$10,000/month needed at retirement**
    

> 💡 *Shock factor: let the number land before moving on*

**Step 4 — Get them to calculate**

-   "Take out your phone. What is $10,000 × 12 months × 20 years?"
    
-   Answer: **$2.4 million**
    
-   Follow up: "How much do you need to save per month to reach that?"
    
-   $2.4M ÷ 40 years ÷ 12 months = **~$5,000–$5,400/month savings needed**
    

**Step 5 — Reveal the investing solution**

-   "Is saving $5,000/month realistic for you right now?"
    
-   Most say no → *"With a proper investing plan, we can reduce this to as low as $1,000/month."*
    
-   Then hit the procrastination point: starting at 25 vs. 45 — cost difference is dramatic
    

**Key message:** *Start early. Be consistent. Don't stop-start.*

## 3\. PWV Staircase Diagram

**Purpose:** Show how the portfolio is built in tranches and how dividends compound layer by layer.

**Example: $500K Portfolio, Built Over 5 Years in $100K Tranches**

```
Year 1: Put in $100K → Dividends = $500/mth (6% of $100K = $6K/yr)
Year 2: Put in $100K → Dividends = $1,000/mth (2 layers × $500)
Year 3: Put in $100K → Dividends = $1,500/mth (3 layers × $500)
Year 4: Put in $100K → Dividends = $2,000/mth
Year 5: Put in $100K → Dividends = $2,500/mth

Total Portfolio: $500,000 → ~$30,000/yr → ~$2,500/mth
```

**Key Features to Explain at This Stage:**

1.  **Capital Guarantee on Death** — No matter what happens to fund value, the $500K is guaranteed upon death. Value can never fall below original capital at point of claim.
    
2.  **Secondary Insured Feature** — Client can nominate a beneficiary (e.g., child) to inherit the entire $500K portfolio — not just cash, but an *active income-generating portfolio*.
    
    -   Secondary insured inherits the portfolio → continues receiving $2,500/mth dividends for life.
        
    -   Upon secondary insured's death → capital guarantee is then triggered.
        
3.  **Two Lines of Defense:** Pass on the portfolio first (secondary insured), then pass on the capital (capital guarantee). Maximises multi-generational value.
    

**Engagement Question to Ask Client:**

> "If you're 50 now and live until 90 — which do you think is more, the $500K capital or the total dividends you'd receive?"

*Walk them through the math:*

-   90 − 55 = 35 years of dividends
    
-   35 × $30,000 = ~$1,050,000 in dividends
    
-   Dividends **exceed** the capital. The capital is preserved on top of that.

---

## 3\. ILLUSTRATIVE CALCULATION (Age 58, RA = $250K)

**Step 1 — Project RA balance to age 65:**

-   Current RA: $250,000
    
-   Grown at ~4% p.a. over 7 years → projected balance ≈ **$328,983**
    

**Step 2 — Monthly CPF Life payout from age 65:**

-   Estimated monthly payout ≈ **$1,772/month**
    

**Step 3 — How long does the capital last?**

-   $328,983 ÷ $1,772 = ~185 months
    
-   185 ÷ 12 = **~15.4 years**
    
-   Capital is depleted at approximately **age 80–81**
    

---

## 4\. THE THREE PROBLEMS WITH CPF LIFE AFTER AGE 81

Once the RA is depleted (around age 81), three issues arise:

1.  **Capital/bequest = $0** — Nothing is left to pass on
    
2.  **CPF Life payouts cease** — Income stops when RA hits zero
    
3.  **RA is illiquid** — You cannot withdraw the lump sum; it is locked in
    

> **Additional constraint:** CPF Life payouts are **fixed** — you cannot increase them if your expenses go up.

### Key Insight

> Doubling your capital **does not double your payout.** You get approximately **1.9x the payout for 2x the capital** — this is a capital inefficiency.

Additionally:

-   The extra capital is still **illiquid**
    
-   Bequest **still goes to zero** at ~81
    
-   No additional flexibility is gained

## 7\. PRESENTATION FRAMEWORK

When drawing this out for a client, use a two-column comparison:

```
CPF Life (Anchor)          |    PWV / Dividend Plan (Alternative)
- Fixed payout             |    - Flexible payout
- Illiquid                 |    - Liquid after 8 years
- Bequest = $0 at 81       |    - Capital preserved
- No growth after 65       |    - Continues to grow
```

**Positioning tip:** Present CPF Life as the **anchor/baseline**, then introduce the dividend plan as an **enhancement or alternative** — not a replacement.

---

## 8\. KEY TALKING POINTS TO USE WITH PROSPECTS

-   *"After 65, your RA stops earning interest. It's purely being drawn down."*
    
-   *"At current payouts, the capital runs out around age 81. What happens after that?"*
    
-   *"Even if you double the amount you put in, your monthly payout doesn't double — it's about 1.9x."*
    
-   *"With PWV, the bequest doesn't go to zero — your capital is guaranteed. And your payouts can grow."*
    
-   *"CPF Life alone may only give you $1,700–$2,000/month. If you need $6,000/month, we need to build a plan around that gap."*
    

---

## 9\. SUMMARY

CPF Life is a **foundation**, not a complete retirement plan. Use the RA depletion timeline and the capital efficiency analysis to help prospects understand its limitations — then introduce PWV (or a comparable dividend plan) as a solution that addresses liquidity, bequest, and income flexibility gaps.
