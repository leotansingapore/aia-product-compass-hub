## What is GoalsMapper?

GoalsMapper is AIA's award-winning financial planning software that allows clients to map out their financial goals and make important life decisions easily and confidently. It transforms complex financial planning into a visual, interactive experience with just a few simple clicks.

**Key value proposition:** Clients can *visualize* how their life will look in the future — making abstract financial concepts tangible and real.

---

## Two Core Use Cases

### 1. Retirement Planning

- Helps clients determine precisely how much they need to set aside for retirement
- Shows whether their savings can last throughout their retirement years
- **Example scenario:** Client wants to retire at age 60 but isn't sure if funds will be sufficient → GoalsMapper calculates the exact amount needed and projects how savings sustain them through their golden years

### 2. Protection / Insurance Gap Analysis

- Prepares clients for life's unexpected crises (e.g., disability)
- Identifies the **exact shortfall** in insurance coverage
- Evaluates whether existing policies are sufficient to safeguard the client and their family
- Creates urgency: "Cover those gaps while you are still healthy — so why wait?"

---

## Key Selling Points to Emphasize with Clients

1. **Simplicity** — "Just a few simple clicks" — removes the intimidation factor from financial planning
2. **Visualization** — Clients can *see* their future, not just hear about numbers
3. **Precision** — Calculates exact figures (retirement needs, coverage shortfalls) rather than vague estimates
4. **Confidence** — Empowers clients to make life decisions "easily and confidently"
5. **Goal-centric approach** — Positions the advisor as someone who places the client's goals, aspirations, and concerns as the top priority

---

## Advisor Application Notes

- **Use GoalsMapper as a consultative tool, not a sales pitch.** Let the software do the heavy lifting — when clients see their own numbers and gaps, the need becomes self-evident.
- **Retirement planning opener:** Ask the client their desired retirement age, then let GoalsMapper reveal whether they're on track. The gap speaks for itself.
- **Protection planning opener:** Ask "What would happen to your family if disability struck tomorrow?" then use GoalsMapper to show the exact coverage shortfall.
- **Urgency through data, not pressure:** The tool naturally creates urgency by showing shortfalls in black and white — no need for high-pressure tactics.
- **Positioning:** Frame yourself as a Financial Consultant who uses technology to put the client's goals first, not as someone pushing products.

---

## Key Takeaway

GoalsMapper shifts the conversation from "What product should I buy?" to "What does my financial future look like, and what gaps do I need to close?" — this is the foundation of consultative, relationship-first financial planning.

# Lecture Notes

## Overview

Ghost Mapper is a financial planning tool used to increase advisor credibility and deepen client conversations. It allows you to visually map out a client's financial journey, create problem awareness, and demonstrate the value of insurance and investment solutions.

---

## 1. Starting the Conversation: Life Goals Planning

After discussing existing policies, transition into Ghost Mapper to explore the client's life goals in greater depth.

**Example milestones to map out:**
- **Marriage** – Ask when they plan to get married and show the estimated costs.
- **Property purchase** – e.g., $450K property → 20% down payment = ~$90,000. Highlight that OA funds can be used for the down payment and monthly instalments. This helps allay fears about large cash outflows.
- **Household expenses** – Input ongoing costs associated with the home.
- **Children** – Map out child-bearing expenses, monthly costs, school fees, and timing (e.g., second child at age 42).

**Key tip:** This is a great opportunity to **bring in the partner**. The client can go back and say, *"My financial planner can do this planning together with us,"* which helps you capture both parties as clients.

---

## 2. Visualising the Financial Timeline

Ghost Mapper generates a visual projection of the client's finances over time:

- As the client ages, **income rises** and **cash savings grow**.
- At retirement (e.g., age 55), income stops and **cash savings begin declining**.
- Without any life events, everything looks fine on paper.
- Once you layer in real milestones (marriage, property, children), the picture changes — cash savings drop significantly. For example, at age 38 when buying property, the client may hit a **temporary deficit**.
- Without planning, the client **runs out of cash around age 59**.

This is where problem creation begins.

---

## 3. Problem Creation: Critical Illness Scenarios

Layer in health risks to dramatise the financial gap:

- **Early-stage critical illness at age 42** – $1,000/month in medical costs + 5 years of lost income → cash savings drop to zero.
- **Progression to major-stage CI at age 52** – Another 5 years of income loss → financial catastrophe.

**Key message to the client:** *"Things can happen more than once. Without proper planning, the consequences are severe."*

This naturally transitions the conversation into the need for **insurance protection**.

---

## 4. Solution 1: Investment Planning (Retirement Shortfall)

To address the retirement shortfall:

- Input an investment plan — e.g., unit trust growing at 7%, contributing $1,000/month from age 32 to 55.
- By age 55, the projected value is approximately **$735K**.
- The client can begin drawing down from age 55 for retirement income.

**Result:** Without an investment plan, the client runs out of cash around age 63. With the plan and drawdown starting at 55, the shortfall is **significantly reduced**, potentially lasting until age 90.

---

## 5. Solution 2: Multi-Stage Critical Illness Coverage

Even with proper retirement planning, a lack of CI coverage still creates vulnerability.

- Show the scenario: CI events at 42 and 52 without coverage → financial catastrophe.
- **Solution:** Recommend a whole life plan with multi-stage CI benefits.
  - e.g., $200K for early CI + $200K for major CI (may need to increase to ~$500K to fully close the gap).

**Toggle between "without insurance" and "with insurance"** to visually demonstrate the impact of protection.

---

## Key Takeaways for Advisors

1. **Use Ghost Mapper to deepen conversations** — go beyond product features and into real life planning.
2. **Create problems visually** before presenting solutions — let the client see the gap themselves.
3. **Layer scenarios progressively** — start with a rosy picture, add life events, then add health risks.
4. **Solve with two pillars:** investment planning (for retirement) and insurance (for protection against illness/income loss).
5. **Involve the partner** — use the planning tool as a reason to bring in the spouse for joint planning.
6. **Show, don't tell** — toggle between "with" and "without" scenarios to make the value of your recommendations undeniable.

## Purpose of This Segment

This is the **"why $1,000/month"** justification — working backwards from a retirement goal to derive the monthly investment figure. It anchors the recommendation in the client's own numbers rather than an arbitrary product premium.

---

## Step-by-Step Retirement Goal Calculation

### 1. Current Expenses → Future Expenses (Inflation Adjustment)

- Current monthly expenses: **$2K/month**
- Expected to rise to **$3K/month** with kids
- Apply **3% annual inflation** over ~39 years (age 26 → 65)
- $3K/month inflates to approximately **$9K/month** at retirement

### 2. Total Retirement Fund Needed

- $9K/month × 12 months × 20 years of retirement = **~$2 million**
- This becomes the **target retirement goal**

### 3. The "Saving Only" Problem

- $2M ÷ 40 years ÷ 12 months = **~$4,167/month** in pure savings needed
- **Key message:** *"That's going to be quite tough"* — this is the pain point that justifies investing over saving

### 4. The Investment Solution

- By investing **$1K/month (~$970)** starting at age 26, compound growth does the heavy lifting
- Projected value at 65: **~$2.2M** (without bonuses) — exceeds the $2M target
- This is how the $1K/month recommendation was derived — it's not arbitrary, it's mathematically anchored

---

## The Cost of Delay

| Start Age | Monthly Investment | Value at 65 |
|---|---|---|
| **26 (now)** | $1,000 | ~$2.2M |
| **35 (after expenses settle)** | $1,000 | ~$1.0M |

- **Starting 9 years later cuts the outcome roughly in half**
- This is the single most powerful urgency point — no pressure tactics needed, just math

---

## Scaling Up (Optional)

| Monthly Amount | Annual Amount | Value at 65 |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000 | $12,000 | ~$2.2M |
| $1,500 | $18,000 | Higher (shown earlier as ~$5.45M net worth) |
| $1,600 | $20,000 (using bonus) | ~$3M+ |

- $1,600/month essentially equals using the annual bonus to fund the investment
- **Framing:** *"You're using basically your bonus to pay for this"* — makes it feel like money the client doesn't currently "see" anyway

---

## Guardrails Reinforced

- **Maximum recommended budget: $29K/year** (already established from surplus analysis)
- Even adding $30K of investments, the client can still cover near-term expenses (property, marriage, etc.)
- Advisor asks client to decide their **comfort level** before next meeting — empowers the client rather than pressuring

---

## Key Presentation Techniques

**1. Reverse-engineer the goal** — Start with the retirement number, work backwards to the monthly figure. The client sees *their own need*, not a product pitch.

**2. Inflation as a wake-up call** — $3K becoming $9K is visceral. It makes the retirement gap feel real and urgent without being pushy.

**3. "Saving vs. Investing" contrast** — $4K/month saving vs. $1K/month investing to reach the same goal. This single comparison justifies the entire recommendation.

**4. Cost of delay** — $2.2M vs. $1M by waiting 9 years. Simple, visual, undeniable. No hard sell needed.

**5. Bonus money framing** — Positioning the $1,600/month option as "just your bonus" reframes it as money the client doesn't rely on for daily life.

**6. Client autonomy** — *"Let me know your budget"* and *"this is optional"* — the advisor presents options and lets the client choose within a safe range, building trust rather than applying pressure.

---

## Coaching Takeaway

This segment demonstrates how to make the **product recommendation feel inevitable** — not because of sales pressure, but because the client's own numbers demand it. The formula is:

> **Future need (inflation-adjusted) → Savings gap → Investment solution → Cost of delay → Client decides the amount**

When the math tells the story, the advisor doesn't have to sell.

1. **Compliance risk:** Showing real client portfolios (even anonymized) with specific dollar amounts could be problematic — use hypothetical or fund-level performance instead

3. **"No rush, but..."** — The urgency framing is slightly contradictory; be more direct: *"The promotion is available until 31st July — I'd love to help you lock it in if you're comfortable"*
4. **Inflation math could be cleaner** — Walk through the $3K → $9K step more slowly with a visual rather than rattling through the calculation verbally

# Lecture Notes

## Overview

This is a short process walkthrough showing how to use meeting recordings + AI (ChatGPT) + GoalsMapper to generate a professional client financial report quickly after a meeting. This is the **backend workflow** — what happens after the client leaves.

---

## Step-by-Step Workflow

### Step 1: Download the Meeting Recording & Transcript
- Record the client meeting (video/audio)
- Download the video file
- Download the **transcript as a Word document** (most recording tools like Zoom, Tactiq, or Lark offer this)

### Step 2: Feed Transcript into ChatGPT with Prompt Questions
Copy the full meeting transcript into ChatGPT along with specific questions to generate the financial report content.

**Example prompt structure:**
> *"Take the above meeting transcript and answer these questions for my financial report to send to the client:"*

**Suggested report questions:**
1. What are the client's **main priorities and key concerns** that need to be addressed immediately?
2. What are the client's **financial gaps, problems, and challenges** identified from the meeting?
3. Explain the **suggested recommendations** to help the client achieve their financial goals.
4. Are there any **concerns that can only be addressed in the future** due to constraints or limitations?

## Purpose

This is a **daily operational task** — not a one-time exercise.

## 1. The Verification Workflow

### Source of Truth: iSmart

- iSmart is the **true source of information** for all policy data.
- Every day, check iSmart for any updates related to upcoming meetings that week.
- Cross-reference what has been entered (e.g., by a team member like Elijah) against what iSmart shows.
- Any discrepancies must be corrected in the Google Docs policy summary **before** the meeting.

### Daily Routine:
1. Log in to iSmart (request login credentials if needed)
2. Check for new plans or updates related to this week's meetings
3. Verify Elijah's (or any team member's) data entry against iSmart
4. Update the policy summary in Google Docs accordingly
5. Flag and correct any errors

---

## 2. What to Check Per Policy

For every policy in the client's summary, verify the following:

### A. Policy Status
- Is the policy **in force** or not?
- Look for statuses like **"Extended Term Insurance"** — this means the policy is **no longer active**. It should be flagged or removed from the active summary.
- Example: A SmartWealth Saver showing "Extended Term Insurance" in iSmart = not active, even if it appears in the summary.

### B. Premium Amounts
- Verify the **exact premium** — premiums can change over time (e.g., HealthShield premiums rise with age).
- Check both the **cash premium** and the **Medisave portion** separately.
- Example: HealthShield Gold Max showing $786.97 total — cash portion and Medisave portion ($583.67) must be entered separately and correctly.

### C. Coverage Amounts (Sum Assured)

This is where errors are most common, especially for plans with **boosters**.

**Understanding Boosters (GPP Accumulator example):**
- The plan has a **base coverage** and a **booster** (typically 2x until age 65).
- You must **add the base + booster** to get the total coverage.
- Example: Base $35K + Booster $35K = **$70K total** death/TPD coverage.

**Breaking down coverage by type:**
- **Death coverage** — base + booster
- **TPD coverage** — base + booster
- **Major CI coverage** — base + booster, **plus** Early CI amount (Early CI contributes to Major CI total)
- **Early CI coverage** — base + booster (separate line item)

**Worked example (GPP Accumulator — larger policy):**
- Death: $133K + $266K = **$400K**
- TPD: $133K + $266K = **$400K**
- Major CI: $100K (Early CI) + $200K + $100K = **$400K** total
- Early CI: $30K + $60K + additional = **$100K**

**Key point:** Early CI coverage **also counts toward** major CI coverage. You must understand this layering to verify totals correctly.

### D. Maturity Benefits
- Check the **maturity value** and the **age at which it pays out**.
- Use the SIS (Statement of Insurance Summary) document in iSmart → scroll to the **Surrender Value** table.
- **Important decision:** Use the maturity value at a **practical age** (e.g., 65), not at age 100, since the age-100 figure is largely theoretical.
- Example: Maturity at age 100 = $380,401, but maturity at age 65 = $110,555 — use the age-65 figure for the client summary as it's more relevant.

### E. Start Dates & Payment Status
- Verify start date matches iSmart.
- Check the **next premium due date** — this tells you where the client is in their payment cycle.
- Note: Next premium due date ≠ start date. It indicates the next payment, not when the policy began.

## 3. Special Cases

### Investment-Linked Plans (e.g., ProAchiever 3.0)
- **Current policy value** changes constantly — always pull the latest figure from iSmart, not old data.
- Example: Summary showed $8,077, but iSmart showed **$9,207.36** — must update.
- Investment plans typically have **no insurance coverage** (shows "nil").
- **Maturity value** is not fixed for ILPs — it depends on fund performance. You can optionally include surrender value projections (e.g., surrender value at age 66) but it's not mandatory.

### Accident Plans (e.g., Solitaire PA)
- Usually straightforward — verify and move on.

### Plans Not Yet in the System
- If a policy exists in iSmart but hasn't been entered into the summary (e.g., Elite Health), it needs to be added.

## 6. Post-Meeting Deliverables & Next Steps

### After the First Appointment:
1. Use a **dummy account** for the presentation if needed — screenshot the report and have your VA edit the client's name/details into the PDF
2. Send the client a **post-meeting text** along with their personalised financial summary
3. The first meeting is primarily about **information gathering and perceived value**

### Setting Up the Second Meeting:
- Send the case details to your manager/mentor
- Do a **pre-meeting discussion** to strategise how to tackle the client
- Second meeting is a **joint fieldwork** session — the actual recommendation and closing appointment

---

## 7. Key Takeaways

1. **"Demo, don't do"** is the guiding principle of the first appointment. Show capability, don't deliver the full service yet.
2. **Policy retrieval is the #1 priority** — not scenario planning, not goal mapping. Get the policies, then question the mindset.
3. **Keep income/expense questions simple** — one question for income, one for expenses. Don't itemise.
4. **Start with CPF, not cash** — it's less intrusive and builds momentum for deeper questions.
5. **Poke holes in existing policies** — every policy type has an angle to question. This creates the gap that your recommendation fills.
6. **First meeting = gather data + build credibility.** Second meeting = recommend + close.
7. **Always send a post-meeting summary** — personalised PDF report + follow-up text. This is what differentiates a professional from a casual agent.
