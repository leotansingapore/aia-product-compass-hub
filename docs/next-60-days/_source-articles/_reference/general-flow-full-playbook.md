## Old Version

### Overview

Before we get into the nitty gritty of sales, let’s first talk about some limiting beliefs. Some financial advisors don’t want to sell, thinking that selling is a smelly word. They would rather service and help and guide instead. And yes, that’s a very noble belief, but knowledge is useless unless it actually causes change. A prescription, no matter how complex, how valuable, or how sound it is, will fail to be effective if the patient doesn’t feel convinced it works, or understands the importance of it.


In the same way, as financial doctors, a large part of our job is to convince someone to take their financial medicine, and acquire them as a customer. People are not going to line up to you to let you service them, unless you convince them that you’re worth their time and money.

No matter how good you think you are, it will be worth nothing if people don’t know how good you are. Just as a gym trainer cannot train you if you didn’t “buy” from them, nor can a doctor treat you if you didn’t consult them, in the same way you cannot advice someone if there isn’t any form of sales involved.

So, we cannot service if we don’t sell. And selling is very much an art as much as it is a science. There is a framework to it. But instead of memorizing a certain script, it is best to identify what makes sets a bad or good sales pitch apart.

So without further ado, let’s jump straight into the flow and structure of every sales appointment.

# Lecture Notes

## Overview

This is a walkthrough of a customizable slide deck used for **first client appointments**. The slides are not presented in strict linear order — they're modular, selected and rearranged based on the client's interests and the flow of conversation.

---

## Opening & Personal Branding Slides

### About Me / Introduction

- Brief personal introduction with a professional photo
- Outline the services you provide
- **Customize:** Change the name, photo, and details to your own. Use a professional headshot. If you need help editing, request a VA (virtual assistant) for slide editing support.

### Services Menu

Present the range of services and ask: **"Which of these would you prefer?"**

Typical responses fall into two buckets:
- **Insurance** → dive deeper into existing coverage, gaps, and needs
- **Investment** → transition into ProAchiever or ILP pitch

This early question **lets the client self-select** the conversation direction.

### Trust-Building Slides

- "I put my client first"
- Regular reviews commitment
- Staying with clients through different life stages
- **Spend minimal time here** — skim through quickly, don't linger

### Testimonials & Social Proof

- Display client reviews/testimonials
- **How to collect reviews early in your career:** Create a list of potential review statements → ask clients which ones they endorse → get a checkmark → use those endorsed reviews in your deck
- Rotate and update testimonials as you build your book

---

## The Total Wealth Concept

### Two Types of Wealth

| Type | What It Is | Solution |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Human Capital** | Your earning ability — your greatest asset | Protect with insurance |
| **Financial Capital** | Your accumulated wealth and investments | Grow with investment plans |

**Purpose of this slide:** Educate clients that they shouldn't only invest — they need to protect their income-earning ability too. And vice versa.

### Transition Question

> "Which aspect interests you more — growing your financial capital or protecting your human capital?"

- **Financial capital** → go straight into investment pitch (ProAchiever / ILP)
- **Human capital** → deeper fact-find on existing insurance, identify coverage gaps

---

## Financial Health Check (Four Ratios)

Use this framework for a more comprehensive first appointment. The ratios serve a **dual purpose**: they showcase your expertise AND they extract key financial data for fact-finding.

### The Four Ratios

| Ratio | Formula | What It Reveals | Sales Angle |
|-------|---------|----------------|-------------|
| **Liquidity Ratio** | Savings ÷ Monthly Expenses | Should be 3–6 months | Reveals income, expenses, and savings |
| **Savings Ratio** | (Income − Expenses) ÷ Income | How much they're actually saving | Gap between earning and saving → investment opportunity |
| **Invested Assets to Net Worth** | Invested Assets ÷ Net Worth | How much is working for them | If investing very little → pitch investments |
| **Debt to Asset Ratio** | Total Debt ÷ Total Assets | Should be below 50% | Usually not an issue for most clients |

> The first three ratios are really about **angling the conversation** — the main point isn't whether they're financially healthy. It's to fact-find their expenses, income, wealth, and investment status while positioning yourself as a professional.

---

## Investment Presentation Slides

### The Power of Starting Early

Use the story of a 45-year-old client:

> "I met a 45-year-old man who contributes $1,000/month. But because he started so late, his returns still can't match someone who started at 25 with a much smaller amount."

**Key message:** Save first, spend later. Monthly investing may not look impressive short-term, but compounding makes it powerful long-term.

### DIY vs. Professional Management

Show the complexity of buying even one stock on your own (research, monitoring, rebalancing, emotional decisions) versus entrusting it to a professional.

**Slide message:** "If you're entrusting us with your investments, it's going to be so much easier."

### Tangible vs. Intangible Costs

| Option A: DIY | Option B: Hire Us |
|---------------|-------------------|
| Research time | Managed for you |
| Emotional decisions | Disciplined process |
| Monitoring burden | Regular reviews |
| Rebalancing effort | Professional allocation |
| Hidden mistakes | Value-added services |

> "The fees are lower than you think, plus we give value-added services on top."

Clients prefer Option B — this is a **closing slide** used right before asking for the commitment.

---

## Value-Added Services (Raises Close Rate)

### Why This Matters

When you present value-added services, the client is no longer just buying a product — they're buying **you, your services, and your time**.

> "$200/month or $300/month is nothing compared to the value they get by engaging me."

### Examples of Value-Adds

- Client discounts on partner services
- Medical concierge (help with doctor referrals, claims management)
- Consolidated policy management ("manage all your policies under one roof")
- Regular portfolio reviews
- Access to workshops and educational content

### Medical Concierge Positioning

> "I'm not just an insurance agent. I can help you with medical inquiries, find the best doctors, and manage your claims."

**Close:** "Would you prefer to figure out everything on your own (Option A), or have someone manage it all for you (Option B)?"

## Insurance Presentation Slides

### Problem → Solution Framework

| Problem | Statistic/Story | Solution |
|---------|----------------|----------|
| Savings eroded by inflation | Low bank interest rates | Investment plan |
| High critical illness probability | Statistics on CI occurrence | CI plan |
| Unexpected hospitalization costs | Real examples of bills | Hospitalization plan |
| Accidents happen unpredictably | Types of accidents | Accident plan (~$15/month) |
| Income stops at disability/death | Liability protection chart | Whole life or term plan |

### Liability Protection Chart

As you age: income rises → expenses rise → savings accumulate. But if CI, disability, or death strikes: income drops to zero, expenses continue or increase, savings become liabilities.

**Solution:** Whole life plan (cash value, lifetime coverage) or term plan (limited coverage, no cash value, lower cost).

### Three Product Categories

| Category | Description | Products |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| **Pure Investment** | Flexible, good returns, risk-profiled | ILP investment component, unit trusts |
| **Pure Protection** | Hospital plan + accident plan + whole life or term | Shield plans, PA, whole life, term |
| **Hybrid (ILP)** | Investment + protection combined, flexible premiums and coverage | Investment-linked policies |

---

## Closing the Appointment

### The Automation Close

Use this as the **very last slide** before the client commits (enters credit card details):

**For investment plans:**
> "This is great that you're doing this today. I can't help you earn more or spend less, but I can help with the automation part — it'll be linked to your debit card or bank account. Set and forget."

**For insurance plans:**
> "You've already got the investing part done. Today, we're covering the protection side. This completes the picture."

This frames the purchase as the **natural final step** in a process they've already committed to.

### Professional Wrap-Up

Don't just say "bye bye." End with a professional summary slide recapping what was accomplished and what the next steps are.

---

## Referral System

### When to Ask

After the close — while the client is in a positive emotional state.

### How to Ask (Don't Just Ask)

Weak: "Can you refer me to anyone?"

**Strong approach:**
1. Show your **track record** of referrals received
2. Show **social proof** — "This client referred 10 friends, this one referred 10 friends..."
3. Show **reasons why** people refer you
4. Create a sense that referrals are **normal and expected** behavior

> "When they see so many referrals, they feel a bit pressured to refer as well."

### Referral Collection Methods

- Type names in Zoom chat (for virtual meetings)
- Screenshot a contact list
- Send individual contacts via text
- You follow up with a text or call

### Follow-Up Message

Send a simple reassurance text after the appointment — confirms the purchase, thanks them, and keeps the relationship warm.

---

## Key Principles

- **Slides are modular** — rearrange based on the conversation flow, not a fixed script
- **Spend minimal time on intro/trust slides** — get to the client's interest area quickly
- **Let the client self-select** — "Which would you prefer?" narrows the conversation efficiently
- **The financial health check is really fact-finding in disguise** — you look professional while extracting the data you need
- **Value-added services raise close rates** — the client buys you, not just the product
- **Always end professionally** — summary slide, next steps, referral ask
- **Customize everything** — change names, photos, testimonials to make it authentically yours

# Lecture Notes

## Overview
This lecture covers the systematic approach to conducting a personal financial health check before proposing any insurance or investment products, plus the proper way to introduce yourself and transition into investment planning discussions.

---

## Part 1: Personal Financial Health Check

### Purpose & Philosophy
- **Always conduct a financial health check BEFORE recommending any plans**
- Ensures the prospect has the financial capacity to proceed
- Builds consultative credibility rather than appearing pushy
- Allows you to identify clients who may not be ready to purchase

### The Four Key Financial Ratios

#### 1. Liquidity Ratio
**What it measures:** Cash available vs. monthly expenses

**Standard:** 3-6 months of expenses in liquid savings

**Example:**
- Monthly expenses: $1,000
- Required liquidity: $3,000-$6,000 in bank account

**Red flags:**
- Client has $3,000 saved but spends $2,000/month (only 1.5 months coverage)
- Young adults with minimal savings
- When liquidity is insufficient, focus on building emergency fund first before proceeding

**Action:** If ratio is poor, advise client to focus on building liquidity first and schedule follow-up later

---

#### 2. Savings Ratio
**What it measures:** Percentage of income consistently saved/invested

**Standard:** 10-20% of gross income

**Example:**
- Income: $5,000/month
- Target savings/investment: $500-$1,000/month

**Important distinction:**
- This does NOT include static savings in bank accounts
- Must include active investment plans or regular savings programs
- Shows whether client already has systematic savings habits

**Consultative approach:**
- Use this to discover existing investment plans indirectly
- Instead of asking "Do you have an investment plan?", you're assessing through their savings behavior
- More professional and consultative than direct questioning

---

#### 3. Investment Assets to Net Worth Ratio
**What it measures:** Percentage of net worth actively invested

**Standard:** At least 50% of net worth should be invested

**Calculation:**
- Net Worth = Assets - Liabilities

**Example:**
- Assets: $10,000 in bank
- Liabilities: $0 (no loans)
- Net Worth: $10,000
- Investment assets: $0
- Ratio: 0% (needs improvement)

**Why it matters:**
- Identifies clients who are "saving but not investing"
- Provides non-pushy way to recommend starting investments
- Frames recommendation as financial planning guidance rather than sales

**Positioning:**
- "You should be investing at least 50% of your net worth"
- Consultative tone: helping them improve their financial position
- Not selling, just identifying a gap in their financial health

---

#### 4. Debt Servicing Ratio
**What it measures:** Monthly debt payments vs. income

**Standard:** Should be below 50% of income

**What to assess:**
- School loans
- Education loans
- Other commitments
- Note: Most prospects don't have significant debt yet

**Red flag:** Anything above 50% is cause for concern

## Part 3: Transitioning to Investment Planning

### The Services Discovery Question

**After introduction, ask:**
"So today, what are you more interested in? The insurance planning part or the investment planning part?"

**Alternative phrasing:**
"Of all these services, what specifically are you looking for?"

**Benefits of this approach:**
- Client-directed conversation
- Efficient use of meeting time
- Skip irrelevant sections
- Shows you're listening to their needs

---

### Investment Planning Opening (If Client Chooses Investments)

#### The Bank Account Problem
**Key message:** "Everyone wants to start investing, but they're currently just saving in bank accounts with low interest rates."

**The progression:**
1. Highlight low bank interest rates
2. Show impact of inflation on savings
3. Present the erosion of purchasing power

#### The Retirement Goal Framework

**Use the $2 million retirement example:**
1. Show what they'll need at retirement
2. Back-calculate to present day
3. Reverse engineer the monthly savings required
4. Demonstrate the gap between current savings and needs

#### Two Alternative Opening Methods

**Method 1: The Inflation Approach**
- "Inflation is very high right now"
- "It's eating into your savings as we speak"
- Creates urgency without being pushy

**Method 2: The Risk Preference Question**

Use the risk spectrum diagram and ask:
"At the end of the day, we can help you grow your savings faster. There are two approaches:
1. Low risk method
2. Higher risk method

Which would you prefer?"

**Typical response:** Most clients choose medium to high risk

**Next step:** Proceed to the investment solution that matches their risk preference

## Key Teaching Points

### Consultative vs. Pushy Selling
- Frame everything as financial health assessment
- Use ratios and benchmarks (objective standards)
- Let the numbers tell the story
- Position yourself as advisor, not salesperson

### The Power of Indirect Questioning
- Don't ask "Do you have investments?" directly
- Instead, assess through savings ratio
- More professional and less threatening
- Builds trust through expertise

### Qualifying Clients Early
- Not every prospect is ready to buy
- It's okay to identify this early
- Better to focus on building emergency fund first
- Schedule follow-up when they're financially ready

### Setting Proper Expectations
- Health check comes BEFORE product presentation
- This is standard financial planning practice
- Protects both client and advisor
- Builds foundation for long-term relationship

# Lecture Notes

## I. Total Wealth Concept Overview

### Core Philosophy
- **Total Wealth = Financial Capital + Human Capital**
- Financial Capital: Money in bank accounts and investments
- Human Capital: Your ability to earn income over your lifetime

### Two-Pronged Strategy
1. **Aggressively grow Financial Capital**
   - Earn more through hard work
   - Spend less
   - Invest consistently

2. **Protect Human Capital**
   - Your greatest asset is yourself, not your bank account
   - Health issues and medical problems can rapidly deteriorate earning ability
   - Must reduce risk to human capital

### Risk Categories
- **Manageable risks**: Common fever, temporary retrenchment
- **Critical risks**: Death, disability, critical illness (long-term impact on earning ability)

---

## II. The Four Types of Insurance Coverage

### Coverage Priority Framework

**1. Hospitalization Insurance (First Line of Defense)**
- Most fundamental coverage - all incidents typically involve hospitalization
- Accidents → hospitalized
- Critical illness → hospitalized
- Death/disability → hospitalized

**Key Features:**
- Coverage: Up to $2 million per year
- Resets annually (unlimited lifetime claims)
- Pricing: ~$50/month (private), ~$20/month (public)

**Critical Timing Consideration:**
- Pre-existing conditions can make you uninsurable
- Must purchase as early as possible while healthy
- Hospitalization bills can reach $1-2 million

---

**2. Accident Insurance (Outpatient Coverage)**

**Key Distinction:**
- Hospitalization = Inpatient coverage
- Accident = Outpatient coverage

**Real-World Example:**
- Client's child needed stitches on forehead
- Not admitted/warded = no hospitalization claim
- Only accident insurance would cover this

**Pricing:** Starting from $19/month

---

**3. Critical Illness Insurance**

**Recommended Coverage:** 3-5 years of annual income

**Why Early Purchase Matters:**
- Lock in lower premiums at younger age
- Example: 25 years old = $1,900/year | 35 years old = $2,400/year
- Difference of $500/year by waiting 10 years

**Key Features:**
- Limited payment period (e.g., 25 years)
- Lifelong coverage
- Full premium refund if no claims (confirm-win concept)

---

**4. Death/Disability Coverage**

**Recommended Coverage:** 10 years of annual income

**Rationale:**
- Provides significant financial buffer for family
- Even if emotional loss cannot be replaced, resolves financial burden
- $50k or 1-year coverage is inadequate

---

## III. Financial Planning Framework: The One-Third Rule

### Income Allocation (Based on $3,000/month example)

**First Third ($1,000):** Short-term expenses

**Second Third ($1,000):** Medium-term goals
- House
- Car
- Wedding
- Holidays

**Third Third ($1,000):** Long-term wealth building
- Insurance: 10-15% of income
- Investments: 15-20% of income

---

## IV. The Income-Expense Gap Crisis

### Normal Scenario
- Income increases over time
- Expenses increase over time
- Gap = Savings (wealth accumulation)

### Medical Crisis Scenario
- Income drops to ZERO
- Expenses INCREASE (medical costs added)
- Previous savings become deficit spending
- All accumulated wealth can be depleted

**Case Study:**
- Client invested $3k/month into investment policies only
- Diagnosed with critical illness
- Unable to work for years
- Had to cancel all investments (with losses)
- Lost income + medical bills = financial catastrophe

**Key Insight:** Insurance is like a seatbelt - you don't plan to use it, but you must have it

---

## V. Coverage Recommendations Summary

| Risk Type | Recommended Coverage | Typical Existing Coverage | Gap to Fill |
|-----------|---------------------|---------------------------|-------------|
| Death | 10 years of income | Often $50-100k | Significant upgrade needed |
| Disability | Adequate protection | Variable | Assess individually |
| Critical Illness | 3-5 years income ($200k+ example) | Often outdated policies from parents | ~$200k shortfall common |
| Hospitalization | $2M annual, unlimited lifetime | Often none | URGENT priority |
| Accident | Outpatient coverage | Often none | Essential addition |

---

## VI. Critical Illness Policy Deep Dive

### Example Policy Structure (25-year-old, $200k coverage)

**Premium:** $1,900/year ($158/month) before 20% discount
**Payment Period:** 25 years
**Total Premium Paid:** $48,000
**Guaranteed Return:** $190,000 (if no claims)

**Comparison if Started at 35:**
- Premium: $2,400/year
- Total Paid: $60,000
- Return: $169,000

# Lecture Notes

## The Bridge: From "Impossible" to "Achievable"

### Reframe the Problem

The previous module established that the client needs ~$3,000/month in pure savings to reach $1.5 million by 65 — clearly not feasible on a $1,000/month income.

Now flip the script:

> "With a proper investing plan, we can help you get to $1.5 million with as low as **$450/month**."

### Anchoring the Savings

| Approach | Monthly Amount | Difference |
|----------|---------------|------------|
| Pure savings (no investing) | $3,000/month | — |
| With investing plan | $450/month | **$2,500 freed up** |

> "We're helping you free up $2,500/month in cash flow. $450 versus $3,000 — which sounds more achievable?"

---

## The Step-Down Close: Start Small

### Don't Push $450 Immediately

> "I'm not saying you have to hit $450 right now. You can start with a more conservative amount — maybe **$200/month**."

This removes pressure and makes the commitment feel manageable. The key insight: **get them started**, not maxed out.

### What $200/Month Achieves

| Scenario | Amount at 65 | Amount at 85 |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| $200/month from age 19 | ~$600–700K (70% of goal) | ~$2.5 million |

> "Even at $200/month, you'd hit about 70% of your $1.5 million target by 65. And if you let it compound to 85, it grows to roughly $2.5 million."

---

## The Cost of Waiting (Urgency Without Pressure)

### The Procrastination Trap

> "Some people say they'll start after university, after they start working, after getting married, having kids. Before you know it, you're 29. What do you think happens to the returns?"

### Interactive Question

> "Do you think the returns will be the same? 10% lower? 20%? 30%?"

Let the client guess — then reveal the actual numbers.

### The Shocking Comparison

| Start Age | Monthly Amount | Value at 85 |
|-----------|---------------|-------------|
| **19** | $200 | **~$2.5 million** |
| **29** | $200 | **~$1.3 million** |
| **Difference** | — | **$1.2 million lost** |

> "By waiting just 10 years, you lose $1.2 million. That's $120,000 per year of delay."

This makes procrastination tangible — every year of inaction has a measurable dollar cost.

---

## The Step-Up Close: $1/Day More

### Incremental Increases

After establishing $200/month as the baseline, introduce small increments:

| Monthly Amount | Value at 85 | Additional vs. $200 |
|---------------|-------------|---------------------|
| $200 | ~$2.5 million | — |
| $250 (+$50) | ~$3.2 million | **+$700K** |
| $300 (+$100) | ~$3.8 million | **+$1.3 million** |

### Make It Relatable

> "Just an additional $50/month — that's basically **$1 a day** — gets you an extra $700,000. That's worth a condo. That's worth several cars. Just from $1 a day."

This reframing makes the increment feel trivial while the outcome feels massive.

---

## The Choice Close

### Present Three Options (Not One)

> "Given your current income and expenses, which would you prefer: **$200, $250, or $300?**"

This is a **choice close** — the question isn't "will you invest?" but "how much will you invest?" All three options are acceptable. The client is choosing between yeses, not between yes and no.

### Why Three Options Work

- **$200** = easy entry, low commitment, client feels no pressure
- **$250** = the "sweet spot" — small stretch, massive compounding benefit
- **$300** = aspirational, for clients who want to be aggressive

Most clients pick the middle option ($250), which is exactly where you want them.

## Key Selling Principles in This Module

| Principle | How It's Applied |
|-----------|-----------------|
| **Anchoring** | Start with $3,000 (impossible) → bring down to $450 (relief) → offer $200 (easy) |
| **Loss aversion** | Show what they lose by waiting ($120K/year) rather than what they gain |
| **Compounding visualization** | Small amounts → massive outcomes over time |
| **$1/day reframing** | Makes $50/month feel insignificant |
| **Choice close** | Three options, all acceptable — eliminates the yes/no decision |
| **Start low, increase later** | Removes commitment anxiety; plan allows top-ups over time |

# Phase 2: Financial Health Check - Lecture Notes

## Overview and Positioning

**Objective**: Gather client's financial information (income, expenses, savings) in a tactful, professional manner without being too direct.

**The Framework Analogy**: Position yourself as a "financial doctor"
- Just as doctors check blood pressure, height, weight, and blood sugar before treatment
- Financial advisors must understand current finances before making recommendations
- This establishes credibility and makes data collection feel purposeful

---

## The Four Financial Ratios

### 1. Liquidity Ratio

**Definition**: How many months of expenses you have saved in the bank

**Formula**: Bank Savings ÷ Monthly Expenses

**Example**: 
- Monthly expenses: $1,000
- Bank savings: $5,000
- Liquidity ratio: 5 months

**Target Range**: 3-6 months of expenses

**How to Ask**:
1. "How much are you currently spending a month?"
2. Wait for response
3. "How much have you saved in the bank so far?"

**Creating the Need** (works for ANY scenario):
- **High liquidity**: "That's great you have 6 months saved, but most of it is just sitting in the bank. We should look at investing some of this."
- **Low liquidity**: "Your liquidity is low. We should build up your emergency fund before we invest."

---

### 2. Savings Ratio

**Definition**: Percentage of income being saved monthly

**Formula**: (Income - Expenses) ÷ Income × 100%

**Target Range**: 10-20% minimum for long-term savings/investments

**How to Ask**:
1. "How much do you earn a month?"
2. "How much do you spend a month?"
3. Calculate the difference = savings

**Creating the Need** (works for ANY scenario):
- **High savings ratio (e.g., 50%)**: "That's impressive! But you're saving most of it in the bank. With 0.05% interest and 2.8% inflation, you're actually losing 2.75% annually. We need to get at least 10-20% into long-term investments."
- **Low savings ratio**: "You're not saving enough. You need a disciplined savings habit—a regular savings plan to build wealth systematically."

**Key Insight**: Even high savers have a problem if money sits in bank accounts

---

### 3. Invested Assets to Net Worth Ratio

**Definition**: Percentage of total savings/net worth that is invested (not in bank)

**Formula**: Invested Assets ÷ Total Net Worth × 100%

**Target**: Minimum 50% of net worth should be invested

**How to Ask**:
- "Currently, do you have any investments?"
- "Where's your money at?"

**Examples**:
- Client has $10K savings, all in bank → Invested assets ratio = 0%
- Client has $20K savings, all in bank → Invested assets ratio = 0%

**Creating the Need**:
"As we get older, our net worth increases. We should aim for at least 50% to be invested so our assets don't erode due to inflation over time."

**The Problem Statement**: If ratio is 0% or low, emphasize wealth erosion through inflation

---

### 4. Debt Servicing Ratio

**Target**: Below 50%

**Typical Situation**: Most prospects don't have significant debts

**Strategy**: Usually skip through this quickly unless client has debt issues

---

## The Inflation Problem: Building Urgency

### The Math That Shocks

**Current Reality**:
- Bank interest rate: 0.05%
- Inflation rate: 2.8%
- **Real loss**: 2.75% per year

**The Compounding Effect**:
- After 2 years: Lose 5.5% purchasing power
- After 10 years: Lose 27.5% purchasing power
- Over a lifetime: Massive wealth erosion

**The Message**: "If you save in the bank your whole life, you're going to lose a lot of your wealth over time."

**The Solution**: Move to medium/higher-risk investment accounts to:
- Grow savings better
- Beat inflation
- Achieve retirement objectives

## Risk Profiling

### The Question
"Are you more of a low risk, medium risk, or high risk investor?"

### Age-Based Recommendations

**For Younger Clients (Recommended: Higher Risk)**

**The Logic**:
- Smaller portfolio values ($5K-$20K)
- 10-20% loss is not catastrophic in absolute terms
- Longer time horizon to recover
- Can afford to be more aggressive

**For Older Clients (Recommended: Medium to Low Risk)**

**The Logic**:
- Larger portfolio values (potentially $1M+)
- 20% loss = hundreds of thousands of dollars
- Less time to recover from losses
- Need capital preservation

**The Strategy Explanation**:
"As a young adult, start with higher risk. As you get older and your portfolio grows, gradually shift to lower risk."

### Pillar 3: Financial Independence Framework

-   Cover self first (insurance)
    
-   Automate investing process
    

**What Agent CANNOT Help With:**

-   Earning more (client's responsibility)
    
-   Spending less (client's discipline)
    

**Manual vs Automated Investing:**

-   Manual = forgetting, impulsive buying, poor timing
    
-   Automated = discipline, consistency, removes emotion
    
-   Buy high (when excited), sell low (when scared) = bad pattern
    
-   Monthly automation = dollar-cost averaging regardless of market
    

**Reality Check:**

-   "This is NOT a magic pill"
    
-   Must also focus on earning more AND spending less
    
-   Temptation: earn more → spend more (must resist)

## 1\. Human Capital vs. Financial Capital

## 4\. Budget Framework & Rules of Thumb

-   **Insurance allocation:** ~10–15% of income

-   **Budget split (Rule of Thirds):**

-   **$100K + 3 to 5 years of gross income**

## 2\. WHY We Need to Plan — Income Dependency Framework

### Mapping Current Income Dependencies

Walk the client through what their income currently supports:

-   **Personal expenses** — food, clothes, daily living
    
-   **Family expenses** — utilities, contributions to household

### Two Types of Risk

**Temporary Risks** (recoverable):

-   Get fired, retrenched, or resign

**Permanent Risks** (potentially unrecoverable):

-   Critical illness (cancer, stroke, coma)
    
-   Death
    
-   Hospitalization / major accident
    
-   Retirement (voluntary loss of income)
    

### The $1 Million Question (Emotional Anchor)

Use a scaling exercise to make the risk feel real:

-   *"If you had a medical emergency, can your family lend you $1,000?"* → Yes, probably
    
-   *"How about $10,000?"* → Maybe, ask some relatives
    
-   *"How about $1 million?"* → Not possible
    

**Punchline:** *"If you don't have your own financial plan, your family becomes your financial plan."*

### Calculation Example

-   Annual expenses: ~$25K
    
-   Years remaining (age 25): ~40 years
    
-   **Total exposure: $25K × 40 = $1,000,000**

## Part 1: Canned Sales Track (CST)

### What Is a CST?

A **memorized, structured opening presentation** for new/inexperienced consultants. Like a car showroom script — if you practice it 100 times, you can present as if you've been doing it for years.

**The opposite of CST:** No structure, just "anyhow say" — leads to poor first impressions and lost prospects.

### CST Structure — Three Questions

The entire opening presentation is organized around:

1.  **Why Plan?**
    
2.  **What to Plan?**
    
3.  **How Much to Plan?**
    

### The Sales Advisory Process

-   **1st Appointment:** Opening → Fact-finding/Discovery → Get budget commitment
    
-   **2nd Appointment:** Solutions/Implementation → Closing
    

**The goal of the CST is NOT to close on the spot.** It's to:

-   Educate the prospect
    
-   Secure a second appointment
    
-   Get a budget commitment (income allocation for risk management + wealth accumulation)
    

### CST Demo Recap (Director Winnie's Presentation)

**Opening / Rapport:**

-   Reassure: *"I'm not here to sell you anything you don't wish. If you proceed, it's because you find value and feel comfortable with me."*
    
-   Brief self-introduction: credentials, specialization (financial health check), strengths (claims processing, portfolio summary, regular updates)
    
-   Emphasize: *"Financial planning is a journey — I'll be with you through your milestones."*
    

**Why Plan — Income Dependency:**

-   Example: Person earning $4K/month ($50K/year)
    
-   Breakdown: 10% food, 10% transport, 10% parents, 10% misc = 40% lifestyle + 20% CPF = 60% committed → 40% remaining
    
-   Ideal: save that 40%. Reality: most people spend it.
    

**Two types of income loss risk:**

-   **Temporary:** Sacked, resigned, retrenched → solution is contingency cash (3–6 months expenses in bank)
    
-   **Permanent:** Death, disability, critical illness, accident, old age/retirement → these are irreversible
    

**The $1 Million Question:**

-   Age 25, needs $25K/year expenses × 40 years = $1,000,000
    
-   Can family lend $1K? Probably. $10K? Maybe. $1M? No.
    
-   *"We don't want clients to beg, borrow, or steal."*
    

**Three Risk Management Methods:**

1.  **Avoid** — unrealistic (can't avoid illness)
    
2.  **Retain** — need $1M in savings first (don't have it)
    
3.  **Transfer** — use 10% of income to protect 90% ✅
    

**What to Plan — Three Stages (Pyramid):**

1.  **Risk Management (Foundation):** Life insurance, accident, hospitalization → 10% of income
    
2.  **Wealth Accumulation:** Savings goals, retirement, kids' education → 10–20% of income
    
3.  **Wealth Preservation:** Legacy/estate planning → 10–50% of liquid assets (future stage)

## Product Deep Dive 1: Guarantee Protect Plus (GPP)

### Key Features

-   **Whole life participating plan** — participates in the par fund profits (7/8 of bonuses returned to policyholders)
    
-   **Limited premium payment** — pay for a set period (15, 20, or 25 years), covered for life
    
-   **Cash value** — guaranteed component + non-guaranteed bonuses from par fund
    
-   **Multiplier coverage** — 2×, 3×, or 5× base sum assured
    

### Participating vs. Investment-Linked — The Key Distinction

### Multiplier Mechanics

The base sum assured is multiplied by your chosen factor until the drop-off age:

**Shorter tenure = better rate of return** for the same premium amount.

### CI Riders on GPP

-   **CPL (Critical Payment of Lump Sum):** Covers 42 major critical illnesses — **non-participating, no cash value** (important to know)
    
-   **Early Critical Protector (ECP):** Covers early-stage conditions (e.g., carcinoma in situ, stage 0–1 cancers)
    
-   **Waiver Rider:** Waives premiums upon CI or early CI diagnosis
    
-   **Payer Benefit:** For child policies — waives premium if policy owner (parent) gets CI
    

### GPP Yield Reality

-   At 65 years old, par fund earning 4.25% → policy yield is ~2.15%
    
-   GPP yields are like savings/fixed deposit rates — not endowment-level (3.5–3.8%) or ILP-level (7–8%)
    
-   **GPP is a protection product with savings, not a savings product**
    

### Three Ways to Use GPP at Maturity

1.  **Surrender at 65:** Cash out the surrender value (e.g., $122K on $77K paid = profit)
    
2.  **Income Drawdown (Tail-End Annuity):** Don't surrender — draw down over 10 years as income stream (ages 65–75). Makes GPP feel like an annuity.
    
3.  **Keep for Legacy:** If client is wealthy and doesn't need the cash, keep the policy growing. Nominate beneficiaries through nominations or wills/trusts. At 80, sum assured could be ~$353K to distribute to children.
    

### Life Stages Benefit

At certain qualifying milestones, the client can increase sum assured by $100K without medical underwriting.

## Product Deep Dive 2: Secure Flex Term (SFT)

### Key Features

-   **Pure term insurance** — no cash value, nothing back if nothing happens
    
-   **Covers:** Death, TPD, terminal illness (including terminal cancer — first-in-market at launch)
    
-   **Premium:** Guaranteed, locked in for the chosen tenure
    
-   **Tenure options:** 5, 10, 20, or 30 years (renewable term) OR level term to 65 or 75
    
-   **Conversion option:** Can convert to whole life, investment, or endowment plan without medical underwriting
    
-   **Vitality integrated:** First year 10% discount, ongoing discounts up to 15% based on vitality status
    

### Renewable vs. Level Term

### SFT Pricing Example (30M, Non-Smoker, $1M Death)

<table class="tiptap-table" style="min-width: 50px;"><colgroup><col style="min-width: 25px;"><col style="min-width: 25px;"></colgroup><tbody><tr><th colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>Coverage Period</p></th><th colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>Annual Premium</p></th></tr><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>To age 65 (level term)</p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>$882</p></td></tr><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>To age 75 (level term)</p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>$1,403</p></td></tr><tr><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>30-year renewable term</p></td><td colspan="1" rowspan="1"><p>$749</p></td></tr></tbody></table>

**Sales angle:** *"For less than an Anytime Fitness gym membership, AIA guarantees your family $1 million if anything happens to you before retirement."*

### Adding CI to SFT

-   **Major CI ($250K) to age 65:** Total premium ~$1,500/year (death $1M + CI $250K)
    
-   CI rider is **accelerated** — CI payout reduces death benefit (claim $250K CI → death becomes $750K)
    
-   **Early CI ($100K)** can be added on 5/10/20/30-year tenures BUT **not** on level term to 65/75 (limitation)
    

### Payout Sequence Example (SFT with $1M death, $250K major CI, $100K early CI)

1.  Early CI diagnosed → pay $100K (accelerated from CI)
    
2.  Major CI 10 years later → pay $150K ($250K – $100K already paid)
    
3.  Death before term expires → pay $750K ($1M – $250K total CI paid)
    

### Handling the "What If I Outlive the Term?" Objection

-   *"65 means 66 I die I get nothing, right?"* → Yes, that's correct for term insurance
    
-   Show the cost difference: 65 = $882 vs. 75 = $1,403 → client decides if the extra coverage is worth it

## Product Deep Dive 4: Pro Lighter Protector (PLP)

### Key Features

-   **Investment-linked policy (ILP)** — hybrid of insurance + investment

-   Don't hold coverage all the way to 100 — mortality costs escalate dramatically at older ages

## Part 1: Calculating Retirement Needs

### The Financial Calculator (TVM — Time Value of Money)

Download a TVM financial calculator app on your iPad/phone. Key parameters:

-   **PV (Present Value):** Value of money today (e.g., current monthly expenses)
    
-   **FV (Future Value):** What that money needs to become in the future
    
-   **I (Interest/Inflation Rate):** Rate of growth or erosion over time
    
-   **N (Period):** Number of years
    
-   **Compounding:** Usually annual
    

### Retirement Calculation Walkthrough

**Example:** Client is 35, wants to retire at 50, current expenses $2,500/month

1.  **Inflate the expenses:** PV = $2,500, I = 3%, N = 15 years → FV = **$3,894/month**
    
    -   *"To maintain the same purchasing power in 15 years, you'll need $3,894/month."*
        
2.  **Calculate the lump sum needed:** $3,894 × 12 months × 35 years (to age 85) = **~$1.6 million**
    
3.  **Two drawdown methods:**
    
    -   **Capital Preservation (4% Rule):** Withdraw 4% annually from total assets without depleting capital. $1.6M × 4% = ~$65K/year perpetually. Works if investment returns ≥ withdrawal rate.
        
    -   **Capital Depletion:** Withdraw more than 4% (e.g., 6–8%) → capital will eventually run out. Higher risk of outliving your money.
        

### Key Teaching Point for Clients

-   If investment return > withdrawal rate → capital preserved indefinitely
    
-   If withdrawal rate > investment return → capital depletes (red line goes down)
    
-   *"Your money must work harder than you."*

## Part 6: Platinum Wealth Elite (PWE)

### What Is PWE?

An **ILP-based life insurance plan** that sits between term insurance (no cash value) and whole life GPP (high cash value). Think of it as: **term insurance with investment returns.**

### Key Features

-   Covers: Death, terminal illness, TPD
    
-   No CI coverage
    
-   Death benefit: **Higher of** insured amount OR policy value
    
-   Minimum sum assured: $5K (but typically positioned at $500K–$1M+)
    
-   Can do single premium or multi-year premium (up to 10 years)
    
-   Premium top-ups and withdrawals allowed

## Part 1: Using Policy Summary to Open Cases & Do Reviews

### The Advisory Sales Flow

```
Lead → Appointment → Needs Analysis / Policy Review → Fact-Finding → Presentation → Solution Implementation → Servicing → Referrals
```

**Key principle:** The entry point for warm market is almost always a low-barrier offer — never *"Can I sell you a plan?"* Always *"Can I help you review your finances?"*

### How to Position the Review

Opening approach for warm/referral prospects:

-   *"Hey, I'm introduced by \[mutual friend\]. Shall we do a policy review?"*
    
-   *"I'd love to share some updates on recent changes in hospital plans / investment markets / CI definitions."*
    
-   *"Let me help you consolidate and review your existing policies."*
    

### The Four Financial Parameters (Fact-Finding)

Every review must capture:

1.  **Income** — salary, bonus, other income
    
2.  **Expenses** — monthly breakdown
    
3.  **Assets** — property, CPF, investments, savings, shares
    
4.  **Liabilities** — mortgage, car loan, credit cards
    

These are the **hard facts**. Soft facts include goals, retirement age, risk appetite, and coverage preferences.

### Policy Summary Document

A one-page or two-page summary that gives the client an at-a-glance view of their entire financial position.

**Upper section — Risk Management:**

-   Policy name, number, date, coverage amounts, cash values, premiums
    

**Lower section — Wealth Accumulation:**

-   Investment policies, projected maturity values, APA projections at 65, etc.
    

**Coverage gap analysis:**

-   Map existing coverage against rules of thumb (10× income for death, 3–5× for CI, etc.)
    
-   Example: Income $60K → CI need $180–300K → existing CI $75K → **gap of $105–225K**
    

**What clients actually want to know:**

1.  *How much am I paying in total?*
    
2.  *What am I covered for?*
    
3.  *What am I lacking?*
    

**Formats vary:** Some use the two-page template, others use Ghost Mapper summary (David's personal preference). Use whatever format communicates clearly.

### Servicing Touchpoints

Once a prospect becomes a client, maintain contact through:

-   **Annual reviews** (at minimum every 1–2 years)

### What Is SG FinDex?

A Singapore government digital service that lets clients consolidate financial data from multiple institutions using their **Singpass**.

### What It Can Retrieve

1.  Client logs into **AIA+** app (formerly AIA SG app)
    
2.  Navigate to **SG FinDex Data**

4.  Select financial institutions to link (tick which companies)
    
5.  Give consent → data is pulled

-   **Send a step-by-step PDF guide** to the client before or during the meeting
    
-   Be **sensitive about banking data** — don't push for credit card and savings balances unless the client volunteers

```

```

-   Once a cold prospect buys from you, they become your **warm market**
    
-   Your job: nurture them into advocates who refer without being asked
    
-   **The dream scenario:** Client messages you saying *"Hey, my girlfriend is looking for CI, can you help?"* — that's the open goal
    

### Two Types of Appointments

**1\. Education/Fact-Find Appointment (Direct)**

-   Client has agreed to a review or opening presentation
    
-   You bring your toolkit: policy summary, fact-find sheets, marketing kit
    
-   This is your actual working appointment
    

**2\. Warm-Up Appointment (Indirect)**

-   Client isn't ready for a formal meeting yet
    
-   Meet casually — coffee, dinner, gym
    
-   Be honest about your career, share naturally
    
-   If opportunity arises, transition to booking an education appointment
    
-   If not, plant seeds for the future
    

**Conversion estimate:** Every 3 warm-up appointments → ~2 will convert to education/fact-find appointments

### How to Approach Warm Friends — The Casual Call/Text

**DO:**

-   Sound like a friend calling a friend
    
-   Be genuine — mention genuine shared history
    
-   Share your career transition naturally
    
-   Compliment them sincerely
    
-   End with a low-pressure ask: *"No pressure — whether you engage me is totally up to you"*
    

**DON'T:**

-   Sound like a financial advisor reading a script
    
-   Open with *"Hey, I want to sell you an APA"*
    
-   Be overly formal or objective-driven with hot/warm friends

### Why Referrals Matter

Referred leads are **pre-warmed** — the referrer has already vouched for you. Appointment-setting is dramatically easier.

### The Referral System — Four Steps

**Step 1: Plant Seeds (Before the Presentation)**

Say early in the meeting:

-   *"My business runs on recommendations from happy clients."*
    
-   *"If you find today's session valuable, I'd really appreciate it if you could recommend some friends to me."*
    
-   *"I won't be surprised if at the end of this session, you want to refer me to some of your friends."*
    

**Step 2: Ask at the Right Time (After They Buy or Give Positive Feedback)**

Best moments to ask:

-   After the client signs a policy (trust is at its peak)
    
-   After a positive feedback moment (*"Which part did you learn the most?"*)
    
-   After completing a review that uncovered real value
    

**Step 3: Collaborate — The Phone Technique**

This is the most effective method:

1.  Ask client to take out their phone
    
2.  Open WhatsApp or Telegram
    
3.  Look at their first 10 most-messaged contacts
    
4.  Use the referral paper to write down names one by one
    
5.  Guide them: *"Who among these friends do you think would benefit from what we just discussed?"*
    

**Target:** 3–5 names per appointment

**Step 4: Handle Objections**

### The VIP Technique (Value → Importance → Process)

1.  **Value:** *"Thanks for signing up for this plan."*
    
2.  **Importance:** *"I need to go through something very important with you."* (Pause — let them ask "What is it?")
    
3.  **Process:** *"As I mentioned at the start, my business runs on referrals. Can we spend 5 minutes brainstorming together? Just take out your phone and let's go through a few friends."*
    

### Social Proof Method

Take out your referral booklet showing existing names (blacked out for privacy). When the client sees that other clients have already referred, it normalizes the ask: *"This is something all my clients do — it's very natural."*

### Calling the Referral — Script Framework

**Structure:** Build rapport through shared connection → get them to ask what you do → pitch the meeting

**Sample script:**

> *"Hey Gabriel, thanks for taking my call. I'm David from AIA. Hussein asked me to reach out to you."* *(Pause — let them register who Hussein is)* *"Hussein and I were doing some work together, and he spoke very highly of you. He mentioned you guys did your final year project together and even won best project — that's impressive!"* *(Build rapport through shared knowledge)* *"A lot of what I've done for Hussein may be of interest to you. I'm not sure if my work will be valuable to you, but I'd love to meet for 30 minutes to introduce myself and share some ideas."* *(They ask: "What's this about?")* *"Thanks for asking — I'm with AIA Financial Advisors. We help clients build assets and plan to retire 10 years earlier. Hussein felt you'd be a great person to connect with. Would next week — weekday or weekend — work for you?"*

**Key principles:**

-   Never mention what you do until they ask
    
-   Reference the referrer's name and relationship warmth
    
-   Keep it about them, not about you
    
-   The call only works if it's a **named, warm referral** (not anonymous)
    
-   Best case: the referrer sends a heads-up message to the prospect before your call
    

### Why Referral Systems Fail

The **#1 reason:** The advisor simply doesn't ask.

If you run 6 appointments per week and forget to ask at 2, you still get referrals from 4. At 3–5 names each, that's **12–20 warm leads per week** — more than enough to sustain a pipeline.

## Part 4: Running Ads — ROI Is Guaranteed

### Why Ads Work for Financial Advisors

Unlike a clothing e-commerce business where the customer must buy immediately, **your leads are reusable assets:**

-   Lead doesn't buy today → follow up next month
    
-   Lead isn't ready now → nurture for 3–6–9 months
    
-   The lead never expires if you keep following up
    

### Gabriel's Ad ROI

-   Spent **$2K on ads** → generated **$12K FYC** = **6× ROI**
    
-   Has **never lost money** on any ad campaign
    
-   Leo historically spent $1–2K/month on ads with consistent positive ROI
    

### Scaling Advice

-   Start with what you can manage (e.g., $500–1K/month)
    
-   **Don't overspend** to the point where you can't follow up on all leads — Gabriel once had 20–25 Zoom slots filled in one weekend with no room for more
    
-   Always call leads when they're **fresh** — the moment they sign up, they're most interested
    
-   If you fall behind, stop ads temporarily and work through existing leads before restarting

## Part 5: Peaks & Valleys — Emotional Management

### The Complacency Trap

-   Close 3–5 cases in a weekend → feel great → stop doing activity → pipeline dries up
    
-   **Gabriel's rule:** Even after closing 5 APAs in one weekend, continue activity the next day as if nothing happened
    

### The Despair Trap

-   A free-look cancellation or a lost case → feel terrible → stop doing activity → pipeline dries up further
    
-   **Gabriel's rule:** Take **one day** to feel bad. Next day, resume full activity. No exceptions.
    

**Gabriel's December reality:** 22 cases submitted, but 3 free-looked (cancelled). Plus one software engineer he expected to close $1,000/month who ended up walking away. All in the same month as his best performance ever.

### The Analogy

*"If someone stole $10 from you, would you spend $2,000 chasing them? If someone ruined 10 minutes of your day, would you throw away the next 7 days dwelling on it?"*

Don't let one bad outcome destroy a week of momentum.

### Dealing with Self-Doubt

Common doubts every new advisor faces:

-   *"Am I suitable for this career?"*
    
-   *"I don't know the products well enough"*
    
-   *"I can't present / can't cold call / can't close"*
    

**Answer:** Just do it. Theory without action produces nothing. Even the best product knowledge in the industry is worthless without the four business-generating activities.

## Part 6: Cold Calling — The Pain Point Script

### Why Traditional Scripts Fail

-   Old approach: Generic script, present financial planning broadly → 1 appointment per hour of calling
    
-   **Pain point approach:** Find the specific problem for each individual → **2–3 appointments per calling session**
    

### The Framework

**Step 1: Position yourself as relevant to their situation**

-   *"We help a lot of young adults like yourself learn how to grow their money rather than letting it sit in the bank losing to inflation."*
    

**Step 2: Ask the qualifying question**

-   *"Currently, are you investing already, or are you still saving in the bank?"*
    

This splits prospects into two paths with different pain points:

**Step 3: Match their pain point to your Zoom agenda**

-   Whatever their pain point is = your agenda for the meeting
    
-   Be specific to their situation — don't pitch a generic "financial planning" session
    
-   *"Since you mentioned you're scared of losing money, maybe we can hop on a 30-minute Zoom and I can share some strategies on how to reduce your investment risk. No harm, right?"*
    

**Step 4: Elicit, don't tell**

-   Get them to tell YOU their problem — don't announce it
    
-   Ask questions that naturally surface the pain
    
-   Once they verbalize the problem, they become emotionally invested in finding the solution

## Key Takeaways

1.  **APA is not just a lump sum product** — pitch the monthly dividend income, the zero supplementary charge, and the fund-switching flexibility

3.  **Spend your time on business-generating activities** — calling, opening, closing, following up. Everything else is secondary.

5.  **Manage your emotions** — one day to feel bad, then back to work. Don't let peaks make you complacent or valleys make you quit.
    
6.  **Pain point selling beats generic scripts** — find their specific problem, make it your Zoom agenda, and they'll show up.
    
7.  **Conviction matters** — believe that what you do genuinely helps people. That authenticity is what prospects sense, and it's what makes you sustainable long-term.
