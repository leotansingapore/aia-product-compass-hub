## 💼 2 Hour Read - Overview Of Prospecting

### 🔥 What Sets Successful Agents Apart From The Rest

*“Approach Each customer with the idea of helping him or her solve a problem or achieve a goal, not selling a product or service”*

~ Brian Tracy

The art of prospecting is the key factor that differentiates successful agents from those who struggle. Most of us are not fortunate enough to have strong connections or wealthy parents to fall back on. Relying solely on a warm market will only take you so far, as it eventually runs dry. Chances are, you don’t have a large pool of clients ready to buy—those who are ready have likely already made their purchases.

---

### ✅5 Essential Daily Activities in FinancialAdvisory

Mastering the five essential daily activities in financial advisory is crucial for building a sustainable and thriving practice.

**The first pillar is lead generation**—the foundation of any successful business. Contrary to what many believe, leads are everywhere. Your existing social network—Facebook friends, LinkedIn connections, Instagram followers, even casual acquaintances—are all potential leads. The misconception that leads are scarce often stems from a lack of awareness, not a lack of opportunities. You don’t need a marketing agency to generate leads; you need to be proactive in engaging with the people already within your reach.

**The second pillar is lead nurturing**, which involves fostering relationships and increasing engagement over time. A common mistake advisors make is assuming that a lead will convert simply because they exist. However, leads need to be warmed up and nurtured before they become clients. This can be done through regular social media engagement, personalized check-ins, or inviting them into value-driven conversations. Creating an ecosystem—like a Telegram channel, a newsletter, or an Instagram page showcasing insights and testimonials—keeps you top of mind while establishing credibility and trust.

**The third pillar is sales.** At the end of the day, financial advisors are in the business of selling, not just consulting. You don’t get paid for generating leads or setting appointments—you get paid when you **close deals**. This means refining your sales process: knowing the right questions to ask, mastering appointment structures, handling objections, and smoothly transitioning from one appointment to the next. The advisors who struggle the most are often the ones who resist selling, but those who embrace it as an essential skillset create long-term success.

**The fourth pillar is service delivery.** Selling a plan is only the beginning—the real work begins after a client signs on. The best advisors don’t just close deals; they build **lifelong relationships.** Think of your clients as members of an exclusive club—what perks can you offer that go beyond policies and premiums? Whether it’s personalized financial reviews, ongoing guidance, or simply checking in over coffee, clients value a trusted relationship over just another transaction. When clients **feel genuinely cared for,** cross-selling and upselling become effortless, and referrals flow naturally.

**The final pillar is retention and reselling.** Too many advisors chase new clients while neglecting the ones they already have. The true measure of a sustainable business isn’t just in acquiring clients but in **keeping them engaged** and **ensuring they continue to see value in working with you.** A well-serviced client is a lifelong client—and a source of repeat business, referrals, and testimonials. Ask yourself: how many of your clients would **immediately** pick up your call and genuinely view you as their go-to financial advisor? If the answer isn’t most of them, then your focus should shift towards strengthening those relationships.

By mastering these five activities—**lead generation, lead nurturing, sales, service, and retention**—you don’t just build a business; you build an ecosystem of trust, value, and long-term success.

### 🔄 The Three Key Activities In A Sales Cycle

![](https://hgdbflprrficdoyxmdxe.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/knowledge-files/editor-images/1771987210640-gl4674ufysh.png)

There are mainly three key activities in the sales cycle:

1.  Setting the appointment (Calling): Calling, or appointment setting, sets the stage for higher, and more quality appointments.
    
2.  Conducting the appointment (Closing): Closing, and mastering the art of influence, persuasion, and conversational skills to make the prospect feel heard, increases the likelihood of closing the case.
    
3.  After sales service (Servicing): Servicing the lead, helps you to increase the likelihood of referrals, or repeat business.
    

Ultimately, how well you **call, close, and service** clients determines your sales cycle efficiency. Effective calls lead to meaningful connections, strong closing skills convert those connections into clients, and excellent service keeps those clients satisfied and loyal. All three aspects work together to create a seamless and productive sales process.

### 🚀 Important Levers in Raising Your Sales Outcomes

Your outcome, and your sales income can simply be broken down by the following formula:

**Quantity of Outreach** (QO) X **Appointment Set Rate** (ASR) X **Show Up Rate** (SUR) X **Close Rate** (CR) X **Lifetime Value** (LTV).

The game of sales is purely a game of increasing any one of these “levers” for your business. We have control over many of these levers for our business.

1.  **Quantity of outreach (QO)** simply refers to how many attempts you’ve made to set appointments. This can be as simple as just calling your leads, reaching out to a friend, giving them an email, running facebook ads and getting impressions. This can be improved by simply doing more of the same thing, over and over again.
    
2.  **Appointment Set Rates (ASR)** can be improved by improving the **quality** of the outreach, perhaps by being more targeted, more genuine, gaining more trust, or more simply nurturing the lead slowly and consistently over time. Im a strong proponent of being patient in nurturing a lead. We have limited leads, and it usually takes time to “warm up” or trust an individual to want to meet them.
    
3.  **Show Up Rates (SUR)** can be improved by reminding the lead consistently before the call, ensuring the lead understands the purpose of the appointment, or increasing the perceived value of the appointment.
    
4.  **Close Rates** (Close Rates) can be improved through better relational skills, the use of analogies, making things easy and clear to understand, demonstrating competency, making the problem clear, urgent, and evident enough for prospects to take action. Interestingly, I think consistent follow ups play a part too, as prospects may not want to decide on the spot, and sometimes it may take weeks or months or even years for them to make a decision.
    
5.  **Lifetime Value** (LTV) can be improved through regular close contact with an existing client. I remember most of my clients first started off by buying plans of very low case sizes when I first started out. However, 90% of my business now is from repeat business, and trust being built up over time with my clients. Clients won’t just buy once, but many times as you stay with them over the years. So, being available, initiating calls and client reviews are really important, to keep yourself top of mind, before they are “stolen” by other consultants.
    

Let’s have an example.

200 Quantity of Outreach (QO): Suppose you make 200 calls per week to reach out. So that’s 200 for this metric.

5% Appointment Set Rate (ASR): Suppose out of this 200, only 5% agree to meet, so that’s just 10 potential appointments.

30% Show Up Rates (SUR): Suppose out of these 10 appointments, only 3 actually showed up.

30% Close Rates (CR): Supposed out of this 3, only 1 decided to make a purchase.

$5k Lifetime Value (LTV): Suppose out of this 1 close, the total commissions (new and repeat business, and all his potential referrals, work out to be around $5k)

So let’s plug those numbers in -

So your weekly sales income can simply be calculated as;

200 X 5% X 30% X 30%$5k = $5k

And that works out to be **$20k per month.**

These are not unrealistic numbers.

What is a good metric for each? To set expectations, let’s break them down to see if they are realistic and achievable or not.

## QUANTITY OF OUTREACH (QO)

A good QO really depends on whether you are doing warm or cold market. As a rule of thumb, warm market outreaches tend to be more targeted and, hence of a lower quantity compared to the cold market.

Cold market QO, which usually can come in the form of

1.  **One to one outreach:** Cold calling, roadshows, door knocks
    
2.  **One to many outreach:** Running ads
    

Warm market QO, which can usually come in the form of

1.  **One to one outreach:** catching up with a friend, DMing them on social media, calling your list of acquaintances.
    
2.  **One to many outreach:** Posting on instagram, facebook, linkedin. Content creation on social media. If you have a strong social media following, it will be helpful to leverage and tap on that. Building up a strong organic presence, although a lengthy process, will help you to reduce the effort needed to build up trust and hence improve closing rates (CR), appointment setting rates (ASR), and lifetime value (LTV) over time.
    

For me, my QO was in the form of cold calling, at a rate of around 500-1000 per week.

This is a metric that is fully within our control. How much, how often we reach out sometimes just boils down to our “inner drive” and our motivation. Out of all of the above metrics, it is the single most important “lever” that we can control. Sometimes, we can’t actually control how many percent of people buy, how much they buy, how often they buy, but we **can control** how many people we reach out to.

Given that this is the **single most controllable** metric, then we ought to squeeze the most out of it. Remember, **repetition negates luck.** So it's not “lucky”that you got a referral, or someone decided to buy. It is simply, just the law of large numbers, working for, or against you, over a long period of time. Not tomorrow, not next week, but over months of sheer dedicated outreach to see this work to your favor. And sometimes, the “little bits’ of effort compound over time. That extra 1 hour of calling, translates to 50 additional calls, and over a month that means an additional whopping 1500 calls.

So what is a good “QO” number?. Is is simply a measure of how many hours you work doing outreach X the intensity of each hour.

As a student, I was only able to call around 1-2 hours per day. Hence, the “intensity” of each hour doing outreach had to be as high as possible. As such, I was calling with **two phones at each time.** It sounds crazy, but it actually makes the most sense. I can’t really “double” my show up rates, nor my closing rates. In fact, it would be very hard for someone to do so. Perhaps, little adjustments in their slide decks, or body language, or choice of words can perhaps increase the closing or show up rates by perhaps 10-20%. But it would be very hard to **double** the levers at the “back” of the formula.

Hence, you should be looking to maximize the “front” part of the equations, which refers mainly to **QO**. This is the single most controllable lever that you can pull. Simply by doubling my call rates by using two phones, I could double the “flow” into the sales system. By doubling my repetitions of sales calls, I had the side benefit of being more able to split test various sales scripts, presentation decks, and gain more competency and confidence in my zoom appointments, on top of doubling my sales compared to everyone else, if all else remains the same, and spending the **same amount of time** doing outreach.

So let’s go back to the question, **so how much outreach is enough?**

Just for context, I pay my virtual assistants $5/hour, to cold call a minimum of 40-50 numbers per hour. Hence, if you’re focusing on doing cold calling, and you can only spend 3 hours per day doing it, then you should be dialing at least 150 numbers a day. Take note that usually only 20% of them will pick up the phone call, so that works out to be a QO of 30 per day, or 200 per week.

Everyone has 3 hours a day, and hence the bottleneck here usually even the lack of time, it is simply the lack of motivation, discipline, or willpower to follow through with 3 hours. Hence, a good remedy for this might be to “pair” QO with something more enjoyable, such as watching a show whilst cold calling.

Alternatively, focus on reaching out to people that you already know, if you’re not up for the sheer hard work and grit required for cold prospecting.

Ironically, whilst it is idealistic to think that you don’t want and don’t need to reach out to your friends, sometimes the reality is that reaching out to people that you know **will drastically reduce the QO required.** But of course, reaching out to your warm market, or friends, may be something that cannot be sustained over time, if you don’t have a new source of fresh leads, such as warm referrals from the friends who have bought/met you. Hence, the “evergreen” nature of cold prospecting, that requires, more often than not, sheer hard work and repetition.

Fortunately, I have the privilege of honing my cold prospecting over the years, and I strive to support my team of consultants in that regard, through a good database of leads, a team of virtual assistants, and a strong social media advertising platform.

## APPOINTMENT SETTING RATES (ASR)

There is usually a balance between the volume of your leads and the intent of your leads.

Perhaps, you might want the most ready to buy leads, or the most qualified leads. But usually, that is not the case. These ready to buy leads, if they are truly ready to buy, and wanting to buy, would have already bought.

Hence, most of the leads that “agree to meet” you simply may just be problem-unaware people, or, people who don’t have a true intention to buy **yet**, or are simply just curious to what you have to say, or just want to learn more.

Further, with the rising competition of other financial advisors, and the increasing rate of scams, and also the increasing “busyness” of people and proliferation of easy information on the internet, people are thus increasingly wary and hesitant to set aside time for you.

Hence, there is a great barrier, with regards to people actually showing up for an appointment.

Further, people may be wary that you are just trying to “sell them something”. Thankfully, there are three thumbs and principles that i have for you, when applied collectively, can help to squeeze out and maximize the show up rates of all your appointments.

In general, the end result of all these rules and principles are simply to give prospects a better perception of value towards the appointments.

Three ASR Improvement Tips:

1.  Have a lead magnet in place (provide free value)
    
2.  Increase the perceived value of the appointment (this also applies for closing rates too)
    
3.  Be highly targeted in your messaging (the riches is in the niches)
    

#1: HAVE A LEAD MAGNET IN PLACE

This usually comes in the form of a free guidebook or checklist Something that demonstrates you as an authority leader AND something that your target audience would want to read.

Alex Hormozi’s book $100m offer talks about the idea of a “Grand Slam Offer”. Basically, make an offer so good that it looks dumb to say no to your appointment. Part of that would be the use of a lead magnet, something of so much value that it is worth their time to actually show up to the appointment. You knowing how valuable you are isn’t going to be enough if you don’t **communicate** or raise the value of it to outsiders. Even if your appointment is free, people are still constantly thinking about the opportunity cost of that time. What can I do instead of showing up to your appointment?

It should be easy to create a guidebook using the help of some creativity, chatgpt, and a graphic designer (you can hire or virtual assistant for this). To create one, you can follow these steps:

-   Ideate the topic
    
-   Create the outline
    
-   Use chatgpt to flesh out the content or write it manually
    
-   With your branding + logo, get a graphic designer to create the lead magnet.
    

A lead magnet is usually something that can be read within 30 minutes. With a lead magnet, leads would usually be more incentivized to then show up for the appointment.

Here are some examples; **Checklist or Cheat Sheet:** “10 Steps to Financial Freedom Before Age 50.

**Quiz or Assessment:** “Are You On Track for Retirement? Take Our 5-Minute Quiz.

Be creative and use chatgpt to come up with more ideas, flesh out the topic, and bring your ideas to reality!

#2: INCREASE THE PERCEIVED VALUE OF THE APPOINTMENT

Why should leads meet with you? What will they get? They could have stayed at home, did their own thing, so why should they go out of their way to meet you? There are so many ads, so many consultants, and so many people trying to sell them something. You have to raise the value of your appointment

Here are some ideas:

1.  You will receive a set of financial planning resources after the session
    
2.  You will get a custom financial report after the consultation
    
3.  You will get \[benefit 1\], \[benefit 2\], during/after the consultation.
    

Here’s an example:

*“In our complimentary consultation, we’ll help you identify gaps in your plan, discover hidden opportunities for growth, and create a step-by-step roadmap to achieving your goals faster. You’ll also receive a free retirement readiness assessment, a personalized portfolio review, and actionable tips to help you cut decades to reaching financial freedom. My slots usually get filled up quickly, but would XX time work for you?”*

Do think about demonstrating credibility even before the lead meets you, such as a well designed website with testimonials and other authority signals.

#3: BE HIGHLY TARGETED IN YOUR MESSAGE

As the saying goes, the riches in your niches. It's not just about being targeted in your messaging, but also the messaging in your outreach.

To be highly targeted in your messaging, focus on deeply understanding your ideal audience, tailoring your message to their specific needs, and delivering it in the right way at the right time.

The main principle is that, just imagine that you are directly talking to that person. Tune your messaging to their goals, their pain points, and their language, and make them feel as though you deeply understand them, and as though your service is just “perfect” for them and not just for anyone and everyone else.

**Example:**

For a financial advisor targeting young adults:

-   **Pain Point:** “I want to invest but don’t know where to start.”
    
-   **Goal:** “I want to grow my money faster, but don’t want to spend too much time and effort learning/doing investing myself”
    
-   **Language:** Simple, clear, straight to the point.
    

## SHOW UP RATES (SUR)

Good show up rates should be around 50-60% or more, after they have confirmed the appointment.

Many of the psychological principles used in the appointment setting rates do overlap with the objective of improving show up rates. For instance, increasing the **perceived value of the appointment**, via demonstrating authority, social proof, scarcity, being very targeted in your messaging, and so on

Hence, we will move on to slightly more mechanical and hard rules when it comes to improving show up rates.

One problem that I notice with new consultants is the fact they miss out on not so warm leads.

Perhaps, they only meet leads that confirm the first time, and assume that the lead will show up. Or perhaps, they don’t make an effort to reschedule with leads that didn’t show up.

Or maybe, they forget to remind leads to show up, increasing the likelihood of missed appointments.

However, we will dive into some real and concrete example of how we never miss out on a lead.

**#1: No Missed Follow Ups**

As the name implies, a rule of thumb is always to have consistent follow ups for all leads, for at least 2-3 times before the appointment.

**#2: Maintain Calendar 0**

This is an extremely important rule. The main idea here is that once a calendar event shows up on your calendar, it NEVER leaves or erodes, and there will always be another event in the future for follow up. For all follow ups, missed events, or no shows, simply just shift the calendar event to a another timeslot later in the week to be “acted upon again”, so that you end the calendar day with zero events, even if you started with 10

-   Missed appointments? -> shift it to sunday 10pm, recurring weekly
    
-   Need to remind yourself to “follow up” with a lead? -> shift it to sunday 10pm, recurring weekly.
    
-   Need to set a follow up appointment? -> shift it to a calendar event a few weeks from now, to remind yourself to follow up with the lead, and make it recurring if you don’t want it to be leaked.
    

For instance, suppose you have 3 appointments today on your calendar. Out of the 3 appointments, 1 **didn’t show up**, 1 **agreed to a meeting**, and 1 is **not a qualified lead** now, but may become one, 2 years from now upon graduation from university.

For the appointment that didn’t show up - shift it to Sunday 10pm recurring.

For the appointment that agreed to a second meeting, shift the event to the date that the lead wants to meet.

And lastly, for the lead that isn’t a qualified lead for now, place the lead to an event 2 years from now to wish him/her a milestone to follow up again and arrange another call.

What you can do is to shift the google calendar event so a recurring time slot whereby you place all missed appointments at that time, for example -> Sunday 10pm, repeating weekly on your calendar.

## CLOSE RATES (CR)

Now that the prospect in front of you, how easy is it to close? How well can you influence him/her to take action? What do we do during the appointment to spark action?

Well, it involves creating, and magnifying the problem enough such that it is painful enough for him/her to take action. Without a problem, there can be no solution. Without an condition, there can be no prescription.

A doctor’s job is to diagnose a patient’s condition. Hence, your role is to diagnose your prospect, to show him a clear problem, to point him/her to the right direction.

Problem magnification is so important. There can be no solution, without a problem. This topic is large enough that I will be dedicating a chapter on it on its own. The perceived value of a solution is also equally important.

Apart from magnifying the problem, persuasion principles come into play, which I will expound in the later parts of this book.

## LIFETIME VALUE (LTV)

Recently, I met a client whom I first sold in 2019 a really small savings policy. He lapsed the policy after a few months due to a lack of financial stability.

Moving forward 5 years today, he’s now a chef at Fico, a well known Italian restaurant. He doesn’t have any policies yet, and it’s almost a confirmed closed deal that he will buy an investing plan and coverage policies with me of total $600 per month starting in Feb once his pay increases further (a jump of 50%)

Nothing special in my presentation, just the very same one again and again. But it was just the power of **playing the long game**. Most agents would have given up on him ever since he lapsed his policies.

The majority of my cases now are simply just repeat business from my first few customers more than 5 years ago. They had really small policies, but as their income got bigger, they bought more. When I first met my clients, they might have just been students, without a job, but many of them are now lawyers, doctors, engineers, accountants, and so on.

Many advisors simply just give up really fast. They only think about the immediate sale that the prospect can give them. They only think about the immediate commissions that they can get from the sale. Most advisors only think about whether the prospect is **qualified** now, but not in the future.

Its not just about the immediate sale, but the future sale. It's not about the immediate commissions, but about the LTV of all the lifetime commissions and repeat business. Its not just about whether the prospect is qualified **now,** but also in the future. The LTV of each customer, is also the LTV of each **prospect**, and that’s how far I’m willing to **wait**, because I know the LTV is worth it.

In essence, the LTV of a customer is the summation of how well you play the long game, and how far in terms of timeline you see in the future. The LTV can get from each customer is the sum of the initial sale **and repeat** sales from a certain customer.

As long as you can play the long game, customers will buy from you, repeatedly. As long as you follow up enough, add value, give them consistent micro touchpoints long enough, they will remember you and buy from you again.

In fact, if you don’t follow up with them, they will just go and buy from someone else. It is the process of keeping in touch, the little touchpoints, the little birthday gifts, calls, and messages that I’ve sent to keep them “in my list” that has contributed to that.

There should always be touchpoints, constantly with every client. It doesn’t have to be a physical 2 hour review all the time, but just little gestures to show that “you’re still there” and you’re still there for them.

I would estimate that the LTV of every customer is approximately $5,000 on the lower end, and $15,000 on the higher end. By recognising and understand the immense LTV of each customer, this gives us more **waiting power** and **buying power** to acquire each and every prospect.

If the LTV of each customer is only $1,000, then perhaps you can only afford a few hours of your time, and maybe a few hundred dollars to acquire each customer.

But if you can recognise that the LTV of each customer is $5,000, then you will spend many more hours of your time, and more than just a few hundred dollars to acquire each new customer, because you know its worth it.

Hence, understanding LTV is so important. Sum up the lifetime commissions of the sale + future business. Even if the lead isn’t qualified, you can convert it, eventually.

Here are some other tactical ways to increase LTV:

1.  **Increase Case Size**: Encourage clients to purchase larger or more comprehensive packages to boost the value of each sale. Tailor your offerings to meet more of their needs, demonstrating the benefits of upgrading.
    
2.  **Increase Referability**: Make yourself referable by providing excellent service and delivering beyond expectations. Create an experience so positive that clients are eager to recommend you to others.
    
3.  **Have More Touchpoints**: Stay in regular contact with your clients through newsletters, emails, calls, or social media. These touchpoints help to maintain top-of-mind awareness and reinforce your relationship.
    
4.  **Consistent Client Reviews**: Schedule regular reviews with clients to assess their evolving needs and offer solutions that adapt to their changing circumstances. This ensures that you stay relevant and continue adding value.
    
5.  **Build Relationships Over Time**: Develop a long-term relationship by showing genuine interest in your clients' lives. Celebrate milestones with them, check in periodically, and be consistent in your communication.
    
6.  **Ask for Referrals**: Actively seek referrals from satisfied clients. If they’re happy with your services, ask if they know anyone else who could benefit from what you offer. This helps to expand your network and increase lifetime value (LTV).
    
7.  **Maintain a Calendar of Follow-Up Events**: Keep track of client milestones—such as policy anniversaries, birthdays, or important life events—and use them as opportunities to reach out and provide personalized follow-up, which can lead to increased loyalty and additional sales.
    

## TWEAKING THE LEVERS

Now that we know that the total output of our business is described by the following formula:

**Quantity of Outreach** (QO) X **Appointment Set Rate** (ASR) X **Show Up Rate** (SUR) X **Close Rate** (CR) X **Lifetime Value** (LTV), which lever should I focus on first? Do I optimize from back to front, or from front to back?

I would recommend going from front to back. Meaning, one should always seek to improve and maximise QO, ASR, SUR, CR and then LTV in that order.

Why?

1.  You can’t just double your LTV overnight, it's more of a “mindset” and long term thing.
    
2.  You can’t really double your CR with a snap of your fingers either, because it takes practice and consistent effort. In fact, a closing rate of 30% is good, and a rate of 50% is insanely good
    
3.  You can’t just magically double your Show Up Rate either (following best practices, marginal improvements tends to taper off over time)
    

Essentially, as the levers at the back, tend to be harder to optimise after a certain point. But, the quantity of outreach and appointment setting rate can be improved much easily, by intentional efforts.

For example ,instead of dialling 100 dials per day, you can just raise it to 200 dials per day (e.g using two phones, hiring another person to dial for you, or just putting in more effort through sheer brute force). If you have a 1% ASR, you can double it to 2% by following the best practices previously mentioned. Improving the flow into your sales system is a low hanging fruit you need to capitalize on, before making tactical adjustments and optimisations in the “back-end” of your sales system.

By doubling your QO and doubling your ASR, you essentially **quadruple** your input and flow into the system**.** By tripling your QO and tripling your ASR, your essentially raise the output of your business by **nine times.** It would be hard, if not impossible to raise the CR, and LTV of each customer nine times.

Hence, the bottleneck for most new advisors, simply just **getting more flow** into the system is the biggest bottleneck they are facing. It's not about skill yet. How you improve your skills is through greater and more frequent **repetitions and having more volume.**

Most advisors get stuck thinking it's about a lack of a special formula or a special tactic or trade secret. But what's most important is really just about repetitions and the idea of having more **quantity.**

## QUANTITY LEADS TO QUALITY - A PARABLE

With more **repetitions** through reaching out more, new advisors can practice how to set appointments. And with more appointments being set, they can then practice more, close more, close better, and close more confidently.

In David Bayles and Ted Orland’s book, Art & Fear:

*“ \[A\] ceramics teacher announced on opening day that he was dividing the class into two groups. All those on the left side of the studio, he said, would be graded solely on the quantity of work they produced, all those on the right solely on its quality. His procedure was simple: on the final day of class he would bring in his bathroom scales and weigh the work of the “quantity” group: fifty pound of pots rated an “A”, forty pounds a “B”, and so on. Those being graded on “quality”, however, needed to produce only one pot — albeit a perfect one — to get an “A”. Well, came grading time and a curious fact emerged:* ***the works of highest quality were all produced by the group being graded for quantity.*** *It seems that while the* ***“quantity”*** *group was busily churning out piles of work – and learning from their mistakes — the* ***“quality” group had sat theorizing about perfectio****n, and in the end had little more to show for their efforts than grandiose theories and a pile of dead clay.”*

So **Quantity leads to Quality.** Instead of worrying and perfecting your sales script to close big and close a perfect case, chase after quantity first. Start with repetition, not perfection.

### 📅 Obey the 30 Day Rule

More often than not, a slump in sales is usually caused by a few factors -> you were either complacent, due to a good sale in the previous month, or, you had a slump in prospecting **30 days prior.**

So what is the 30 Day Rule?

*“The 30-Day Rule states that the prospecting you do in this 30-day period will pay off for the next 90 days. It is a simple, yet powerful universal rule that governs sales and you ignore it at your peril. When you internalize this rule, it will drive you to never put prospecting aside for another day. The implication of the 30-Day Rule is simple. Miss a day of prospecting and it will tend to bite you sometime in the next 90 days. Miss a week and you will feel it in your commission check. Miss the entire month and you will tank your pipeline, fall into a slump, and wake up 90 days later desperate, feeling like a loser, with no clue how you ended up there.”*

In sales, or in any endeavour worth doing, consistency is key. All your prospecting efforts may not pay off right now, but will pay off later, in 30 days time.

If you skip a day or a week of prospecting, you create gaps in your pipeline. Even if they may not be immediately apparent, they will manifest eventually as a shortage of new clients or closed deals **down the line.** Any missed day has the potential to disrupt your momentum, and dry up your pipeline.

Missed a day? It may not seem significant, but it can disrupt your routine and **momentum.** These missed days can accumulate, leading to fewer leads and appointments.

Don’t expect sales today or tomorrow. Adopt the attitude of a consistent farmer. The seasons may change, but what you can do is to consistently plant the seeds, which may or may not harvest 30 days from now. There might be a storm, and there may be many external factors out of your control, but what you **can control** is to simply plant the seeds, day or day. It is simply not a matter of “if”, but “when” your seeds will sprout.

The more seeds you plant, and the more consistent it is being done, the more crops you can harvest when the time is ripe.

### 📉 Why Salespeople Face a Slump

A sales slump is often the result of activities—or lack thereof—performed 30 days prior. This is why many salespeople experience the “rollercoaster” effect in their sales performance.

For example, in one month, a salesperson may focus solely on closing hot deals, neglecting the need to generate new leads. This might result in high sales for that month, but the following month sees a sharp decline because there are no fresh leads in the pipeline. This pattern highlights an underlying issue in activity balance and consistency.

To diagnose the cause of a slump, consider these two common problems:

1.  **Complacency and Overconfidence**
    

Success can sometimes breed complacency. After achieving a good month, a salesperson might ease off on their efforts, allowing temporary success to distract them from ongoing activities. This issue is more emotional and discipline-related.

The phrase “Don’t let success get to your head, and don’t let failure get to your heart” applies well here. Many advisors experience stellar months only to follow them with little to no sales. This happens when they reach a goal and begin to stagnate. Complacency sets in, leading to a lack of growth.

The solution? Shift the focus from external goals and rewards, such as recognition or material gains, to the intrinsic joy of the sales process itself. Growth should remain a constant pursuit, not tied to any particular milestone. Happiness in sales should stem from daily efforts and the process, rather than the fleeting satisfaction of achieving a target.

2.  **Lack of Capacity and Systems**
    

Another reason for a slump may be a lack of capacity to prospect for new leads. When a salesperson spends all their time closing deals without time for prospecting, the result is a dry pipeline. This often indicates insufficient systems, routines, or habits to maintain a consistent flow of new leads.

-   **Investing in support staff**: Personal assistants can handle administrative tasks, freeing time for prospecting.
    
-   **Dedicated telemarketers**: These professionals can take over lead generation by making calls daily.
    
-   **Paid advertising**: Online ads can help maintain a steady influx of prospects without direct effort.
    

By creating systems that ensure daily lead generation, salespeople can maintain a healthy pipeline and avoid future slumps.

### ❤️‍🔥 Loving Rejections

There was a time when I was in the office making my own calls, and there was a telemarketer sitting beside me. She was some leads sharing with them about the importance of integrated shield policies. I could tell that she was really putting in a lot of effort to a particular sales call, spending a lot of time trying to convince a clearly uninterested and skeptical prospect about the importance of integrated shield policies. After the long and exhausting call, I could tell that she was clearly de-motivated about it.

After a few weeks, I didn't see her in the office anymore. It was clear that she poured a lot of emotions into every single call, and these rejections started to wear her down. What I would suggest is that we need to *flip the switch* and label rejections as a good thing instead. The fact is that we need to overcome *149* rejections before a sale is made. So, in fact, we need to **love rejections**

These are usually the ratios to follow: *150 Opening Approaches (e.g calls) -> 3 Appointments set -> 1 Close.*

So, based on this formula, it is **perfectly normal** to face 149 rejections before having that 1 close. In fact, rejections are **perfectly normal**, based on the law of large numbers. **.** Instead of counting the number of appointments you set, count the number of rejections you have and just aim for as high a number as possible. The more rejections you have, great, that means you're on the right track! By the law of large numbers, rejections are an inevitable part of the sales process.

**Abundant Mindset**

An abundant mindset, simply, is one that knows that there is something more, and greater than you currently have right now. This is in comparison to a *scarcity* mindset, whereby you think that resources are limited.

With a scarcity mindset, you start to see each rejection as an obstacle hindering you from achieving your goals, and start to get frustrated thinking that there will be no more leads next.

With an abundant mindset, you start to see each rejection as **normal**, and **necessary** for you to achieve your goal, and feel refreshed thinking that you can simply just move on *to the next lead* and not waste time further/energy/resources on a bad lead.

A rejection is a rejection, and we can't change that, but what we can change is our attitude towards it. Having an *abundant mindset* is one that says "yes, there are indeed delulu people out there, but the world is so big, so even if I meet one delulu person, there are many many many more people who are not as delulu as this one". There is ripe, fresh fruit ready to be plucked, but just that you haven't found these people yet. By keeping up the activity and volume, the sale will happen eventually.

Every single "no" is a step closer to the next "yes". This reinforces the importance of high activity levels and persistence.

To apply consistent prospecting effectively, it’s essential to incorporate structured practices into your daily routine. Here’s how you can ensure consistent growth and maintain a steady pipeline:

Start by setting **daily prospecting goals**. Establish a minimum number of activities—such as calls, emails, or meetings—that you must complete every day. This ensures you’re actively building your pipeline, no matter how busy or successful you are at any given time.

Next, make it a habit to **track your progress**. Keep a detailed prospecting log to record your daily activities. This simple practice keeps you accountable and allows you to review your efforts regularly. By evaluating your progress, you can identify any shortfalls and make adjustments before they escalate into larger issues.

Treat prospecting as a **non-negotiable task**. Schedule dedicated time for prospecting activities each day and adhere to it with the same discipline as you would for client meetings or key deadlines. Making prospecting a priority in your schedule helps solidify it as an unshakable habit.

To stay consistent, focus on **the long-term benefits**. Remind yourself that the effort you put into prospecting today will yield results in the weeks and months ahead. This perspective can help you stay motivated, especially during periods when immediate outcomes aren’t visible.

Lastly, **address gaps quickly**. If you miss a day or fall behind on your goals, act swiftly to make up for the lost time. Whether it’s doubling your efforts the next day or spreading the additional workload across the week, addressing these lapses ensures you maintain momentum and avoid compounding setbacks.

By following these steps, you can establish a sustainable rhythm in your prospecting efforts, setting yourself up for consistent success in the long run.

### 🌱 The Power of Nurturing Semi Warm Leads

The way you approach a prospect should depend on the **temperature of your relationship** with them. Just like how hugging a close friend feels natural while hugging a stranger feels awkward, the same message can be received very differently based on familiarity. **Hot prospects**—close friends and family—are easy to approach but may not always be ready to buy. **Warm prospects**—those you interact with frequently—are open to conversation but may need more time before making a decision. However, the **semi-warm market** is where **hidden potential** lies. This group includes acquaintances, casual friends, referrals, and people you once had a good relationship with but haven’t spoken to in a while. Unlike cold leads, who require significant effort to build rapport, or hot leads, who are quickly exhausted, **semi-warm prospects offer the best balance of familiarity and opportunity** with far less competition.

Many advisors make the mistake of either **only focusing on hot leads**, which quickly run out, or **spending too much time on cold leads**, which require extensive nurturing. The key is to recognize that **relationship temperature and market readiness are separate factors**—a close friend might not be interested in financial planning, while a semi-warm lead might be open to it. By **strategically nurturing** semi-warm prospects, offering valuable insights, and gradually warming them up, you **create a pipeline of future clients**. Instead of constantly chasing new prospects, you develop relationships that naturally **convert over time**. Focus on **re-engagement, consistency, and adding value**, and you’ll find that some of your best opportunities come from those you least expect.

### 🛑 Identifying Bottlenecks in Your Pipeline

Every sales professional, no matter their experience, faces bottlenecks—specific weaknesses that limit their growth and potential. These bottlenecks act as invisible barriers, restricting progress despite the effort put in. The problem is that most people don’t take the time to diagnose the real issue. Instead, they blindly work harder, thinking more calls, more pitches, or more leads will magically solve the problem. But effort alone isn’t the solution—*precision is.*

Think of a sales career as a pipeline. When water flows through a pipe, any clog along the way will slow or completely stop the flow. If you try to increase the water pressure without fixing the blockage, nothing improves. Sales operates the same way. If you’re making 100 cold calls and only booking a handful of appointments, your outreach strategy is the bottleneck. If you’re setting plenty of meetings, but no one buys, your closing skills are weak. Without identifying the actual problem, all your additional effort will be wasted on fixing the wrong thing.

To systematically break through bottlenecks, you must examine three key areas: **skills, traits, and beliefs.**

1.  **Skills: The Mechanics of Sales**
    

Skills are the technical abilities that directly affect performance—financial knowledge, communication, negotiation, and objection handling. These are things you can learn, practice, and improve through training. If your sales process isn’t yielding results, ask yourself: *Do I lack the skills necessary to execute effectively?*

For example, a common bottleneck for many salespeople is weak communication. If you fail to articulate value clearly, you won’t inspire confidence in your prospect. If your presentations lack conviction, your close rates will suffer. Fortunately, skills are the easiest bottlenecks to fix because they can be developed through deliberate practice, coaching, and structured learning.

2.  **Traits: The Internal Foundation for Success**
    

While skills determine *what* you can do, traits determine *how consistently* you apply them. Sales is a field where rejection, inconsistency, and unpredictability are part of the game. Traits like discipline, confidence, resilience, and adaptability separate top performers from those who struggle.

A salesperson with strong closing skills but no discipline may start strong but fail to follow up, losing deals that could have been won. Someone with deep industry knowledge but lacking self-confidence might hesitate when pitching to high-level clients, leading to missed opportunities. The truth is, no amount of skill can compensate for a lack of consistency and drive.

3.  **Beliefs: The Hidden Scripts That Run Your Business**
    

Perhaps the most overlooked but powerful bottleneck is belief. Beliefs shape how you see the world, how you interpret rejection, and how you approach your work. If you believe that cold calling doesn’t work, you’ll avoid making calls or do them half-heartedly. If you think social media is ineffective, you won’t optimize it for lead generation.

The most successful salespeople don’t just *do* the work—they *believe* in the process. They see rejection as feedback, not failure. They understand that selling is a skill that can be refined, not an innate talent. When you shift your beliefs, you change the way you act, and over time, your actions will create better results.

### **How to Identify and Fix Your Bottleneck**

Most salespeople fail because they either don’t track their performance or don’t analyze the right metrics. You can’t fix a problem if you don’t know where it exists. The best way to identify your bottleneck is to systematically track your numbers:

1.  How many calls are you making?
    
2.  How many meetings are you booking?
    
3.  How many meetings result in a closed deal?
    
4.  Where in your process are people dropping off?
    

If you struggle to book meetings, your prospecting or outreach strategy needs work. If people meet with you but don’t buy, your presentation or closing skills need refining. If your sales cycle is inconsistent, your discipline or follow-up process is weak.

Once you identify the bottleneck, take targeted action. If it’s a **skill issue**, invest in training and mentorship. If it’s a **trait issue**, build routines and systems that force consistency. If it’s a **belief issue**, challenge and reframe your limiting thoughts.

### **The Fastest Path to Growth**

Success in sales—and in life—isn’t about working harder, it’s about working *smarter.* A clogged pipeline doesn’t need more water pressure, it needs a clear path. If you can pinpoint and eliminate your biggest bottleneck, you’ll unlock rapid, measurable growth.

The question is, are you willing to be brutally honest with yourself? The answer to your sales struggles isn’t always to push harder—it’s to fix the one thing that’s truly holding you back.

### 🎯The Art of Prospecting: Playing the Long Game

Prospecting is the lifeblood of sales, yet many struggle with it because they expect instant results. Just as a well-structured financial plan aligns with realistic goals, successful prospecting follows a strategic process—not a one-step shortcut. Expecting to close a deal with a cold prospect immediately is like planting a seed today and expecting a fully grown tree tomorrow. It doesn’t work that way.

Many advisors make the mistake of misaligning expectations with their prospecting efforts. If you send a message to someone you haven’t spoken to in years and expect them to book an appointment on the spot, you’re setting yourself up for disappointment. Even catching up over coffee with an old friend and assuming they’ll immediately say yes to an insurance or investment proposal is unlikely. Instead, prospecting requires following a structured appointment ladder—warming up the relationship, educating the prospect through fact-finding, presenting a proposal, following up, and eventually closing.

While it would be ideal to jump straight to closing with ready buyers, these individuals make up only about 3% of the market and are heavily sought after. Instead, the real opportunity lies in the 97% of the market that isn’t actively looking to buy today but could be nurtured into becoming clients. About 33% are open to discussions like policy reviews and financial planning, while the remaining 67% respond better to casual catch-ups and relationship-building before they’re ready for more formal conversations.

Building trust over time is what sets top advisors apart. When you prioritize genuine relationships over immediate transactions, you increase the likelihood of securing future appointments. The key to long-term success in prospecting isn’t pushing for a close too soon—it’s playing the long game and allowing relationships to mature naturally.

Sustainable success isn’t just about acquiring more leads but about **improving production and client relationships** at every stage.

Instead of focusing solely on lead generation, advisors should look at the bigger picture. Doubling the number of leads might increase production, but **doubling the closing ratio** from 10% to 20% is often a more effective and sustainable strategy. By refining skills such as **effective messaging, overcoming objections, and relationship-building,** advisors can **maximize the value of each lead** rather than constantly chasing new ones. The key to long-term success isn’t just more leads—it’s mastering the entire sales process.

### 🎯Cold Calling Training

## **1\. The Mindset of a Successful Cold Caller**

-   **Belief & Conviction in Your Product**
    
    -   You need to truly believe in the **value of insurance** and financial planning.
        
    -   If you doubt your offering, **prospects will sense your lack of conviction**.
        
    -   **Sales is a transfer of conviction**—the more convinced you are, the more you can convince others.
        
-   **Handling Rejection**
    
    -   Expect rejection; **it's not personal, it's part of the process**.
        
    -   The goal is not to avoid rejection but to **develop resilience and adaptability**.
        
    -   Understand that **"no" often means "not now"**, not never.
        
-   **Confidence & Energy**
    
    -   Your **tone and enthusiasm** dictate the energy of the call.
        
    -   **80% of a good cold call is energy and confidence**, not just the script.
        
    -   Speak with **authority and clarity**—if you sound unsure, the prospect will not trust you.
        

---

## **2\. The Structure of an Effective Cold Call**

A cold call should follow a **clear structure** to guide the conversation efficiently.

### **Step 1: Opening (First 10-15 Seconds)**

-   The **goal** is to **capture attention and establish credibility**.
    
-   Start with confidence and clarity.
    
-   Example:
    
    -   **"Hi \[Prospect’s Name\], this is \[Your Name\] from \[Company\]. I'm reaching out because we work with young professionals like yourself to help with financial planning. Just a quick check—do you have a moment to chat?"**
        
-   **Common Mistakes to Avoid in the Opening:**
    
    -   Speaking too softly or too fast.
        
    -   Sounding like you’re reading from a script.
        
    -   Using a **weak or hesitant tone**.
        

---

### **Step 2: Establishing Relevance (15-30 Seconds)**

-   **Why should they listen?**
    
    -   Relate to their situation: **"We’ve helped many young professionals like you manage their finances better and take advantage of financial opportunities they never knew existed."**
        
    -   Address a common pain point: **"Many people I speak to say they don’t really understand financial planning, but they know they should."**
        

---

### **Step 3: Asking for the Appointment (30-45 Seconds)**

-   Keep it simple and direct.
    
-   Offer **clear time slots** to make it easy for them to say yes.
    
-   Example:
    
    -   **"Would Saturday at 10 AM or Sunday at 2 PM work better for a quick 30-minute Zoom call?"**
        
    -   **"We can run through some essential financial planning strategies—it’s free, and no obligations."**
        
-   **Pro Tip:**
    
    -   Never ask, **“Would you be interested?”**—this gives them an easy way to say **no**.
        
    -   Instead, **assume the meeting will happen** and lead them toward a **choice of time**.
        

---

## **3\. Objection Handling – Common Objections & How to Respond**

### **1\. "I'm Not Interested"**

**Response:**

-   **"I completely understand. Most people tell me that at first, but after speaking with us, they find a lot of value. Let’s schedule a quick call, and if it’s not relevant, no worries—at least you’ll have the information."**
    
-   **"Many of our clients initially thought the same, but once they learned about what we offer, they realized it was worth their time."**
    

### **2\. "I Already Have Someone Helping Me"**

**Response:**

-   **"That’s great to hear! Just curious, is it a friend or a family member who’s helping you?"**
    
-   **"We’re not here to replace your current advisor but to provide a different perspective. Some of our clients work with multiple advisors to ensure they get the best options available."**
    

### **3\. "I Don't Have Time"**

**Response:**

-   **"I completely understand. That’s why we keep our calls short—just 20-30 minutes. Would a quick call after work be easier for you?"**
    
-   **"Most of our clients are busy professionals, which is why we make it convenient. Would a Saturday morning be better?"**
    

### **4\. "I Need to Think About It"**

**Response:**

-   **"Absolutely! Just to make sure you have everything you need, let’s schedule a call to go over the details. That way, you can make an informed decision."**
    
-   **"I understand, and that’s why we offer a free consultation—it’s just an information session, no pressure."**
    

---

## **4\. Strategies for Effective Cold Calling**

### **1\. The "Soft Close" Technique**

-   Instead of asking for a hard **"yes"**, lead them to a **natural next step**.
    
-   Example:
    
    -   Instead of: **"Are you interested?"**
        
    -   Say: **"Would Saturday morning or Sunday afternoon work better?"**
        

### **2\. Active Listening & Mirroring**

-   Repeat and **paraphrase** what they say to build rapport.
    
-   If they say, **"I don’t have time right now"**, respond with:
    
    -   **"Got it! Sounds like your schedule is packed. That’s exactly why we keep our sessions short—would a 20-minute Zoom call in the evening work better?"**
        

### **3\. The Power of Follow-Ups**

-   Most people won’t say yes immediately—**the fortune is in the follow-up**.
    
-   **Send a WhatsApp message immediately after the call** to confirm details.
    
-   **Follow up a day before the appointment** to remind them.
    

---

## **5\. Advanced Techniques for Maximizing Success**

### **1\. The Energy Transfer Principle**

-   **People buy energy and enthusiasm more than the product itself**.
    
-   Speak as if **you’re excited** to share something **genuinely valuable**.
    
-   **Fast, high-energy speech** = excitement.
    
-   **Slow, hesitant speech** = lack of confidence.
    

### **2\. Managing Your Own Psychology**

-   Cold calling can be **mentally exhausting**. To stay motivated:
    
    -   Set **daily targets** (e.g., 50 calls per day).
        
    -   Track your **success rate** and **improvement**.
        
    -   Remember, **every "no" brings you closer to a "yes"**.
        

### **3\. Creating an Authority Presence**

-   Position yourself as an **expert**, not a salesperson.
    
-   Use phrases like:
    
    -   **"Many clients are unaware of this..."**
        
    -   **"A lot of people I speak to struggle with..."**
        
    -   **"This strategy has helped dozens of young professionals..."**

### ⚡ The Efficient Sales Cycle

An efficient sales cycle is one that is mainly focused on business generation activities such as appointment setting and closing, rather than anything else. As you can see, as a student advisor, the majority of my time was spent on business generation activities, and less on admin. The time I spent on travelling can be arguably counted not as work time, but if you purely count business generation activities, it was not more than 25 hours a week.

We need to be relentless in cutting out all the noise and distractions, and focus on the most important.

![](https://hgdbflprrficdoyxmdxe.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/knowledge-files/editor-images/1771987928694-8ax6el2b3x7.png)

**Self-Reinforcing Sales Cycles**

Most salespeople do have a big problem - which is the lack of leads. In fact, sometimes there is a slump because the more appointments are being set, the more leads are being "burnt".

Hence, if you are not **generating leads fast** **enough** to replace the leads that you're burning through, then your business will be unsustainable over time. Hence lead generation must be of equal weightage to the act of closing itself.

However, the problem with most salespeople is this: the more sales appointments they go for, the less time they have to generate leads. And vice versa - the more time they spend to generate leads, the less time they have to go for sales appointments.

Hence, there is a *see-saw effect*. The more one invests in calling to set more appointments, the less leads they have left, which means more time needed to get leads again and to restart the sales cycle. Then, they need to start investing time to generate leads, and so on. It is a **self-destructive cycle that works against itself**.

Hence, you want to create a **self-reinforcing** cycle, whereby

1.  The more leads you have, the more calls you can make leads
    
2.  The more calls you make, the more appointments you have
    
3.  The more appointments you have, *the more leads you have*
    
4.  Cycle 1-3 repeats itself
    

![](https://hgdbflprrficdoyxmdxe.supabase.co/storage/v1/object/public/knowledge-files/editor-images/1771987959293-hwz1ma8c31w.png)

So, the secret to creating a **self-reinforcing sales cycle** is to focus on getting referrals. For every single appointment, you **must** try to get referrals. In fact, that should be your main obective rather than the sale itself, ironically. Because as long as you generate enough referrals, then sales will naturally come. Referrals are mandatory to **replace** the leads that were burnt in the process of getting the appointment. The topic of getting referrals is a whole chapter on its own, but for now, we need to drive home the importance of referrals as a more sustainable source of lead generation.

The end goal is that appointment setting + lead generation must both reinforce each other, instead of being in conflict. We need to be relentless in all three activities:

-   Getting Leads
    
-   Making calls
    
-   Meeting the people
    
-   Eventually, get referrals by knowing how to ask for referrals during every appointment

## Social Media Branding

### Cold Prospecting on Social Media

Engaging cold prospects on social media is both an art and a science. Unlike warm leads who already have some level of familiarity with you, cold prospects have no prior connection, making it crucial to capture their attention, establish credibility, and nurture interest—all within a limited window of opportunity. To do this effectively, you need a **structured approach**. Here are the **five essential pillars** to successfully turning cold prospects into engaged leads and eventually, loyal clients.

1.  **Define a Highly Targeted Audience**
    

A common mistake in social media prospecting is **trying to appeal to everyone**. The reality is, if you market to everyone, you market to no one. Instead of casting a wide net, hone in on a specific **niche audience**. A targeted approach helps **position you as an expert** rather than just another salesperson. For instance, rather than marketing broadly to all business owners, narrowing your focus to **tech entrepreneurs or fitness coaches** allows you to tailor your messaging and solutions, making your content far more relevant and impactful. Specificity enhances credibility, authority, and ultimately, conversion rates.

2.  **Craft a Compelling Value Proposition**
    

Cold prospects don’t care about your backstory or struggles—they care about **what you can do for them**. A compelling value proposition clearly answers the question: **“Why should I pay attention to you?”** Avoid generic statements like *“I help people grow their wealth”*—instead, introduce a **unique framework** that makes your offer more tangible and memorable. For example, a financial advisor might present their process as **P.I.S.A (Protect, Income, Scale, Assets)** or **F.A.T (Foundation, Accelerate, Transfer)**—these structured approaches differentiate them from competitors and make the service more digestible.

3.  **Optimize Your Social Media Profile**
    

Your profile is your **online storefront**, and first impressions matter. If a prospect checks your profile after reading your message, it needs to **instantly establish trust**. Here’s how:

-   **Showcase your framework or expertise** in your bio.
    
-   **Highlight credibility indicators** such as client results, industry achievements, or media features.
    
-   **Include social proof** with testimonials or case studies.
    
-   **Stay active** by posting valuable insights at least twice a week.
    
-   **Remove any unprofessional content** to maintain a polished and authoritative presence.
    

An optimized profile **does the selling for you**, reassuring prospects that you are a trusted professional worth engaging with.

4.  **Craft Sharp, Value-Driven Messages**
    

Cold outreach messages must be **concise, relevant, and engaging**. Avoid long-winded introductions or hard-selling tactics—**cut straight to what’s in it for them**. The best messages:

-   **Address common pain points** your prospects face.
    
-   **Offer value upfront**, such as a free eBook, an industry report, or an insightful article.
    
-   **Differentiate you from spammy sales messages** by making your approach warm, human, and insightful.
    

For platforms like LinkedIn, where people receive countless sales messages daily, standing out requires **clarity, brevity, and immediate value**.

5.  **Follow Up Consistently**
    

Most cold prospects won’t respond on the first try—not because they’re uninterested, but because they’re busy or simply missed your message. A well-structured **follow-up sequence** can **double or triple your response rate**. However, following up doesn’t mean sending repeated “Just checking in” messages. Instead, make follow-ups valuable:

-   Share an **additional insight or resource** related to their interests.
    
-   Reference a **recent event or trend** that might be relevant to them.
    
-   Use different formats—**text messages, voice notes, or even videos** to stand out.
    

Persistence, when done thoughtfully, **builds familiarity and trust**, increasing the chances of conversion over time.

### **Final Thoughts: The Power of a Structured Approach**

Cold prospecting isn’t about luck—it’s about **strategy and execution**. By implementing these **five pillars**—**narrowing your audience, crafting a strong value proposition, optimizing your profile, sending compelling messages, and executing consistent follow-ups**—you can transform cold leads into warm conversations, and warm conversations into long-term clients.

Success in social media prospecting is **not about who can send the most messages**, but about **who can engage the right people in the right way**. Build trust, offer value, and focus on **relationship-building over hard selling**, and you’ll find that even the coldest prospects can eventually turn into loyal clients.

### 5 Questions to Answer on Social Media

Before reaching out to you on social media, prospects will subconsciously ask themselves five critical questions. The more effectively your content addresses these concerns, the easier it becomes to build trust and convert cold prospects into engaged leads.

1.  **Can I Trust That Others Value Your Work?** Just as we read reviews before making a purchase, prospects seek **social proof** before engaging with a financial advisor. Testimonials are powerful here, but they must feel **authentic and structured**. The best testimonials follow a **pre-during-post format**: *"Before working with me, Ryan felt lost about his finances. During our consultation, we built a clear strategy tailored to his needs. After implementing our plan, he now has a structured roadmap to financial security."* This format ensures **relatability and credibility**, especially when supported by screenshots, video testimonials, or real-life success stories.
    
2.  **Do You Actually Know Your Stuff?** Expertise is non-negotiable. Prospects need to see you as someone who **understands their problems and can provide solutions**. Instead of vague industry jargon, focus on **real challenges your clients face** and break them down with practical advice. If you're new, frame your insights as **your perspective rather than authority**, which allows you to provide value **without the pressure of appearing as an expert**.
    
3.  **Why Are You Doing This?** Skepticism towards financial advisors is common—many assume the motivation is purely financial. To **break this barrier**, share your **genuine purpose**. What defining moments led you to this career? What personal experiences shaped your financial philosophy? Authentic storytelling fosters **emotional connection** and allows prospects to see you as a **person, not just a salesperson**.
    
4.  **Will We Get Along?** Trust isn’t just about expertise—it’s also about **connection**. Prospects want to work with someone they can relate to. By sharing **your personality, interests, and even quirks**, you naturally **attract the right clients and repel those who aren’t a good fit**. A financial advisor who enjoys **traveling, fitness, or personal development** can share content around these topics, creating **natural points of connection** with like-minded prospects.
    
5.  **How Can I Approach You Without Feeling Awkward?** Most people hesitate to reach out because they don’t know how to start the conversation. **Lower the barrier** by crafting an **offer post**—a subtle call to action embedded in a testimonial. For example: *"After implementing this strategy, Sarah now feels financially secure and confident about her future. If you want to explore how this approach can work for you, DM me ‘plan’ to learn more."* This makes reaching out **easy and frictionless**, removing any hesitation prospects might have.
    

By addressing these five key questions in your content, you position yourself as **trustworthy, knowledgeable, purpose-driven, relatable, and approachable**. This strategy transforms your social media presence into a lead-generating machine, attracting the right prospects and **seamlessly converting them into clients**.

## Why Touchpoints Matter

The biggest hurdle in financial planning sales is **procrastination**. After an appointment, prospects will commonly say things like "I'll think about it," "when I get promoted," "when my pay increases," etc. They will always find reasons to delay.

**The difference between a sale and no sale is how closely and meaningfully you follow up.**

However, this does not mean following up incessantly or randomly. Effective follow-up uses **touchpoints** — messages tied to major milestones and events in the prospect's life. When people realize you remember the important events in their life, their receptiveness towards you increases by 3–4x.

## How to Gather Touchpoint Information

During your initial meeting, ask these key questions:

1.  **"How old are you? When do you turn \[age\]?"** → Gets you their birthday
    
2.  **"When is your POP?"** → Lets you extrapolate enlistment date and ORD date
    
3.  **"When is your ORD?"** → Plan university-related touchpoints
    
4.  **"When are you entering university?"** → Map out semester and graduation touchpoints
    

From just a few data points, you can extrapolate the entire timeline.

## SOP: Setting Up Touchpoints in Google Calendar

### Step-by-step:

1.  Open Google Calendar → **Create an Event**
    
2.  Select **Event** (not Task or Appointment)
    
3.  Use this naming format for the event title:
    

> **Wish \[message\] — \[Prospect Name\] — \[Prospect Number\] — \[Your Name\]**

Examples:

-   `Wish Happy Birthday — John Tan — 91234567 — Leo`
    
-   `Wish Congrats on ORD — John Tan — 91234567 — Leo`
    
-   `Wish All the best for upcoming semester — John Tan — 91234567 — Leo`
    

4.  Set the correct **date**
    
5.  Mark it as an **All Day Event**
    
6.  Set **repetition**:
    
    -   Birthdays → Repeat **every year** (indefinitely)
        
    -   Semester wishes → **Custom repeat**, every year for **4 years** then end
        
    -   One-off milestones (POP, ORD, etc.) → No repeat needed
        
7.  Set event colour to **Tangerine** for easy identification
    
8.  **Share the event** to your manager's email so they can track your follow-ups
    
9.  Save

## Key Principles

-   **Every single lead is valuable** — follow up with all of them, not just the "hot" ones.
    
-   **Be genuinely interested in their lives.** The more you learn about their milestones, the more touchpoints you naturally discover.
    
-   You can extend this beyond NS milestones: ask about holidays, exams, assignments, career decisions — anything meaningful to them.
    
-   Touchpoints are not about selling in every message. They're about **staying present and building trust** so that when the prospect is ready, you're the first person they think of.
    
-   The goal is for the prospect to feel: *"This person actually remembers and cares about what's happening in my life."*
