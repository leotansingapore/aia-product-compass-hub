# Lecture Notes: Introduction to Total Wealth Concept (TWC)

## Core Philosophy: Human Capital vs Financial Capital

### The Golden Goose Principle
- **When young, human capital > financial capital**
- Your ability to work and earn income is your greatest asset, not your bank account
- You are your own "golden goose"
- Common misconception: People focus on investing before protecting their earning ability

### Health Over Wealth
- Fundamental question to clients: "Health or wealth - which is more important?"
- Universal answer: Health
- **Key insight**: Must protect health and human capital BEFORE focusing purely on investments
- Protection mechanism: Proper insurance coverage

---

## The Four Pillars of Human Capital Protection

When planning comprehensive coverage, address these four areas:

1. **Hospitalization** - Ensure all medical bills are covered
2. **Accident Coverage** - Protection for accidental injuries
3. **Critical Illness (CI)** - Income replacement during serious illness
4. **Death/Disability** - Protection for dependents and income loss

### Discovery Approach
- Ask client: "Which one do you think is most important for you?"
- Follow their priority to guide the conversation
- Use their answer to establish budget and planning direction

---

## Budget Framework: The 10-15% Rule

### Income Allocation Guidelines
- **Investment**: ~20% of income
- **Insurance**: 10-15% of income
- **Question to ask**: "If I can create a comprehensive package for you, what is your maximum budget you can set aside?"

### The Three-Thirds Budget Model
- **1/3**: Short-term needs
- **1/3**: Medium-term needs  
- **1/3**: Savings & investment needs

---

## Critical Illness Coverage Guidelines

### Coverage Amount Formula
**CI Coverage = 100K + (3-5 years of income)**

#### Why 100K Base?
- Cancer drug treatments not fully covered by hospitalization plans
- Single dose can cost ~10K
- Chemotherapy partially covered, but specialized cancer drugs often are not
- Creates significant out-of-pocket expenses

#### Why 3-5 Years Income?
- **Emotional argument**: "Imagine you can't work for a few years on 10K/month salary. Who will give you that money? Parents? For how long?"
- **Key distinction**: 
  - Hospitalization plan = Pays the hospital
  - CI plan = Pays YOU when unable to work
- Removes financial burden during recovery (emotional burden from cancer already heavy enough)

---

## Sales Strategy: Staged Closing Approach

### Meeting Structure

**First Meeting:**
- Use TWC framework to establish holistic planning need
- Identify priorities and budget
- Avoid overwhelming with too much information at once

**Second Meeting:**
- Present comprehensive proposal
- Close specific priority first

### Progressive Closing Method

**If Priority = CI:**
1. Close CI first
2. Note: "Now that we've closed CI, your base coverage is well covered"

**If Priority = Hospitalization:**
1. Close hospitalization plan first
2. Then transition: "Do you know hospitalization doesn't cover outpatient?"
3. Close accident coverage next
4. Then pivot to CI: "Even with hospital plan, CI is crucial. What happens when you can't work for 5 years? Hospital plan pays hospital, CI pays YOU during recovery"

### Key Transition Scripts

**Hospitalization to Accident:**
- "Hospital plan doesn't cover outpatient procedures"

**Hospitalization to CI:**
- "Hospital plan pays the hospital, but CI pays you when you can't work"
- "Who will replace your income if you can't work for years? Parents can't sustain that indefinitely"
- "We can't remove emotional burden of illness, but we can remove the financial burden"

---

## Important Coverage Rules of Thumb

### Traditional Guidelines (Reference)
- Premature death coverage: [Amount to be specified]
- CI coverage: As detailed above
- Accident coverage: [Amount to be specified]

**Note**: These are general guidelines. The staged approach prevents overwhelming clients with all requirements simultaneously.

---

## Key Teaching Points

1. **Always lead with human capital concept** - shifts mindset from "insurance is expense" to "insurance protects my greatest asset"

2. **Health before wealth** - universal value that resonates with all clients

3. **Progressive disclosure** - Don't present all four pillars comprehensively in first meeting; too cumbersome to close everything at once

4. **Follow client priority** - Let them tell you what's most important, then structure the close around that

5. **Cross-sell naturally** - Each product closes leads organically to the next through benefit gaps and complementary coverage

6. **Use emotional + practical reasoning** - Financial burden arguments PLUS "who will support you?" scenarios

---

## Conclusion

The TWC approach reframes insurance from "expense" to "asset protection." By starting with human capital education and following client priorities, you create natural pathways to comprehensive coverage without overwhelming the prospect.

# Financial Planning Overview - Lecture Notes

## Introduction: The Three Core Questions

When conducting a financial planning overview appointment, structure the presentation around three fundamental questions:

1. **Why** do we need to plan?
2. **What** do we need to plan for?
3. **How much** should we plan?

---

## Part 1: Why We Need to Plan

### The Income Dependency Framework

Start by establishing the client's current income situation:
- Employment status and income source
- Current financial obligations (personal expenses, family expenses)
- Savings and investment capacity
- Future goals (house, car, etc.)

**Key Insight**: "If you fail to plan, you plan to fail"

### The Income Loss Scenario

Present the consequences of losing income:
- Cannot pay personal expenses
- Cannot support family obligations
- Must liquidate savings and investments
- Cannot save for future goals

### Types of Income Loss Risks

#### 1. Temporary Risks (Recoverable)
- Job termination/poor performance
- Retrenchment due to economic conditions
- Voluntary resignation

**Client response**: Find another job (possible when young and skilled)

#### 2. Permanent Risks (Non-recoverable)
- **Critical Illness**: Cancer, stroke, coma
- **Death**: Complete loss of income
- **Hospitalization**: Medical expenses
- **Accidents**: Disability or incapacity
- **Retirement**: Voluntary cessation of work

---

## Part 2: The Million Dollar Question

### Calculating Lifetime Income Needs

**Example Calculation**:
- Annual expenses: $25,000
- Years remaining to live (age 25): 40 years
- Total needed: $25,000 × 40 = **$1,000,000**

### The Family Borrowing Test

Progressive questioning technique:
- Can family lend $1,000? → Yes (emergency funds)
- Can family lend $10,000? → Yes (with relatives' help)
- Can family lend $1,000,000? → **Not possible**

**Key Message**: "If you don't have your own financial plan, your family becomes your financial plan"

---

## Part 3: Managing Risk - Three Approaches

### 1. Avoid the Risk
- Example: Stay home to avoid accidents
- **Limitation**: Cannot avoid critical illness even at home
- **Verdict**: Unrealistic for most risks

### 2. Retain the Risk
- Requires having $1 million in savings already
- Self-insurance through personal wealth
- **Limitation**: Most people don't have sufficient savings
- **Verdict**: Difficult to achieve quickly

### 3. Transfer the Risk ✓ (Recommended)
- Transfer to insurance company via premiums
- Use 10% of income to protect 90% of income
- **The Golden Goose Analogy**: Your income-generating ability is like a golden goose laying golden eggs - you must protect it

---

## Part 4: What to Plan - The Three-Tier Pyramid

### Foundation Layer: Manage Risk (Protection)
**Must come first** - Insurance coverage to protect income

### Second Layer: Accumulate Wealth
Once income is protected, build:
- Savings
- Investments
- Growth assets

**Warning**: Building savings without protection means a $200K medical bill could wipe out all accumulated wealth

### Third Layer: Preserve Wealth
- For established wealth (multiple millions)
- Legacy planning
- Estate preservation
- Future generations' planning

---

## Part 5: Maximizing Wealth - Two Capitals

### Human Capital (Most Valuable Asset)
**Calculation Example**:
- Working years remaining: 40 years
- Annual income: $50,000
- **Total potential income: $2,000,000**

This is your most valuable commodity, not your current savings!

### Financial Capital
- Current savings and investments
- Must grow to overcome inflation
- Bank savings alone (0.05% interest) lose value vs. 3% inflation
- Requires proper investment strategy

---

## Part 6: The Four Types of Insurance

### Medical Expense Insurance (First Line of Defense)

#### 1. Hospitalization Insurance
- Covers 95% of medical expenses
- Client pays maximum 5%, capped at $3,000
- **Without it**: A $50,000 medical bill could be financially devastating
- **Critical limitation**: Only covers if hospitalized

#### 2. Accident/Outpatient Plan
- Covers non-hospitalization incidents:
  - Stitches needed
  - Fishbone stuck in throat
  - Food poisoning
  - Minor accidents
- Fills the gap for treatments not requiring admission

### Income Protection Insurance

#### 3. Critical Illness Insurance
**Coverage Formula**: 5× annual income
- Example: $50K income → $250K coverage
- Lump sum payment upon diagnosis
- Covers 3-5 year recovery period
- Major conditions: stroke, cancer, coma

#### 4. Death & Disability Insurance
Protects income for dependents/long-term needs

---

## Part 7: How Much to Plan - MAS Guidelines

### Coverage Amounts by Risk Type

| Risk Type | Coverage Amount | Additional Notes |
|-----------|----------------|------------------|
| **Premature Death** | 10× annual income | For dependents |
| **Critical Illness** | 3-5× annual income + $100K | $100K buffer for treatments not covered by hospitalization insurance |
| **Accidents** | 5× annual income | If death due to accident |
| **Hospitalization** | Depends on ward preference | Private vs. public hospital |

---

## Part 8: Budgeting Framework - The One-Third Rule

### Monthly Income Allocation

**Equal division into three categories:**

1. **Short-term needs (1/3)**: Daily expenses
   - Food
   - Clothing
   - Transportation
   - Utilities

2. **Medium-term needs (1/3)**: Major purchases
   - House
   - Car
   - Wedding
   - Holidays

3. **Long-term needs (1/3)**: Future security
   - Insurance (10-15% of income)
   - Retirement savings
   - Investments (15-20% of income)

### Specific Allocation Recommendations

**Insurance Budget**: 10-15% of monthly income
- Can achieve comprehensive coverage at 10%

**Savings/Investment Budget**: 15-20% of monthly income
- For wealth accumulation
- Long-term growth

### Budget Example

## Part 10: Next Steps & Proposal Structure

### Three-Option Proposal Strategy

Present client with three tailored options:

1. **Comprehensive Package**: Full coverage across all areas
2. **Best Value Package**: Optimal coverage-to-cost ratio
3. **Budget Package**: Essential coverage for limited budget

### Overcoming Implementation Barriers

**Common objection**: "I need to ask my parents"

**Solution**: Joint meeting approach
- Meet with client and parents together
- Provide confidence through direct explanation
- Address all questions immediately
- Opportunity to review parents' existing policies
- Check for overpayment on outdated policies

**Additional value**: Offer complimentary policy review for parents' existing AIA policies

---

## Key Teaching Points & Sales Principles

### 1. The Value-First Approach
Always establish value and need before discussing price

### 2. Relatable Scenarios
Use concrete examples (fishbone, food poisoning) to make abstract risks tangible

### 3. The Impossibility Technique
Show what's unrealistic (avoiding all risk, having $1M saved) to make insurance the logical solution

### 4. Progressive Questioning
Build agreement through escalating commitment (borrow $1K → $10K → $1M)

### 5. Visual Framework
Use pyramid/triangle diagrams to show proper planning sequence

### 6. Anchoring Through Calculations
Make large numbers real ($25K × 40 years = $1M)

### 7. Family Involvement Strategy
Convert "I need to ask" objection into opportunity for broader client base

## III. GUARANTEE PROTECT PLUS (GPP) - DETAILED

### Key Features:

**1. Participating Whole Life Plan**
- Participates in PAR fund profits
- AIA invests premiums through bonds
- Declares bonuses over time
- Client offloads investment responsibility to AIA

**2. Multiplier Coverage System**
- Base sum insured × multiplier (2x, 3x, or 5x)
- Multiplier drops at age 65 or 75
- Example: $100,000 base × 5 = $500,000 coverage until drop-off age

**3. Limited Premium Payment**
- Pay for 15, 20, or 25 years
- **Selection Guide:**
  - Ages 20-30s: 20 years recommended
  - Ages 40s: 15 years recommended (finish before retirement)
  - Shorter tenure = better returns

**4. Income Draw Down Feature**
- Available at age 65-85 (after multiplier drop-off)
- Can withdraw as 10-year income stream instead of lump sum
- Makes GPP function like tail-end annuity

**5. Riders Available**
- **CPL (Critical Protector Life):** Major CI coverage
- **Early Critical Protector:** Early stage CI coverage
- **Waiver Riders:** Premium waiver upon CI diagnosis
- **Payer Benefit:** For child policies (waives if parent gets CI)

### Sample Illustration (30-year-old male, non-smoker):

**Scenario:** $500,000 death coverage with full CI
- 3x multiplier until age 65
- 20-year payment term
- Premium: ~$7,300/year
- Total paid over 20 years: $147,000

## XIII. KEY SALES CONCEPTS & TECHNIQUES

### 1. Budget Reality Principle

**Critical Insight:** Clients can typically afford **DOUBLE** their stated budget without discomfort.

**Example:**

### 2. Value Before Price Philosophy

**Always VALUE first, then PRICE**

Never lead with premium. Instead:
1. Build comprehensive benefit understanding
2. Show real-world scenarios

4. Anchor price perception through estimation exercises
5. Then reveal actual premium

**Technique:** Present 3x multiplier GPP first, then show 2x comparison

**Script:** "Mr. Client, by adjusting to 2x multiplier and paying just $1,600 more per year, your cash value increases by $75,000 at age 70, and your coverage drop-off is much less severe. Would this additional value be worth the investment?"

**Result:** Natural upsizing without being pushy

## Part 1: Using Policy Summary to Open Cases & Do Reviews

### The Sales Flow

The standard client engagement flow follows this path:

**Introduction → Review/Needs Analysis → Fact-Finding → Presentation → Solution Implementation → Servicing → Touch Points**

When approaching warm prospects, friends, acquaintances, or referrals, always start with something **low-bait** — never lead with "I want to sell you a policy." Instead, position it as: *"Can we meet up to do a review of your finances and give you some updates on recent changes in hospital plans / investment markets / CI definitions?"*

### Fact-Finding (FF) Parameters

Once you secure the review appointment, you collect four major financial parameters: **Income, Expenses, Assets, and Liabilities.** These feed into the needs analysis using standard rules of thumb:

- **Premature Death:** 10-20x annual income
- **Disability coverage**
- **Critical Illness:** 3-5x annual income (e.g., income $60K → CI coverage should be $180K-$300K)
- **Hospitalization**
- **Accidental coverage**
- **Retirement planning**

If a client earning $60K has only $75K CI coverage, the gap is immediately visible.

### The Policy Summary Document

The summary template has two main blocks:

1. **Risk Management Policies** (upper block) — Death, TPD, Occupational Disability, Critical Illness, Early CI, Hospitalization, Accidental, Accidental Reimbursement
2. **Wealth Accumulation** (lower block) — Investment-linked policies, endowments, projected maturity/investment values

For each policy, you capture: policy number, policy date, plan name, coverage amounts, cash values, and total premiums.

**What clients want to know at a glance:**
- How much am I paying?
- What am I covered for?
- What am I lacking?

You then map all coverage against planning norms, identify shortfalls, and use this as the basis for your opening and recommendation.

### Servicing & Touch Points

After a prospect becomes a client, conduct reviews every 1-2 years or at every major life stage (graduation, marriage, children). Maintain systematic touch points — direct, indirect, subtle — to stay top of mind.

Some advisors complete needs analysis and solution implementation in one appointment; others split it into two.

## Part 2: SG Findex — Digital Policy Consolidation

### What Is SG Findex?

SG Findex is a Singapore government-backed digital service that allows clients to consolidate all their financial data in one portal by linking their **Singpass**. It pulls data from insurance companies, banks, CPF, CDP shares, and more.

You can access it via the official SG Findex website or through insurer-specific apps (e.g., AIA+ app → SG Findex Data section).

### How to Use It

1. Client logs into AIA+ (or official Findex site) using Singpass
2. Selects which financial institutions to link (insurers, banks, CPF, etc.)
3. Gives consent to share data
4. Downloads/shares the consolidated report with their advisor

**Message template approach:** Send the client a step-by-step guide (PDF available in the Google Drive). They complete the linking process and share the report with you.

### What You Can See vs. What You Can't

**Can see:** Policy names, premium amounts, coverage amounts, surrender values, total premiums, CPF data, bank balances (if linked), CDP shares

**Cannot see:** Detailed fund allocations within ILPs (e.g., which funds a Prudential ILP is invested in). The system is not yet that sophisticated.

### Important Sensitivities

- **Banking data** — Clients may not want to share credit card balances, savings amounts, or loan details. Be mindful. You can always ask verbally during fact-finding instead.
- **Insurance data** — Most clients are comfortable sharing this. This alone is already very useful.
- **CPF data** — Most people are okay sharing this.

Never push clients to link everything — start with insurance data and add more only if they're comfortable.

### Key Insight: Clients Forget Their Own Policies

**Real case study:** A 45-year-old client synced his SG Findex data showing CI coverage of only $30K with AIA (income $100K). During the review, he recalled having more coverage. Upon logging into the Prudential portal on the spot, two additional policies totaling ~$190K in CI coverage were discovered — policies he had forgotten about.

**Takeaway:** SG Findex is a starting point, not the complete picture. Always verify with individual insurer portals when something doesn't add up.

### SG Findex vs. Ghost Mapper

SG Findex is a **policy consolidation e-service** — it pulls and displays data. Ghost Mapper is a **financial planning software** — it analyzes, projects, and presents recommendations. They are complementary, not interchangeable.

### When to Use SG Findex

- Best for **slightly older clients** with policies scattered across multiple companies
- For clients who can't recall what they have or lost their policy booklets
- Can be sent before the meeting (for hot/warm market who already trust you) or explained during the meeting (for referrals and newer relationships where trust must be established first)

## Part 3: Introduction to Warm Prospecting

### Market Temperature Model

Your existing network falls into concentric circles based on relationship closeness:

| Circle | Type | Description | Approx. Distribution (of 300 contacts) |
|--------|------|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| Center | **Hot Market** | Parents, siblings, best friends. They buy because of who you are, regardless of company. Easiest to meet. | ~30 |
| Second | **Warm Market** | Friends, ex-colleagues, relatives (not super close). They respond to your messages and IG comments. | ~60 |
| Third | **Semi-Warm Market** | Acquaintances, people you haven't spoken to in years. **Most untapped market** due to sheer quantity. | ~210 |
| Outer | **Cold Market** | Lead gen, grab vouchers, NSF, cold calls, roadshows. Low conversion (20 leads → 3 meetings → maybe 1-1.5 sales). | Unlimited |

**Critical concept:** When a cold prospect buys from you, they become your **client** — which means they move into your warm market. Your job is then to nurture them from semi-warm client → warm client → A-client (advocate who refers). This is like "brewing old soup" — slowly nurture, follow up, add value over the years.

### A/B/C Client Classification

- **A Clients:** Advocates — they actively refer people to you
- **B Clients:** Buy from you, open to repurchase, but don't actively refer
- **C Clients:** Minimal engagement

The dream scenario: A client messages you saying *"Hey bro, my girlfriend is looking to buy some CI. Can you talk to her?"* — that's an open goal. Build toward this through consistent nurturing.

### Two Approach Methods for Warm/Semi-Warm Prospects

**Method 1: Direct Education/Fact-Find Appointment**
For prospects who are ready. You set the appointment with a clear agenda: *"Let's meet to review your finances, consolidate your policies, and go through some updates."*

**Method 2: Warm-Up Appointment First**
For prospects who aren't ready or where the relationship needs rebuilding. Meet casually — catch up over dinner or coffee. Be honest about your career transition. If an opportunity arises naturally, transition to setting an education appointment. If not, plant the seed for a future meeting.

**Conversion expectation:** Out of every 3 warm-up sessions, approximately 1-1.5 will transition to an education fact-find appointment. This is worth the effort.

### Warm Market Calling/Messaging Script — Key Principles

1. **Sound casual, not like a financial advisor.** You're calling as a friend, not a salesperson.
2. **Be genuine** — share what you're doing, acknowledge the career transition naturally.
3. **Praise the person** — "I know you're someone responsible, you love your family..."
4. **End with a no-pressure close** — "Whether you choose to engage me is totally up to you."
5. **Keep calls structured but brief** — don't chat for an hour before getting to the point.

**Sample warm call flow:**
- Casual greeting → mention your IG posts/career change → share briefly what you do → compliment them → suggest meeting at a familiar location → no-pressure ask → fix the appointment

**Key mindset beliefs:**
1. Deeply believe that what you're doing genuinely helps people.
2. Many of your friends are actually just waiting for you to reach out.

### Content Posting Strategy

Posting on Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok serves as a passive nurturing tool. Your hot friends already know about your life whether you post or not — content posting primarily reaches your **warm and semi-warm market.** It keeps you visible and gradually warms people up.

### Project Number One Objectives

1. Reach out to all warm and semi-warm friends (calling, social media, messaging)
2. Do policy reviews with hot market
3. Actively post content on social media
4. **Goal:** Build initial success in the first 6 months to sustain momentum

## Part 4: Introduction to Referral Asking

### Why Referrals Matter

- 5 referral calls → ~3 appointments (60% conversion)
- 20 cold/anonymous calls → ~1-2 appointments (5-10% conversion)

Referrals are fundamentally easier because trust is pre-established through the referring party.

### The Two Types of New FCs

| Type | Strength | Risk |
|------|----------|------|
| **FC with strong warm market** | Explosive start (potentially $20K FYC in first 2 months) | Production cliff after warm market is exhausted (~6 months) |
| **Hardworking cold-calling FC** | Consistent activity, gritty work ethic | Must maintain high volume indefinitely without referral pipeline |

**Both types need referral systems** to create sustainability beyond month 6.

### When to Ask for Referrals

The best times are:
- **After closing a sale** (they've demonstrated trust by purchasing)
- **After a positive feedback moment** (they've just told you they found your presentation valuable)

### The Referral Process — 4 Steps

**Step 1: Plant Referral Seeds (Before Presentation)**
At the beginning of your appointment, say something like: *"My business is built on referrals. If after today's presentation you find what I share valuable, I really hope you can recommend some friends to me."*

This works for both warm and cold market appointments.

**Step 2: Ask at the Right Time (After Presentation/Closing)**

Use the **VIP Method** — Value, Importance, Pause:
1. After closing, say: *"I need to go through something very important with you."* Then **pause.**
2. They'll naturally ask: *"What is it?"*
3. You say: *"As I shared at the beginning, my business is built on referrals. Can we spend 5 minutes brainstorming together some friends I can reach out to?"*

Get feedback first: *"Are you happy with the process? Which part did you learn the most?"* Use their positive response as a bridge to the referral ask.

**Step 3: Collaborate — The Phone Technique**

Ask them to take out their phone (Telegram/WhatsApp). Guide them through their recent chats — their first 10 commonly messaged friends. Use a physical **referral paper** and write down names one by one.

Target: **3-5 names per appointment.** If you have 6 appointments/week and ask at 4 of them, that's 12-20 warm referral names weekly.

**Step 4: Handle Objections**

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| *"Let me ask my friends first"* | "No problem, but what I'll do is just drop them a WhatsApp text first. If they're not interested, I won't pursue it. Wouldn't you want your friends to benefit from what you just learned?" |
| *"I'm not comfortable giving names"* | Remind them of the value they just acknowledged. Reassure them of your professional process — text first, respect boundaries. Do NOT offer to call anonymously (this turns it into a cold call, which defeats the purpose). |
| *"I need to think about it"* | Use social proof — show your referral booklet with existing names to normalize the process. |

### Referral Calling Script — Key Structure

When calling the referred person (e.g., Hussein referred Gabriel):

1. **Introduction:** "Hi Gabriel, I'm David from AIA. Hussein asked me to call you." → **Pause.** Let them register who Hussein is.
2. **Establish connection:** "Hussein and I were doing some work recently. He actually spoke very highly of you — mentioned you guys did the final project together and even won best project." → Build rapport through shared knowledge.
3. **Create curiosity (without revealing what you do yet):** "It seems a lot of things I've done for Hussein may be of interest to you."
4. **Soft ask:** "I'm not sure if my work will be of value to you, but I'd be happy to meet for half an hour next week to introduce myself and share some ideas."
5. **They ask "What is this about?"** → Now you reveal: "I'm with AIA Financial Advisors. We help clients build assets and retire earlier."
6. **Leverage the relationship:** "Hussein seems very convinced that you'd find this valuable."
7. **Close for appointment:** "Would next week — weekdays or weekend — work for you?"

**Pro tip:** If the referrer can drop the referred person a message *before* you call, the conversion rate increases significantly.

### Three Referral "Reasons" You Can Use

1. **"I want to cold-call less"** — "If you refer me friends, I can spend more time servicing your portfolio instead of always looking for new clients."
2. **"Your friends deserve this too"** — "The best way to reach responsible people is through people like you. Wouldn't they think better of me coming through you than as a random cold caller?"
3. **"I need your help"** — "I'm new and growing my practice. I'd love to reach more people like you who are responsible and working hard."

### Why Referral Systems Fail

The #1 reason: **The advisor simply doesn't ask.** It's not a skill problem — it's a habit problem. Build it into every appointment until it becomes second nature.

## PART 1: ProAchiever (APA) — Advanced Sales Angles

### Positioning APA as Monthly Income (Not Just Lump Sum)

A common mistake is only presenting APA as a lump sum retirement product. A more compelling approach is to position it as a **monthly income solution**.

**The Sub-Charge Advantage:**
Competitors like Great Eastern, Manulife, SingLife, Prudential, and HSBC charge a supplementary fee of **0.6% to 1%** annually on the fund value. For example, a $2,000,000 fund with GE at 0.7% = **$14,000/year** being eaten away. AIA has **no sub-charges after age 65**, meaning clients can keep their accumulated funds inside the policy without erosion. This allows them to comfortably withdraw monthly income (e.g. $10,000/month) while keeping the principal intact — a very long-term, sustainable concept.

**Key Presentation Flow:**
- Show them their CPF Life projected payout
- Then show the APA investment account generating ~$10K/month
- Together, this paints a **comprehensive retirement solution**

**Withdrawal Flexibility:** Clients can withdraw from age 50, 55, 60, or 65 — the earlier they withdraw, the lower the monthly income. It depends on their preference.

### The Dividend Mode Feature

Inside the APA presenter (iPulse/iSmart), you can illustrate the dividend mode:
1. Select the premium amount (e.g. $1,000/month)
2. Set accumulation until age 65
3. Switch to **Dividend Mode** from age 65 onwards
4. Set premium holiday from age 65
5. Show projected dividends (~$150K/year or ~$10K+/month)

**Unique Selling Point — No other product does this:**
- Robot advisors, ETFs, stocks — none can instantly switch from accumulation to dividend mode with a click
- No fees to switch funds
- **Capital guaranteed upon request** (only fixed deposits offer this, but with much lower returns)
- No estate tax, no dividend withholding tax, no currency risk (unlike direct US stock/ETF investments)

### Loyalty Bonuses — A Powerful Closing Tool

**Where to find it:** Presenter → Details → Welcome Bonus Calculator

For a client putting in **$12K/year**, the bonuses are highest:
- Year 1: ~$6,300
- Year 2: ~$2,700
- Year 3: ~$1,100+
- Bonuses increase over time

**Loyalty Bonus Structure:**
- After 10 years: **5% bonus**
- After 20 years: **8% bonus**
- A client putting $12K/year earns ~$600/year in bonus cash (withdrawable immediately)
- **Total bonuses over 30 years: ~$16,000**

### Linearizing Returns — Proving Your Value & Low Cost

This technique helps clients see both their returns and your low earnings in simple monthly terms:

**For the client ($200/month premium example):**
- Total projected value minus total premiums paid = net gain
- Divide by number of years, then by 12 months
- Result: Client earns ~**$600-$700/month** in equivalent returns

**For the advisor:**
- Distribution cost (e.g. $6,194) ÷ 40 years ÷ 12 months = ~**$12/month**
- After subtracting loyalty bonuses returned to client, advisor earns ~**$1/month or less**

**Why this works:** When the client sees they earn $600/month while you earn $1/month, it builds massive trust and removes the "you're just doing this for commission" objection. AIA is essentially **losing money** on long-term policies when bonuses exceed distribution costs.

- APA offers diversification across different markets
- Additional risks of direct S&P investing: dividend withholding tax, currency risk, estate tax implications
- Reference: Search "S&P" in the LARP document for the full breakdown

**Positioning for sophisticated investors (C-type profiles):** APA is a **complement and diversification tool** alongside their existing ETFs, stocks, crypto, and robo-advisors — not a replacement.

**Background:** Met the prospect in March. He was pre-enlistment, extremely flaky, constantly rescheduling.

**The follow-up timeline:**
- March: First meeting, no close
- July: Prospect enlisted, Gabriel reached out
- August–September: Messaged almost weekly, kept requesting meetings
- November: Prospect went completely silent
- Multiple reschedules — once the prospect cancelled *as Gabriel was heading out the door*
- Eventually secured a meeting

**Result:** $900/month total budget — $400/month APA + $500/month PPP ($450K coverage) + $650K SFTLA (~$50/month)

**The math:** ~6–7 hours total time invested → ~$4K FYC (with long-term renewals = ~$12K). That's effectively **~$2K/hour earning potential**.

### Key Lesson 1: The Power of Follow-Up

- Set **calendar reminders** to follow up with every prospect
- Use the **CRM** religiously — audit your pipeline, check who's set for closing meetings
- Our agency has one of the best CRMs (credit to Leo) — use it
- Just because someone doesn't buy now doesn't mean the lead is dead
- There are no "wasted" leads — every lead can be followed up on later

### Key Lesson 2: Ads ROI — Scale With Confidence

Gabriel's results: Spent **$2K on ads** → Generated **$12K+ FYC** = **6x ROI**

Key points on running ads:
- Insurance leads are NOT like e-commerce — you don't need instant conversion
- You have the **accountability to follow up** and close over time
- ROI is essentially **guaranteed** if you follow up properly
- Don't overspend beyond your capacity to meet leads (Gabriel once had 20–25 appointments stacked with no availability)
- Call leads **when they're fresh** — recent enquirers are most receptive
- Even Leo historically spent $1–2K/month on ads with consistent positive ROI

### Key Lesson 3: Efficiency & Business-Generating Activities

**The 4 highest-leverage activities (in order):**
1. **Cold calling** — setting appointments
2. **Opening** — conducting first meetings
3. **Closing** — getting commitments
4. **Following up** — nurturing pipeline

**Audit your time:** If you're spending most of your hours on training, learning products, or admin — that's not what generates income. Knowledge without action produces nothing.

**If you can't present yet:**
- Option 1: **Role play** with teammates (Ben, Chris, Gabriel, Leo)
- Option 2: **Go for meetings yourself** even if you don't know all products — do the fact-finding, learn by doing
- Gabriel's own experience: Watched countless Fireflies recordings of Leo but couldn't internalize it until he started **doing it himself**
- Christie example: Going for her own meetings and doing fact-finding independently even without full product knowledge

### Key Lesson 4: Peaks and Valleys — Emotional Management

**The Peaks & Valleys concept (Leo's recurring emphasis):**

Don't let emotional swings kill your momentum:
- **After closing:** Don't get complacent. Gabriel closed 5 APAs in one weekend but continued activity the next day.
- **After rejection/free-look:** Don't spiral. 3 of Gabriel's cases free-looked. One high-potential software engineer ($7,500 income, expected $1K APA) only committed to a small amount — felt terrible, but took only ONE day to recover.

**The analogy:** If someone steals $10 from you, do you throw away $2,000 chasing them? If someone ruins 10 minutes of your day, do you waste the next 7 days dwelling on it?

**Practical approach:**
- Allow yourself to feel bad — but cap it at ONE day maximum
- Next day: Back to activity
- When you close: Celebrate briefly, then treat it as expected and move on
- Always ask: "Who can I close next?" rather than dwelling on wins or losses

### Key Lesson 5: Speed of Execution & The Flywheel Effect

The business works like a **flywheel** — once spinning, momentum carries you forward. But if your flywheel is slow, everything takes forever.

- Example: Jiro completed his FTS e-modules in one day while attending school lectures — that's speed of execution
- Example: Jitter went from setting zero appointments to **3 appointments in one day** — baby steps compound
- Don't wait until you're "ready" — start now and improve along the way

**Mindset shift:** You're not selling — you're **serving**. Once you internalize that what you do is meaningful, rejection becomes easier to handle, and you'll stay in the business long-term.

## PART 4: Cold Calling — The Pain Point Approach

### The Framework

Traditional adoption-style scripts: ~1 appointment per hour of calling
Pain point script: **Consistently 8–10 appointments per coding session**

### Core Principles

**Principle 1 — Create Awareness of the Problem**
- People know their problems at the *back* of their head but not the *front*
- Your job is to bring the problem to their conscious awareness
- Analogy: Most people know they're not at peak fitness, but it's not front-of-mind, so they don't act
- An NS guy isn't thinking about inflation. A fresh grad who's gone 40 years without financial planning doesn't think anything's wrong.

**Principle 2 — Answer "What's In It For Me?"**
- Every cold call recipient is thinking: *Why should I talk to you? Why should I meet you?*
- Position yourself as someone who **helps** — a doctor, a consultant — not a salesperson
- Tailor your positioning to their demographic (e.g., for pre-retirees: "We help people around your age with retirement planning")

### The Pain Point Discovery Process

**Step 1 — The Qualifying Question:**

The single most important question:
> *"Have you started investing, or are you still saving in the bank?"*

(For parents ads: "Have you started planning for your children, or not yet?")

This binary question splits prospects into two categories with **different pain points**:

**If they HAVEN'T invested:**
- Ask: *"How come you haven't invested? Is it because you've never learned, don't know where to start, lack knowledge, or you're scared to lose money?"*
- Whatever they answer = their specific pain point

**If they HAVE invested:**
- Ask: *"What are you investing in? US stocks?"*
- Pain point might be: Over-concentration in US markets, not diversified enough
- Reference recent events: "When Trump announced trade tariffs, US stocks dropped 25–30%"

**If they did retirement planning long ago:**
- Pain point: "Is your plan from 20 years ago still relevant today?"

**Step 2 — Convert Pain Point into Zoom Agenda:**

Whatever their pain point is becomes your meeting agenda. Examples:

| Pain Point | Zoom Agenda |
|---|---|
| "Scared to lose money" | "I'll share strategies to reduce your risk when investing and how to diversify properly" |
| "Don't know where to start" | "I'll help you learn about investing so you can grow your money instead of leaving it in the bank" |
| "Only investing a small amount" | "I'll show you how to invest more confidently with risk management strategies" |

**Step 3 — Set the Appointment:**
> *"Maybe we can have a quick 30-minute Zoom to discuss that — no harm, right?"*

### Critical Mistakes to Avoid
- Don't offer something too broad: "I have a financial plan / a needs analysis I can show you" — too generic, too big a request
- Don't be too direct: "What's your pain point?" — let them reveal it through your questions
- Don't pitch insurance on the cold call — they won't show up
- **Curate the agenda to their specific situation** — personalization is everything

### Improving Your Cold Calling

- You **will** be bad at first — Gabriel's early opening was "damn shit" and his mentor thought he wasn't suited for the job
- Improvement comes from **doing**, not studying
- Practice through role play AND real calls
- Baby steps compound: Going from 0 to 3 appointments in a day is real progress
- Don't overthink — just start calling

## Key Takeaways

1. **APA is not just a lump sum product** — position it as monthly income + dividends with no sub-charges after 65
2. **Know your objection handling cold** — especially S&P 500 comparisons and fee transparency
3. **Linearize returns** to show clients how much they earn vs. how little you earn
4. **Follow up relentlessly** — use calendar reminders and CRM; 9-month follow-ups close cases
5. **Ads have guaranteed ROI** if you follow up on leads — scale with confidence
6. **Prioritize business-generating activities**: calling, opening, closing, following up
7. **Manage peaks and valleys** — cap bad days at one day, don't get complacent after wins
8. **Cold call with pain points** — qualify with one question, discover their specific problem, make that the Zoom agenda
9. **Action over theory** — doing beats studying every time; start before you're ready
10. **Serve, don't sell** — authenticity and genuine care for prospects sustains long-term success
