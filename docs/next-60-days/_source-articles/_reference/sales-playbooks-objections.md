## generic

### I already have an agent

*Prospect claims they already have an existing financial advisor or insurance agent.*

**Tags:** common, rapport, switching

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Acknowledge & Pivot:**
> 
> "That's great that you already have someone looking after your finances! I'm not here to replace them. Many of my clients actually work with multiple advisors — it's like getting a second opinion from a doctor.
> 
> Could I ask — when was the last time your agent did a full review of your portfolio? Sometimes a fresh pair of eyes can spot gaps."
> 
> **Key:** Don't bash the existing agent. Position yourself as complementary, not competitive.

**Response 2 — System (Alternative)**

> **The Review Angle:**
> 
> "I totally respect that! Quick question though — does your current agent cover all five areas: protection, savings, investment, medical, and retirement? Most people find they have strong coverage in one or two areas but gaps in others.
> 
> I'd be happy to do a complimentary gap analysis — no obligation at all."

**Response 3 — FINternship Team**

> "That's great. That means you see value in [solution]. What's one thing you wish was better with your current provider?"
> 
> **Why this works:**
> - Affirms their existing choice (no confrontation)
> - Acknowledges they already believe in the product category
> - Opens a gap by asking about dissatisfaction
> - Positions you as someone who listens, not someone who hard-sells
> 
> > 💡 **Tip:** Common answers include response time, clarity of explanation, or proactive reviews. Use whatever they say as your wedge to differentiate.

**Response 4 — FINternship Team**

> **Texting EQ Version (for warm market / friends):**
> 
> "Ahhhh I see — that's great that you actually have some planning done up! 👍
> 
> I actually wanted to reach out because recently I've been working hard to learn about retirement planning and I've been helping friends with their retirement and tax planning.
> 
> I understand you have an advisor working with you also. I have no idea whether you need any form of additional planning, but I was hoping we can have a discussion about how you're doing your planning, then we can see whether I'll be of value to you!
> 
> Something clients have shared is how they wanted an advisor in the same life stage — someone who'll be there for the next 4-5 decades, while an older advisor can be a concurrent mentor at the same time 😊
> 
> Anyway, I'm really hoping we can have a super casual chat about this. I'll catch you before the end of the year for a meal? 🍽️"
> 
> **Case Study Result:** Prospect initially objected that her aunt was her advisor and she avoids mixing friendships with financial dealings. After this approach, she replied: "Lemme give it some thought." The advisor later secured the meeting.
> 
> > 💡 **Key Concept:** Position yourself as *complementary*, not *replacement*. The "concurrent advisor" frame removes the threat to their existing relationship.

*Prospect deflects by putting the ball in their court, signaling disinterest without confrontation.*

**Tags:** warm-market, deflection, texting-eq

Actually most of my clients also met me without feeling interested — they didn't know how I plan for my clients' portfolios prior to meeting me. I just wanted to show you how I've been helping our friends with their investments. Then from there you can decide for yourself if it's relevant? 😊 Which days are you usually in office?"
> 
> **Texting EQ Framework (4 Steps):**
> 1. **Acknowledge** — Validate their position casually
> 2. **Common ground** — "Most of my clients felt the same way"
> 3. **Different perspective** — Show what you actually do
> 4. **Make them feel safe** — They decide, no pressure
> 
> > 💡 **Tip:** Never leave the ball in their court. If you say "sure, let me know!", you lose all follow-up rights. Instead, pivot to an easy-to-answer question that keeps the conversation alive.

### I'm not interested

*A flat rejection with no specific reason given. Often a reflex response.*

**Tags:** cold-call, opening, common

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Pattern Interrupt:**
> 
> "I appreciate your honesty! And honestly, most of my best clients said the exact same thing when we first spoke. They weren't interested in *insurance* per se — they were interested in making sure their family was protected if something unexpected happened.
> 
> Can I ask — if something happened to you tomorrow, would your family be financially okay for the next 5 years?"
> 
> **Key:** Shift from selling a product to addressing a real concern.

**Response 2 — System (Alternative)**

> **The Curiosity Hook:**
> 
> "Totally fair! Most people aren't interested in insurance — I wasn't either until I learned how much money I was leaving on the table with tax savings and CPF optimization.
> 
> Would you be open to a 10-minute coffee chat? If it's not relevant, I'll be the first to tell you."

**Response 3 — FINternship Team**

> "Totally understand. Most people I talk to feel the same way at first. Just out of curiosity — when was the last time you revisited [problem area]?"
> 
> **Why this works:**
> - Validates the prospect's feeling immediately ("Totally understand")
> - Normalises the objection ("Most people feel the same way")
> - Pivots with a soft, curiosity-driven question that re-opens the conversation
> - Lets you identify a pain point without being pushy
> 
> > 💡 **Tip:** Replace `[problem area]` with something specific to the prospect — e.g. "your family's protection coverage" or "your retirement planning."

**Response 4 — FINternship Team**

> **Texting EQ Version (Casual/WhatsApp):**
> 
> "Hahaha YES I expected you to be not interested — if you're interested in it you would have already texted me 😂
> 
> Actually most of my clients also met me without feeling interested in anything I have to say because after all they didn't know how I plan for my clients' portfolio prior to meeting me.
> 
> So I want to show you, how I improve my clients' investment returns to complement what you have.
> 
> Then from there you can decide for yourself if it's relevant to you? Heh sometimes the conclusion would be to keep your portfolio unchanged also 😄
> 
> Which days are you usually in office? / Do you work from home or office?"
> 
> **Why this works (Texting EQ 4-Step):**
> 1. Acknowledge with humour — disarms defensiveness
> 2. Normalise — "most clients felt the same"
> 3. Redirect to value — show, don't tell
> 4. Safety — they decide, not you
> 5. Easy question — keeps convo alive
> 
> > 💡 **Pro Tip:** Split this into multiple short messages (chunking) for a more natural texting feel. Don't send it as one block.

### I don't believe in insurance

*Prospect has a fundamental objection to the concept of insurance.*

**Tags:** mindset, education, common

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Reframe with Math:**
> 
> "I get it — insurance can feel abstract. Let me put it in practical terms:
> 
> If you earn $5,000/month, that's $60,000/year. Over 20 working years, that's $1.2 million in income. A critical illness policy essentially *protects* that $1.2M asset for a fraction of the cost.
> 
> You insure your car for $30K. Why not insure your ability to earn $1.2M?"
> 
> **Key:** Use concrete numbers, not abstract concepts.

### Now is not the right time

*A generic timing objection — prospect feels the timing isn't ideal.*

**Tags:** common, stalling, timing

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **The Cost of Waiting:**
> 
> "I understand — timing matters. But here's something most people don't realize:
> 
> **Every year you wait:**
> - Premiums increase by 3-8% (age-based pricing)
> - Health conditions may develop (loading or exclusions)
> - You're unprotected during the gap
> 
> A 30-year-old who waits until 35 to buy CI coverage pays roughly **25-35% more** for the same coverage — and that's assuming perfect health.
> 
> Is there a specific event you're waiting for? Maybe we can plan around it rather than delay entirely."

### The market is too volatile right now

*Prospect is hesitant due to current market conditions.*

**Tags:** market, volatility, investment

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Volatility is Normal:**
> 
> "I completely understand the concern. But consider this:
> 
> - In the last 30 years, markets have crashed in 2000, 2008, 2020, and 2022
> - **Every single time**, they recovered and hit new highs
> - People who stayed invested through 2008-2009 saw 300%+ returns over the next decade
> 
> Timing the market is nearly impossible — even professional fund managers can't do it consistently. What works is **time IN the market**, not timing the market.
> 
> With dollar-cost averaging through a regular premium plan, volatility actually *helps* you — you buy more units when prices are low.
> 
> Would you like me to show you a backtest of what would have happened if you started investing during the 2008 crash?"

### I want to wait until I earn more

*Prospect believes they need a higher income before starting financial planning.*

**Tags:** income, career, young-professional

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **The Lifestyle Creep Warning:**
> 
> "That makes logical sense — more income = more budget for insurance. But here's what actually happens:
> 
> 📈 Income goes up → Lifestyle goes up → Savings stay the same
> 
> This is called **lifestyle inflation**, and it's the #1 reason people never "get around to it."
> 
> The advisors I respect most told their younger clients: **"Lock in your protection at today's low premiums while your health is perfect. You can always upgrade coverage later, but you can't undo a health condition."**
> 
> Even $100/month now gets you meaningful coverage. And as your income grows, we gradually expand the plan. Sound reasonable?"

### Just send me an email / brochure

*Prospect tries to end the conversation by requesting written materials instead.*

**Tags:** deflection, cold-call, common

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Agree and Advance:**
> 
> "Sure, I'd be happy to send you something! But honestly, a generic brochure won't be very useful — every person's situation is different.
> 
> How about this: let me ask you 3 quick questions so I can send you something actually relevant to YOUR situation. Would that be okay?"
> 
> **Key:** Don't refuse the request. Agree, then pivot to engagement.

**Response 2 — FINternship Team**

> "Happy to, but I don't want to waste your time. Usually, people find it more valuable to see if this is relevant first. Can I ask a couple of quick questions?"
> 
> **Why this works:**
> - Shows respect for their time (not just yours)
> - Reframes email as potentially wasteful for *them*
> - Asks for a micro-commitment (just a couple of questions) instead of a full meeting
> - Keeps the conversation alive rather than ending with "sure, what's your email?"
> 
> > 💡 **Tip:** If they still insist on email, send a short personalised message with ONE compelling insight — not a generic brochure.

**Tags:** ghosting, follow-up, texting-eq

>

> "No worries! I'll check in with you around [month]!" — This keeps the ball in YOUR court for follow-up rights.

> 1. **Never take it personally** — laugh it off, maintain confidence

> 5. **Set the follow-up date further away** — "I'll text you in Feb to arrange a date?" gives them breathing room
> 
> > 💡 **Mindset:** "I've had plenty of people ghost me, but I have 175 clients that I love and serve, and I honestly can't remember who the last person that ghosted me was."

> - No behavioral coaching when markets drop
> 
> When you add it all up, the *net* cost difference is often much smaller than the headline fee suggests.
> 
> **The real question is:** Do you value having protection + investment in one solution with someone to guide you?"

**Response 2 — System (Alternative)**

> **Acknowledge and Redirect:**
> 
> "That's a valid concern. Let me be transparent — in the early years, charges are higher because the insurance coverage cost is front-loaded. But after year 5-7, the fund value typically starts to compound meaningfully.
> 
> For clients concerned about charges, I often recommend **Pro Achiever** which has a welcome bonus that effectively offsets the early charges. Would you like me to show you the numbers?"

**Tags:** endowment, returns, smart-wealth-builder

**Response 1 — System (Best Practice)**

> **Reframe the Purpose:**
> 
> "You're comparing apples and oranges. An endowment isn't meant to beat the stock market — it's designed for **guaranteed, disciplined savings** with a modest bonus on top.

> - 🎯 Endowment: 2-3% guaranteed + non-guaranteed bonuses, **forced savings**
> 
> For goals like a wedding fund, child's education, or house down payment, the *certainty* of having the money when you need it is worth more than chasing higher returns.
> 
> What specific goal are you saving for?"
