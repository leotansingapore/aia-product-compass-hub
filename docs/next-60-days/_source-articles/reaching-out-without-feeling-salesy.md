# How to Reach Out to Friends Without Feeling Salesy

Most advisors stay silent with friends to avoid seeming pushy. Silence costs both sides. Here's the 6-step honest message.

> **Scope:** This is the **opening text** for first warm-market contact. For **follow-up** after silence, see [[winning-warm-market-value-first]] (value-first reframe, not another ask).

---

## The 6 Steps

### 1. Start With Real Connection
Don't jump into business. Reference them first.
> *"Hey! Hope Vietnam's been treating you well — saw your travel posts, looked like a blast!"*

### 2. Be Honest About Why You're Reaching Out
Vulnerability beats polish.
> *"I've been meaning to reach out about something a little more personal — financial planning and investing. Honestly, I was a little scared to bring it up."*

### 3. Say the Awkward Thing Out Loud
Name the elephant to take its power away.
> *"I didn't want to make things weird or seem like I was reaching out just to pitch something."*

### 4. Offer Help, Not Pressure
Invitation, not sales call.
> *"If you're open to it, I'd love to do a quick, no-obligation review of where you're at financially and share how I've been helping others grow their money."*

### 5. Give Them an Easy Out
The freedom to say no makes yes easier.
> *"Of course, if it's not something you're keen on right now, I totally understand."*

### 6. End With the Relationship First
Close by circling back to the friendship.
> *"Would love to catch up properly too — it's been way too long. Where are you working now?"*

---

## Full Sample Message

> *"Hey [Name]!*
>
> *Hope Vietnam's been treating you well — saw some of your travel pics, looked like a blast!*
>
> *This is a little out of the blue, but I was chatting with a few friends in Events recently and thought of you. I've been meaning to reach out about something a bit personal — financial planning and investing.*
>
> *I'll be honest, I always admired how put-together you've seemed since secondary school — which is probably why I hesitated. I didn't want it to feel like a pitch or make things awkward between us.*
>
> *But lately I've reminded myself: if I genuinely believe in what I do and truly care about the people in my life, I shouldn't stay quiet.*
>
> *So — if you're open to it — I'd love to offer a no-pressure, no-obligation review of your current financial setup and share a few ideas on how I've been helping others grow their money, especially with recent market shifts and rising living costs.*
>
> *Totally understand if it's not your thing, no worries at all. I just didn't want to look back later and regret not saying something to someone I really respect.*
>
> *Would be nice to catch up properly too — been way too long! Where are you working now?"*

---

## The Core Reframe

> **When you reach out with warmth, honesty, and no pressure, you don't sound like a salesperson. You sound like a friend who wants to help.**

Ask yourself:
*"If I truly believe in what I do, and I genuinely care about this person, why would I stay silent?"*
