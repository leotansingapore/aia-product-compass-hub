# Strategic Target List — Segment, Project, Pack

> **Thesis:** Success is not about goal-*setting* — it's about goal-*getting*. You plan leads in advance, segment the database, project revenue per appointment, then fill the year.

---

## Part 1 — The Yearly Math (Pack the Schedule)

> If you want **6–8 first-factfind / review appointments per week × 40 working weeks** → **240–320 appointments per year**.

Where do they come from?

1. **Current inventory** — existing clients & prior prospects
2. **New leads** — passive referrals, active referrals, seminars, webinars, marketing ads, strategic collaborations, social media

### The trap
If you have 200 clients and 30% meet for review → 60 appointments → that only packs **10 weeks**. The other **30 weeks must come from new leads.** Plan for it.

---

## Part 2 — Database Segmentation

Two axes: **ABC** (transacted customers) and **1/2/3** (prospects / natural market).

### ABC — Current Clients

| Tier | Definition | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | Strong relationship **AND** strong buying power. Treats you as a financial confidant, refers clients to you. | **The future.** Clients you want more of. |
| **B** | Strong buying power *OR* strong relationship — not both. Either transactional-but-wealthy, or cooperative-but-average-income. | **The present.** Day-to-day bread and butter. |
| **C** | Has a past with you — transacted once, no relationship momentum, no referrals. | **The past.** Maintain via broadcast/email, low-effort. |

> *"A represents the future. B represents the present. C represents the past."*

Alternative labelling: Platinum / Gold / Silver. Or business-class / economy / budget airline.

### 1 / 2 / 3 — Prospects & Natural Market

| Tier | Definition |
|---|---|
| **1 — Hot** | Fresh opt-ins, well-positioned / nurtured, close warm-market. High conviction. |
| **2 — Warm** | Fresh referrals (active — client introduced them to you). Not opted-in; will require outreach, but the relationship via the referring client is the bridge. |
| **3 — Cold** | People who know you but haven't kept in touch. Awkward to reach out both directions. Old "no"s from years ago now warmer because you've established yourself. |

### The full target list sources
ABC + 1/2/3 + new-lead sources (passive referrals, active referrals, seminars/webinars, marketing ads, strategic collaboration, social media following).

## Part 3 — Revenue-per-Appointment Math (Case Study)

> A stagnant long-time MDT advisor, paid coaching at $1,000+/hr, projected her year via this tool.

### Her ABC Projection (Year View)

| Segment | Count | Meetup % | Close % | Avg Case | Transactions | **Revenue** | **Rev / Review Appt** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | 100 | 40% | 70% | $15,000 | 28 | **$420,000** | **$10,500** |
| **B** | 200 | 50% | 60% | $6,000 | 60 | **$360,000** | lower |
| **C** | large | 10% | 50% | $4,000 | 5 | **$20,000** | negligible |

**Teaching point:** *"If you missed each A-client review, you lose out $10,500. How do you feel? That's where you spend your time. Not on C."*

### Qualified Prospects (1/2/3 and referrals not yet closed)
For every 1 client acquired, there are **1–3 prospects met but not closed**. Tracking these yields another ~$60k+ on the table.

## Part 4 — Total Projection (Same Case Study)

| Source | Appointments | Close × Case | **Revenue** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Current inventory (ABC + 1/2/3) | 181 | mixed | **$864,000** |
| Passive referrals (30/year × 80% meet × 80% close × $8k) | 24 | 19 × $8,000 | **$153,000** |
| Active referrals asked at end of FF (70% of 181 × 3 refs × 40% meet) | 109 | — | *additional capacity* |

**Total potential** for this stagnant MDT advisor: **>$1M revenue** — a strong COT level. All from organizing the database, no new paid marketing.

> *"Her referrals alone and existing clients alone are more than enough to keep her busy."*

## Part 6 — The Strategic Target List System

> The antidote to *"what is more tiring than a full day of appointments? A day with no appointments."*

### Start Every Month With a Target List

- One sheet with 20 names minimum
- Columns: **Name · Mobile (optional) · ABC/123 Category · Trigger · Approach**
- Organize names by segment (A first, then B, then 1, 2, 3)
- Customize **trigger + script per individual** — generic script = generic result
- Comb down weekly; if > 20 names, make a second list
- Whether you do it weekly or monthly — **don't skip it**

### The Axe Metaphor

> *"Working hard without the right strategy, script or technique is chopping the tree with a blunt axe. Every mistake costs you $3,000–$5,000 per case in lost revenue. If you think education is expensive, try ignorance."*

## Part 7 — Inventory Vocabulary

Author's accounting frame:

| Type | Analogy (Time Value of Money) | What It Is |
|---|---|---|
| **Current Inventory** | **PV** — Present Value | Your ABC + 1/2/3 database today |
| **Recurring Inventory** | **PMT** — ongoing payment | The monthly new-lead flow (passive referrals, active referrals, marketing, events, collaborations) |

Formula frame: **FV = PV × (1 + r)^n + PMT stream.** Your advisory revenue compounds if both inventory types are managed.

---

**Teaching angle:** Most advisors operate on vibes — call whoever comes to mind. This framework forces **triage** (A before C) and **projection** (if you don't fill the list, the math tells you what you'll miss). Pairs with the **CAR framework** (from [[social-proof-scripts-and-sales-cycle]]) — CAR tells you which bottleneck to unblock; Target List tells you *whom* to call.

**Critical mindset reframe:**
> **Not having appointments is more tiring than having them.** An empty calendar kills energy. Packing it — even with imperfect leads — sustains motion.

**Reflection prompt:** *"Open a spreadsheet. List your top 20 clients/prospects for next month. Tag each A/B/C or 1/2/3. For each, write one sentence: the trigger + script variant. Book the first 5 this week."*
