> **The gist.** This is your business plan for the next five years, on one document. You set the income and savings you're aiming at, work backwards to the FYC and client numbers that get you there, decide who you'll sell to and how you'll reach them, lay out your week, write your calling script, and name the first three people you'll sit with. Then you present it to your mentor and run it.

Everything else in the track builds one skill at a time. This pulls them into a single plan you can actually steer by: your [why](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/100-whys) and your [vision board](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/vision-board) set the direction, your [Project 200 list](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/project-200) feeds the markets, and your [market survey practice](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/market-survey-practice) and [CST roleplay](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/cst-roleplay) are how you'll work it. The plan is where it all connects.

## The six sections

**1. My goals.** Start with the income you want a month in five years, and the cash you want saved by then. Then do the maths backwards: what FYC per year, what Club tier, how many clients and cases. The numbers don't have to be perfect on the first pass - the point is to see what your goal actually asks of you each week.

**2. My strengths and areas to grow.** Three strengths and how each helps you advise well. Three weaknesses and how you'll work on each. Write the honest version. Your mentor coaches the plan you give them, so a polished one that hides the gaps just wastes the coaching.

**3. My lead generation.** Three target markets you can operate in, with the real reason each fits - usually existing trust or a network you already belong to. Tie it back to your Project 200 list, decide how you'll prospect it, and set the monthly FYC you'd stand behind.

**4. My weekly timetable.** A typical week, colour-coded, split across prospecting, preparation, me time, learning, and study if you're still in classes. Building it in your calendar makes the trade-offs obvious: there are only so many prospecting slots in a week, and your goal in section one tells you how many you need.

**5. My calling system.** The script you'll actually use to reach people - how you introduce yourself, your purpose for the call, how you close and keep the door open if they say no. Plus the system you'll use to track every prospect and follow up. Pick something you'll keep up, not the most elaborate one.

**6. Sales competency.** Name your first three prospects from the list: who they are, how you know them, what they likely need, and what you'd propose. These are the appointments your mentor will observe on the way to sales certification.

## What good looks like

- The numbers in section one connect. The income goal, the FYC, the client count, and the prospecting slots in your calendar all point at each other instead of being five separate guesses.
- The target markets have a real reason behind them, not "everyone needs insurance". Name the trust or the network that makes each one yours to work.
- The calling script reads the way you talk. Read it out loud - if it sounds like a brochure, rewrite it until it sounds like you.
- The first three prospects are real people you could call this week, with a specific need next to each name.

## A note before you present

Nobody runs the plan they first write down. The value is in writing it - seeing how much the goal actually asks of the week, and where your plan is still thin. Bring the honest version to the review. You're pre-licence, so you can't sell any of this yet, but the plan is what you'll run from the day you can.
