> **The gist.** Build and rehearse two Client Strategy Talks (CSTs) - the Wealth Angle and the Risk Management Angle - each stopping at the door-opener, neither closing a product. When both are ready, you run them live past your trainer, get them approved, then submit a short checklist plus the flow you've compiled for each. The point is to internalise both flows so you can pick whichever fits the prospect in front of you.

**The shape of it:** build your flow for each CST → rehearse until it's clean → run both past your trainer on a call and get them approved → tick the checklist, summarise each flow in your own words, and add a short reflection. No video to upload - your trainer reviews your delivery live.

## Why two CSTs and not one

A CST is the 5-15-minute structured concept presentation you deliver between rapport-building and the recommendation meeting. It's not a sale - it's the *frame* that earns the next meeting.

There are two anchor angles every FC must master:

| Angle | Source | Lead with this when... |
|---|---|---|
| **A - Wealth Angle** (Retirement Step-by-Step) | [Day 52](../../first-60-days/week-9/day-52.md) | The prospect is interested in *growing money*, retirement, investing, or has explicitly said they want to plan for the future |
| **B - Risk Management Angle** (Total Wealth Concept / TWFPS) | [Day 14](../../first-60-days/week-3/day-14.md), [Day 53](../../first-60-days/week-9/day-53.md) | The prospect is interested in *protection*, has a young family, has a fear of losing income, or is risk-averse |

Most prospects respond to one angle more than the other. The producer who can deliver both - and pick the right one in the first 5 minutes of rapport - converts more meetings than the one who only knows one angle.

This assignment forces you to record both, so when you sit down with a real prospect, the right CST is already in your hands.

## Part A - The **Wealth Angle CST** (Retirement Step-by-Step)

> **Source-of-truth for this part:** [Day 52 - Retirement Step-by-Step CST](../../first-60-days/week-9/day-52.md). The walkthrough below is the *succinct version*; the full delivery script (including extensions, common reactions, and compliance) lives there. Re-read Day 52 in full before you run it.
>
> **Two delivery modes - pick one:**
> - **Paper-and-pen** - your notebook / whiteboard, in front of camera. Best when meeting in person without a screen.
> - **Live interactive tool** - open the **[Total Wealth Concept . Retirement Step-by-Step](https://present.financeillustrator.com/total-wealth-concept/retirement?subtab=step-by-step)** page in front of the prospect, set their five inputs, click *Draw All* on each tab. Best when you have a screen between you. *Mention which mode you're using in your self-review.*

### The script - concept-draw FIRST, slides come second

Day 52's foundational rule: don't open the laptop and *explain* slides. Draw the concept on a single page first; the presenter slides come after, as confirmation. Run it in this order so the live draw lands before any slide does.

### 1. Capture the 5 inputs (1 min)

Ask, write across the top of the page, confirm:

| # | Input | Default if unsure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Current age | (must have) |
| 2 | Retirement age | 65 |
| 3 | Life expectancy | 85 |
| 4 | Target monthly lifestyle (today's dollars) | match current monthly spending |
| 5 | Inflation | 2.5% (long-term Singapore average) |

### 2. Draw Diagram 1 - The Problem (5 steps, ~5 min)

Horizontal timeline with three tick marks: today / retirement age / life-expectancy.

| Step | What you draw | What you say (key beat) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Red dot on today's age. Inflation slope rising up to retirement. Label *"$X/mth - your desired lifestyle"* + *"2.5% p.a. inflation"* | *"This line is the cost of the same lifestyle, going up over the next N years."* |
| **2** | Wait - **let them guess** the inflated monthly cost. Then write *"Need ~ $X/mth"* at the slope's peak. | *"What do you think this same lifestyle will cost at 65?"* (Most guess too low - that's the conversion.) |
| **3** | Below the slope: `inflated x 12 x retirement years = $X.XM`. Make this the **biggest number** on the page. Underline it twice. | *"Your retirement pot needs about $X.XM sitting there ready to draw down."* (Pause. Let it sit.) |
| **4** | Dashed grey depletion line from retirement peak down to $0 at end-age, with down-arrows. Label *"Capital depletes -> $0"*. | *"Best case, perfect plan - there's nothing left at the end. No buffer. No legacy."* |
| **5** | Calculate live: `lump sum / working-years-remaining / 12 = $X/mth`. Write it **very large**. | *"You need to put away about $X every single month - and still end at zero."* **Stop. Don't transition.** |

### 3. Draw Diagram 2 - The Solution (4 steps, ~4 min)

Fresh page or different colour. Same timeline. Curve grows steeper because of compounding.

| Step | What you draw | What you say (key beat) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Green growth slope from today to retirement. Label *"Invest $X/mth"* - dramatically smaller than the Step-5 number above. | *"Same target. Not $X/mth saving - about $X/mth invested."* (Let the contrast land.) |
| **2** | Bigger green dot at retirement. Label *"Achieve $X.XM -> reinvest for dividends"*. | *"At 65, you've hit the target. Now how you take income matters more than the size of the pot."* |
| **3** | Two paths from the retirement dot - grey dashed depletion (old version) vs **solid green horizontal line staying at the same height** with dividend arrows hanging down. Label dividends *"~$X,XXX/mth"*. At end-age write *"$X.XM still preserved"*. | *"Capital stays intact. You're not draining a tank - you're drinking from a stream. Plus the $X.XM becomes a legacy."* |
| **4** | Side-by-side cards - **WITHOUT investing** (Save $X/mth -> $0 at end-age) vs **WITH investing** (Invest $X/mth -> capital preserved + $X,XXX/mth dividends). | *"Same goal, same lifestyle. The only thing that changes is the choice to invest instead of just save."* |

### 4. Door-opener (30 sec - the delivery ends here)

> *"I'm not going to suggest anything specific today. The right plan depends on what's already in CPF, your time horizon, your real risk tolerance - none of which we've covered yet. Mind if I run a proper fact-find with you, then come back with an actual plan that gets you to $X.XM at $X/mth instead of $X/mth?"*

### Optional - pick ONE extension if you have time inside the 15-min cap

These are Day 52 sections 5-9. Use them to deepen the close *only if you have spare time*. Don't try to fit all five into the delivery - you'll rush.

- **The 1/3 income rule** - *"$X/mth fits inside the 'long-term' third of your take-home"* (collapses the *I can't afford it* objection)
- **The dividend twist** - *"$X.XM at 6% throws off $X,XXX/mth, more than your inflated lifestyle"* (the math behind why capital stays intact)
- **AIA vs DIY (the full 8-point stack)** - dividend withholding tax (30% -> 0%), USD/SGD currency risk (DBS forecasts SGD parity with USD by 2040), US estate tax (40% above $60K exemption), concentration risk (S&P 500 = only ~18% of Elite Adventurous), lost-decade risk (2000-2010: S&P -9% while Int'l Small Cap Value +191%), execution on death, secondary-insured continuity, and the macro USD pressure cluster. **Before you run it, skim the [SP500-vs-ILP conviction reference](/learning-track/first-60-days/reference/sp500-vs-ilp-conviction)** - pick *one* number that matches your roleplay prospect's likely objection and land that one cleanly. Don't try to land all eight; that's a lecture, not a CST.
- **The 0.7%-on-$2M sub-charge** - *"$14K/year removed from a $2M portfolio at other insurers; AIA doesn't have that after 65"* (when prospect compares fees)
- **Bonuses sweetener** - *"Year 1/2/3 welcome bonus + 5%/8% lifetime"* (only after value is clear; never lead with this)

### Wealth-Angle delivery checklist

- 8-15 minutes end-to-end (target ~12 for a tight delivery)
- Both diagrams drawn live in front of your trainer - paper, whiteboard, iPad, **OR** the live interactive tool. Not pre-drawn.
- The "guess the number" pause in Step 2 of the Problem is there
- The pause after the no-investing reveal in Step 5 is at least 3 seconds
- End at the door-opener - do **not** name a product

## Part B - The **Risk Management Angle CST** (Total Wealth Concept)

### What you must cover (in order, on paper, in front of camera)

#### 1. Draw the two boxes (1 min)

On a piece of paper, draw two boxes side by side. Label them **Financial Capital** and **Human Capital**.

> *"Which one do you think you have more of right now - Financial Capital or Human Capital?"*

Almost everyone correctly answers Human Capital. That's the opening - they've conceded the bigger asset is the one nobody insures.

#### 2. Surface Human Capital (1 min)

> *"Human Capital is your future earning power - your career, your years of working ahead. For most people in their 30s, it's worth $2-4 million in future income. Way bigger than what's in the Financial box today. But you've probably never had it valued."*

The "you are your own golden goose" line works here:

> *"You are your own golden goose. Your bank account is small. Your ability to keep producing income is the asset that matters."*

Pause. Let it land.

#### 3. Draw the conversion arrow (1 min)

Draw an arrow from Human Capital -> Financial Capital. Write four words on it: **earn, spend less, save, invest**.

> *"Building wealth is just this loop, repeated for 30 years."*

#### 4. The "health or wealth" bridge (30 sec)

> *"Health or wealth - which is more important?"*
>
> (Prospect: *"Health, of course."*)
>
> *"Then we can't just talk about investing. We also have to talk about protecting the engine that produces the wealth in the first place."*

#### 5. Draw the TWFPS four pillars (3-4 min)

Under Human Capital, draw four boxes. Under Financial Capital, draw one box.

| Pillar | Benchmark coverage |
|---|---|
| **Death / Disability** | 10-20x annual income |
| **Critical Illness** *(weakest link, often ignored)* | $100K + 3-5x annual income |
| **Hospital & Accident** | Hospital plan + Medishield upgrade + PA |
| **Investment & Savings** | Global balanced strategy |

Walk through each. Use the *"hospital plan pays the hospital, CI plan pays YOU"* line for the CI pillar. Use the cancer-drug rationale for the $100K buffer (one vial of certain cancer drugs is ~$10K per dose, often not covered by hospital plans).

#### 6. The protection-question close (1 min)

Underline Human Capital. Ask:

> *"If this stopped tomorrow - accident, illness, anything - how long does what's in the Financial box last your family?"*

Most prospects can't answer. That silence is the assignment for the next meeting.

#### 7. Door-opener (30 sec - the delivery stops here)

> *"I'm not going to recommend anything today. The right plan depends on the size of both boxes today and where you want them in 20 years. Mind if I run through a few questions to actually understand your situation? Then I can put a real plan together for you."*

### Risk-Angle delivery checklist

- 8-15 minutes end-to-end
- Pen-and-paper diagrams drawn live (two boxes + arrow + four pillars)
- The "which one do you have more of?" opening question is in there
- The "health or wealth" bridge is in there
- All four pillars drawn - not just named
- End at the door-opener - do **not** name a product or quote a premium

## What "passing" looks like - for both CSTs

### The pass criteria (both CSTs, separately)

| Criterion | What good looks like |
|---|---|
| **Diagram integrity** | Both diagrams drawn live on paper/whiteboard, fully visible to your trainer |
| **Numbers confidence** | Math done in front of the prospect, no calculator panic, lump-sum number written large |
| **Concept fidelity** | The CST follows the source-material structure (Day 52 for Part A, Day 14 for Part B) - no skipped steps |
| **Silence discipline** | At least one 3+ second pause after a closing question or big-number reveal |
| **Door-opener landed** | Specific permission asked for the next meeting (fact-find), no product named |
| **Natural delivery** | Script is internalised, not read. You can glance at notes - you can't read off them |
| **One objection handled** | Brief the actor to throw at least one objection (e.g., *"that's too big a number"* or *"I need to ask my parents"*) and you handle it with a Day 14 / Day 53 reframe |

### Common reasons people don't get signed off

- **Pre-drew the diagrams.** The whole point is the live drawing - the prospect watching the gap appear in real time. Pre-drawn = run it again.
- **Skipped the guess-then-reveal in Wealth Angle Step 2.** That pause is the conversion. Don't skip it.
- **Named a product at the end.** The CST opens the door. The product comes in the *next* meeting. Naming a product on this CST = run it again.
- **Read off a script.** Memorisation is the assignment. A glance at off-table notes is fine; reading line-by-line isn't.

## How to run it with your trainer

### Rehearse first

- Whiteboard, iPad, or A4 paper - whatever you'll actually use in front of a prospect. The diagrams must be legible.
- Run each CST end-to-end in one go - no stopping. If you stumble, start that CST over. You're building the muscle to deliver it live, uninterrupted.
- Draw both diagrams live as you talk. Pre-drawing defeats the point - the prospect watching the gap appear in real time is the whole move.

### Practise on a real person before the trainer

- A friend, spouse, sibling, or fellow Pre-RNF cohort member playing the prospect:
  - **For Part A (Wealth Angle):** Age 25-40, working professional, some savings, no specific retirement plan, vaguely interested in investing
  - **For Part B (Risk Angle):** Age 30-45, has a family or partner, mortgage, may have employer hospital coverage but nothing else, slightly resistant to "buying insurance"
- Ask them to throw one objection per CST - otherwise let it flow.

### Then run it past your trainer for sign-off

- Book a call and deliver both CSTs to your trainer as if they were the prospect.
- They give feedback live and approve each one when it's ready to use in a real meeting.
- If a CST isn't there yet, tighten it and re-run it. When both are approved, tick the checklist.

You'll rebuild both CSTs at least once during your first year - usually after Month 3 (when you've delivered them in real meetings) and again at Month 12 (when your delivery has matured).
