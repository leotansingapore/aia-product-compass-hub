# Assignment - Policy Summary for a Family Member

> **What you're producing:** A one-page Policy Summary built in the Total Wealth Concept tool at [present.financeillustrator.com](https://present.financeillustrator.com), using the real policy documents of a family member who agreed to share them with you.

## Why this matters

Most new FCs have never actually read a policy document in detail - not their own, not anyone's. Reading one policy is fine. Building a *summary* across five or six policies forces you to do the thing every client conversation eventually requires: sit with messy documents, extract the few numbers that matter, and present them on one page that a normal person can read without a glossary.

A family member is the right starting point for three reasons:

1. They already trust you, so the awkward part - asking to see someone's policies - is removed.
2. Their portfolio is real and messy, not the tidy demo data in a training module. Real life has lapsed riders, missing nominations, two life policies from two different agents, and a hospitalisation plan they forgot they bought.
3. You produce something genuinely useful for them by the end. Most family members have never seen their own portfolio laid out on one page, and many will quietly book a proper review with you off the back of it.

## What you'll learn from doing it

- Where to look in a policy document for the seven numbers that actually go on the summary (sum assured, premium, mode, premium payment term, coverage term, riders, nominees).
- How to spot the three most common Singapore household gaps: under-insured for hospitalisation, no critical illness past 65, and no clear claim contact.
- How to translate ILP / par-plan / endowment jargon into one sentence a non-FC family member will understand.
- How to use the Total Wealth Concept layout so the same summary later becomes the spine of a real fact-find.

## How you complete this assignment

1. **Ask one family member** for their existing policy documents. Frame it honestly: *"I'm learning how to read these properly - would you let me practise on yours and give you a one-page summary at the end?"* Almost everyone says yes.
2. **Open the tool**: [present.financeillustrator.com/total-wealth-concept/policy-summary-builder/summaries/v2](https://present.financeillustrator.com/total-wealth-concept/policy-summary-builder/summaries/v2)
3. **Enter each policy** as a row. Skip nothing - even the policies they think are dormant.
4. **Walk through the summary with them** once it's done. Read it together. The conversation that happens at this step is the whole reason for the exercise.
5. **Save the share link or export a PDF**, then submit it in the form below alongside the gaps you surfaced.

## What good looks like

- At least three policies on the summary. If the family member has only one, ask a different family member - you need a few rows to see overlaps.
- Every row filled in completely. No empty "premium term" or "nominee" cells.
- The gaps paragraph names something specific - *"father has only S$50k hospitalisation, ward B1, and no rider past age 75"* - not generic - *"could be better protected"*.
- The family member, after seeing the summary, says something like *"I didn't realise that"* or *"I should fix that"*. If they don't, the summary is probably too generic.

## A note on confidentiality

These are real documents from real people who trust you. Treat them that way: don't share the file with anyone, don't paste real names or NRIC numbers into the form, and delete the source PDFs from your laptop once the summary is saved in the tool. If your family member changes their mind later, delete the share link too.

The skill you're practising here is the skill that earns the second meeting in every client relationship you'll ever have. Treat the practice the way you'd want a future client to treat your portfolio.
