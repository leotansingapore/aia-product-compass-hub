> **The gist.** Run **30 warm-market surveys** off your [Project 200 list](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/project-200), across your study weeks, while the "I can't sell you anything yet" framing is still honestly true. It's the reps - not the reading - that make the survey script yours.

**This one fills up over time.** Run your surveys in blocks across your CMFAS / RES5 study window, then come back and update your numbers as they grow. Build your [Project 200 list](/learning-track/pre-rnf/assignments/project-200) first - the surveys come off those names.

## Why 30, and why before RNF

**The rule:** complete **30 warm-market surveys before your RNF goes through.** That's the floor, not a ceiling. The licensing window (CMFAS / M9 / M9A / HI / RES5 study and exams) is exactly when you put these reps in. By the time your name shows up on MAS as a registered representative, the script is already yours.

- **Pre-RNF you literally cannot sell.** That makes the survey framing honest in a way it never will be again. Once licensed, "I'm just doing a survey" reads as a soft pitch. Right now it reads as the truth, because it is.
- **The first 5 feel awkward.** You over-apologise, over-explain, freeze on the binary close. That's expected.
- **Surveys 6 to 15 are when the script settles.** Tonality matures. You stop reading. The rapport intro shrinks from 90 seconds to 30.
- **Surveys 16 to 30 are when you actually become good.** The binary close lands, the appointment ask doesn't trigger reflex objections, the social-add at the end happens by reflex.
- **By Survey 30, the script is yours.** When your RNF drops, you call the next 170 names on your Project 200 list with no friction.

If you do fewer than 30, you carry the awkwardness into your real warm market - and burn the contacts that should have been your easiest closes.

## The market survey script (use word-for-word until it's yours)

**Phone opening (rapport, under 60 seconds):**
> Hello, good morning, is this [name]?
>
> [Name], this is [your name]. Do you have a moment to speak?
>
> How are you this period?

**The agenda (the survey ask):**
> As you may have learnt, I am intending to embark on the career of a Financial Advisor (or: currently interning with a Financial Services Agency) and I am tasked to do a simple market survey with the people that I know. Would you be able to help me by giving me 5 minutes of your time?

**Run the 4 survey questions:**

1. Do you have any insurance policies or financial plans? *(Yes / No - if No, why?)*
2. When was the last time you did a review of your policies?
3. How do you feel about your existing consultant's service?
4. As you may be aware, I am undergoing training to become a licensed financial advisor. As part of my training I am tasked to conduct Financial Health Reviews. Will it be alright if I can meet you for a 30-min session to share with you the work that I do? *(Use the ABCD promises - I won't force you, future meetings depend on whether you find this useful.)*

Q1 to Q3 are the *survey*. They take 2 to 3 minutes. Don't react, don't sell, don't recommend - just listen and write the answers down. The *data* isn't the point; the *practice* of running a structured 5-minute conversation is the point. Q4 is the appointment ask.

**Setting the appointment (using Question 4):**
> Thanks [name]. By the way, when I am officially licensed to start my practice, one of my priorities is to extend my service to my closest friends. You are one of those whom I highly regard. Till then, may I get in touch with you for a 30-minute session to share with you the type of solutions I can offer?
>
> You have my assurance that during our meeting, I will not ask you to do anything you don't want to and any future meetings will depend entirely on whether you feel my services would be of help to you. Is that fair?

**The binary close (offer two specific options, never "when are you free?"):**
> Good. Would it be best to get together at your office, your home, or through Zoom?
>
> Would weekdays or weekends be better for you?
>
> Office hours or evenings?
>
> Could I see you on [day] at [time], or would [day 2] at [time 2] be better?

**Closing off (capture data + open the social loop):**
> Really appreciate your help. Will it be alright if I keep you posted of any AIA promotions that are relevant to you? May I have your email address and date of birth so I can connect better with you?
>
> *(Optional)* May I have your permission to add you on Instagram or Facebook so we can keep each other updated?

The full version with the 10 reflex-objection responses lives in [Day 43 - Scripting Your Approach](../../first-60-days/week-8/day-43.md).

## How to train (the 4-stage practice ladder)

Don't dial cold into your real semi-warm market on Day 1. Climb the ladder. Each stage builds the muscle for the next.

| Stage | Reps | Who you call | Goal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Solo rehearsal** | 5 to 10 read-throughs | Yourself - mirror, voice memo | Words flow without freezing. Tonality varies. Pace settles. |
| **2. Cohort roleplay** | 3 to 5 paired reps | Another pre-RNF FC | Take feedback. Swap roles. Throw reflex objections at each other. |
| **3. Hot reps (warm-up)** | 3 to 5 calls | Family, partner, best friends | Gentle live audience. They will say yes. Settle nerves before stage 4. |
| **4. Real semi-warm reps** | The remaining ~20 | Project 200 Priority A and B | This is where the actual practice happens. The yes/no is real. |

**Cluster the calls.** 5 to 7 surveys in a focused 90-minute block produces better tonality than 1 a day. Your voice warms up around call 2 and peaks around call 5. Schedule survey blocks across 2 to 3 weeks of your study window.

## What to track (the survey log)

A simple sheet with these columns. Fill it in immediately after each call - within 5 minutes, before memory fades.

| Column | What goes here |
|---|---|
| **#** | Survey number (target 30+) |
| **Date / time** | When |
| **Name + temperature** | Hot / Semi-Warm |
| **Pickup outcome** | Picked up / VM / not free |
| **Survey completed?** | Yes / No (if no, what stopped it) |
| **Q4 answer** | Yes (book appointment) / Maybe / No |
| **Appointment booked?** | Yes (date) / No |
| **What worked** | One line - the moment something clicked |
| **What didn't** | One line - tonality, pace, an objection you fumbled |

Review the log every 5 surveys. The conversion rate on Surveys 16 to 30 should be visibly higher than on Surveys 1 to 15. If it's not, the gap is usually one specific moment - the binary close, the survey-to-appointment pivot, or the rapport intro - and the log will surface which one.

## Common failure modes (what derails new FCs)

- **Trying to upgrade Question 4 into a soft pitch.** Don't. Q4 is a question, not a pitch. The appointment ask comes AFTER they answer it.
- **Skipping the social-add at the end.** That's how 30 surveys becomes 100+ social touchpoints over the next 12 months. Don't drop it because you're rushing.
- **Doing all 30 with hot market only.** Hot market exhausts. The point of practice is to walk into your semi-warm market with confidence - so most of the 30 must be semi-warm.
- **Stopping at 10 to 15 because "it's working".** Below 30, the muscle isn't built. The script feels okay because the calls are easy - they're family. Semi-warm is where the script is actually tested.
- **Treating the appointment as a sale.** You can't sell. The appointment is rehearsal of the canned CST (Insurance or Investing) - see [Day 43 - pre-licensed practice framing](../../first-60-days/week-8/day-43.md).

## What a passing submission looks like

At least 25 of 30 surveys done (with a credible plan to clear the last 5 before RNF), and a conversion rate that visibly improved between the first and second halves of your log.
