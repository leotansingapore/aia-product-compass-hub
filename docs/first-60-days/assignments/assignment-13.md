> **The gist.** A post-meeting text is what you send after a meeting: a series of photos of the concepts you drew, each paired with a short text that explains it in the client's language. This assignment has two parts. Part 1 - understand what a good post-meeting text is made of. Part 2 - recreate the drawings from the two CSTs (Wealth Accumulation and Risk Management), describe each one as if texting the client, and log them here so they add up to a full post-meeting text.

## Part 1 - What a post-meeting text actually is

People forget most of a meeting within a day. What they don't forget is the picture you drew with them. The post-meeting text sends those pictures back with a short recap, so the whole conversation lives on one page they can re-read on their own time - and show their spouse.

It is not one long message. It is a **sequence of pairs**: a photo of one drawing, then a few lines explaining that drawing, then the next photo, then its lines. Each concept gets its own picture and its own short caption. Open the [sample post-meeting texts](https://t.me/+Vc1pwrngE_0xYzNl) and you'll see the pattern immediately - photo, caption, photo, caption - written warm and simple, the way you'd actually text someone.

**The anatomy of each caption:**

1. **Name the concept** in one line, the way it was drawn. *"firstly, your death / TPD..."*, *"next, retirement..."*
2. **Say what the drawing showed** - the two or three things that mattered, in their words, not product language.
3. **Keep it warm and plain.** Short sentences, no jargon. It should read like the person who sat across from them, not a brochure.

And across the whole text: open with a warm line (*"here's a summary of what we covered today"*), keep the concepts in the same order you drew them, and close on an easy next step - never a hard ask. Match the voice in the sample bank.

## Part 2 - Recreate the drawings for both CSTs

Your job is to **draw these yourself** - by hand or on an iPad - the way you would live in front of a client, then describe each one. The protection angle, drawn as one map of what could interrupt income:

Recreate those too if your CST includes them - each one is its own photo-and-caption pair.

## What to do

1. **Get the reference drawings** from the Google Doc or your CST screenshots, and understand what each one is showing before you copy it.
2. **Draw each one yourself** - by hand or on an iPad. Neat enough to read, the way a client would see it. Don't trace; draw it so you could reproduce it live in a real meeting.
3. **Photograph each drawing** clearly. Leave out or blur anything that identifies a real person.
4. **Write the client-facing caption for each** - the text you'd send with that photo. Plain, warm, in order.
5. **Log each drawing below** - one entry per concept, tagged to its CST. Together, your entries are the full post-meeting text for that CST. Do both CSTs.

## What good looks like

- Both CSTs are covered - the Wealth Accumulation drawings and the Risk Management drawings, each recreated in your own hand.
- Every drawing has a caption. No photo is left to explain itself, and no caption floats without its picture.
- The captions are in the client's language and in the order you'd draw them - they read as one flowing recap, not disconnected notes.
- Read end to end, the photos and captions together feel like a real post-meeting text you could send - the same voice as the sample bank.
- You could sit down tomorrow, redraw any of these live, and talk a client through it without the reference.

## The outcome

By the end you can do three things that separate a confident advisor from a nervous one: **draw** each core concept live, **explain** each drawing in plain language, and **send a post-meeting text** that makes the meeting stick. Every concept drawing carries its own explanation - that pairing is the whole skill.
