### [`00:08`](#t=8) - Why Week 3 exists
Week 1 was inner game, Week 2 was industry context. Week 3 is where the conversation finally gets concrete: how clients actually compare jobs and protection, how the wealth math works against waiting, and what you're quietly compounding under the hood for yourself. Six days, one arc: coverage frame -> protection map -> wealth math -> recurring income -> mindset -> action principle.

### [`00:19`](#t=19) - [Day 13](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/13) . Job A vs Job B
The opening frame for every coverage conversation. Two jobs side by side. **Job A** pays 100% when well, zero when anything goes wrong. **Job B** asks for ~10% of income and pays you when hospitalised, when you can't work, when you're gone, on retirement, and for the kids' education. When the columns only show "salary," everyone picks Job A. When you fill in the other columns - death, disability, CI, hospitalisation, retirement, kids - the choice flips. The script is "which job do you prefer?" *after* the columns are filled, not before.

### [`02:58`](#t=178) - [Day 13](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/13) . Company insurance comes with a leash
Two ways company cover fails the client. **(1)** Lose the job, lose the cover - Leo's friend was laid off, hurt himself playing sport during a six-month gap between jobs, surgery wasn't covered. **(2)** Get diagnosed while employed and you're now stuck in the role just to keep the policy. Even when the cover exists, the typical gaps are: capped daily hospitalisation limits, no major-CI lump sum, no early-CI multi-claim, death benefit of only 1-3x income (vs ~10x for private). The personal plan exists to make the cover follow the person, not the payslip.

### [`06:44`](#t=404) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The Total Wealth Concept - human capital first
Every client holds two pools: **human capital** (their ability to earn) and **financial capital** (what's in the bank). A 30-year-old on $100k with 30 working years left is sitting on ~$3M of human capital - more than most properties they'll ever own. Most people insure the golden eggs (car, house) but never the goose. Of the four levers - earn more, spend less, save, invest - earning ability is the load-bearing one. Without it the other three don't exist, which is why protecting it goes ahead of growing it.

### [`10:02`](#t=602) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Two analogies that defuse the "I'll buy later" objection
**Ship and lifeboat:** wealth is the ship, health is the lifeboat - the ship doesn't help if there's no lifeboat when it sinks. **Seatbelt:** you don't wear it because you expect a crash, you wear it because you can't buy one *after* the crash. Insurance is the only product where the moment you actually need it, it's either no longer for sale or repriced beyond reach. Use whichever lands cleaner with the client - same idea, two doors in.

### [`11:33`](#t=693) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The four quadrants
The Total Wealth diagram has four protection quadrants, walked clockwise:
- **Death / disability** - legacy, 10-20 years of income. Anchor question: "If your death cover is only $100k, does that mean you value your life at $100k?"
- **CI** - 5-10 years of income *plus* a ~$100k buffer for cancer-drug treatments, because you're not earning during recovery.
- **Hospitalisation (IS)** - the first line of defence, covered next in detail.
- **Personal accident (PA)** - outpatient layer, covered after IS.

### [`13:42`](#t=822) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Hospitalisation - $2M, 13 months either side, guaranteed renewable
The IS frame to memorise: **$2M/year cap, resets yearly, covers 13 months before and 13 months after admission, guaranteed renewable, unlimited claims**. Private is ~$70-80/month, public ~$15/month. The selling move: ask the prospect to guess the price *before* you reveal it ("how much do you think a plan covering you $2M/year, 13 months either side, unlimited claims, would cost?"). They'll guess "a few hundred dollars." The actual number then does the work for you.

### [`16:58`](#t=1018) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Personal accident - the layer Leo claims most often
PA is the plan most clients underestimate and the one Leo claims most often in his own book. ~$30/month, **$3k per accident, unlimited claims** for sprains, fractures, slips, falls, broken bones, food poisoning, dengue, dental from accidents. The KK A&E story: an agent told a client "just get hospitalisation, it covers everything." Daughter went to KK A&E for stitches - outpatient - bill was uncovered because IS only triggers on 6+ hours admission or surgery. PA would have covered it. The bigger point: because PA bills are small, most clients can't be bothered to claim. Your job as their agent is to do it for them from a phone photo of the bill.

### [`21:27`](#t=1287) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . CI plan vs hospital plan + real claim figures
The two are not interchangeable. **Hospital plan pays the hospital. CI plan pays you.** Hospital cover alone, no CI - bills are taken care of, but the 5 years you can't work, the rent and food still come due. CI alone, no hospital cover - the lump sum gets eaten by the very bills it was never meant to cover, and a $50-100k payout disappears fast. The team's recent approved claims back it up: **$200k for breast surgery, $145k for lung-cancer chemo ($12k/month), $20k for two days of sleep-apnoea treatment**. Most claims approved within a week.

### [`26:11`](#t=1571) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Whole life vs term - buying vs renting
The cleanest analogy in the deck. **Term = renting**: cheaper per month but you pay forever (until 65). **Whole life = owning**: more expensive monthly, but you stop paying at year 20 and the premiums refund at age 65. Same as a mortgage vs rent - mortgage is more painful early but ends with a paid-off asset. Use this when the client objects on price - it isn't about cheaper vs more expensive, it's about *how long* they're paying.

### [`28:04`](#t=1684) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . TPD, dismemberment, and CashLife
Three things prospects routinely confuse:
- **TPD** (from whole life/term) triggers only on losing two arms, two legs, *or* two eyes - one finger doesn't count.
- **Accident plan dismemberment** fills the partial-loss gap with a lump-sum menu (eyes worth more than a hand, hand more than a finger).
- **CashLife disability income** pays a *monthly* income for as long as the client can't perform 3 of 6 ADLs (bathing, dressing, eating, toileting, transferring, continence). For a 25-year-old male the payout is ~**$1,008/month**. It's cashless - so the only reason a client doesn't have it is that nobody offered it.

### [`32:51`](#t=1971) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The pricing playbook - 10% frame, three-quote anchor, the urgency math
Three moves stacked, in this order:
1. **Anchor on 10% of income before asking budget.** "This is what you should be setting aside." Optional supporting frame - the 1/3 rule (1/3 short-term, 1/3 mid-term, 1/3 long-term, with the 10% insurance + ~20% investing inside that last third).
2. **Quote three CI tiers** ($100k / $200k / $300k) on screen. Let the client pick from a menu rather than negotiate against you.
3. **Show the urgency math live.** Same $200k CI plan: **$1,004/year at 25, ~$9,600/year at 55, with 40 years of cover at 25 vs only 10 years at 55**. The point isn't fear - it's that buying later means buying less for more.

### [`36:45`](#t=2205) - [Day 15](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/15) . The cost of waiting - $120k/year, not 10%
Same plan, same monthly outlay, just shifted later. **Start at 24 - retirement pot ~$2.5M. Start at 34 - ~$1.2M.** Most prospects guess the delay costs 10-20%. It's actually ~**$120k of foregone wealth per year of delay** (~$1.2M over 10 years). The required monthly contribution to reach the same target jumps from $1,100/month starting at 24 to $4,800/month starting at 44. Run it on the long-term investment illustrator while they watch - it's more persuasive than describing it.

### [`39:13`](#t=2353) - [Day 15](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/15) . Sell the monthly, not the lump sum
Live demo on the retirement-lifestyle calculator. Desired lifestyle income today $4-5k/month inflates to ~$13-14k/month at 65, which equates to a ~**$3.3M target** across a 20-year retirement. Don't sell $3.3M - no one starts there. Sell the **$1,300/month** the illustrator says will produce it (vs the ~$7k/month required if they saved in the bank). The script: hand them the calculator and let them multiply $14k × 12 × 20 themselves - the number lands harder when they punch it in.

### [`41:41`](#t=2501) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . Recurring revenue - what you're actually building
Property agents earn big on one transaction and start the next day from zero. This business is a subscription - same model as Netflix, Spotify, gym memberships, software. **Every policy you write is an asset on your own book, not just a paycheque.** The PA math makes it concrete: 100 PA policies × ~$15/month FYC = ~$1,500/month recurring = ~$18k/year - the dividend-equivalent of holding ~$300k of capital (18,000 / 0.06). Two PA cases a month for five years gets you to a basic financial-freedom number without ever touching renewals.

### [`44:31`](#t=2671) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . The four-layer income stack
The layers most new FCs don't see until year 3:
- **FYC** - first-year commission, year 1 only.
- **Renewals** - 50% of prior year + first-year FYC, paid years 2-6 per policy.
- **Career benefit** - 80-100% of renewals, paid years 3-6 per policy.
- **APF** - 1.5% of every premium on your book, paid from **year 7 onwards, forever**.

Renewals and career benefit each have a 5-6 year tail per policy. APF doesn't - as long as the client keeps paying, you keep being paid. By year 10 about two-thirds of income is passive, and APF eventually outgrows everything else.

### [`49:21`](#t=2961) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . Touchpoints - the lifetime value of one client
This is B2C, not B2B - sell-and-disappear quietly destroys the asset you just built. Build a touchpoint calendar: poly graduation, enlistment, ORD, semester start, graduation, first job, BTO, move-in, renovation, wedding, anniversary, honeymoon, EDD, kids' birthdays, CPF Life payouts, plus the bi-monthly client engagement events the team runs. Serve the same client well across 10 years and you'll earn **5-10x more from that one relationship** than the first sale paid you - referrals, life-event upgrades, family policies. Most of the income is *after* year one, not on the day of close.

### [`52:35`](#t=3155) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Obsessed, not interested
Mindset can't be transferred, only signposted. Leo's own pointer: in his first year he committed to one book a week on sales, mindset, or business. Pair that with podcasts, YouTube, Instagram - the mentors are outside the office too. The bar for this career isn't "motivated" (motivation runs out) - it's **obsessed**: thinking about the work before sleep and on waking. The forcing question to apply daily: "If I stretch the timeline of what I'm doing now by 365 days - two years, three years - does this still earn its place?"

### [`58:07`](#t=3487) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Accountability + discomfort
Two ways to interpret the same week. **Outsourced:** "market is bad, leads are thin, economy is off." **Owned:** "I wasn't calling last week, I didn't ask for referrals, my script needs tightening." You can't move the economy. You can move what you do with it. Pair that with discomfort: cold calls, rejections, awkward conversations are reps you're banking - the more you absorb, the more numb the next one feels. The trade-off underneath: pay the discomfort tax today or pay it later as a comfortable career that never gets off the ground.

### [`61:43`](#t=3703) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Peaks, valleys, and stoicism
Two failure modes that look opposite but produce the same cold pipeline. **Wins trap:** close three cases in a weekend, decide you've earned a break, no activity for two weeks, no income a month later. **Losses trap:** three cancellations, spiral on "I'm not cut out for this," stop calling, same dead pipeline. Stoicism is the discipline of feeling the same after a great week as a bad one and showing up to the activity either way. Neither outcome dictates the calendar - the calendar dictates the outcome.

### [`63:51`](#t=3831) - [Day 18](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/18) . The 10x rule - put a zero on the goal
Ordinary goals don't have enough gravity to change how you work. **Aim for 5 calls and you'll find a reason to make zero. Aim for 50 and you might hit 30** - and 30 still beats whatever 5 would have produced. The goal isn't a forecast; it's a forcing function for method. The better question is "what would I have to change about how I work to hit 10x?" rather than "what's a realistic number?"

### [`65:00`](#t=3900) - [Day 18](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/18) . The 0.05-second margin + 10x your inputs
The gap between gold and silver in a 100m race is ~0.05 seconds - and behind that 0.05 seconds is ~1,000 extra hours of training. Sales follows the same shape: one extra call, one sharper objection response, one faster reply produces a tiny marginal lift in conversion - and that tiny lift decides yes vs no. So **expect failures, not successes** - failures are feedback, not stop signs. Then the action principle: you can't 10x outcomes (too many things outside your control), but you can 10x **inputs** - calls, posts, reps, hours of practice. That's exactly why the activity tracker measures inputs and not outputs.

### [`69:07`](#t=4147) - Wrap-up . Six days, one shape
Day 13 frames the coverage decision (Job A vs Job B) -> Day 14 fills in the four-quadrant protection map and the pricing playbook -> Day 15 supplies the wealth math against waiting -> Day 16 makes the recurring-revenue case for staying long -> Day 17 builds the mindset that survives the valley -> Day 18 closes with the action principle: 10x inputs, not outputs. Week 4 turns the principle into a daily operating system.
