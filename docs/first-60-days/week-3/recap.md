### [`00:08`](#t=8) - Why Week 3 builds on Weeks 1 and 2
Leo opens by setting the frame: with the inner game (Week 1) and the industry context (Week 2) in place, Week 3 is where the conversation finally gets concrete - how clients actually move money, what they're protecting, and what you're quietly building under the hood for yourself. Skip Week 3 and you walk into pitch-week with confidence but no numbers behind it.

### [`00:19`](#t=19) - [Day 13](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/13) . Job A vs Job B - the framing for coverage
Two jobs side by side. **Job A** pays you 100% when well, zero when anything goes wrong. **Job B** asks you to set aside ~10% of income, and in return pays you when you're hospitalised, when you can't work, when you're gone, when retirement comes, when your kids need school fees. Most prospects pick Job A on instinct - until you fill in the other columns. Then they pick B.

### [`02:58`](#t=178) - [Day 13](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/13) . Company insurance is never really yours
Two failure modes: (1) lose the job, lose the cover; (2) get diagnosed while employed, and you're stuck in the role just to keep the policy. Leo tells the story of a friend laid off, mid-job-switch, who injured himself playing sport in the gap - surgery was uncovered because his "company" cover had walked out the door with the job. Even when corporate cover exists, it's usually capped per day, no CI lump sum, no early-CI multi-claim. The personal plan exists to plug those holes.

### [`06:44`](#t=404) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The Total Wealth Concept - protect the human capital first
Everyone holds two pools of wealth: **human capital** (your ability to earn) and **financial capital** (what's in the bank). When you're young, human capital is the highest it'll ever be - a 30-year-old on $100k/year is sitting on roughly $3M of future earning ability. Most prospects insure the car and the house but never the engine producing all of it. Earn more, spend less, save, invest - but earning ability is the load-bearing one of the four.

### [`10:02`](#t=602) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Ship and lifeboat, seatbelt - the rule of buying early
Two analogies to use in the meeting. **Ship vs lifeboat**: your wealth is the ship, your health is the lifeboat - you need both. **Seatbelt**: you don't put it on because you expect a crash, you put it on because you can't fit it after the crash. Insurance only sells while you don't need it. After the event it's either off the menu or repriced beyond reach.

### [`13:42`](#t=822) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The four quadrants - IS, accident, CI, death cover
Leo walks the four-quadrant diagram clockwise. **Death/disability**: legacy for the family, 10-20x income. **CI**: 5-10x income because you're not earning during recovery, plus a $100k buffer for cancer-drug treatments. **Hospitalisation (IS)**: $2M/year, resets annually, 13 months before and 13 months after admission, ~$70-80/month private or ~$15/month public. **Personal accident**: the outpatient layer at ~$30/month, up to $3k per claim, unlimited claims. The PA story Leo tells - the dad whose daughter went to KK A&E for stitches and got nothing back because hospitalisation only kicks in for inpatient or surgery - is the exact misconception you'll meet in the field.

### [`21:27`](#t=1287) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . CI plan vs hospital plan - what each one actually pays
The hospital plan pays the **hospital**. The CI plan pays **you**. They are not interchangeable. Hospital cover only, no CI - bills are taken care of, but the five years you can't work, you starve. CI only, no hospital cover - the lump sum exists but gets eaten by the bills it was never meant to cover. Then the real claim numbers from the team's book: $200k for breast surgery, $145k for lung-cancer chemo at $12k/month, $20k for two days of sleep-apnoea treatment. A $50k or $100k lump sum looks small fast.

### [`26:11`](#t=1571) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . Whole life vs term, TPD, CashLife
**Whole life vs term** = buying vs renting. Term is cheaper per month but you pay forever; whole life is more expensive but you stop paying at 20 years and the premiums refund at 65. Use whichever frame the client objects on. **TPD vs dismemberment**: TPD pays only on losing two arms, two legs, or two eyes - one finger or one eye doesn't trigger it, which is why the accident plan still earns its place as a lump-sum dismemberment cover. **CashLife** is the disability income supplement most prospects have never heard of - cashless, $1,008/month for a 25-year-old male, pays out for as long as the client can't perform three of six activities of daily living. The pitch on CashLife is that it costs them nothing to buy, so the only reason not to take it is that no one told them it existed.

### [`32:51`](#t=1971) - [Day 14](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/14) . The 10% frame, price anchoring, urgency
Lead with **10% of income** before asking budget - "this is what you should be setting aside" anchors the conversation before the price discussion ever starts. Then **three quotations** for CI ($100k, $200k, $300k) so the client picks from a menu rather than negotiating against you. Then **urgency**: a $200k CI plan at 25 is $1,004/year; at 55 it's $9,600/year, with 30 fewer years of cover. Same product, very different math. The frame isn't "buy now or lose out" - it's "buying later is buying less for more."

### [`36:45`](#t=2205) - [Day 15](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/15) . The cost of waiting - $120k/year you lose by delaying
Same client, same plan, same monthly outlay - just shifted later in life. Start at 24 and the retirement pot is ~$2.5M. Start at 34 and it's ~$1.2M. That's not a 10-20% drop the way most prospects guess - it's roughly **$120k of foregone wealth for every year you wait**. To hit the same number starting at 44, the required monthly contribution jumps from $1,100 to $4,800. The math doesn't change because no one wants it to.

### [`39:13`](#t=2353) - [Day 15](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/15) . Sell the monthly, not the lump sum
Live demo on the retirement-lifestyle calculator. A desired lifestyle income of $4k/month today inflates to ~$14k/month at 65 - which equates to a ~$3.3M lump-sum target across a 20-year retirement. Don't sell the $3.3M. Sell the **$1,300/month** the calculator says will produce it. "Save $5k/month" never starts. "Set aside $1.3k/month systematically" does. The frame is "$1.3k now or $7k later" - same target, vastly different willingness.

### [`41:41`](#t=2501) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . Recurring vs non-recurring revenue
Property agents earn big on a single transaction and start over the next day. Insurance is a **subscription** - Netflix, Spotify, gym memberships, software, insurance. Every policy you write is an asset on your own balance sheet, not just a paycheque. 100 PA policies at ~$15/month FYC = ~$1,500/month recurring, which is the dividend equivalent of holding $300k of capital. You don't buy that capital. You build it one policy at a time.

### [`44:31`](#t=2671) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . Renewals, career benefit, APF - the part that compounds forever
The three layers stack: **FYC** (year 1 only), **renewals** (50% of prior year + first-year FYC, paid years 2-6), **career benefit** (80-100% of renewals, also years 2-6), and **APF** - 1.5% of every single premium under your book, paid from year 7 **forever**. Renewals and career benefit eventually cap because they trail off after year 6 of each policy. APF doesn't - as long as the client keeps paying, you keep being paid. By year 10 about two-thirds of income is passive. The first two years are the price of admission.

### [`49:21`](#t=2961) - [Day 16](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/16) . Touchpoints and the lifetime value of one client
The career is **B2C**, not B2B - sell-and-move-on quietly destroys the asset you just built. The fix is a touchpoint calendar: enlistment, ORD, semester start, graduation, first job, BTO appointment, move-in, renovation, wedding, anniversary, honeymoon, EDD, kids' birthdays, CPF Life payout, retirement, plus the bi-monthly client engagement events the team runs. Serve the same client well for 10 years and you earn 5-10x more from that one relationship than the first sale ever paid you.

### [`52:35`](#t=3155) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Obsessed, not interested
Mindset can't be transferred - only signposted. Leo's pointer: read one book a week, follow operators on Instagram and YouTube, find the mentors outside the office because the office mentor only sees you nine hours a day. The bar isn't "motivated" - motivation runs out. The bar is **obsessed** - thinking about the work before you sleep and the moment you wake up, addicted to the long game rather than the dopamine. Stretch every decision out by 365 days and ask if it still earns its place.

### [`57:23`](#t=3443) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Accountability and the discomfort principle
Two mindsets: outsource the blame ("market is bad, leads are thin, economy is off") or pull control back to yourself ("I wasn't working last week, I didn't ask for referrals, my script needs tightening"). You can't change the economy - you can change the way you respond to it. Pair that with discomfort: every cold call, every rejection, every uncomfortable conversation is reps you're banking. **Choose your pain today or choose your pain later** - one of those two happens for everyone.

### [`61:43`](#t=3703) - [Day 17](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/17) . Peaks, valleys, and stoicism
Two failure modes that look opposite but produce the same outcome. Close three cases in a weekend, decide you've earned a break, pipeline goes cold two weeks later, income drops a month later. Get three cancellations, spiral on "I'm not cut out for this," stop calling, same cold pipeline. **Stoicism** is the discipline of feeling the same whether the week went well or badly, and showing up to the activity either way. Wins and losses are both distractions when you let them dictate the calendar.

### [`63:51`](#t=3831) - [Day 18](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/18) . The 10x rule - put a zero on the goal
Ordinary goals produce ordinary results - they don't have enough gravity to change how you work. Aim for 50 calls and you might hit 30; aim for 5 and you'll find a reason to make zero. The goal isn't a forecast, it's a forcing function. "What would I have to change about my method to hit 10x this?" is a more useful question than "What's a realistic number?"

### [`65:38`](#t=3938) - [Day 18](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/18) . The 0.05-second margin - massive input, marginal output
The gap between gold and silver in a 100m race is ~0.05 seconds - and the gap between the 1,000 training hours that bought that 0.05 seconds. Sales is the same: one extra call, one sharper objection response, one faster reply, one more follow-up that no one else made. The result of 1,000 extra hours of effort is a small marginal lift in conversion - but that small lift is everything. **Expect failures, expect rejection, expect silence.** Failures are data, not stop signs.

### [`68:35`](#t=4115) - [Day 18](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/18) . 10x your inputs, not your outputs
You can't 10x outcomes - they depend on too many things you don't control. You can 10x **inputs**: calls made, posts published, hours of practice, lines of script rehearsed. That's why the activity tracker exists - it measures only the things you can actually move today. Track the inputs, the outputs follow.

### [`69:07`](#t=4147) - Wrap-up . Six days, one shape
Leo ties Week 3 into one arc: Day 13 frames the coverage decision (Job A vs Job B) -> Day 14 fills in the four-quadrant protection map and the pricing playbook -> Day 15 shows the cost of waiting -> Day 16 makes the recurring-revenue case for staying long -> Day 17 builds the mindset that survives the valley -> Day 18 supplies the action principle (10x inputs) that keeps the calendar honest. Week 4 turns this into the daily operating system.
