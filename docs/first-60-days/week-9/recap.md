### [`00:04`](#t=4) - [Day 49](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/49) . SPIN is just pain, then solution
Week 9 is the implication and need-payoff half of SPIN. Leo strips it down to one memorisable shape: **bad thing first, then the solution.** Every slide in the opening flow is built that way. Insurance: temporary risk vs permanent risk, this might happen, and *hence* we transfer it. Investments: you can't save enough on your own, *hence* you invest; you can't diversify easily on your own, *hence* this plan diversifies for you. Memorise the pattern, not the sentences - once the pattern is automatic you can improvise on any slide without losing the arc.

### [`01:22`](#t=82) - [Day 49](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/49) . The $12-a-month fee objection, live
A prospect told Leo the ~$12/month distribution cost was expensive because his iFAST fee was a few cents. Leo's reflex answer: that's a **percentage-of-portfolio fee on a $1K portfolio** - roughly $10 a year, so of course it looks like nothing. Run the same 1% on a $1M portfolio and it's **$10,000 a year, forever**. Our fee at $1M is $0 perpetual. The knee-jerk you need to build is to move the comparison from today's dollar amount to the fee *at scale, over the lifetime of the portfolio*.

### [`03:31`](#t=211) - [Day 49](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/49) . The problem-solution bank
Every objection has a paired slide. Cost of buying and selling stocks, cost of your time, cost of hiring a planner, other companies' allocation charges, policy fees, underwriting charges, benefit charges - all problems. Property has agent commission and a bid-offer spread. Currency conversion loses you money both ways. **Switching from a China fund to an India fund to a US fund inside the policy costs nothing, and the rebalancing is free.** Same shape for CPF LIFE vs the dividend fund: CPF LIFE is illiquid, the capital goes to zero, the amount can't be changed, returns are 2.5-4%. The dividend fund is liquid after eight years, ~6% dividends, and the capital stays intact.

### [`05:43`](#t=343) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . The Colombo Close is a pre-yes
The whole point of need-payoff is to arrive at an either/or that gets agreement **before** the proposal exists: *"If I can create a plan that gets you to this goal within a few hundred dollars a month, can you see any reason why we can't go ahead and implement it?"* In the CST the team ran, that was: death, TPD, CI, early CI, hospitalisation and accident, all inside $300/month - any reason you wouldn't go ahead? If they say "should be okay," the case is close-ready and the next meeting is pre-framed. Same mechanic on warm market: *"next time if I'm around the area, can I buy you a coffee and we can go through your coverage?"* They say yes because there's nothing to say no to yet.

### [`07:49`](#t=469) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . From a meeting, book a meeting
Leo's rule: every meeting produces the next meeting. Meet someone on 1 August, turn the calendar event into a **recurring all-day annual review on 1 August every year**. Next August the text writes itself - "we set an annual review around this time last year, let's arrange it." Then the maths: 365 clients in year one is 365 annual reviews booked for year two. Stack that and it becomes 700, then 1,000, then 1,300. Ten years of doing it and you're sitting on ~3,650 annual review touchpoints - roughly 10 appointments a day, which is COT/TOT territory on autopilot. **Birthdays and annual reviews are the two recurring touchpoints; the rest are one-off milestone hooks** (after the holiday, after RNF, after the baby, after graduation) that nobody says no to because they're always in the future.

### [`10:26`](#t=626) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . The FHR has to be muscle memory
The live demo starts here. Target: **fill the FHR in under 5 minutes**, timed, repeated until it's automatic. The reason isn't speed for its own sake - it's that you cannot be figuring out fields while you're closing. Same principle as writing the retirement numbers out on Excalidraw while you talk: presenting and executing at the same time is its own skill, and it has to be drilled separately.

### [`12:38`](#t=758) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . Pick SMS, not the Singpass QR
Small choice, big consequence. Choosing Singpass QR means showing the client a code and waiting - dead air where you can do nothing else. **Choosing SMS lets them retrieve their own data while you fill in the rest of the FHR in parallel.** Everything in this flow is engineered to buy you working time while the client is occupied.

### [`19:04`](#t=1144) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . Needs must match the product you're selling
Rules of thumb that gate the whole application: **death/TPD = 10 years of income, CI = 5 years of income.** Then the mapping - sell a GPP and the death/TPD/CI section must be filled; sell a Solitaire PA and the PA section must be filled; sell an APA and it's savings and retirement; sell a PLP and it's both; sell a UCC and it's CI only; sell a hospital income plan and it's the hospital income line. Fill the wrong section for the plan you're proposing and it will not go through.

### [`20:40`](#t=1240) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . Why applications bounce - the ceiling rule
**What you propose has to sit under the need you declared.** Client earns $50K, CI need is 5 x $50K = $250K, so a $300K GPP cannot be proposed - the ceiling is $250K. Earn $100K and the death need is 10 x $100K = $1M, CI is 5 x $100K = $500K; you don't *have* to propose $500K, but $500K is the maximum you're allowed to. Declaring a $100K income and then keying five years of $50K is the exact mismatch that gets the case rejected and refilled from scratch.

### [`22:23`](#t=1343) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . ILPs: match the goal to the surrender value at 65
For an APA the goal isn't a sum assured, it's the retirement number - **20 years of an annual amount that lands slightly above the projected surrender value at 65**. Memorise the two anchors: a **$200/month APA is roughly $400K** of surrender value at 65, so the goal line is 20 x $20,000. A **$1K/month APA is roughly $2-3M**, so the goal is 20 x $100,000. Propose $1K/month against a $20,000 goal and it bounces.

### [`24:20`](#t=1460) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . Solitaire PA tiers, and the income that justifies them
Pin the Basic Medical Insurance slide in your browser and know the death-and-disability sums cold: **Plan 1 = $100K, Plan 2 = $250K, Plan 3 = $500K, Plan 4 = $750K.** Whatever tier you sell is what you key. And the tier has to fit the income - Leo had a Plan 4 case bounce because the client earned $3K/month; underwriting told him to downgrade to Plan 3. Rough gate for Plan 4 is around $10K/month.

### [`26:58`](#t=1618) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . HealthShield tiers and the rider question
The three-line map to have in your head: **HSG Max A = private, Max B = public A ward, Max B Lite = public B1 ward.** Anything below B Lite isn't worth selling. On the FHR, "cover for reimbursement" is yes if you're proposing a rider, no if you aren't. Leo answers yes to the cancer care booster (it lifts the CDL/non-CDL limits) and no to the emergency care booster - outpatient emergencies should already be covered by the Solitaire PA you proposed alongside.

### [`29:58`](#t=1798) - [Day 50](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/50) . Risk profile has to match the fund
Key a conservative risk profile and then try to submit 100% into the Adventurous Index Fund and the system blocks it outright: *"the selected risk profile does not match the selected plans."* The profile has to be aggressive, or you have to walk the client into choosing aggressive - otherwise the whole application dies at submission.

### [`32:10`](#t=1930) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . The one line that stops most bounces
When the declared need is bigger than what you're proposing - $500K of death need, $100K GPP proposed - the shortfall has to be explained. Leo's standard wording: **"Shortfall will be reviewed in future appointments."** Type it into reasons for recommendation every time. It's also honest: the plan really is bottom-up and the gap really is the next conversation.

### [`32:57`](#t=1977) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . Replacement declarations and clawback
If you're replacing an AIA cash-value plan with another AIA cash-value plan, **declare it**. Anything cancelled within one year counts, including a policy sold by a different agent, including ten months ago, including when it wasn't your doing. Skip the declaration and the FYC on the new case gets clawed back. AIA is strict on this now.

### [`35:29`](#t=2129) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . Linearise the returns, then linearise the cost
Two numbers do the persuading. **Fund management fee: 1% on the AIA Index Fund vs 1.45% on the Elite Adventurous** - the cheaper fund is why the projection looks better. Then the linearisation: take the end value minus what was paid in, divide by the years, divide by 12, and show what they're earning per month. Do exactly the same to the distribution cost - divide it by 44 years and it lands at about $12 a month, right next to the monthly return it bought.

### [`38:53`](#t=2333) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . The multitask - fill the e-app while the closing slides run
This is the chapter to rewatch. While the client is being walked through the **closing slides** - force of destiny, cost of buying stocks, opportunity cost, cost of hiring a planner, Option A vs Option B, AIA's track record, fund performance, "my job is to help you grow a wealth machine" - Leo is silently keying selected plans, insurance history, replacement and switching, and additional documents on the back end. **The closing slides are the filler that buys the admin time.** Skip them and you're left filling forms in silence while the client sits there forming doubts. His framing: it's a sleight of hand, and like any sleight of hand it only works if it's rehearsed.

### [`44:47`](#t=2687) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . Signature, then payment - GIRO or card
Sign, complete, then e-payment. **Face to face, choose GIRO** - the client can key their own iBanking username and PIN. **Over Zoom, choose credit card**, because asking someone to read out their banking credentials on a call is absurd. Practise the whole run including submission and withdrawal (E-form NB request) so nothing in the flow is new on the day.

### [`47:39`](#t=2859) - [Day 51](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/51) . What you can pre-fill, and what you can't
Jing Lu asks the obvious question. Answer: **the FHR you can pre-fill** - priorities and needs, current portfolio, finances, budget, needs analysis, risk profile - using a placeholder name. **The e-application you cannot**, because starting a new application resets everything the moment the quotation changes. So pre-fill the FHR, walk in needing only the Singpass retrieval, and keep the closing slides as cover for the e-app.

### [`49:53`](#t=2993) - [Day 54](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/54) . Map every plan to its priority category
The part that eats the most time in the appointment is the mapping, so know it by heart. **APA / PWV / AWV → savings, investment, retirement. Hospital income plan → hospital income. HSG Max → hospital expenses. Solitaire PA → personal accident. SFT, GPP, UCC and PLP → death, TPD, CI.** Leo flags PLP as the one worth learning next - it's the in-between of APA and GPP for clients who want coverage without GPP's lock-in and with the option of a premium holiday.

### [`51:55`](#t=3115) - [Day 52](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/52) . The retirement CST, in running order
The whole flow in sequence: desired monthly lifestyle, inflated → what they'd need to save monthly (impossibly high) → investing as the solution → the 1/3 rule → diversification → AIA vs other companies → bonuses → FHR → quotation → **linearisation of returns, then linearisation of cost** → cost of investing (this is where the e-app gets filled) → quotation a second time → FHR check (name, NRIC, date of birth) → quotation and linearisation once more → skip through the application → sign → bank or card details → submit.

### [`54:16`](#t=3256) - [Day 53](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/53) . "I already have an agent" - don't flinch
Everybody has an agent and everybody has policies, so being scared of the sentence means being scared of the whole market. The counters Leo runs through: **robo-advisors carry a perpetual fee, AIA doesn't** - and with a robo there's no reason to buy early, whereas with AIA the long-term bonuses reward starting now. On self-picked portfolios: *"you are the biggest risk in your own portfolio"* - one over-leveraged decision across decades of market cycles wipes you out. On flexibility, split it into two objections and answer both: **flexibility to spend the money** (temptation vs discipline, the 1/3 rule, don't shortchange your 20 years of retirement) and **flexibility to trade** (fear and greed vs outsourced discipline; time in the market, not timing it).

### [`57:00`](#t=3420) - [Day 53](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/53) . The US-ETF and Manulife counters
A US ETF is **one country and one asset class**. What you're offering is multiple countries, multiple asset classes, multiple industries, rebalanced - equity-like returns with the volatility smoothed out; better than an FD, safer than 100% equities. Against Manulife specifically: **AIA's FMC is ~1% against many Manulife funds at ~1.5% with a perpetual charge**, AIA runs company stewardship rather than leaving fund selection to the agent, and AIA pays bonuses. The skill to build is finding the numbers yourself - Google the fund name for the FMC, and read the product summary for the perpetual fee (on InvestReady it's the ~0.7% p.a. administrative charge after the MIP; their 10/13 Flexi also carries a $5/month policy fee and locks longer than an APA).

### [`61:22`](#t=3682) - [Day 53](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/53) . The risk-angle CST, and fighting for the case
Four quadrants - death/TPD, CI, early CI, hospitalisation and accident - then pull their existing policies and work through each one. Whole life gets restructured, term gets shown as not enough, hospitalisation is where AIA's plan wins. Leo is blunt about the frame: **this is a competitive industry, not a collaborative one.** If you don't fight for the case, the other agent will. The defence is the same as the offence - a relationship and a service standard good enough that switching never occurs to them.

### [`64:20`](#t=3860) - [Day 53](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/53) . When they really are fully covered
Then don't force it. Show a quotation quickly, don't burn the meeting on it, and move to the investment side - there is almost always an opening there. And **plant the seed**: a little doubt about whether their current agent is the best agent they'll ever have, plus a clear invitation to come back to you later. In Leo's experience there's always a gap somewhere; a client who looks fully covered today is a client who comes back in two years.

### [`65:07`](#t=3907) - [Day 54](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/54) . The concept library
Concepts, not features. **Death plan:** we can't replace your life with a million dollars, but we can leave your family a life. **CI:** the indentation in the income line, the concentric circles of early vs major, the squares for multi-claim, and the one-year gap between claims. **Hospitalisation:** inpatient vs outpatient vs surgery, 13 months before and 13 months after, $2M a year. **Riders:** without a rider you co-pay 10% uncapped; with a rider it's 5% capped at $6,000. Clients still on a pre-1 April rider are on pricing that can total roughly half a million dollars over a lifetime - that alone is a reason for them to move to you.

### [`68:02`](#t=4082) - [Day 54](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/54) . GPP base plus term riders - the "free insurance" structure
The newer concept Leo has been running. A GPP gives lifelong death/TPD/CI coverage plus cash value, but claim once and the death cover is gone. So deliberately strip early CI out of the GPP and bolt on **standalone SFT (death/TPD) and a standalone UCC (multi-claim CI and early CI)**. The numbers he sketches: if the GPP profits about $60K over 40 years, that's ~$1.5K a year; a $100K UCC is roughly $70/month and a $1M SFT roughly $70/month - about $1.5K a year combined. **The whole life plan's profits pay for the term riders.** Structurally, it's free insurance on top of a lifelong base.

### [`71:34`](#t=4294) - [Day 54](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/54) . The seven product categories
Worth memorising as a grid. **Investment-linked:** APA (10-year, min $2.4K/yr), PWV (5-year, min $18K), AWV (8-year, ~$650/month), plus Pre and PWE. **Whole life:** GPP and PLP. **Endowment:** Retirement Saver (limited pay, matures, not for legacy), PGLP (perpetual annuity - put in $100K, draw ~3.6% forever, and the $100K still comes back on death or surrender), PIW (perpetual ~5.5% growth, no maturity, splittable). **Term:** UCC and SFT - and note the SFT rider that attaches to an APA at ~20% off, a pure death/TPD cover cheap enough to compete with group term life. **Disability income:** CareShield Life. **Excellent health:** Solitaire PA and HSG Max. Plus the niche plans - Star Protector for kids, Mum2Baby from 13 weeks pregnant (~$900/year or ~$2,000 single premium for three years of cover on mother and baby), Hospital Income for the self-employed, Centurion PA for seniors with the dementia benefit, Glow of Life with free health screening every two years.

### [`75:51`](#t=4551) - [Day 54](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/54) . PIW, and why it doesn't end
The plan Leo calls insane once you read the brochure: **it never matures, so the policy can be split indefinitely** - the owner splits to his son, the son splits to his children, and on down. In the illustration, ~$1M of premium becomes ~$100M of total policy with ~$54M of accumulated cash value sitting in two of the split policies, still compounding. That's the legacy conversation in one picture.

### [`79:50`](#t=4790) - Wrap-up . There is no best plan
Leo closes on the line that ties the whole week together: **"There's no best plan. There's only the most appropriate plan for this person."** The homework is iResource and the presenter - go through the IPOs, the promotions and the touchpoints until they're familiar, because product knowledge is what lets you stop selling and start matching. Week 9's arc in one sentence: SPIN to make them feel it, the FHR to capture it, the CSTs to reframe it, concept selling to name the recommendation.
