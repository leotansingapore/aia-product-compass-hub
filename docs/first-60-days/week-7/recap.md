### [`00:41`](#t=41) - [Day 37](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/37) . The funnel as a Ferris wheel
Sky had just run a full cycle - lead in, call, Zoom opening, face-to-face close - so Leo uses it to set the week's frame. The job now isn't learning new steps, it's **looking at your own flow and making every segment faster**: lead to text, text to call, call to Zoom opening, Zoom opening to close. Picture a Ferris wheel turning thousands of times. **A 1-2% friction point anywhere in the loop compounds** - text in three days instead of three minutes and the delay doesn't cost you one prospect, it drags the whole wheel down for months. That's why one of the team values is just move fast.

### [`03:00`](#t=180) - [Day 37](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/37) . Client Lifetime Value, worked live
Sky's PLP close that day: **1.2K FYC, which is ~3.6K of total lifetime income** on the 1.1x-plus multiplier. Then Leo does the arithmetic out loud - five minutes of texting, one minute on the call, three minutes of Zoom opening, 30 minutes of closing. **That's roughly a $3.6K-per-hour job.** It doesn't arrive as a lump sum - it pays out monthly as the client pays - but the comparison stands: most people work a full month for $3.6K. This is the structural difference between a sales role and a corporate job, and it's why the answer to "should I study more or work more" is work more.

### [`05:09`](#t=309) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . Warm is half your market - don't be noble about it
Cold is what the team is running now (freebie ads, McDonald's ads) and it scales, but it costs money and time. Warm costs nothing and **accounts for about 50% of your market**, even though it runs dry in roughly two years. Leo's blunt read on "I don't want to sell to my friends": it sounds humble, but it means **you don't have conviction in what you do** - and if you can't do warm on conviction, you won't survive cold either, because cold is pure volume and rejection. Helping a friend isn't selling them something; it's being the best consultant they'll ever have. And the more you prospect, the less desperate you are, which is exactly why you stop being pushy.

### [`07:25`](#t=445) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . You can't Google the best FA
You can Google the best restaurant and the best movie. **You cannot Google the best financial adviser** - so people fall back on someone they trust. That's why the relationship matters more than any MDRT or COT title, and why a lot of your contacts are quietly waiting for you to reach out rather than the other way round. Don't lead with closing; people can smell intent. Just be genuinely proud of the job.

### [`08:13`](#t=493) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . Taxi Cab Theory
There's nothing special about the taxi that picks you up - it was just **at the right place at the right time**. Cheers can sell a drink at $2 for the same reason. To be at the right place at the right time you have to be at *all* places at *all* times, which means 99% of the time you'll be at the wrong place at the wrong time. **That's the job.** Prospecting is a volume game precisely because you have to absorb the wrong-place-wrong-time reps to be present for the right one.

### [`09:44`](#t=584) - [Day 39](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/39) . Project 200/500 - the lukewarm middle is the prize
Don't stop at family and besties. Out of ~500 contacts, only about **4% is hot market**; the bulk sits in **lukewarm** - people you spoke to once a few years ago. That's where the opportunity is, and the only barrier is breaking the ice: reply to a story, start any conversation, anything. Leo's diagnosis of why new advisers stall isn't product knowledge - it's **failing to consistently work a database you already have.**

### [`10:59`](#t=659) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . Courtesy, empathy, WIIFT - the ask-for-help opener
Asking for help is a skill. The **market survey** frames it honestly: you get the reps and the practice, *and* what's in it for them is a policy summary and real clarity on their own finances. Say both halves out loud. The script Leo uses: *"as part of my training I need to do a quick market survey - what policies do you have, who's your agent, and how open would you be to hearing more about financial planning?"* There's no pitch anywhere in it. The parallel opener is the **policy summary angle**: *"part of my internship assignment - can I practise on you? I'll put together a one-page summary of everything you're covered for."*

### [`13:21`](#t=801) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . Privilege to serve, and the MIA agent
Second framing, softer: *"I'm joining an FA soon and it would be my privilege to service any of your financial needs - no pitch, no agenda, just let me know."* Then the version with a bit more spine: *"I'd rather you message me than a stranger - second opinion, money questions, CPF questions, I'm here."* Most people do have an agent, but **the agent is usually MIA** - no annual review, no birthday message, often it's a parent's agent they've never spoken to, sometimes one who has left the industry entirely. And when a claim finally happens at 50, that agent is long gone. Servicing means an annual review, a life-stage check-in and a chat once a year. That gap is your opening.

### [`15:39`](#t=939) - [Day 42](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/42) . Cold: the only bottleneck is dial speed
On the ad leads, the whole game is speed - call and text the moment it lands. Texting is capped (about 20 messages a day); **calling isn't - you can make 300 calls a day.** So the ceiling on your FYC is literally the speed of your calling. Leo's most-repeated instruction: **dial with two phones.** Doubling call speed is the single most obvious way to double FYC, and it's the only lever that has no cap.

### [`17:08`](#t=1028) - [Day 42](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/42) . Three things to write down after every meeting
Non-negotiable, every single time: **(1) the annual review** - convert the meeting into a yearly recurring calendar event on the spot, so an annual review is guaranteed; **(2) the birthday** - a yearly recurring calendar entry; **(3) the milestones** - exams, start of studies, university entry, job search. Store them all, then harvest. Leo's CRM is exactly this: it's July, so he calls every June baby and asks for a review; next month he calls the July babies. **All of his appointments now come from wishes.**

### [`19:28`](#t=1168) - [Day 42](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/42) . Call, don't text, to set the appointment
Texting is indirect and slow, and slow compounds - back to the Ferris wheel. Pick up the phone. And keep feeding the warm list real resources between calls: the CPF calculator, the investment guidebook, a checklist, an infographic, a comparison video. **Give value first, then meet.** The team keeps building these specifically so warm outreach has something to carry.

### [`21:39`](#t=1299) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . The market survey is three questions and five minutes
Not a review, not a policy summary, not a two-hour session. **Three questions:** what policies do you have, do you have an agent, and would you be open to me sharing something in the future. Then get the milestones and get off the phone. Leo did exactly this when he started - went down his contact list and called everybody. His correction to the team: **do not mistake a three-hour catch-up with a friend for an opening.** Warm market opening = market survey, the same way cold market opening = Zoom opening. Three minutes, milestones captured, then send the guidebook. "Not interested," "no money," "no need" aren't objections here - there's nothing being asked for.

### [`24:27`](#t=1467) - [Day 38](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/38) . ABCD - the four promises
When the market survey converts to an actual review, open the Zoom call with these four: **A - Assurance**, I'm not here to sell you anything, just to find out about your goals, and you'd only proceed if what I share is genuinely valuable and you're comfortable with me. **B - Best interest**, whatever I plan for you is exactly what I'd do in your shoes. **C - Confidentiality**, everything you share is in strict confidence. **D - Full disclosure**, so give me as much information as you can and I'll make my best recommendation. Then close the loop at the end: **ask for the referral**, every time - it's the highest compliment a client can give, and it's how the practice was built.

### [`26:48`](#t=1608) - [Day 41](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/41) . Texting EQ
Link back to the previous conversation ("the other time you mentioned you were at Tanjong Pagar..."). Make every text interesting. **Ask questions that are easy to answer.** Make them feel safe about the meeting - it's a review, and you might well tell them everything's fine. Reply quickly, stay casual, build touchpoints first. And one hard rule: **never use the word "free."** Nobody is free. Offer specific slots instead - *"would 30 minutes next Thursday work better, or..."* - so they choose rather than think. Plant the seed, make a soft ask, get a yes, follow up when the time comes.

### [`28:25`](#t=1705) - [Day 40](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/40) . Your marketing kit sells you, not AIA
The kit is the Zoom opening rebranded to your face and your style - make a copy and change it. The order that works: **cover, self-introduction, service proposition, testimonials, life journey, philosophy, professional pledge, then the company, agency and product.** Everyone already knows AIA is good; **what's unclear is what's good about you.** That's the whole job of the kit, and the life-stage planning section is what signals this isn't a one-off transaction.

### [`30:03`](#t=1803) - [Day 40](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/40) . How Leo got testimonials in his first three months
He wrote ten testimonials himself, put them in a Google Form as multiple choice, and asked clients to **pick the number that best fitted their experience.** No writing required, so nobody dropped off. He's explicit that nobody taught him this - it came from reverse-engineering the problem: to buy from an agent you need trust; a new agent has none; so how do you manufacture trust honestly and fast? Same story with the presenters - at 21 he hired a **$10/hour Upwork VA** (now charging $100/hour) and built his own decks in PowerPoint, because AIA only supplied raw BI documents and you cannot sell a BI. **The value being taught here is resourcefulness**, and it's the thing that makes AI useful rather than replaceable.

### [`33:13`](#t=1993) - [Day 37](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/37) . Four data points make a lifetime client
Get these and the relationship runs itself: **how old are you, when's your birthday** (recurring forever), **when's your POP** (from which you extrapolate enlistment, ORD and university entry), and **when do you start university** (from which you get every semester and graduation). Then the correction that matters most: **the Zoom opening is not for convincing anybody to buy.** It's for positioning and milestones. Sky's opening ran five minutes in a car while Leo was ill - positioning, milestones, straight to a face-to-face close. Save the NS chain as all-day calendar events - enlistment, first book-out, POP, posting results, SCS, graduation, ORD, each semester, year-four graduation - and that person is your client by default.

### [`35:27`](#t=2127) - [Day 41](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/41) . The script library and the content studio
Every script already exists: Sales Playbooks → Scripts, filtered by cold calling, initial texting or warm market. Then the digital half of the week - **just start posting.** IG, LinkedIn, Facebook, anything: lifestyle, relatable, authority, professional, pick a lane. Leo demos the **AI content studio** he's building: pick an interest, an identity, a topic and a market, generate five hooks, choose one, and it writes the post. A bot scrapes trending formats every week or two and feeds new angles in - FAQ one-question-one-answer, parody skits riding pop culture, micro-lessons on a whiteboard. **There is no "I have no ideas" excuse left**; film it, hand it to the video editor, post it.

### [`47:00`](#t=2820) - [Day 42](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/42) . Four content pillars, then wrap-up
**Interest** (hobbies, what's relatable), **identity** (fresh grad, career switcher, mother of two), **topic** (budgeting, investments, protection, CPF, estate planning), **market** (the audience you want). Social content leans on identity, life stage, role and passions; authority content leans on CPF, CI, investments, grads, families and SMBs. Have a clear pillar, a clear identity, and be unique. The failure mode has a name - **posting and ghosting.** Leo's close for the week: Week 7 was the approach - the financial planning process, reaching out, the market survey script, Project 100, and social media. The measure of whether it landed is whether the team starts posting.
