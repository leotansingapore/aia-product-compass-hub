## Part 1: Using Policy Summary to Open Cases & Do Reviews

### The Sales Flow

The standard client engagement flow follows this path:

**Introduction → Review/Needs Analysis → Fact-Finding → Presentation → Solution Implementation → Servicing → Touch Points**

When approaching warm prospects, friends, acquaintances, or referrals, always start with something **low-bait** — never lead with "I want to sell you a policy." Instead, position it as: *"Can we meet up to do a review of your finances and give you some updates on recent changes in hospital plans / investment markets / CI definitions?"*

### Fact-Finding (FF) Parameters

Once you secure the review appointment, you collect four major financial parameters: **Income, Expenses, Assets, and Liabilities.** These feed into the needs analysis using standard rules of thumb:

- **Premature Death:** 10-20x annual income
- **Disability coverage**
- **Critical Illness:** 3-5x annual income (e.g., income $60K → CI coverage should be $180K-$300K)
- **Hospitalization**
- **Accidental coverage**
- **Retirement planning**

If a client earning $60K has only $75K CI coverage, the gap is immediately visible.

### The Policy Summary Document

The summary template has two main blocks:

1. **Risk Management Policies** (upper block) — Death, TPD, Occupational Disability, Critical Illness, Early CI, Hospitalization, Accidental, Accidental Reimbursement
2. **Wealth Accumulation** (lower block) — Investment-linked policies, endowments, projected maturity/investment values

For each policy, you capture: policy number, policy date, plan name, coverage amounts, cash values, and total premiums.

**What clients want to know at a glance:**
- How much am I paying?
- What am I covered for?
- What am I lacking?

You then map all coverage against planning norms, identify shortfalls, and use this as the basis for your opening and recommendation.

### Servicing & Touch Points

After a prospect becomes a client, conduct reviews every 1-2 years or at every major life stage (graduation, marriage, children). Maintain systematic touch points — direct, indirect, subtle — to stay top of mind.

Some advisors complete needs analysis and solution implementation in one appointment; others split it into two.

## Part 2: SG Findex — Digital Policy Consolidation

### What Is SG Findex?

SG Findex is a Singapore government-backed digital service that allows clients to consolidate all their financial data in one portal by linking their **Singpass**. It pulls data from insurance companies, banks, CPF, CDP shares, and more.

You can access it via the official SG Findex website or through insurer-specific apps (e.g., AIA+ app → SG Findex Data section).

### How to Use It

1. Client logs into AIA+ (or official Findex site) using Singpass
2. Selects which financial institutions to link (insurers, banks, CPF, etc.)
3. Gives consent to share data
4. Downloads/shares the consolidated report with their advisor

**Message template approach:** Send the client a step-by-step guide (PDF available in the Google Drive). They complete the linking process and share the report with you.

### What You Can See vs. What You Can't

**Can see:** Policy names, premium amounts, coverage amounts, surrender values, total premiums, CPF data, bank balances (if linked), CDP shares

**Cannot see:** Detailed fund allocations within ILPs (e.g., which funds a Prudential ILP is invested in). The system is not yet that sophisticated.

### Important Sensitivities

- **Banking data** — Clients may not want to share credit card balances, savings amounts, or loan details. Be mindful. You can always ask verbally during fact-finding instead.
- **Insurance data** — Most clients are comfortable sharing this. This alone is already very useful.
- **CPF data** — Most people are okay sharing this.

Never push clients to link everything — start with insurance data and add more only if they're comfortable.

### Key Insight: Clients Forget Their Own Policies

**Real case study:** A 45-year-old client synced his SG Findex data showing CI coverage of only $30K with AIA (income $100K). During the review, he recalled having more coverage. Upon logging into the Prudential portal on the spot, two additional policies totaling ~$190K in CI coverage were discovered — policies he had forgotten about.

**Takeaway:** SG Findex is a starting point, not the complete picture. Always verify with individual insurer portals when something doesn't add up.

### SG Findex vs. Ghost Mapper

SG Findex is a **policy consolidation e-service** — it pulls and displays data. Ghost Mapper is a **financial planning software** — it analyzes, projects, and presents recommendations. They are complementary, not interchangeable.

### When to Use SG Findex

- Best for **slightly older clients** with policies scattered across multiple companies
- For clients who can't recall what they have or lost their policy booklets
- Can be sent before the meeting (for hot/warm market who already trust you) or explained during the meeting (for referrals and newer relationships where trust must be established first)

## Part 3: Introduction to Warm Prospecting

### Market Temperature Model

Your existing network falls into concentric circles based on relationship closeness:

| Circle | Type | Description | Approx. Distribution (of 300 contacts) |
|--------|------|-------------|----------------------------------------|
| Center | **Hot Market** | Parents, siblings, best friends. They buy because of who you are, regardless of company. Easiest to meet. | ~30 |
| Second | **Warm Market** | Friends, ex-colleagues, relatives (not super close). They respond to your messages and IG comments. | ~60 |
| Third | **Semi-Warm Market** | Acquaintances, people you haven't spoken to in years. **Most untapped market** due to sheer quantity. | ~210 |
| Outer | **Cold Market** | Lead gen, grab vouchers, NSF, cold calls, roadshows. Low conversion (20 leads → 3 meetings → maybe 1-1.5 sales). | Unlimited |

**Critical concept:** When a cold prospect buys from you, they become your **client** — which means they move into your warm market. Your job is then to nurture them from semi-warm client → warm client → A-client (advocate who refers). This is like "brewing old soup" — slowly nurture, follow up, add value over the years.

### A/B/C Client Classification

- **A Clients:** Advocates — they actively refer people to you
- **B Clients:** Buy from you, open to repurchase, but don't actively refer
- **C Clients:** Minimal engagement

The dream scenario: A client messages you saying *"Hey bro, my girlfriend is looking to buy some CI. Can you talk to her?"* — that's an open goal. Build toward this through consistent nurturing.

### Two Approach Methods for Warm/Semi-Warm Prospects

**Method 1: Direct Education/Fact-Find Appointment**
For prospects who are ready. You set the appointment with a clear agenda: *"Let's meet to review your finances, consolidate your policies, and go through some updates."*

**Method 2: Warm-Up Appointment First**
For prospects who aren't ready or where the relationship needs rebuilding. Meet casually — catch up over dinner or coffee. Be honest about your career transition. If an opportunity arises naturally, transition to setting an education appointment. If not, plant the seed for a future meeting.

**Conversion expectation:** Out of every 3 warm-up sessions, approximately 1-1.5 will transition to an education fact-find appointment. This is worth the effort.

### Warm Market Calling/Messaging Script — Key Principles

1. **Sound casual, not like a financial advisor.** You're calling as a friend, not a salesperson.
2. **Be genuine** — share what you're doing, acknowledge the career transition naturally.
3. **Praise the person** — "I know you're someone responsible, you love your family..."
4. **End with a no-pressure close** — "Whether you choose to engage me is totally up to you."
5. **Keep calls structured but brief** — don't chat for an hour before getting to the point.

**Sample warm call flow:**
- Casual greeting → mention your IG posts/career change → share briefly what you do → compliment them → suggest meeting at a familiar location → no-pressure ask → fix the appointment

**Key mindset beliefs:**
1. Deeply believe that what you're doing genuinely helps people.
2. Many of your friends are actually just waiting for you to reach out.

### Content Posting Strategy

Posting on Instagram, Facebook, or TikTok serves as a passive nurturing tool. Your hot friends already know about your life whether you post or not — content posting primarily reaches your **warm and semi-warm market.** It keeps you visible and gradually warms people up.

### Project Number One Objectives

1. Reach out to all warm and semi-warm friends (calling, social media, messaging)
2. Do policy reviews with hot market
3. Actively post content on social media
4. **Goal:** Build initial success in the first 6 months to sustain momentum

## Part 4: Introduction to Referral Asking

### Why Referrals Matter

- 5 referral calls → ~3 appointments (60% conversion)
- 20 cold/anonymous calls → ~1-2 appointments (5-10% conversion)

Referrals are fundamentally easier because trust is pre-established through the referring party.

### The Two Types of New FCs

| Type | Strength | Risk |
|------|----------|------|
| **FC with strong warm market** | Explosive start (potentially $20K FYC in first 2 months) | Production cliff after warm market is exhausted (~6 months) |
| **Hardworking cold-calling FC** | Consistent activity, gritty work ethic | Must maintain high volume indefinitely without referral pipeline |

**Both types need referral systems** to create sustainability beyond month 6.

### When to Ask for Referrals

The best times are:
- **After closing a sale** (they've demonstrated trust by purchasing)
- **After a positive feedback moment** (they've just told you they found your presentation valuable)

### The Referral Process — 4 Steps

**Step 1: Plant Referral Seeds (Before Presentation)**
At the beginning of your appointment, say something like: *"My business is built on referrals. If after today's presentation you find what I share valuable, I really hope you can recommend some friends to me."*

This works for both warm and cold market appointments.

**Step 2: Ask at the Right Time (After Presentation/Closing)**

Use the **VIP Method** — Value, Importance, Pause:
1. After closing, say: *"I need to go through something very important with you."* Then **pause.**
2. They'll naturally ask: *"What is it?"*
3. You say: *"As I shared at the beginning, my business is built on referrals. Can we spend 5 minutes brainstorming together some friends I can reach out to?"*

Get feedback first: *"Are you happy with the process? Which part did you learn the most?"* Use their positive response as a bridge to the referral ask.

**Step 3: Collaborate — The Phone Technique**

Ask them to take out their phone (Telegram/WhatsApp). Guide them through their recent chats — their first 10 commonly messaged friends. Use a physical **referral paper** and write down names one by one.

Target: **3-5 names per appointment.** If you have 6 appointments/week and ask at 4 of them, that's 12-20 warm referral names weekly.

**Step 4: Handle Objections**

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| *"Let me ask my friends first"* | "No problem, but what I'll do is just drop them a WhatsApp text first. If they're not interested, I won't pursue it. Wouldn't you want your friends to benefit from what you just learned?" |
| *"I'm not comfortable giving names"* | Remind them of the value they just acknowledged. Reassure them of your professional process — text first, respect boundaries. Do NOT offer to call anonymously (this turns it into a cold call, which defeats the purpose). |
| *"I need to think about it"* | Use social proof — show your referral booklet with existing names to normalize the process. |

### Referral Calling Script — Key Structure

When calling the referred person (e.g., Hussein referred Gabriel):

1. **Introduction:** "Hi Gabriel, I'm David from AIA. Hussein asked me to call you." → **Pause.** Let them register who Hussein is.
2. **Establish connection:** "Hussein and I were doing some work recently. He actually spoke very highly of you — mentioned you guys did the final project together and even won best project." → Build rapport through shared knowledge.
3. **Create curiosity (without revealing what you do yet):** "It seems a lot of things I've done for Hussein may be of interest to you."
4. **Soft ask:** "I'm not sure if my work will be of value to you, but I'd be happy to meet for half an hour next week to introduce myself and share some ideas."
5. **They ask "What is this about?"** → Now you reveal: "I'm with AIA Financial Advisors. We help clients build assets and retire earlier."
6. **Leverage the relationship:** "Hussein seems very convinced that you'd find this valuable."
7. **Close for appointment:** "Would next week — weekdays or weekend — work for you?"

**Pro tip:** If the referrer can drop the referred person a message *before* you call, the conversion rate increases significantly.

### Three Referral "Reasons" You Can Use

1. **"I want to cold-call less"** — "If you refer me friends, I can spend more time servicing your portfolio instead of always looking for new clients."
2. **"Your friends deserve this too"** — "The best way to reach responsible people is through people like you. Wouldn't they think better of me coming through you than as a random cold caller?"
3. **"I need your help"** — "I'm new and growing my practice. I'd love to reach more people like you who are responsible and working hard."

### Why Referral Systems Fail

The #1 reason: **The advisor simply doesn't ask.** It's not a skill problem — it's a habit problem. Build it into every appointment until it becomes second nature.

## Key Takeaways

- **Policy summary** is your door-opener — it's a low-pressure reason to meet anyone
- **SG Findex** accelerates policy consolidation but always verify with individual portals
- **Warm market** is your most natural pipeline — approach systematically using the market temperature model
- **Content posting** on social media nurtures semi-warm contacts passively
- **Referral asking** must be installed as a non-negotiable part of every appointment
- **Sustainability** comes from having multiple lead sources: hot, warm, semi-warm, referrals, and cold — never rely on just one channel
- Cold prospects who become clients are now your warm market — nurture them into advocates
- Start building referral habits from month 1, not month 6
