## Lecture Notes

Despite the importance of prospecting, many agents struggle with prospecting because of limiting beliefs. They fear rejection, are uncomfortable with selling, or are unable to step out of their comfort zone. Overcoming these mental barriers is essential for effective prospecting, as it allows you to continually expand your network and reach new clients. Remember, success in this industry is built on consistently connecting with new people and staying proactive.

Remember, prospecting is the **lifeblood of our business.** Without it, there is no foundation to sell or service clients effectively. We need a consistent pipeline of people to talk to. Without people and appointments, you are akin to a teacher with no students, or a doctor with no patients.

No matter how great the level of service you can provide, there is no point in that, if we don’t have people to speak to.

You also need to **convince** people to trust you, and to follow your advice. A doctor who can’t convince his/her patients to take medication or get treated, is no good a doctor at all. A teacher who knows it all, but can’t build rapport with the students or respect from the students to do their homework, isn’t an effective teacher either.

As a financial consultant, you play the role of a doctor and a teacher -> you teach your “students” about financial planning, and also prescribe “medication” in the form of various insurance or investment policies.

However, people won’t “queue” up to buy from you. Unlike a school or a clinic, where there is a constant inflow of students and patients every day or year, you don’t have a physical storefront. Your “storefront” is your brand, the “top of mind” awareness that people have about you, and how “there” you are, when people start “thinking” about purchasing something. And for those people who are not in the purchase mode or problem aware stage yet, your job is to get them to that stage.

In sales, you don’t earn just by being smart or knowledgeable. How successful you are, or how much you earn is a direct proportion of your activities. In sales, **activities = income**, or, in other words how many “patients” you see every week.
