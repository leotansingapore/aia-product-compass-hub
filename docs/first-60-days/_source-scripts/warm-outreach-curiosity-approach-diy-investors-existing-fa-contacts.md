# Warm Outreach – Curiosity Approach
**Best for:** Contacts who are financially savvy, already self-managing investments, or have an existing FA

---

## The Script

I get you, and I honestly respect that you are very hands-on with your finances. That shows how intentional you are with your financial planning — especially with insurance, tax-saving plans, and dividend investing.

Do you have someone helping you with all of these? :)

What I usually do in situations like this is a one-time portfolio assessment — just to spot any blind spots, overlaps, or things that might be worth a second look. No harm in checking.

If it sounds useful, we can have a chill chat over coffee or tea. Up to you. :)

---

## Why This Works

- Leads with a genuine compliment — validates their effort without making them feel inadequate
- Reframes the ask — not a sales pitch, but an offer to check if everything is optimised
- Blind spots is a powerful word — it implies there could be gaps without being insulting
- Low commitment ask — a chill chat over tea feels very different from a formal consultation
- Up to you returns control to them, reducing resistance

---

## When to Use This

- Contact mentions they already have an FA
- Contact seems financially independent or handles their own investments
- Contact brushes off financial conversations as already sorted

---

## Key Principles

- **Never compete with their current FA** — position yourself as a second opinion, not a replacement
- **Portfolio assessment framing** — one-time, low-pressure, no commitment
- **Casual setting** — suggest tea, coffee, or a quick Zoom, never a formal presentation

---

## Tips from Jana

> When someone says they already have an FA or manage their own investments, that is not a dead end. I offer a one-time portfolio review — not to replace anyone, just to give them a fresh set of eyes. Most people are open to a second opinion when you take the pressure off.
