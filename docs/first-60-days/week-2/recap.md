### [`00:04`](#t=4) - Why Week 2 builds on Week 1
Leo opens by setting up the next stretch: Days 7 to 12 cover the industry context and the freedom-business frame around the foundation Week 1 laid. Skip Week 2 and you carry the inner-game work without ever earning the right to talk about products with conviction.

### [`00:28`](#t=28) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . The five purposes of insurance
The misconception is that "insurance is insurance." Leo separates it into five distinct purposes - **death/TPD** (someone has to pay the bills when you're gone), **critical illness** (income replacement during recovery), **hospitalisation** (the bill-payer for medical events), **personal accident** (the outpatient layer), and **wealth accumulation** (ILPs and savings). One client, one conversation, five different jobs to do.

### [`02:56`](#t=176) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . CI - multi-claim, early CI
One in three Singaporeans contracts cancer. Leo's frame: it's not just *major* CI - early CI catches the diagnosis before it bankrupts the household, and **multi-claim** matters because a survivor of one CI is statistically much more likely to face another. Single-claim plans treat the first event as if it were the only event the client will ever have.

### [`06:10`](#t=370) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Hospitalisation - private vs public
Integrated Shield: ~$60/month for $2M private cover, ~$15/month for $1M public. The premium is small relative to a single ICU stay, and the cover resets - it's effectively unlimited claims for life once you're in. The catch: you must buy when healthy. Diagnosed first, you can't buy it at all.

### [`08:54`](#t=534) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Personal accident - the outpatient layer
PA reimburses up to $3k per accident and $750 per TCM visit at about $30/month. It plugs the gap that hospitalisation plans don't cover - GP, physio, food poisoning, dengue, insect bites, dental from accidents. Leo's package frame: ~$1M death/TPD + $200k early CI multi-claim + IS + PA, the "simple package" Leo quotes for most young professionals.

### [`13:24`](#t=804) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Why AIA, not an IFA
"AIA is the Rolex shop, IFAs are the department store." IFAs sell every brand because they earn on every brand; tied agents go deep on one product line because that's the only place to compete. The handoff that hurts clients is at claim time - one specialist with the system end-to-end is faster than five generalists handing off a file.

### [`16:04`](#t=964) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Why AIA, not a bank
Banks bundle insurance with mortgage and accounts - the relationship ends at the sale. Tied agents are paid through the lifetime of the policy, so the relationship *starts* at the sale. Even if the premium is $5/month higher, that's $1,200 over 20 years for a faster, cleaner claim and someone whose number you keep.

### [`20:56`](#t=1256) - [Day 8](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/8) . Freedom from the company plan
"My company has hospitalisation for me" sounds safe until the day it isn't. Lose the job, lose the cover. Get diagnosed mid-employment, and you're stuck in the role just to keep the policy. A personal plan is freedom of choice - to switch jobs, to take a sabbatical, to start something of your own without exposure following you around.

### [`23:22`](#t=1402) - [Day 8](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/8) . The freedom business . compounding income + EPS
Salaried careers are linear (hours x rate). This one is compounding - by Year 5 about 50% of income is passive, by Year 10 about 75%. The EPS scheme matches first-year commission for two years, so the strong-start case is real: front-load prospects before you join so the matching window isn't wasted. Pair this with infrastructure - CRM notes, weekly content, deliberate referrals - or activity decays.

### [`30:56`](#t=1856) - [Day 9](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/9) . Real claim moments + assets vs liabilities
The single mum whose CI plan paid for her child to stay in school. The retiree who didn't sell his home because his savings matured on time. The young family whose ICU stay would have bankrupted them without a hospital plan. Then the wider lens: the **poor accumulate liabilities** (cars, designer goods, lifestyle creep), the **rich accumulate assets** (insurance + systematic investments + their own business). Your job is to move the client one column over.

### [`38:58`](#t=2338) - [Day 10](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/10) . Your biggest purchase is freedom
"Yes, it's expensive. But what's the biggest purchase in your life?" It isn't the car or the wedding - it's financial freedom, and there's a number on it. Leo flips the conversation from price-comparison ("$5 cheaper at Bank X") to outcome-comparison ($2.4M vs $600k surrender value over 30 years on the same monthly outlay).

### [`43:54`](#t=2634) - [Day 11](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/11) . The Financial Freedom Number - live demo
Leo runs the calculator on screen: $5k/month today at 2.5% inflation becomes ~$13k/month at 65, which translates to a ~$2.4M lump-sum target over a 20-year retirement. Then the reframe: don't sell the lump sum, sell the *monthly income stream* that produces it. $1k/month invested systematically gets there; "save $5k/month" never starts.

### [`47:32`](#t=2852) - [Day 11](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/11) . Cashflow Quadrant + recruit from Day 1
E (employee), S (self-employed), B (business owner), I (investor). New FCs land in S - the goal is to cross to B (a producing team) and I (capital working without you). Three income streams: first-year commissions (where most quit), renewals (~2x first-year), overrides (recruiting). Day-1 referrals matter because every month delayed is a year of compounded team production never recovered.

### [`57:44`](#t=3464) - [Day 12](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/12) . The pyramid as a client menu
Leo screens the pyramid checklist live: term/whole life for death, standalone CI for income replacement, IS for hospital, PA for outpatient, ILP/endowment for accumulation, will + nomination + LPA + AMD for legacy. Read top to bottom in the meeting - tick off what's covered, name the gaps, name the level. The gap is the next conversation.

### [`63:56`](#t=3836) - [Day 12](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/12) . Real client portfolio restructure
A live before-and-after: a client moved his Prudential portfolio (Proactive, Provider, ProSaver, etc.) over to AIA at the same monthly outlay. Surrender value lifted from ~$600k to ~$2.2M, death cover from $300k to ~$500k, CI from ~$150k to $350k with multi-claim added. The takeaway isn't switching brands - it's that a competent policy summary surfaces structural waste most clients can't see in their own paperwork.

### [`68:56`](#t=4136) - Wrap-up . Don't oversell . the pyramid recap
Leo closes on the discipline: even when quota is tight, build bottom-up. The "common mistake" - selling Level 3 to a client with a Level 1 gap - destroys the relationship the moment the gap turns into a claim. Smaller, correct, and structured beats larger, mis-placed, and surrendered.
