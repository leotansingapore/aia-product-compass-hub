### [`00:04`](#t=4) - Why Week 2 builds on Week 1
Week 1 was the inner game. Week 2 is the **industry context** - what insurance is actually for, why AIA over the alternatives, why this career compounds when most don't. Leo's frame: skip Week 2 and you'll keep selling product features instead of the only thing clients actually buy, which is the **freedom** that sits on the other side of the protection map.

### [`00:28`](#t=28) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . The five purposes of insurance
"Insurance is insurance" is the misconception. There are **five distinct jobs** insurance does:
- **Death / TPD** - someone has to pay the bills when you're gone. ~10 years of income; Leo usually targets ~$1M minimum. A 20-something term plan does this for ~$70/month. Same cover on a whole life is ~$600-700/month - more expensive, but with cash value.
- **CI (income replacement)** - 5 years of income during recovery.
- **Hospitalisation** - the bill-payer.
- **Personal accident** - the outpatient layer.
- **Wealth accumulation** - ILPs, savings, endowment.

One client, one meeting, five different jobs to be aware of.

### [`02:56`](#t=176) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . CI - early CI and multi-claim
One in three Singaporeans contracts cancer. The two-concentric-circle frame: the inner circle is **major CI** (harder to claim), the outer is **early CI** (easier to claim). Most old whole-life policies cover only major CI, single claim. Two questions to ask the client about their existing plan: "if you claim once, can you still buy again? Can you still claim again?" The answer is no on both counts - which is why **multi-claim** matters. A cancer survivor is statistically much more likely to face another serious illness, and single-claim plans treat the first event as if it'll be the only one. Leo's line in the room: "my job isn't to make things cheap, it's to make things comprehensive."

### [`06:10`](#t=370) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Hospitalisation - private vs public
The framework to memorise: **$2M/year for private (~$60/month), $1M/year for public (~$15/month). Resets annually, covers 13 months before + 13 months after admission, unlimited claims for life, guaranteed renewable**. The "guaranteed" word is the load-bearing one - if you buy now and get diagnosed next year, you stay covered for life. If you wait and then get diagnosed, you cannot buy it at all. Leo's worry frame for the client: "if your hospital bill is anything more than $50k, I'm worried for you. The $2M cap is just for peace of mind - the point is you're covered, full stop, no matter what."

### [`08:54`](#t=534) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Personal accident - the outpatient layer
Death, CI, and hospitalisation only kick in on serious events. **PA fills the everything-else gap** - fractures, slips, falls, sprains, food poisoning, dengue, insect bites, dental from accidents. Up to ~$3K per accident, ~$750 per TCM visit, ~$30/month. **Unlimited claims.** Of all the claims Leo personally files for clients, PA is the most frequent - because the bills are small enough that clients don't bother claiming themselves. The package quote he uses for most young professionals: ~$1M death/TPD ($70/mo) + $200k early CI multi-claim ($120/mo) + IS public ($15/mo) + PA ($30/mo) = ~$250/month, roughly 5% of a $5k salary. Fully covered.

### [`13:24`](#t=804) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Why AIA, not an IFA
"AIA is the Rolex shop. IFAs are the department store." Chanel doesn't sell other brands; Rolex doesn't sell other brands; tied agents go deep on one product line because that's the only place they compete. IFAs sell every brand because they earn on every brand - which sounds neutral until claim time, when the agent is juggling claims processes across 5-10 different insurers and ends up effectively an order-taker. AIA-only agents know the system end-to-end. Leo's frame: "even if AIA is $5/month more expensive - that's $60/year, $1,200 over 20 years - you're buying the service relationship, not just the policy."

### [`16:04`](#t=964) - [Day 7](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/7) . Why AIA, not a bank
Bankers sell insurance (DBS = Manulife, OCBC = Great Eastern, UOB = Prudential, HSBC = HSBC) as a side-pull on whatever else the client walked in for - a mortgage, a savings account, a credit card. The classic scam: "sign up for this 20-year endowment to unlock the higher savings-account interest rate" - except the bonus rate is only for **year one**, and the endowment locks for 20 years at ~2.5% (about inflation). The relationship ends at the sale. Tied agents are paid through the lifetime of the policy, so **the relationship starts at the sale.** Bring this up specifically when restructuring a client's pro-wealth or true-wealth policy bought through a banker - because those are usually the ones that look fine until you put two numbers side by side.

### [`20:56`](#t=1256) - [Day 8](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/8) . Company insurance is freedom in reverse
"My company covers me" sounds safe until the day it isn't. Two failure modes: **(1)** Lose the job, lose the cover. **(2)** Get diagnosed while employed and you're now stuck in the role just to keep the policy - the cover becomes a leash. Leo's church friend got laid off, was between jobs, played sport, needed surgery, paid for it himself because the corporate cover walked out with the payslip. Even when the cover exists, it's usually capped per day, no major-CI lump sum, no multi-claim. The personal plan exists so the protection follows the person, not the employer.

### [`23:22`](#t=1402) - [Day 8](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/8) . The freedom business - compounding income + EPS
Salaried careers are linear (hours × rate). This one is compounding - by Year 5 about 50% of income is passive, by Year 10 about 75%. The trap: most quitters compare **Year 1 incomes**. The honest comparison is Year 5 - by then your peers are doing 5-10% annual raises while half your income shows up whether or not you turn on your phone. The EPS scheme matches first-year commission for two years - students at ~$2k/month matching, full-timers ~$3-4k/month, which means **first two years are the golden window**. Front-load prospecting *before* you even formally join so day one isn't a cold start. Then pair the matching with infrastructure - CRM notes, weekly content, post-meeting follow-ups - or the matching window goes to waste.

### [`30:56`](#t=1856) - [Day 9](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/9) . Real claim moments + assets vs liabilities
Three claim stories Leo uses with prospects: **(1)** the single mum whose CI plan paid for the child to stay in school after diagnosis; **(2)** the retiree who didn't have to sell his home because his savings plan matured on time; **(3)** the young family whose ICU stay would have wiped them out without the hospital plan. Then the bigger frame:
- **Poor** = income covers expenses, nothing left over.
- **Middle class** = income up, but liabilities up too (car loan, credit card, renovation loan) - net surplus stays close to zero.
- **Rich** = income comes from **assets**, not the salary.

The poor accumulate **liabilities** (cars, designer goods, lifestyle creep). The rich accumulate **assets** (insurance + systematic investments + their own business). Your job is to move clients one column over, one decision at a time.

### [`38:58`](#t=2338) - [Day 10](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/10) . The biggest purchase in life isn't the car
"Yes, it's expensive. But what's the biggest purchase in your life?" It isn't the car. It isn't the wedding. It isn't the house. It's **financial freedom** - and there's a number on it. The reframe flips the conversation from price-comparison ("$5 cheaper at Bank X") to outcome-comparison ($2.4M vs $600k surrender value over 30 years on the same monthly outlay). Once a prospect sees that number, the $5/month comparison stops mattering.

### [`43:54`](#t=2634) - [Day 11](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/11) . The Financial Freedom Number - live demo
Run it live on the calculator. Desired lifestyle income today, $4-5k/month → at 2.5% inflation, that's ~$13k/month at 65. Multiply by 12 × 20 years of retirement → ~**$2.4-3.1M lump-sum target**. Saving that in the bank at 5-6k/month is impossible for most people. Investing **$1k-$1.3k/month systematically** gets there. Then the second reframe: don't sell the $2.4M. Sell the **monthly stream** the $2.4M produces - withdraw ~$15k/month at 6% dividend, capital preserved, legacy intact. The frame that lands: **"income, capital, legacy - all three from the same number."**

### [`47:32`](#t=2852) - [Day 11](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/11) . Cashflow Quadrant + recruit from Day 1
Four quadrants: **E** (employee), **S** (self-employed), **B** (business owner), **I** (investor). New FCs land in S. The goal is to cross to B (a producing team) and I (capital working without you). Three income streams sit underneath:
- **FYC** - year 1 only. This is where most people quit.
- **Renewals** - ~2× FYC across years 2-6.
- **Overrides** - what you earn when you recruit and your team produces.

Day-1 referrals matter because every month delayed is a year of compounded team production never recovered. You don't need to be "good first" to recruit - the 60-day course trains them, not you.

### [`57:44`](#t=3464) - [Day 12](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/12) . The pyramid as a client menu
Walk the pyramid in the meeting like a checklist. **Level 1 (Protection)** - term/whole life for death, standalone CI for income replacement, IS for hospital, PA for outpatient. **Level 2 (Accumulation)** - ILP/endowment, retirement saver, SRS, CPF-OA investments. **Level 3 (Legacy)** - will, nomination of beneficiary, LPA, AMD. Read top to bottom, tick off what's covered, name the gap, name the level. **The gap is the next conversation.** This is also why the policy-summary template exists - it makes the gap visible in one page.

### [`63:56`](#t=3836) - [Day 12](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/12) . Real client portfolio restructure
The case Leo screens live. A client had a stack of Prudential policies - Proactive, Provider, Pro Saver, Pro Lifetime, Flexi Cash - paying about $13k/year combined. **Surrender value: ~$600k. Death cover: $300k. CI: ~$150k single-claim.** Restructured to AIA at the **same monthly outlay**: **Surrender value lifted to ~$2.2M. Death cover up to ~$500k. CI up to $350k with multi-claim added** (Flexi-term + UCC, ~$200/month for the CI piece alone). The lesson isn't "switch brands" - it's that a competent policy summary surfaces structural waste most clients can't see in their own paperwork. Most existing plans were sold years ago at the wrong layer, the wrong duration, or the wrong claim structure.

### [`68:56`](#t=4136) - Wrap-up . Don't oversell - build bottom-up
Leo closes on the discipline: even when quota is tight, build bottom-up. Selling Level 3 to a client with a Level 1 gap destroys the relationship the moment the gap turns into a claim. **Smaller, correct, structured beats larger, mis-placed, surrendered.** Week 3 turns the "freedom number" math into wealth-building principles - assets vs liabilities, recurring revenue, the 10X rule.
