### [`00:32`](#t=32) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Prospecting is the lifeblood
The single most important part of the business isn't the slide deck or the product knowledge - it's prospecting. Everything else is downstream. The week's whole arc starts here because if the top of the funnel is dry, none of the closing skill matters.

### [`00:39`](#t=39) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . The 30-to-60-day time lag
Prospecting always pays out on a delay. Kickstart the engine today and you do not get appointments next week or closes the week after. A sales cycle is at least two to three months, often six. Leo's line: "even if you run a billion dollars of ads today, you'll still not close next week." The advisors who struggle are the ones who expect this week's effort to land in this week's income.

### [`01:55`](#t=115) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . The seed simulation - spread, don't concentrate
Leo demos a toy simulator (hours, ad spend, conversations per week) to make the seed metaphor concrete. First one to two months you sink ~$10-11K of your time in and see absolutely zero out. A seed is still a seed - pouring more water and TLC on one seed does not make it sprout faster. The lever isn't depth on a few prospects, it's the *number* of seeds.

### [`03:40`](#t=220) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Don't miss the forest for the tree
The sweet spot is a wide, thin spread: Leo's VA texts 100-200 people every week, a little water on many seeds, not three-hour heart-to-hearts on one or two. Spread too thin and you can't follow up; concentrate too much and you starve the rest of the forest. Max out conversations - small ones count - across the biggest area you can still service.

### [`07:01`](#t=421) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . The cost is invisible for a month
Stop prospecting for one month and you won't feel it now - you feel it three months later. The flip side is the compounding upside: log every contact's birthday and milestones, wish five people a day, and by year five you're sitting on ~1,500 recurring birthdays a year. That becomes free lead generation - not from one wish, but from wishing the same person again and again until the timing is finally right.

### [`09:59`](#t=599) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Your income is set years in advance
"30 to 60 days" undersells it. Leo's leads are now essentially free because of contacts seeded years ago - some clients he never met before 2019, nurtured entirely by his VA's wishes. First contact to client isn't a 30-day window; it's a one-, two-, four-year compounding effect.

### [`10:16`](#t=616) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . A real big-fish case, by the numbers
A live example: a $100k PWV case collected that day. ~$18K in upfront comps, ~$30K with renewals - against $1-2K of ads and roughly 400 leads from last December, 399 of which rejected. One seed out of 400 sprouted, and it took six months of follow-up. That's a 10x ROI, and it only worked because they could afford to wait and had planted enough seeds.

### [`13:45`](#t=825) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Right place, right time = all places, all times
Clients buy when *their* timing arrives - the house finally sells, the T-bill matures, the CPF unlocks - not when your follow-up arrives. One prospect's wife bought a million-dollar plan from a roadshow on the spot, simply because it was the right moment. You can't engineer the moment, so you be everywhere, all the time, knowing you'll be in the wrong place 99% of the time to catch the 1%.

### [`14:57`](#t=897) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . The pre-RNF window
Before your RNF license is your most valuable window, because you can disarm completely: "I can't even sell you anything - this is literally my training assignment." That lowers the guard and plants the seed. By the time you're licensed you should already have a few people queued up, or at least saying "when I need something, I'll come find you."

### [`16:04`](#t=964) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Law of large numbers
Every 100 opening calls -> ~3 appointments -> ~1 meeting that actually happens -> roughly one close. If that math holds, the only real constraint is how fast you make approaches. That's why Leo called with two phones in university - if approaches are the bottleneck, double the approaches and you double your FYC. It's arithmetic, not heroics.

### [`17:26`](#t=1046) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Count rejections, not yeses
You can't control the number of yeses; you can control the number of rejections. So flip the scoreboard - "how many rejections can I get today?" Each rejection is a rep, a battle scar, and a step closer to the next yes. If you're scared of rejection, the cure is to go collect more of it.

### [`18:34`](#t=1114) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Serve, don't sell - and own it
Don't outsource the result to "leads are bad, market is bad." Own the inputs: too few calls, weak script. And reframe the activity as service - the policy summary genuinely helps people get a bird's-eye view of their finances, so reaching out is helping, not self-serving. If someone can't benefit, move on to someone who can.

### [`19:21`](#t=1161) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Touch points and the leaking pipeline
Non-negotiable: 5-10 touch points every single day. Every group chat, every saved contact, every important date is a lead - not now, maybe in 5 or 10 years. Plant even when it feels like the wrong time, because one of those days it'll be the right one. Activity comes before skill: more openings produce more skill and knowledge, not the other way round.

### [`23:14`](#t=1394) - [Day 19](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/19) . Tonality and energy on the call
Sky sets a lot of appointments because his energy reads high - and people can sense energy down the phone. Call like a friend, smile, don't go heavy or over-serious or you scare them off. Energy is a real KPI: it's why Leo protects his smoothie, his walk, his exercise. Selling is a transfer of conviction and enthusiasm - your energy has to sit above the prospect's, because no one buys from someone who sounds like they don't believe their own product.

### [`26:13`](#t=1573) - [Day 20](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/20) . Time efficiency - batch your tasks
New FCs spend the most time on prep, planning and admin and the least on revenue-generating work. Leo's model inverts that: ~90% on prospecting. The trick is batching - when a small admin task lands at 10:00, 11:00, 11:30, don't action each one and shatter your focus. Capture it to a list, ignore it, then clear all the similar small tasks (finance, chores, errands) in one focused one-hour block. "Collect them like Pokemon" all week, then clear in batches - that's how you protect the big block of deep work.

### [`33:26`](#t=2006) - [Day 20](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/20) . The 2-minute, one-touch rules
The batching rule has a sharp exception: if something takes under two minutes, do it now. File a 2-minute task away and you'll re-read it across three days until it has cost you ten. The one-touch rule: when you see it, decide on the spot - do it, calendar it, delegate it, defer it, or delete it. Never look at the same email twice without action, or the little leaks compound into missed important things.

### [`34:54`](#t=2094) - [Day 22](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/22) . Done beats perfect - ship the MVP
Past a point there are diminishing returns on polish. If Leo had waited until the activity tracker or the expense app were perfect, no one would ever have used them. The presenter, the academy, the checklists all started messy and got built up over time with Lovable and vibe-coding. Velocity and reps beat a perfect launch - imperfect-and-shipped is how you actually improve.

### [`37:19`](#t=2239) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . Closing - rapport has a 10-minute ceiling
Rapport matters but lasts maybe 5-10 minutes max. You cannot make someone like you in an hour, and a longer chat does not buy a close - liking is built over months and years, not in one meeting. So cap the rapport phase and move to the agenda. The total wealth concept and Ghost Mapper are the framework that keeps the hour on track.

### [`38:29`](#t=2309) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . The phases of the appointment
Every appointment has a structure. First five minutes: build rapport, get them into a good state - then move on. Don't stretch a Zoom opening to an hour expecting a heart-to-heart to close them; after a few months they won't remember whether the call was 5 or 30 minutes. Finish the agenda in the first appointment instead of dragging it to a slow second meeting.

### [`39:30`](#t=2370) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . Create the gap, then the desire
Set a target that's a stretch - "$3M for retirement," or "$100-200k of coverage" - then show where they are today. The space between is the problem you get them to acknowledge and want to solve. Then sell the *concept*, never the product spec: not "an ILP at $200/month" but what it does for them. Not a multi-pay CI rider, but coverage you can claim again and again. People buy the benefit and the association (confidence, security), the way a perfume ad sells power, not liquid.

### [`41:33`](#t=2493) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . Closing techniques - this or that
Don't ask "do you want to buy?" Ask "would you prefer A or B?" Use urgency where it's real (dividend lock-in dates). And assume the close: when the signals are there, just proceed to the application, fill it in as if they're signing.

### [`42:43`](#t=2563) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . The iPad-and-pen close
Leo says he has *never* asked a client "are you ready to move forward?" He puts the iPad and pen in front of them - "help me check your details are correct, here's the health questionnaire, sign here to acknowledge" - and 99% of the time they just sign. Don't feel paiseh. If they're guarded you'll know; if they say nothing, proceed as though everything is normal. Plus the rapport mechanics that carry through: mirror what they say, use humour, and pause to let them fill the silence.

### [`44:42`](#t=2682) - [Day 21](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/21) . Don't fall into the knowledge trap
You will never memorise every fund and welcome bonus, and you don't need to. Overloading a prospect guarantees they decide nothing - the brain has limited capacity, and confusion kills the close. Keep the opening short: 30 minutes max on a Zoom, 15-20 even better. People appreciate someone who distils the noise. And ask questions that don't make them feel dumb - "on a scale of 1-10, how well do you know your CPF Life?" beats "have you done retirement planning?", and always give a reason the question matters (that's how Leo asks for birthdays).

### [`48:28`](#t=2908) - [Day 22](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/22) . Schedule your priorities
Schedule your priorities; don't prioritise your schedule. Block the calendar, review the week, put the important things in *first*. As a self-employed advisor the calendar is your bible - Leo genuinely doesn't know what tomorrow holds until he opens it. The appointment setting and texting that fill it are run by his VA, which is the eventual end-state: enough volume first, then hire.

### [`50:11`](#t=3011) - [Day 22](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/22) . Lower the inertia - the 10-minute start
Perfectionism is inertia; an MVP lowers it. So does shrinking the commitment: "I'll just do 10 minutes." Ten minutes becomes twenty becomes forty once the ball is rolling - the same momentum trick that makes a "10-minute" scroll become an hour, applied to good habits instead.

### [`50:58`](#t=3058) - [Day 23](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/23) . Feel-good productivity - play, power, people
So you can last 12 months, build in three things. **Play:** gamify it - the points, the corgi outfits, the coins exist to make the grind fun; add stakes ("if I don't hit five appointments I pay you $100"). Treating it as a game makes you more resilient to failure and more willing to experiment - go all out the way you would in Maple Story, take rejection with a pinch of salt.

### [`51:03`](#t=3063) - [Day 23](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/23) . People - do it together
The second energiser is people. Schedule the work with others: come to the office or the house, run in the morning, work in the afternoon, Bishan Park at night. Doing the activity alongside teammates makes the long days enjoyable and keeps you showing up.

### [`54:04`](#t=3244) - [Day 23](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/23) . Remove the blockers
Three blockers. **Procrastination** comes from negative emotion (fear, "too hard") - shake it off, change environment. **Paralysis** comes from too many options - just make a bad decision now; often it turns out to be the right one. **Perfectionism** - 80% is enough; ship it.

### [`54:46`](#t=3286) - [Day 23](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/23) . Conserve, recharge, align
Not everything needs 1,000%. There's no perfect appointment - do enough and that's fine; don't drench one tree and starve the forest. Real rest is sleep, walking, exercise - not scrolling. Leo blocks five hours every Saturday for church as genuine rest that recharges the whole week. And check alignment: when the work feels like helping people toward their goals it sustains you; when it feels like FYC and EPS every minute, it drains you.

### [`57:51`](#t=3471) - [Day 23](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/23) . 60 days is a sprint, the career is decades
The first two months are a sprint, but zoom out and a month with no sales is negligible across a career measured in decades. "When in doubt, zoom out." Run the daily check: which energiser today - play, power or people? which blocker - procrastination, paralysis or perfectionism? and the sustainers - did I spend energy wisely, rest enough, stay aligned?

### [`58:38`](#t=3518) - [Day 24](/learning-track/first-60-days/day/24) . Big rocks first
Close on the time-management principle: put the big rocks in the jar first, then the small rocks fit around them. Do it the other way and the big rocks never go in. Prioritise the most important thing - prospecting, openings, closing - and let the small stuff fill the gaps.
